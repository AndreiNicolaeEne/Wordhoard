Right, I'll tell you about the night I had to get my friend home, cos it still makes me laugh telling it and it still turns my stomach a bit an' all, thinking what could've gone wrong.

By the end of that night my friend was in some state, I'm telling you. Proper gone. Eyes half shut, head lolling about like it weren't attached right, grinning at nothing. Everyone round the fire was still having a laugh, still passing the can round, but I'd clocked long before then that my friend had gone past having a laugh and into somewhere else altogether. The first sign, the one that told me it had actually tipped over, was when my friend went to stand up off the step to go and get another drink and just folded. Straight down, like the legs had been took out from under. Not fell over drunk-daft the way you do for a laugh, but really went, like the legs just weren't answering no more. And then when I hauled my friend back up and said summat, the words come out all soft round the edges, running into each other, no shape to them at all. That's when I knew. You can tell a man's just merry by the talk still making sense even if it's loud. This weren't that no more.

Me: Right, come on now, that's you done, we're getting you home.

My friend: I'm fine. I'm fine. Don't you dare, I'm having a good divvus, best divvus I've had in ages, don't you dare take me home.

Me: You can't stand up on your own two feet, mush, that's not fine.

My friend: I can. Watch. Watch this.

And then straight down again, onto the same bit of ground, and everyone round the fire laughing their heads off at it, and me not finding it half so funny cos I could see where this was going if I left it be. Took a good while to talk my friend round to leaving. Kept saying it was early, kept saying one more wouldn't hurt, kept trying to get up and go find the can again. In the end I just got an arm right round and hauled, told my friend the fire was dying down anyway and everyone else was for their beds soon, which was a lie but it done the job, and off we went.

Whole way back I had my arm round my friend, proper round, not just resting there for show, cos without it my friend would've gone down in the road. Every few steps a lean the wrong way and I'd have to haul back the other way to keep us both up. Talking the whole time an' all, my friend, not a word of it making sense, on about someone from years back, on about the grai they used to have when they were a chavo, on and on, half of it rokker that went nowhere and trailed off into nothing.

Had to stop once along the way, down by the wall near the shop, cos my friend went grey all sudden and said give us a minute, and then was sick right there, holding onto the wall with both hands and me holding the collar back out the way. Sat down proper after that, right down on the kerb, and I let my friend sit a good few minutes to get some colour back before trying to get up again.

A bloke come past while we was sat there, some fella walking his dog, and he give us a right look, up and down, the sort of look that says he's seen worse but not much worse. Didn't say nothing, just walked on a bit quicker with his juk trotting after him, but you could see him deciding what sort we were from the state of us on that kerb, and I didn't much care by that point, too busy keeping my friend upright.

Got up in the end and carried on, and somewhere in there, I swear to you, in all that fuddled state, we went the wrong way entire. Took a turn I shouldn't have took, cos my friend was leaning hard one way and pulling me with, and we ended up down past the wrong row before I clocked it and turned us back round. Add ten minutes onto a walk that should've took five, both of us going the long way for no reason at all except my friend couldn't walk a straight line and I was more bothered about holding him up than looking where we was going.

Tried to get some water in my friend along the way an' all, had a bottle in my bag, and first go my friend wouldn't have none of it. Turned his head away like a right chavo refusing his hobben, saying he weren't thirsty, saying water was for gorjas and he didn't want none. Had to near enough beg before he took a mouthful, and then once he'd had that first bit he couldn't get enough of it, drinking like he'd never seen water before in his life.

Somewhere along that walk, my friend went all soft and sad of a sudden, the way they do, and grabbed hold of my arm proper tight and said the one thing that only ever comes out of a man when he's this far gone.

My friend: You know I love you, don't you. You know that. You're the best mush I ever had in my life and I don't tell you enough, I don't ever tell you.

Me: I know, I know. Come on now, nearly there.

Course I knew already, and course he'd never say a word of it sober, but it come out of him right there in the road like it was the most important thing in the world to get said before we went another step.

Got to the door eventually and that was its own battle. Key wouldn't go in straight for my friend's shaking hand, and then when I took it off him and got it in, my friend went and leant the whole weight against the door so it wouldn't push open, and near enough fell through it when it finally give. Managed to get him in and down onto the couch, more dropped than laid, and he was out near enough before his head hit it.

My friend: Never again. That's it. Never touching another drop, I mean it, never again, you tell everyone I said it.

Me: Right you are. Never again.

Neither of us believed a word of it, not even him, not even as he said it, and we both knew that even in the state he was in. But it's a kind thing to say all the same, and a kind thing to hear said back to you, so I said it back like I believed it.

Went round the next divvus to check on him, and he was in a right state still, holding his head, asking what happened. Didn't remember hardly a thing past the fire dying down, so I filled it in for him bit by bit, the fall, the walk, the wrong turn, the water, all of it, and he went red as anything hearing his own carry-on told back to him, specially the soft bit he'd come out with about loving me, which he swore blind he never said and I swore blind he did. Laughed about it after, the pair of us, the way you do when it's turned out alright in the end, but I told him straight, next time he goes that far I'm not walking him the long way round again, and he just laughed and said there wouldn't be a next time, and we both knew that one for a lie an' all.
