Three of them sat out by the vardo of an evening, kettle just off the boil, and the talk went where it always goes.

The eldest: Kettle's not long done, mush, get yourself a mug before it goes cold.

The second: Ta. Cold's about all I feel lately anyway, this weather don't do my back no favours.

The eldest: Back's nothing next to what I've got. My knees been at me going on ten year now. Can't get down to see to the fire proper no more, one of the chavvies has to do it for me, and I stood there dinlo, watching, not able to do a hand's turn for myself.

The second: Ten year, is that all. You want what I've got. Three, four divvus a month I'm up half the night with it, can't lie one side nor the other, can't get comfortable however I turn myself. Costs me my sleep proper, that does, and there's no hobben nor drop of tea fixes it neither.

The third: The pair of you don't know you're born, and that's the truth of it. I've had mine longer than either of you's known me, longer than this atchin tan's been stood here even. Older than the two of you put together, near enough.

The second: Go on then, tell us, what is it you've got that's so bad.

The third: You'd have to dick at me proper to believe it. Been rokkering about it that long nobody round here listens no more.

The eldest: So who's got it worst then, out the three of us. Go on, say it straight, none of you dodging it now.

The second: Not me admitting nothing.

The third: Nor me. I'll take mine to the grave before I hand either of you the satisfaction of it.

The eldest: Kushti, we'll all three of us go on same as we are then, moaning to the gorja doctors and not believing a word they tell us neither.

And they all three of them laughed, same as always, nobody having won nothing, and nobody about to.
