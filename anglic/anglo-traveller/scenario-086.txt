Now then. You wasn't there so I'm going to tell you the whole of it, far as I can piece it back together, cause half of it's gone off like smoke and the other half I wish had gone with it.

It was my cousin's engagement, down at the atchin tan where her lot's pulled in for the summer, and you know what it's like when that family gets going, there's no such thing as one drink and home by ten. I said I'd only stop a couple of hours. That lasted about as long as it takes to say it. The mother of the house had cooked up something shocking, proper hobben, more of it than any dozen mush could get through, and every time my plate went anywhere near empty somebody's aunt was there with the spoon again. Go on, have a bit more, you're not eating. I wasn't going to say no to that, was I. One more helping, then one more after that, and I'm not even counting the drink alongside it.

The drink's where it all starts going soft round the edges. There was a fire going and someone had a box out playing all the old ones, and the chavvies were still up dancing round everyone's legs long after they should've been put down, little dinlos giggling themselves silly on lemonade while the grown ones got stuck into something a lot stronger. Last thing I can properly swear to is standing by that fire with a can in my hand, rokkering away to my cousin's intended about nothing at all, laughing fit to burst over I couldn't even tell you what now. After that it's only bits. A flash of dancing on the grass with somebody's shoe gone missing, mine or somebody else's I couldn't say. A flash of being sat on the step of a vardo with the whole world tilting a bit sideways, somebody's arm round me keeping me upright. A flash of singing something at the top of my voice that I can only hope nobody got the words of proper. And that's your lot, that's the whole reel, and God knows what happened in the gaps between the flashes.

I know I stopped up long past when I told myself I'd jal home. I remember thinking, right, this is the last one, I'm off after this, more than the once. Didn't happen. The night just kept giving and I kept taking it.

Woke up the next divvus like I'd been hit by a grai. Head going like somebody was in there with a hammer, proper pounding, and the second I even thought the word breakfast my stomach turned right over on me. The light coming in the window felt like it had it in for me personal, and every little sound, a door, a kettle, some juk barking off down the field, went through my skull like a knife. Just getting myself sat up in the bed took the sort of effort you'd want paying for. I lay there a minute just breathing, thinking, why do I do this to myself.

My aunt was up long before me, moving about the place normal as anything, kettle on, breakfast going, like the night before hadn't touched her at all, and the second she clapped eyes on the state of me she couldn't help herself.

Aunt: Well look what's crawled out. You look like something the juk wouldn't even bother dragging in.

Me: Don't. Just don't.

Aunt: I'm only saying. You was singing fit to wake the gavvers last night, you know that.

Me: I do not want to know what I was singing.

Aunt: Kushti, cause I couldn't tell you either, it weren't in any language I know.

She weren't being cruel with it, just having her bit of fun, and fair play, I'd have done the very same to her. But I could've done without it just then, lying there feeling like death warmed through, and her stood there bright as a button with a cup of tea like nothing in the world was wrong.

I made myself a promise right there and then, flat on my back staring at the roof of that room, that I was never letting a night run off with me like that again. Never again, I told myself, proper meant it and all, lying there. Course I know full well that's not the first divvus I've sworn that particular oath, and it probably won't be the last, but you do mean it at the time, don't you, every single time.

Getting up and doing anything after that was a whole other matter. Normal day I'd walk down the lane and back without giving it a thought, but that morning it might as well have been a mile up a hill. Even the little chore of sorting a few bits out round the place felt like too much of an ask, and I ended up just sat there most of the morning not doing a blessed thing, moving about as much as one of the juks stretched out in the sun. Managed a bit of plain bread down me eventually, nothing with any fat or spice near it, just enough to give my stomach something to settle round, and kept myself quiet and still as I could till the worst of it eased off.

And here's the bit that keeps coming back and needling me. Somewhere in one of them flashes I said something to my cousin's intended, harmless enough at the time I'm sure, but from the little bit I can piece together it wasn't kind, might've been about his family, might've been a joke that landed all wrong, I honestly can't be certain what I said, only that the look on his face after tells me it wasn't good. I owe that mush an apology and I know it, and I keep turning it over, going hot in the face every time it comes back to me, wondering exactly what I come out with and how bad it really was. Haven't found the nerve yet to go and say my piece proper, but I will, I'll have to, can't be leaving it sit.

By the evening I was starting to feel like myself again, thank God, steady enough on my feet to eat a proper plate without my stomach turning on it, thinking clear again instead of everything coming at me through cotton wool. Sat by the fire that night same as the one before, only this time with a cup of tea instead of a can, and thinking, well, that's that, lesson learned.

Only I know it isn't, not really. There's always another divvus coming, another one of the family celebrating something, and I know myself well enough by now to know I'll say yes to the one more helping and the one more drink all over again, and swear the very same oath the morning after, lying in the very same state. That's just how it goes with our lot I reckon. Anyway. Wish you'd been there for the good bit of it at least.
