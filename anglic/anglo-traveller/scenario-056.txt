Seven divvus I done, minding that house for the people what own it while they was off somewhere hot, and I'll tell you now, I'd sooner have took the worst pitch on the worst atchin tan in the pouring rain than another week of it, straight up.

My mother didn't want me to go at all.

Mother: What you want going and sitting in some gorja's house for, on your own, away from your own for a whole week?

Me: It's good wonga, and it's only seven divvus. I'll manage.

Well. First night in there and I already knew there was something not right about the place. Big quiet house, nothing moving in it but me, and every little tick and settle of the pipes going through me like it meant something. I'm laid up in this bed that's too soft, can't get comfortable in it at all, listening to the boiler clicking on and off in the dark like it's trying to say something to me, and I'm thinking, a house didn't ought to sound like that when there's no one talking in it. Didn't sleep proper the whole night, kept dicking at the door like something was going to come through it.

Second divvus the juk got out. Little snappy terrier thing, and all I done was open the back door a crack to shake a mat, and it was gone through my legs like it was shot out a gun. Up the lane after it in me socks, hollering, half the street looking at me like I was a proper dinlo, and it took the best part of an hour, chasing that thing over walls and through next door's garden and back again, before I got a hold of its collar down by the bins. Brung it in shaking worse than what I was.

Then the garden started creeping up on me, slow, so as I never even noticed it happening till it was near done. Tubs of tomatoes out the back, and I'm watering them same as I was shown, but by the third or fourth divvus the leaves is going yellow round the edge and curling in on theirselves, and every divvus a bit worse, and I hadn't the first idea what I'd done wrong nor how I was going to tell the woman of the house I'd let her tomatoes go and rot.

Middle of the week the washing machine give up on me. Filled up with water and wouldn't drain, sat there going grey and stinking, and I'm stood kicking it soft like that's going to mend anything. Had to bail the whole lot out with a jug into the sink, on me knees an hour doing it, and the hobben spoiling in the fridge behind me because I hadn't the time to see to dinner, swearing away at a machine like it could hear a word I was saying to it.

No warning at all, there's a knock at the door and it's some mush I never clapped eyes on before, wanting to know is the woman in, he's after borrowing summat off her. I says she's away, and he's looking me up and down proper, like he's working out have I any business being there at all.

Mush at the door: And who might you be, then?

Me: I'm just minding the place for her while she's away.

He went off funny after that, not sure whether to believe a word of it, and I shut the door on him quick as I could and stood with my back to it a minute.

Same divvus near enough, my heart went right up in my mouth. Couldn't find the little key off the ring, the one what unlocks the shed, and I turned that kitchen over top to bottom looking for it, every drawer, every coat pocket, the lot, sure I'd gone and lost it for good and would have to stand there and tell them I'd chor'd nothing off them but ruined the only spare key there was in the house. Found it in the end down the side of the settee, must have jumped clean out my pocket sat there of an evening. Sat down proper shaking after that one, I don't mind saying.

Locked meself out an all, one afternoon. Only went out the front for two minutes to the corner shop and the wind took the door and it swung to behind me, and that was that. Hours I was, up and down that path, round the block twice, knocking on doors nobody answering, before a neighbour woman took pity and had me sit in hers with a cup of tea while her husband come round and got me in through a window at the side, scraped my arm something shocking doing it.

There was one window I'd left open meself, upstairs in the little box room, and a proper storm come in the night, rain going sideways, and I never gave it a thought till morning and the carpet in there soaked clean through, curtains dripping down the wall, water got in under the sill and marked the paint something terrible. Sixth divvus I finally got the better of that machine. Pulled the manual out from under the sink, found the little filter blocked solid with coppers and fluff and a hair grip of all things, cleared it proper, and it drained lovely after that, no more bother out of it at all. Last divvus before they was due back I was cleaning like a woman possessed. Hoovering, wiping every surface twice over, turning the tomato tubs round so the worst side weren't the first thing anyone's eye fell on, touching up that mark on the paint best I could, hoping to God there weren't no sign left on the lawn where I'd chased that juk round in circles, wonga's worth of candles burning to shift the smell of burnt dinner out the kitchen.

Woman of the house: Nothing happen while we was gone?

Me: Kushti as anything. Not a thing.
