Right so, this plot I've had going on three year now, down off the lane near the atchin tan, and this past year was the best and the worst of it all rolled in one, I'll tell it to you straight from the start.

Come the end of March I was sat of an evening with the mother working out what was going in, same as every year, and she's saying put beans in early and I'm saying no, ground's still cold as a gavver's heart, wait it out. In the end we split it down the middle. Put the spuds and the onions in first, they don't mind a bit of chill, and held the beans and the toms back a few weeks after the last frost had properly gone, which round here you never trust till well into April, not really. My old fella always said don't jal in with your seed till the blackthorn's blown over, and he weren't wrong, not once in all the years.

Once it was in the ground we had a right kushti run of weather, three, four weeks of soft warm days and just enough rain of a night to keep it ticking, and everything come up lovely. The onions was thick as your thumb inside a month, the spuds was through and earthed up twice before May was out. I'll not lie, I was dead pleased with myself walking down there of a morning, dick at it all coming up green in rows, thinking this is going to be a proper divvus's work this year and no mistake.

Then the greenfly got on the beans. Come from nowhere, one day nothing, next day the whole top of the plants was black with them, curling the leaves right up. I tried the soapy water first, spraying it on of an evening like my nan showed me, and it knocked some back, fair play, but it never cleared them proper, they was back within the week. Tried marigolds after that, put a row in alongside to draw them off, and between the two it kept them down to where the plants could still crop, but I never did beat them outright. Just learned to live alongside it, same as most things.

Then come July and it turned dry, properly dry, no rain to speak of for near six week. I was down that plot every single divvus hauling water by the can, two trips of a morning and two more of an evening if it was really baking, just to keep the toms and the beans from keeling over. Back was in bits by the end of it. The mush on the next plot along, he was doing the same, and there's only the one tap between six plots, so it weren't long before that turned into a proper ruck.

Neighbour: You've had that tap running near an hour, some of us have got to get home an' all.

Me: I'll be done when I'm done, my beds are dry as a bone same as yours.

Neighbour: It ain't just your water, is it. Committee said turns, not whoever gets down here first.

We went round it a good few times, both of us proper wound up, him saying I was hogging it every divvus and me saying his lot come down whenever they pleased and never once asked. Got that heated one evening the fella from three plots up had to come over and calm the both of us before it turned into something neither of us wanted. In the end we sat down proper and worked a rota out between us, chalked on the shed, him mornings before eight, me evenings after six, and whoever else wanted a go could slot round it. Once it was written down plain there weren't no more of it. He's alright really, that mush, just same as me, frighted of losing a whole bed of work to the dry.

Best thing I grew all year, no argument, was the runner beans. Once the greenfly settled down they went mad, up the canes quick as anything, and I was picking carrier bags full twice a week right through August, giving them away to half the row because there weren't no way the family could eat that many. That was a proper win, that.

Wasn't so kushti for the raspberries though. Birds had the lot of them near enough before I'd even got down there of a morning to look. Put netting over in the end but by then they'd had the best of the crop already, blackbirds mostly, bold as anything, didn't care two bits that I was stood right there. Learned my lesson there for next year, net it before it colours up, not after.

Then the storm come through, middle of August that was, proper wind and rain of a night, and I got down there next morning to find near half my sweetcorn flat on the ground and the runner bean canes half pulled over an' all. Stood there looking at it thinking well that's that then. But you don't just leave it. Spent the best part of two divvus staking the corn back up, tying it in bundles of three or four stalks so it'd hold each other, and rebuilding the bean frame proper this time with the posts driven in deeper. Lost a fair bit of the corn, snapped clean at the base and no saving that, but what I got back up mostly came good in the end, just a few week behind itself.

Come the finish of it, when I sat down and tallied the lot at the end of the season, it come to more than I reckoned for going into it, what with the aphids and the dry and the storm and all. Good haul of spuds, onions kept right through into winter in the shed, beans enough to give half away, and the corn that made it back up gave a fair crop after all that. Swapped a sack of the spuds and a good few jars of chutney I'd made off the toms to the mort with the plot up the top, she does poultry, for a dozen eggs a week right through the back end of the year, which suited the both of us better than either of us asking wonga for it.

Cleared it all down end of October, dug the manure in, covered what needed covering, and left it to rest for the winter same as always. Looking back on the whole of it, it was a hard old year in bits, the dry near broke me and that row with the neighbour I could've done without, but you put a lot of work in and you got a lot back, and that's about as much as anyone can ask of a plot. Kushti year, all told, for all the aggravation in the middle of it.
