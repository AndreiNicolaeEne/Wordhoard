Sit yourself down, mush, because you weren't there for none of it and I want you hearing it proper, start to finish, not the half tale you'll get off anyone else round the fire.

I opened me gob about it before I'd trained a single divvus. Stood right there at the atchin tan and told the lot of them I was going to run the big one, the marathon, twenty-six mile and however many more they tack on the end of it just to finish you off good and proper. Never done a step of training in me life before that, not a proper run, nothing past chasing a chavo who'd nicked summat off the fire. But I'd said it out loud in front of the mother, the cousin, half the chavvies on site stood round with their tea going cold, so there weren't no quiet way of backing out after that. You say a thing like that out loud among your own and it's said, done, no taking it back.

First few weeks near finished me. Body had no idea what was being asked of it. I'd go out them mornings, whatever the weather was doing, the juk off next door setting up the second I stepped out the vardo, letting the whole site know I was up and about whether I wanted them to or not. Come back wrecked in a way I never known off a day's graft, and I've done proper graft, mush, tarmac and scrap and the lot. Every session left me worse than the one before, legs like they weren't mine no more, and I'd lie there after wondering what I'd gone and told everyone I was going to do.

But you keep on. You settle into it. Rain, cold, mood black as anything, don't matter, you jal out that door because you said you would and there's people watching to see if you do. So I kept to it most days, same route, same time near enough, and it started to feel less like dying and more like just hard, which I suppose is progress of a sort.

Then it near stopped altogether. Hurt meself bad, the leg, right through the middle of it, and training stopped dead for weeks. Laid up, no good for nothing, and there was a good stretch there I thought that was that, thought I'd be the dinlo who told the whole site I was running a marathon and then never even started it proper. That was the worst of it in some ways, worse than any of the running, lying there not knowing if I'd get back to it at all.

When I did get back to it I went at it different. Careful, like breaking a green grai in slow instead of jumping straight on and hoping for the best. Cut the plan right back, none of the mad mileage I'd been doing before, just steady, sensible, listening to what me own body was telling me instead of what some sheet of paper said I should be doing.

Round about then the cousin started coming out with me. Had the shoes and everything, said he fancied it too though I don't think he ever meant to run the thing himself, just wanted the company. And that made all the difference, mush, more than I'd have thought. The hard mornings, the ones where you'd never have got out that door on your own, you get up because you know he's stood there waiting, and you don't want to be the one who don't show.

Cousin: You coming or what, I ain't stood here all divvus.
Me: Give us a minute, I'm half asleep still.
Cousin: Should've jal'd to bed earlier then, shouldn't you. Dick at the state of you.

Silly little rokker like that got me through more mornings than any training plan did.

One day stands out off the rest of them, and it near ended the whole thing outright. Long run, further than I'd gone before, and about two-thirds in me legs just went, and me head went with them. Sat down on a bank at the side of the road, couldn't hardly get up, wanted to chor a lift off the first van that passed rather than walk another step. Cousin near had to talk me back onto me feet. Body and will both give out together that divvus, and I didn't know which one was worse.

The middle of the whole season was the worst for doubt and all. The finish was that far off it didn't feel real, just a word people kept saying at me, and some mornings I couldn't picture meself ever getting there, couldn't picture it being an actual thing that happened to me rather than something I'd said once and would have to live down for good.

Then, not long after, a session went kushti. Nothing big, no fireworks, just a run that felt easy where it should've been hard, and that was enough. Renewed the whole thing in me head. If one could go like that, maybe the actual divvus itself wouldn't be so bad after all.

Toward the end I tightened everything right up. Watched the hobben proper for the first time in me life, mind on rest same as running, and in that last week I backed right off, hardly ran at all, which felt wrong after months of the opposite. The mother kept saying, the work's done now, love, no good chor-ing extra out of one week that months of graft already give you, so I left it be and trusted what was already in me.

Night before I never slept, hardly a wink. Lay there in the vardo going over the whole route in me head a hundred times before there was any need to, gorja radio going on downstairs about the weather for the morning, me just staring at the roof waiting for it to be light.

The feat itself, then. Went alright for a long stretch, better than I'd feared, some mort at one of the water stations shouting me on like she knew me from somewhere, a fella marshalling further up stood there directing gorja traffic like he fancied himself a gavver. I thought maybe it'd all be easy after everything I'd put in. It weren't. The last stretch, the very end of it, that's where it near broke me proper. Everything in me wanting to stop, every last bit of me, legs done, head done, and I never did stop, kept the legs going one after the other because stopping felt worse somehow than the pain of not stopping.

And then it was done. And I'll tell you straight, mush, it weren't what I'd pictured, not even close. No great feeling washing over me, none of that. Just stood there, sort of empty, half wanting to sit down and half not sure me legs would let me get up again if I did. Took a good while before it felt like anything at all, before it felt kushti even.

Body took days to come back to being mine. Couldn't hardly get down the step of the vardo the next divvus, everything locked up solid, and it was the better part of a week before I felt like meself again, walking normal, not wincing going down them steps of a morning.

But I'll tell you what it done, that whole season. It changed what I reckon I can do. Before that I'd have said there's things gorja do, things other people do, marathons and the like, not us, not me. Now I know different. I know what I can put meself through when I've said I would out loud and there's people watching to see if I do. That's worth more to me than any bit of wonga they'd have charged me to enter the thing in the first place, and more than the medal they hand you at the end, daft little thing on a ribbon, but it ain't the medal. It's knowing, proper knowing, not just hoping, that when it comes right down to it I don't stop.
