Right, gather round chavvies, I'll give it you straight, the whole of it, don't want no half tale.

Never slept a wink the night before for the nerves, and I was up and out the door hours before I needed be, walking down past the atchin tan while it were still dark, thinking what if I'm the dinlo who can't do nothing right on his first divvus.

Got there dead early and there's this mush stood by the gate having a smoke, turns out he's the one what runs the place. Give us a good look up and down, kushti enough about it though, said come on then, showed us where to hang me coat and that were that.

First job he handed me was shifting stock round the yard all morning, and I'd reckoned it'd be some fiddly little job, sorting bits and bobs, but no, straight into the heavy graft, more than what I'd braced meself for.

Then about mid morning didn't I go and drop a whole stack of it, right in front the lot of them, proper shown up I was, face like a beetroot. One of the older fellas just laughed it off easy, said everyone does that their first divvus in, don't fret over it.

Come the break I sat back with me tea and had a proper dick at who was who round that yard, sizing the lot of them up, who's sound and who ain't worth the rokker.

There was one mort on the line never said two words to no one else all morning, but she come over quiet and showed me the knack of stacking it so it don't come down again, never even asked, just seen I was struggling and helped us out.

Pace of the whole thing were faster than what I'd thought, hardly a minute standing about, straight through nine hour near enough without a proper stop.

Walking home after I couldn't stop going over it all in me head, every last bit, the mush at the gate, the dropped stack, the mort what helped us.

Father: Well? How'd it go then?

Me: Kushti, Dad. Hard graft and no mistake, but kushti. I'll manage it fine.
