Me: Now then! Never thought I'd clock you here, not in a month of Sundays.

Cousin: Look who it is an' all! How long's it been, eh? Must be donkeys' years since I seen your face.

Me: Long enough, cousin. You're looking kushti on it though, whatever you're at these days.

Cousin: Can't complain, can't complain. Same old same old, jal here, jal there, bit of dealing, bit of graft. You still up your way, still on the same atchin tan?

Me: Aye, same spot, far as they'll let us stop these divvuses. Gavvers move you on quick as look at you now, worse than it ever was.

Cousin: Tell me about it. Anyway, how's the family keeping, the chavvies and that?

Me: Growing up too fast, the lot of them. You got any yourself?

Cousin: Two little dinlos, mad as hatters, but they're mine and I wouldn't swap them.

Me: Ha, state of us, eh, look at us now. Feels like five minutes since we was the ones getting a clout round the ear off old, you remember her, the teacher, up the gorja school.

Cousin: Oh don't. Don't you start on her now, my stomach's turning already just thinking on it.

Me: You remember what she was like though. Never mind your maths, never mind nothing else, you said one word out of turn in her class and that was you finished for the day.

Cousin: That was the rule and all, wasn't it. Talk when you're spoke to and not before. Put your hand up and wait on her, and God help you if you never.

Me: And if you did, if you so much as breathed a word without your hand up first, that was the line. A hundred lines, every single time it happened, didn't matter if it was one word or ten words, didn't matter what you said at all.

Cousin: I must have wrote "I will not speak without permission" near a thousand times over them years. Hand cramped up something rotten every time.

Me: A hundred lines exact and all. Never ninety nine, never a hundred and one either. She'd count every one herself, stood over you while you done it.

Cousin: And you remember what she used to say to the whole lot of us, first thing every morning, before anyone so much as sat down proper? "In this room you will be seen and you will not be heard until I ask for you." Word for word that, near enough, in that voice she had on her.

Me: God, that voice. Sent a right chill through you, that did.

Cousin: You remember when she done that mush, the tall one, sat up the back near the window? He'd forgot his homework three divvuses running and she stood him up in front of the whole class and just went at him till he was near crying, poor sod. We all just sat there. Nobody said nothing, nobody even looked at each other.

Me: I remember that like it was yesterday. I remember sitting there thinking, don't let it be me next, please don't you dare pick me next.

Cousin: See, I still say, even now, if I think back to her calling the register and getting close to my name, I go cold. Straight up. All these years gone by and I still go cold thinking on it.

Me: Same here. I used to freeze right up whenever she called on me. Didn't matter if I knew the answer or not, my mind'd just go, gone, nothing there at all, heart going like a good 'un.

Cousin: You remember the time that one mush talked back to her though? That one time?

Me: Oh don't, that's the one story I still can't hardly believe happened, even now.

Cousin: I know! She was on at him for something, I don't even remember what it was now, and he just looks her dead in the eye and goes, "well maybe if you asked nice for once." Just like that, bold as brass. Whole class stopped breathing, I swear to you.

Me: And nothing happened to him! That's the maddest bit of it. We all thought he was dead and buried, thought that was him gone, expelled, the lot. And she just went quiet. Didn't say another word. Just moved on like nothing was ever said.

Cousin: Amazing that, still amazes me now. To this divvus I don't know how he got away with it. Nobody else would've dared, not in a hundred years, not even the hardest of us lot.

Me: And you remember that thing she used to do with her glasses, take them off, clean them on her cardigan, put them straight back on, every five minutes like clockwork, whether they needed it or not?

Cousin: Ha! The class used to count them behind her back, whole rows of us. Whoever got the number right by home time won summat, I don't even remember what, but we all used to sit there mouthing the count at each other so as she wouldn't dick us doing it.

Me: We were terrible, weren't we.

Cousin: We was kids. But do you remember, this is the bit I never forgot, do you remember that quiet mort in our class, the one who never said boo to a goose, and her mother had just died, and she come in that divvus looking like she hadn't slept a wink? And instead of having a go at her for not having her homework done, the teacher just sat with her, quiet like, didn't say a word to anyone else about it, just let her be for the whole lesson.

Me: I'd forgot that till you said it just now. That's true though, that happened. She could be kind when it mattered, when you never expected it off her at all.

Cousin: Funny that, innit. What d'you reckon happened to her anyway, after? You hear anything on her?

Me: Somebody told me she retired years back, gone off down the coast somewhere. Somebody else told me she'd passed on already, God rest her. I don't know which is true, to tell you the honest truth.

Cousin: Same here, heard both them stories myself and all. No way of knowing, really, is there.

Me: No. But looking back on it now, all that fear we had of her all them years.

Cousin: Aye, go on.

Me: It weren't really fear by the end, was it, not when you look back proper on it. It was more like respect, if I'm honest with you. She made us into something, whatever it was she made us into.

Cousin: I'll give her that much. Hardest woman I ever met in my life but she never let none of us off with nothing, and look at us now, we're alright, aren't we, the pair of us.

Me: We are and all! God, listen to us, going on like a right pair of old fools over an old teacher.

Cousin: We are an' all, mush, we are an' all. But you know what, even now, even saying all this to you, if I walked past her in the street tomorrow I'd still feel that flutter go through my stomach. Straight up, I would.

Me: Ha! Same here if I'm honest with you. Some things don't ever leave you.

Cousin: They don't, do they. Right, anyway, enough of the old days now. How's your old mother keeping, is she still with your lot on the road?

Me: She's kushti, still going strong, still telling the lot of us what to do same as she always has.

Cousin: Ha, wouldn't have it any other way from her. And the grai, you still dealing in them or has that dried up on you?

Me: Bit of both if I'm honest. Prices are all over the place these days, hard to make it pay like it used to years back, but I do a bit still, keeps me out of mischief and keeps the wonga ticking over.

Cousin: And how's the atchin tan treating you, you still getting hassle off the gavvers up your way?

Me: Constant, never lets up. Move on here, move on there, no rest for it at all. But we manage between us. You still doing the tarmac work, or you moved off that now?

Cousin: Bit of that still, bit of scrap when it's going, whatever's about really. Got to keep the wonga coming in somehow, ain't we, same as ever.

Me: True enough. And your old juk, the one you had, is he still going or has he gone over the rainbow bridge on you?

Cousin: Gone now, God love him, few years back. Got another one now though, daft as a brush, chors my hobben off my plate soon as I turn my back on him.

Me: Ha, sounds about right. Still got the old vardo or you got shot of that too?

Cousin: Still got her, sat round the back doing nothing much these days, but I couldn't part with her, not for love nor money.

Me: No, I don't blame you there. Right, well, good to see your face, cousin, proper good, it's made my divvus this has. We'll have to have a proper rokker again sometime soon, not just standing about here like this.

Cousin: Definitely we will. Give my best to the family when you're back. And mind how you go with them gavvers, don't be giving them any excuse.

Me: I will and all, don't you worry. You look after yourself now too. Kushti to see you, cousin.

Cousin: And you. Take care now, mush. Proper take care.
