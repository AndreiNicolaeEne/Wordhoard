My friend: Go on then, I ain't asking twice. I heard the pair of you through that wall like it weren't even there. What was all that about.

Me: Ah, don't. I'm still red thinking on it.

My friend: I don't care if you're red, I want the whole of it, the row and the making up after and all.

Me: Alright, alright, sit down then and I'll rokker you the lot.

It started daft, that's the God's honest truth of it, over nothing much more than the wonga what I'd put by for the tax on the motor. I'd had it counted out in the tin three divvus running, saving it up proper so we weren't caught short come the month end, and I go to the tin Friday dinner and there's half of it gone. Half. My partner'd took it down the site to stand a round for a couple of the mushes what helped shift that scrap Wednesday, never asked me, never said a word, just took it.

So I come storming out the vardo when they got back, and I'll hold me hand up, I raised it first, no use saying different.

Me: Where's my tax money gone then. Where's it gone.

Partner: It ain't your tax money, it's ours, and I stood the lads a drink for the help, they broke their backs on that load same as me.

Me: You never asked me. You never even said. I've been counting that out careful three divvus and you've went and pissed half of it up the wall of the pub.

Partner: Don't you raise your voice at me in my own home.

Me: Your home. Your home, is it now.

And it just went up from there, proper shouting, the both of us, back and forth, neither giving an inch, and I know full well that's the bit came through the wall to you.

My friend: It was. Thought the gavvers'd be called, honest.

Me: No fear of that, we're not that sort, just loud when we're roused, and God knows we were roused. And in the thick of it I said a thing I never meant, not a word of it. I told my partner I'd have been better off jalling on me own, that I never needed no one, that I'd have managed kushti on me own two feet me whole life through and not missed a soul. Which is a lie and I knew it soon as it left my mouth, but you can't pull a thing back once it's out, can you.

My friend: No, you can't.

Me: And then it got worse, because I went and brought up the old thing, the thing we don't say. About how my partner's own family never lift a finger, never come round, never so much as offer a hand with the chavvies, but they're first in the queue when there's wonga wants borrowing. I said it wasn't the first time I'd carried the both of us while others sat back watching. That's an old sore one, that, and I know it, and I threw it in anyway because I wanted it to sting.

My friend: And did it.

Me: Oh it stung alright. My partner went that quiet and hard I knew I'd gone too far, but neither of us backed down there and then. The door of the vardo got a right good slamming, went that hard the little dog, our juk, jumped clean off the step and went under the neighbour's trailer and wouldn't come out for love nor money the rest of the evening. That was the anger needing somewhere to put itself, I reckon, same as it always does.

And after that, nothing. Not a word. My partner took the chavvies up their nan's for their tea so I'd have the place quiet, and I never asked where they'd got to, and when they come back it was straight to bed without so much as a dick in my direction. Whole next divvus went the same way an' all, me out round the atchin tan keeping myself busy pretending to mend what never needed mending, my partner in with the telly on loud so there was no need for talking. Better part of a full day we spent like two strangers what happened to be sharing the one trailer.

My friend: Who broke it first then.

Me: My partner did, credit where it's due. Come the evening they put the kettle on, which they never do, that's my job, and brought two mugs through and set one down by me without a word first off, then said it.

Partner: I shouldn't have took that money without asking you. That was wrong of me, plain wrong, and I'm sorry for it.

Me: That was the actual apology, mind, not just a sorry chucked out to end it. For the money, straight out, no dressing it up.

My friend: And you said what back.

Me: I said I was sorry an' all, for bringing up their family the way I did, for saying it to hurt rather than to sort anything, because that's a low thing to do to someone and I know better. Told them straight I never meant it the way it sounded, that I know they can't help what their own lot do or don't do.

And it was my partner called me their old mush then, first time all day, said it soft, "come here then, you old mush," and that was the first bit I noticed things was mending, because we hadn't used a kind word between us in near enough two divvus by then.

My friend: What sealed it though, in the end.

Me: My partner fixed the hinge on the vardo door what got wrenched half off in all the slamming, sat there with the little tools out working at it under the light, and never said nothing about it, just done it quiet, and let the juk back in after and give him his bit of hobben off their own plate. That was it for me, that was the whole thing mended right there, watching them put right what got broke without being asked twice. Didn't need another word said after that.

My friend: And now you've told me the whole of it I'll never let you live it down, the pair of you bawling through a wall like a couple of dinlos and me sat the other side with me tea gone cold, earwigging the lot.

Me: Go on then, have your laugh. I did tell my partner an' all, I said, you do know next door heard every last word of that, and my partner just looked at me proper sheepish and said well they'll have heard the good bit an' all then, and that's near enough true. We're grand now, better than grand if anything, closer for it in a queer sort of way, though I'll not be telling you that's a method I'd recommend to anyone. Just don't go round the site telling it different to how I've told it, or I'll know exactly who's been rokkering.
