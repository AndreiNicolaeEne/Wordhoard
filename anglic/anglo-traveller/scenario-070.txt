He come banging on the side of my vardo first divvus light, before the kettle was even swung over the fire.

The first friend: You heard what he's been saying about me? Telling every mush on the atchin tan I chored his tools off the back of his van. Telling gorjas an' all, not just our own. Next thing I'll have a gavver knocking my door over it.

Me: I ain't heard nothing off nobody yet. Sit down a minute, have a sup of tea, tell it to me proper from the start.

The first friend: Ain't much telling to it. We done a job together, split the wonga wrong way if you're asking me, and now he's got half the site thinking I'm a thief.

Me: Kushti, kushti, I hear you. I ain't taking sides, mind, just listening.

He didn't stop long after that, off to find some other ear. Wasn't the hour out before the second one come up the same track, chavvies trailing behind him, looking like he'd not slept a wink.

The second friend: He's been round telling you his side already I bet. Well he never give me my proper share off that job, and when I said so in front of the others he went at me like a juk let off the chain.

Me: He was here, aye. Said you'd been rokkering round that he chored your tools.

The second friend: I never said no such thing, only that he owes me. And I ain't the one what started it neither, whatever he's telling you.

Me: Right. So tell me summat - was it ever about the tools at all, or was it him stood there in front of everyone and you saying nothing for him, not backing him up when it mattered?

The second friend: ...maybe I could've spoke up that once, I'll give him that much. Still owes me though.

Me: Then it ain't tools you two are rowing over, is it. Why don't you jal down there yourself and rokker to him straight, none of this coming through me. I'll walk you over now if you want it, get the pair of you round one fire and say it to each other's face.
