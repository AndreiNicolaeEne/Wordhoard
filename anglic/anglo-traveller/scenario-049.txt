Been sat on it three month now, the two hundred he had off me for the tyres and the tow bar, and every time I seen him about the atchin tan he'd find some reason to jal off before I could get a proper word in. So this divvus, kettle just on, I caught him by the fire and said it plain, no messing.

Me: You know what you owe me. Two hundred, since the spring, and I ain't been on at you for it, have I. Just asking now, straight and kushti between us.

My friend: Don't start on that now. I'm skint this week, proper skint, the work's dried right up on me.

Me: You said that in June an' all. Stood right there by your vardo and said you'd have it for me by the fair. Fair's been and gone three times over since.

My friend: I never said definite, did I. You're twisting what was said now.

Me: I'm not twisting nothing, I remember it clear as day. My chavvies want feeding same as yours do. I'm not made of wonga, I'm only asking for what's mine.

My friend: So what am I now then, some kind of chor in your eyes? After all the years, after everything I done for you and yours, and you dick me like that?

Me: I never called you nothing. Don't go making me out the bad one for asking for my own money back.

Quiet a minute then, the pair of us just stood there, and he goes into his pocket sharpish, not looking at me proper.

My friend: Here. That's fifty, all I got on me right now. Rest by the week's end, I give you my word on it.

Me: You give me your word before an' all.

Nothing said after that, just the two of us stood by the fire not rokkering, the kettle boiling over and neither one of us minding it.
