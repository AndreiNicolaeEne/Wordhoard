Me: Ere mush, if that scratchcard come up proper tonight, first thing I'm having is a new grai for the field, finest thing ever stood at an atchin tan.

My cousin: A grai. You dinlo. Money like that landing on you and you're straight off buying summat that eats hay and never earns a shilling back. I'd have the motor sorted first, decent one and all, not held together with wire and a prayer like the one you're driving now.

Me: Alright, kushti motor an' all, but I'm still having me grai after. And a new vardo done out proper, none of your gorja flat-pack rubbish neither.

My cousin: Now you're spending it three times over and we ain't even got it yet. Put some by, mush. Divvus like that don't come twice in a life.

Me: Put some by where? In a bank, let some gavver's mate sit watching it come and go? I'd sooner bury it under the trailer, that way it's mine and no one the wiser.

My cousin: Bury it! Hark at you, that's the maddest thing ever rokkered in me life. You'd never dick it again once it's under, some juk'd have it scented out and the chavvies'd have it chored digging for worms.

Me: (laughing) Alright, alright, no burying it. Half in the bank where it's safe, half spent proper, new motor, summat put right for the chavvies' hobben, and I still get me grai out of it.

My cousin: Now that I'll shake on. Half sensible, half daft, that's us all over and always has been.

Me: And if there's owt left after all that we're having a proper divvus out, hobben till we can't move, no arguing over it.

My cousin: There you go rokkering sense at last. Only trouble is we ain't got the scratchcard yet, let alone it coming up trumps.

Me: (laughing) True enough, mush. Here's us planning a whole fortune off tuppence ha'penny and a daydream. Go on, put the kettle on, we'll dick what's really sat in the tin before we plan away any more of it.
