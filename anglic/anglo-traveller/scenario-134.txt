This here's the divvus everyone still rokkers about, the year the hops come in like nothing anyone had seen before or since. We jalled down same as every year, same fields, same atchin tan by the hedge where we always pull in, and you could dick it was going to be a kushti year before September was even out. Bines that heavy the wires was bowing under them. Nearly double what most years give, and most years wasn't bad neither.

It was the hops done it, above everything else on that farm. Past years a good pole might give you forty bushel the acre if the rain come right, and there was one year, must be fifteen year back now, that the old growers still talked up as the best going. This year blew that clean out the water, near enough double it. Bines so full the pickers couldn't keep pace, and the farmer had to send word out for more hands, scrabbling for bodies before the frost got in and turned the lot to nothing. Cousins come down that hadn't picked in years, whole families off the road just for the fortnight.

Once the farmer had what he needed for his own drying, there was that much left over nobody knew what to do with it all. Some got bagged and kept back for the chavvies come winter, some got sold on quiet to them as wanted it, little bit of wonga in everyone's pocket that year that wasn't there most.

The mother had hobben on the go at the fire near every hour just keeping the pickers fed, and the oldest grower on that field, been coming since before the war near enough, stood at the end of a row and said:

The oldest: I've dicked forty year of these fields, mush, and I never seen a divvus like this one. Rain come exactly when it needed to and never a hard frost through the whole stretch. Mildest run I ever known.

Me: Best you've seen then?

The oldest: Best anyone's seen.

We still rokker on it now. Every season somebody says remember that year, and nobody has to ask which one they mean.
