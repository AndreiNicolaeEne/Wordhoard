Me: We jalled up Sunday, love, whole rummage of us pulled in round the atchin tan, proper kushti divvus for it.

The cousin: Kushti it was. Here, you wouldn't have known the young mush off Auntie's side, would you.

Me: Never in my life. Dick at the state of him now, I stood there a good ten minutes before I twigged who he even was. Skinny little chavo he always was, trailing round after everyone, and now he's this great lump of a fella, beard down past his collar, three chavvies hanging off him and a mort of his own besides.

The cousin: And the other one, not changed one bit. Same as we left him twenty year back, same laugh, still first up the hobben table before anyone else has even sat down.

Me: True as I'm stood here. Time never touched that one at all.

The cousin: Then there's them that never showed, mind. Word going round it was the young fella off the other family, couldn't get away, working every hour God sends, no wonga spare for the travelling this year.

Me: Shame, that. Here, did you hear the other thing though, about the one used to be in bother half his life, proper dinlo, chorring off his own uncle even. Got himself a proper yard now, buying and selling grai, doing that well the gorjas come to him for horses.

The cousin: Never!

Me: Straight up, I had it off two people. Makes you look at your own self though, don't it. We changed much, do you reckon, you and me?

The cousin: Not so much. Bit greyer round the edges is all. Still rokkering the same as ever, still can't keep our mouths shut between us for five minutes.

Me: Same old jal about, you and me, same as it always was.

The cousin: Don't leave it so long next time though, eh. Before we're both past knowing either, gavver or no gavver moving folk on.

Me: Next one that's on, we're there. Promise me that.

The cousin: Promise.
