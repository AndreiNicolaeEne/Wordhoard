Me: Well I never, is that you standing there? I don't believe it, I don't! Dickin' round them stalls I catches this face and I thought no, never in life, not here.

The old friend: I clocked you an' all, soon as you turned round! How long's it been, mush, ten year? More than ten, got to be, look at the state of us both.

Me: More than ten easy, has to be. Last time we rokkered proper you was still up living at the old atchin tan by the river, your dad's vardo parked longside ours.

The old friend: Married now, three chavvies, the eldest's near as tall as me already. Doing the block paving same as the old man before me. What about yourself, how's things gone?

Me: Two chavvies myself, a boy and a girl, both at school now. Still on the tarmac and the fencing work, whatever's going. Kushti living though, mustn't grumble.

The old friend: Here, d'you remember the time we chored your uncle's grai out the field and rode it bareback, the pair of us, and the thing bolted straight through his washing line?

Me: I remember his face an' all, chasing us down that lane in his vest, roaring blue murder! Good job there weren't no gavver about that day or we'd have been for it twice over.

The old friend: State of us we were. Listen, whatever happened to the old friend, always getting the pair of us into bother, ain't heard nothing off them in years now.

Me: Not a word, no. Someone told me they was over the other side, married into a family up north, but that's the last I heard.

The old friend: Give us your number then, proper this time, none of this losing it down the back of the van again. I'll rokker to you come Sunday, promise.

Me: Go on then. And I mean it this time an' all, no dinlo excuses off me either.
