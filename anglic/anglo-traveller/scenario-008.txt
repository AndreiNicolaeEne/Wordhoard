The boy: Mum, can I have a juk? A pup of me own, go on.

The mother: No, chavo. Not now, not with things how they are.

The boy: Why not though? Why can't I?

The mother: 'Cos we ain't got the wonga for it, straight up. There's no spare for jabs and the vet and all that comes with it, and no room neither, not with us jalin off this atchin tan again come next week. That's the truth of it, no dodgin round it.

The boy: I'll dick after it meself, feed it an all, and I'll fetch water every divvus if you let me have one.

The mother: Fetchin water's your job anyway, love, dog or no dog. Tell you what though - if your dad gets a kushti price for the grai at the fair, and we land a proper pitch for the back end, we'll rokker about it again then. Not before that.

The boy: But me cousin's got one! His mum let him, and he's younger than me an all!

The mother: What your aunt does in her vardo's her business, not ours. What they've got in't what we've got, and never has been. Just 'cos he's got one don't make it owed to you.

The boy: S'not fair, that.

The mother: Might not be, chavo, but that's how it stands. Sunday though, I'll take you down the fair proper, you can have your go on the stalls, and I'll get you summat off the van after, if you're good. That's on offer. Take it or leave it.

The boy: ...Alright then. Still want a juk though.

The mother: I know, love. Go on now, see if your gran wants owt, and stop your rokkerin, you're worse than a dinlo about it.
