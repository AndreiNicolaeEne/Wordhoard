I had him out with me of a divvus, teaching him the coupling, on account his dad reckoned he was old enough to earn his corner. I backed the motor up nice and easy, showed him with my own two hands, coupling down onto the ball, pin pushed home till it locks, then and only then the chain goes on the tow hook. Twice through, slow, so he'd have it.

Me: Right, you dick that and you do it same.

He backed her up half crooked, jumped down, and had the chain clipped on the hook before the pin was anywhere near home.

Me: No, no, chavo. Pin first. Chain's last, always last.

The chavo: Which chain, the one off the jockey wheel?

Me: There ain't a chain off the jockey wheel, that's a leg, it just winds up and down. The coupling. Onto the ball. Pin down. Then the chain on the hook. Three things. That order.

He went again. Chain on the hook again before the pin was even near.

Me: How many times, mush! You'll have that vardo come off the ball halfway down the road and it'll be the gavvers stood there asking me questions, not you. Dinlo, watch what my hands are doing, not the sky.

The chavo: I weren't - sorry. I heard you, I just get the pin and the chain muddled.

Me: All right. Forget the order I give you. Just this, ball, then pin, say it after me.

The chavo: Ball. Pin.

Me: Then chain, last, once you can't push that pin no further.

He had one more go, slow, saying it under his breath while his hands moved. Ball. Pin. Chain. Sat down proper, locked, clipped on right.

Me: There you go. Kushti. Now get in, we're jalling before the light goes.
