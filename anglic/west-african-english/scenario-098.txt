Our people no be Douala people from beginning. Before, before, before any of us been born, the family dey up for the Grassfield side, up where the land di climb and the air cold small small, where every house get im small farm, coffee, coco-yam, beans, small livestock. Life for there no bad, but land no dey again the way e used to be. Papa go born, born again, born again, and the same small piece of ground wey dem get, dem must share am among plenty pikin, generation after generation, until by my great-grandfather own time, wetin remain no fit even feed one house well, not to talk of all the brothers wey don marry and get their own pikin too.

So na so hunger begin show for that side, small small. Not the kind hunger wey person go fall down for road, no, but the kind wey di follow you anyhow you turn, you look your farm, you see say the ground no go ever give you enough again, and you know say if you stay, na so-so struggle you go dey struggle for nothing. Na him make my great-grandfather, wey be first son for im own house, take the decision say make he go find better road.

He no carry anybody. Na im one waka commot, leave im wife and small pikin them for the village, say make he go Douala, find work, settle small, then come call them.

The journey na the part wey the story always slow down for. Dem no get moto like now. E waka some part, e take lorry after, the kind wey dem di pack pipo like sardine, one on top the other, and if rain catch you for road, na so you go wet reach where you dey go. Dem tell me e take plenty many days, sleeping for strangers' verandah, eating wetin any kind woman fit sell for road side, drinking wetin water dem find. And e no waka alone for that road, e get one cousin wey follow am, say make two of them go together, so if trouble show for road, at least na two person go carry am.

But road no gree. Fever catch the cousin somewhere for the road, and by the time dem see one doctor for one small town, e don already be late. My great-grandfather no fit even bury am well well the way village people for house go want, e just do wetin he fit do for that strange place, and e continue the road alone from there.

Up till today, na only him fit talk about that cousin, and even then, e no talk am plenty. Anybody wey ask, he go answer small, small, never the name, only "the cousin wey follow me for road that time." He no like call the name at all. Some pipo for the family go tell you e get im own reason, say if you call person name too much for that kind story, you di call the spirit make e follow you. Others go just tell you say the pain still dey there even now, and na so old people dey manage their pain, they no go talk am plain plain, they go leave am for corner.

The great-grandfather, as they remember am saying: "Man no go choose road wey go feed am. Road na wetin you make with your own leg, no be wetin you meet ready."

He reach Douala with almost nothing. One small bag, im mama cloth wey e carry for remembrance, and one small carved stool wey im own papa bin give am before e commot, the kind low wooden stool wey old men for village dey sit for evening time, dark now from smoke and from hand. That same stool, e still dey for our house till today. Nobody sit for am anyhow; na like say the stool get im own respect, e just dey for corner watching everybody.

For Douala, nobody just open door give am. E met one man wey be from im same area, one far relation, and that man carry am enter im own compound, give am one small corner make e sleep, no be im own house, e no own am, but at least e no beg for street. That time, work no dey easy to find at all. E go morning, e waka, e ask, e stand for gate of company them, and plenty times dem go tell am make e come back tomorrow, tomorrow, tomorrow, until tomorrow self start to feel like lie.

Small time after, e begin get day work for the wharf side, carry load, carry bag, carry whatever wey dem tell am carry, whenever dem call am. No be steady work, e no fit plan im life around am, but e no complain, because as im own papa bin tell am before e commot, e no forget that word. So he take that wharf work, e save the small small money, e no waste am for anything, sef for beer for weekend e go shine eye pass am.

Na so, after some time, e save enough make e call im wife and pikin them come join am. But e no fit call them one time, e go small small, first send message, then send money, then plan the whole road well well so nothing go happen the way e happen to the cousin. E take reach two years, small pass two years, before the family come whole again for Douala. Two years wey im wife bin dey manage house alone for village, dey answer for pikin them, dey wait, dey pray say make nothing happen for road, dey ask anybody wey pass say make dem greet am if dem see am.

When the wife and pikin them finally reach, na so real life for Douala begin proper. He don already leave the wharf work by then, because carrying load no be work wey person go do till e old, im back no go gree. E begin learn tailor work small small from one man wey get im own shop for the quarter, and by the time e master am, e don save enough buy im own machine. That trade, e no leave am again. E become known for that whole area as the tailor man, and till now, some for our family still dey do that same work, na like say the hand pass down from finger to finger.

Dem settle for one quarter side wey plenty pipo like them been dey settle too. New Bell side, where the houses close-close and everybody know everybody business, sef the one wey you never even greet before. Within the first few years, dem find one small house, no big at all, but na dem own corner, nobody go send them commot, and from there, life continue small small.

Na for that same house my grandfather been born, and im younger ones after am. And that na the thing wey really change everything, because before then, Douala just be place wey dem run come, place wey get work, place wey get road wey no reach village again. But once pikin start to born for there, once dem hear pikin cry for that compound for the first time, Douala turn to home proper. No be the village wey dem di call home again; na this place, this dust, this noise, this river smell, this heat wey no gree cool.

Today, na my grandmother wey still di tell the story better pass anybody, especially when small pikin them di ask am.

A small one, asking as pikin dey ask: Grandma, wusai we come from?

The grandmother: We come from far, my own. From up where the land di climb. But we no reach we self till we reach here.

She go start slow, she go talk about the Grassfield small, she no go dwell for there long, then she go reach the journey part, she go lower her voice small when e reach the cousin, she go point the stool for corner make you see am with your own eye, and she go end am always the same way.

The grandmother: Na so we come meet ourself for this place. No be small thing, o. No be small thing at all.

Na so I di hear am when I be pikin, sitting for floor, my leg cross, watching that same stool for corner like say na person dey sit for am, quiet, waiting. And na so I di tell am now, the same way I hear am, so the pikin them wey go come after me go still know say this ground wey we di stand, na ground wey person work hard for, person die small for, person save small small for, before anybody for our house fit call am our own.
