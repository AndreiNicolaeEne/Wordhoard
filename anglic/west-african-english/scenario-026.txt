My sister, make I tell you the whole ting well-well, no be smɔl gossip I want give you.

Na di generator fuel bring the whole wahala. I bin buy fuel fɔ the whole week, keep the receipt sef, and when my husband come ask for change, na him bring am up first, standing for door like na oga inspection e come do. "Wusai the res of the money go?" na so e start.

Me: I don show you the receipt, wetin you still dey ask.
The husband: The receipt no match wetin I gi you na.

From there e no stop for the fuel again, e carry the ting go back, drag in that old okada business wey we bin lost all wi savings for, na four years bak, when e say make we invest and I say make we no. E tell me, "Na because of you and una long tongue we no fit progress for this life." That word cut me pass anything. I no answer am quick, I jus dey look am, chest full.

Then I sha talk am back: "If na me stop us dat time, wusai the generator money go when you dey dash your junior brother behind my back?" Na dat one pain am well, because e no think I sabi say e dash the brother money for so-so business, and na dat one bin dey the real matter.

We no talk to each other for tri days straight. House quiet like grave, even the children dey fear to ask wetin do. Nobody wan beg first, everybody dey hold una self small-small.

Na him come first sef. E bring food come put for table, e say, "sorry o, make we no let this ting spoil wi house." I no argue again, I jus take am, my sister, because heart no fit hold vex forever.

My verdict be dis: both of us bin wrong, but na him mouth run past line that day. E owe me one big sorry still, but I don forgive am. Money go come go, marriage no suppose spoil for fuel and old okada story wey done pass.
