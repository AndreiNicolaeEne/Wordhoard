Chale, this day don finish me for true. Since morning wey I wake, my body no even ready o, but the work no dey wait for anybody. We start early early, before six, because oga say the truck go reach by eight and everything must dey ready before that time.

The first work wey we start na to carry the blocks from the back to the front, stack them where the truck fit park. That one set the pace for everything wey come after. Once you start with heavy work like that, your body just dey follow the rhythm, no room to rest small.

By nine my hand don begin complain, the palm dey burn small-small, then the back join. Oga dey shout "take-time, take-time" anytime somebody dey carry too fast, but sha nobody fit slow down too much, the other guys dey go, going. We take one short break, maybe fifteen minutes, just to chop small bread and drink water for the shade. That break no reach at all sef, before you know it oga don dey call us again.

Then afternoon come, ei, that one na the hardest part. Sun dey hot well-well, my legs dey shake, and I swear I nearly tell myself make I just go home, forget everything. But I think about the wahala for house if I no bring anything, so I hold myself small and continue.

One thing sha, the sand wey I bin think go take long, we finish that one fast pass, faster than I even expect. That one dey small joy for the day, small small win for tired body.

Oga: "Una don do well today o, that sand work, forty for that one."

So na forty I collect for am, small but I dey grateful sha. Ten hours wey we spend, na so life be. Tomorrow, no rest o, na the same work again, unless the body itself decide say enough is enough. Sorry for me small, my friend, but that na how e be.
