The gate no even close well well before the door open, and na so I see my mother standing there with the wrapper still tied anyhow, like she just jump up from wherever she dey. She look me, look me again, her face just dey change like person wey dey solve maths for exam hall.

The mother: Ah! Ah ah ah! Chai! Na who be this now?! My pikin! My pikin don come back o!

She no even wait make I answer, she just grab me, and that hug wey she hug me, e no be small hug at all, na the kind wey person go hold you like say if she leave you go disappear again. She dey pat my back, she dey squeeze my shoulder, she dey hold my face for both hands like she dey check say na really me.

The mother: Look at you, look at you! You don grow big o, you don change! Na you be this? Wetin you dey chop for that side sef, you don double!

Before she even finish talk, the whole parlour don empty come meet us for the doorway, everybody dey shout my name one time, e turn to noise wey no get order at all.

The eldest: Ah, oga has landed! Eight years, eight solid years, and you no send message say you dey come today-today like this! You wan kill somebody with surprise abi?

The aunty: Welcome o, welcome, welcome! God don do am, God don do am well well!

The eldest: How was the flight now, abi you sit down for that plane the whole night? You never sleep, abi you don sleep? Wait, wait, everybody should hold on, make the person breathe first now, una dey rush am like say na interview!

Nobody hold on sha. The questions just dey fire from every side, one on top the other, and I dey try answer one, another one done land before I even open mouth.

The uncle: How your work? Them never send you back abi you don resign? Weather for that place, e no too cold this time?

The aunty: You don eat? Abeg sit down, oya sit down, make I bring something make you chop small.

Na my aunty carry the tears well, she dey wipe her eye with the edge of her headtie, and she dey laugh at the same time, the two thing dey happen for her face together.

The aunty: Sorry, sorry, no mind me o, na tears of joy be this, na so I be, once I see you my eye just... (she dey fan herself) ah, make I no cry for una front, abeg continue, continue.

Small small, one small pikin dey hide near the corridor, holding the door frame, just dey look me like say I be stranger wey waka enter their house. E no even fit remember me sha, wetin e go remember, e still dey crawl the last time I see am.

The mother: Come greet your senior now, wetin you dey do dey hide like tortoise? This one na the one wey used to carry you for back, you no remember am, but he don grow, see how e tall reach my shoulder now, God is good.

The younger one: (from behind the door frame, small small voice) ...welcome sir.

The eldest: Sir! Ah ah, no be sir, na your senior na him be this, come here jare, e no go bite you!

For that moment I just dey stand there, sniffing, because that smell wey dey the air, na the one I never forget for eight years, the smell of stew wey don dey fry for kitchen with plenty pepper and locust bean, mixed with that Lagos dust wey stick for curtain no matter how you wash am. Na that same smell dey there, exactly, like say time no pass at all for this house.

Me: Na that same soup smell be this o. God, I don miss this smell, I dey tell you, no food for that side smell like this one.

The eldest: You go chop today make your body remember say na home you dey. Nobody dey travel go anywhere sef, drop the bag first, wetin you carry sef?

My bags don just dey stand by the door since, forgotten, nobody even ask make I bring them inside, because news don start to enter my ear from every direction one time.

The eldest: You never hear say our cousin don marry o, the one wey dey Ojota, she don born two children sef, twins for that matter.

The aunty: And the one you used to play with, the small one, he don grow tall pass me now, e don travel go abroad, na Canada I think, or na UK, I dey confuse the two but somewhere cold be that.

The uncle: Oga next door don relocate go Lekki side, big man now, dash us small small when him wife born, you know how e be, once money enter hand, person go remember old neighbour.

Me: Wow. Wow. Everything don move sha. But see this parlour, e still be the same o, same chair, na this same chair I used to sit dey watch TV, only thing na the paint don darken small for that corner.

The mother: We paint am since, but you know how this Lagos dust be, e no gree make anything stay bright.

Me: The generator dey outside still, same sound, that particular knock-knock sound, I hear am from the gate self, I know say na this house be that. Some things sha never change, God bless them.

The eldest: Abi. Some things sef refuse to change, just like some people.

The mother: How long you go fit stay this time now, no be like last time wey you just show face come go so quick, we no even finish rejoice you don dey plane again.

Me: This time I dey around proper o, no be small visit, make una relax, I no dey rush anywhere.

The mother: Ehen! Na so I like am. Make I go tell them make them start prepare something, we must gather everybody, aunty for Ikeja side, uncle wey dey Surulere, all of them must come see you, we no fit just chop small small like say na ordinary Sunday be this.

The aunty: I go cook that jollof wey you like, the one wey I burn small for bottom, you used to fight everybody for that burnt part.

Me: You still remember that one? Nobody go touch that bottom part, e still remain my own till today o, no negotiation.

The eldest: (laughing) See this one, since when you get sense, ehn, "small ogbonge", abi that's still what we dey call you?

Me: (laughing too) Ah ah, "small ogbonge," nobody has called me that in eight years, where una carry that one come out from?

The eldest: Na your name be that, forever, no expiry date for that one.

Nobody explain the joke to the small pikin wey dey still stand there watching, confuse small, but nobody mind am sef, because everybody wey dey the room don already know wetin dem dey laugh about, and that na how family be, some things you no need explain, you just carry them enter the room with you.

For one moment, sha, everybody just quiet down, no be planned, e just happen. My mother hold my hand, my eldest dey lean for the wall, my aunty don wipe her face finally, and everybody just dey look me, like say they dey confirm with their eye say na true, na real, say I don come back, say I dey here for true.

The mother: (soft, almost to herself) My pikin don come home.

Then, small small, the noise start again, but this time e no be shouting again, e don turn to that kind low warm talk, somebody dey ask if I want water, somebody dey clear space for the bag, somebody dey shout give instruction for kitchen, and the whole house just dey settle, like river wey don finish rush come meet the sea, calm, calm, but still moving.
