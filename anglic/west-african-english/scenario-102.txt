Okay make we start from the gate, no be so? You dey see this small gate wey the paint don dey peel since, since I no fit even remember. That gate, once you push am, e go dey make that its own sound, kiri-kiri, like say e dey greet you. And once you enter the compound, na so the smell of pepper wey dem dey fry go just rush meet you for nose, before you even reach the door sef. Any time I dey come back from school and I smell that pepper for gate, my body go just relax, I go know say today no bad, at least belle go correct.

Na this the entrance be that. Dem no build am big-big, but e get character. You go see one bench by the side, na there visitors dey sidon dey wait if the main room busy, and na there the landlord's brother sef dey park im bicycle anytime him come collect rent, God punish delay o. Anytime you dey come in, you go first hear voices before you see face, because the whole house dey open like that, sound just dey waka everywhere.

Now make we enter the main room, the parlour we dey call am. This na the heart of everything for that house. Old sofa wey the spring don dey show for one corner, but nobody complain, we just dey manage am, na Naija life be that. Television dey sit for the middle, and na there everybody gather every evening, senior ones dey watch news, junior ones dey beg make dem change to cartoon, and na wahala full ground until Oga of the house talk make everybody shut up make him hear wetin the newscaster dey yarn. Visitors, family, church people, area boys wey come greet, everybody first pass through that parlour before anywhere else. Na there celebration dey happen too, birthday, promotion, even when person dey travel abroad, na there dem go gather round the person, dey pray, dey drop money for hand, dey shout "God go make way o."

From there make we waka small go the kitchen. Ehen, this one na another gist entirely. The smell wey dey there na permanent, na like the walls sef don soak am inside, palm oil, dry fish, onions wey don fry finish. If you close your eye now, that smell fit carry you back sharp-sharp. The one habit wey always dey happen there be say, every Sunday morning, before anybody even wake well-well, you go hear pestle dey pound for mortar, kpum kpum kpum, because the mother must pound something before church, no be small thing, na tradition. Nobody dey ask why, we just dey wake to that sound like say na alarm clock.

The bedrooms, ehen, make I waka you enter. Two rooms be that for the whole house, no be package flat like dem dey build now. The parents get their own room, small small but theirs. Then all we the children, we dey share the other one, mattress for floor, mattress for bed, anybody wey come late go just meet space wherever dem see am. Me and my immediate senior, we share the same bed, head to leg, because if we sleep face to face we go just dey gist reach midnight and mama go shout make we sleep. My junior ones dem dey sleep for the mat wey dem dey roll up every morning like say nothing happen.

Now, that one corner for the back of the house, everybody know say na my grandmother's spot be that. Small stool, her wrapper always dey fold there, her radio wey dey play only one station wey dey do local news and old highlife. Nobody dey sit there unless she call you come. Even the small ones dem know, if grandmother chair empty, no be say you fit go sit there just like that, e get respect wey dey attach to that corner.

Me sef, I get my own hideout, behind the water tank for the backyard, small space wey nobody notice unless dem know. Na there I dey run go anytime senior send me errand wey I no wan do, or anytime cane dey fly for house because somebody don do wahala. I go just squeeze myself there, mosquito go dey bite me, but at least peace dey there small.

Now the yard, the space wey dey just outside the door, that na another whole gist. Clothes line dey stretch from one wall reach another, and na there mama dey hang wrapper, school uniform, everything. Firewood dey stack for one side for those days NEPA no bring light and gas don finish. Children dey play there too, ten-ten, suwe, any small space wey get ground, we go turn am to playground.

The neighbours, chai, close no be word. You go hear person dey shout for kitchen and you go know na who own voice be that, because the walls no thick reach to hide anything. If quarrel dey happen next door, whole street go know before evening. If person dey cook something sweet, aroma go travel enter your nose make you begin envy them small.

Every morning, same time, same time, you go hear the bread seller dey ring her bell, kilin kilin, dey shout "bread o, agege bread!" Na that sound be alarm clock number two after cock don crow. And every evening, same time same time, you go see the same old man dey waka pass with him walking stick, dey go check him goat wey him tie for bush side. Rain or shine, that man go pass, waka waka waka, slow like tortoise, but sure like sunrise.

One thing wey always dey broken for that house, and dem always dey talk about am, na the front door hinge. That door, e no fit close well since since. Any time person push am, e go creak like say e wan tell you im own wahala. "This door go kill somebody one day o," papa go dey talk, but till tomorrow, till tomorrow, nobody fix am. E just become part of the house sound, like say the house get im own voice.

When rain season reach, water go dey enter that compound like river, and we go dey use bucket dey scoop am comot, everybody dey laugh sef even as we dey vex. When harmattan come, the whole place go dry, dust go cover everywhere, and mama go dey rub our body with cream well-well make skin no crack.

Street games, na for that same street we dey play football with rubber ball, no proper ball o, just anything wey round. Suwe drawn for sand with chalk. In the evening when light come, we go gather for one corner dey play ten-ten, dey clap hand, dey sing song wey nobody remember where e come from.

The last clear memory wey I get of that place, na the day wey we dey pack go, everybody quiet for once, mama dey wipe her eye small small, pretending say na dust enter am. Grandmother chair still dey there, empty, because she don already join her ancestors before that time. I stand for that gate one last time, listen the bread seller bell wey dey ring far far, and I know say something dey end.

The place, as e be now, dem don demolish half of am, new building don come replace the compound, tall one, with gate wey no get that same creak again. Some of the old neighbours don scatter, some don die, some just relocate. If you go there today, you no go recognize am unless person show you exact spot. But me, anytime I close my eye, I fit still smell that pepper for gate, I fit still hear that pestle for kitchen, kpum kpum kpum, like say time no pass at all.

So that na the place I dey talk about, my friend. Na so e be, na so I still carry am for my mind till today.
