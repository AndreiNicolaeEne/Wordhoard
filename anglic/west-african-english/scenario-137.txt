The rice bags wa soak the whole night because somebody no cover the truck before the rain wa come down. By morning everything done wet, the sacks done heavy, some done burst small-small. Oga come out from the office, e vex well-well, and everybody just dey stand round the yard, nobody talking.

Oga: Na you leave that tarpaulin off, abi? You wa close the gate last night, na you last man for here.

The junior: I no do it.

Oga: You dey tell me you no know wetin happen to my rice? I say na you.

The junior: I no do it.

The storeman and the driver, the two who wa forget to tie the tarp before they run go house, just dey stand there quiet, looking for ground, no saying one word.

Oga: I ask you plain question. Is it not you who close last, abi? Answer me now.

The junior: I swear, I no touch that truck. That's all.

The junior no raise voice, no explain nothing more, face still cool like water, no giving oga the vex-face reaction wey e wanted come out. Oga stand there small time, breathing hard, waiting for something else, but nothing come. The junior no beg, no argue again, just dey stand straight, hand by the side.

Somebody from the back say "sorry-o" small, nobody know for who, whether for the rice or for the junior, and that's all anybody say.

Oga shake e head, turn e back, say make yo all find work to do, sha, this wahala done pass for today. Nobody apologize, nobody call the true person wey cause am. The junior just pick up the broom, walk off quiet, the denial still standing there exactly where e leave am.
