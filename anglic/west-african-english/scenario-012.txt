Ma, sit down small, make I talk to you. I no fit carry this thing again, honestly, I don carry am too long, more than twenty years now, and every time somebody see this scar for my leg and ask, I dey lie, same lie, smooth like say I practice am for mirror. Today I want tell you the true one. Abeg just listen finish before you talk anything.

You remember that place we used to swim, that spot near the big tree where all of us small boys and girls used to gather every holiday? That tree, e never leave my mind. We used to climb am small-small, jump from the low branch, nothing serious. But that particular day, the one wey I come home with the "I fell off my bicycle" story, na lie be that, Ma. Straight lie. There was no bicycle anywhere near that water that day. I don carry this false story for you for more than twenty years and today na so I go finally put am down.

That time we were small, small boys, the kind wey follow each other everywhere, four of us always together. There was the tall one who always dey lead everything, and two others, and me. We dey do all sorts for that river spot, small dares here and there, nothing wey person go die from, or so we thought. Some weeks before, one of them had dared me to jump from a higher branch and I no do am. I chicken out. And chale, they never let me forget that thing, weeks and weeks, "you dey fear water", "small thing you no fit do", that kind of talk. Small-small it dey vex me inside, and I dey wait for chance to prove them wrong.

So that day come, and the tall one, the one who always get mouth, e say, "Chale, today make you show us. Climb go the top, jump enter, or we go dey call you fear-fear till we old." I no want give them the satisfaction, sef. I no want hear that kind of talk again. So I climb, and I no even stop for where I normally stop. Something push me, that stubbornness wey dey inside small boy heart, and I go pass the branch I bin dey use before, I go higher than I ever plan to go. My hand dey shake small but I no fit come down again, because they dey watch me, they dey shout small encouragement, small mockery mixed together the way children do.

I jump. And Ma, that jump, I misjudge am well-well. I no calculate the water well, I no see how the current don shift that week, and when I land, na so the wind just leave my body, I no fit even shout at first. And then I feel one sharp thing under the water, something wey I never see, maybe stone, maybe metal, wetin I sef no know till today, and e cut my leg deep, deep. Blood dey mix with the water before I even understand wetin happen to me.

The others, when they see the blood, panic take all of us at once. The tall one jump in come pull me, the small one dey shout for the bank, one of them just dey cry small small, we were all shaking, wet, cold, blood everywhere on my leg. And right there, soaked, shaking, we agree together, all four of us, na so we go tell am: I fell off a bicycle. Chale, we even practice the story small before we go home, "which road", "how the bicycle fall", we build the whole thing together like small engineers. And that story, we never break am. Not one of them ever break am, till today, as far as I know.

I come home that evening, limping, and you believe me straight, Ma. No question, no doubt. You clean the wound, you carry me go the clinic, the woman there stitch am, everybody dey worried but nobody suspect anything. And from that day, anytime anybody ask me, my leg, my scar, wetin happen there, na so I answer, bicycle, fell, road, same story, twenty plus years, same false answer, even when the people asking dey change, even when I myself almost forget the true version for some years.

But Ma, I no fit continue this thing. Small-small e dey weigh on me, especially now that I get my own pikin dey ask me question about scars and stories, about the things wey dey happen to children when they think nobody dey watch them properly. I dey watch my own child near water, near trees, and I dey remember myself, that boy who climb higher than he plan because three friends dey call him fear-fear. I dey think, if my child come tell me one story wey no correct, if my child come hide something serious from me because of pride, because of small shame, I go want the truth, abi? So how I go continue to give you lie for something wey happen so long ago, when it concern only fear and pride and small stubborn heart, nothing shameful pass that?

Also, honestly, Ma, na tiredness sef. Twenty plus years of one lie, e dey heavy for a person soul small-small, even a small lie like this one. Every time somebody touch that scar, every time somebody ask, I dey feel this small pinch inside, like say I dey owe you something I never pay. Sorry, Ma, whether the fault na mine or not, I dey feel sorry for keeping you for the dark this whole time.

So make I just say the whole truth plain: it was not a bicycle. It was a dare, a jump from higher than I should have gone, water I misjudged, and something under that water wey I never see, wey cut my leg open. My friends pulled me out, we panicked together, and together we made up the story you believed, because we were children and we were afraid, afraid of being scolded, afraid maybe you go stop us from going to that place again, afraid of every small consequence a child's mind can imagine in one shocking minute. Nobody force me to lie to you, Ma. I choose am myself, small as I was, and I keep choosing am, every year after, every time the question come.

I dey sorry, Ma. Sorry say I lie to you when you bin dey take care of me with your whole heart, not knowing the truth. Sorry say I let this small lie grow so long that it become almost like part of the scar itself. I no dey ask you forgive me because I feel say I deserve am automatically, I just want you know the truth finally, because you deserve am, and because I no fit carry this thing into old age and still call myself an honest person before you.

The scar still dey there, still the same shape, still the same story written on my skin. But now at least the words wey go with am go match wetin really happen. That's all I wanted to tell you today, Ma. Thank you for listening, and I dey sorry, truly, from my heart, o.
