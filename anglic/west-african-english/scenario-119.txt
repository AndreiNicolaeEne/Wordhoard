My friend, sit down small, make I tell you this whole ting from the start, because if I no tell you proper, you go hear it from somebody else and they go add on top till you no even recognize it self.

You know how we does siddon for the junction under the mango, me and my friend, playing draft since we small pickin. That Sunday na so it wa be, board scratch out for the bench top, bottle-caps for pieces because none of us no get a proper set for the house. Small small crowd wa around us like always, the senior wey does watch from him shop door, two three boy from down the road, the mama wey sell cold water wa stop small to see wetin dey happen.

It wa start as nothing, just talk. My friend say make we play one more, and I say okay, but make it interesting small. That's all. Nobody wa mean nothing serious by it.

Me: If I beat you this game, you go owe me.

My friend: Owe you wetin? You no go beat me today, my friend, forget it.

That's when the small crowd start to push the ting up, because that's how they be, before one move even play, the senior already talking.

The senior: Make yo put something on it now, no just dry talk for people to hear.

Everybody agree small small, laughing, before anybody set one piece down proper. Twenty, that's all, small enough that nobody go feel it too bad, win or lose. But my friend, once they lock it in like that, the whole ting change color. Small joke bet done turn serious business, and everybody standing round like it's a real match for TV.

I wa go in confident well-well, I no go lie to you. My friend wa dey more careful, thinking long before every move, and I just wa dey jump my pieces anyhow because I know that board like my own hand. First half of the game I wa lead, my friend losing piece after piece, the crowd already start to laugh small at him, saying "senior, you finish o." I wa feel myself already, mouth open to talk big talk.

Then the ting turn. I no know how, but my friend find one small opening I no see, and from there the whole thing turn against me quick quick. He carry one piece go, jump, jump again, and just like that the board wa clean for one side and it na my side. Before the game self finish, everybody for the crowd already know say I don lose that bet, you could see it for the board, plain, no need to argue.

When the last move play and it finish for true, I just groan out loud, "ehh, no o," and drop my head for the bench. No point pretending, my friend, the whole junction see it happen with their own eye. I say straight, "okay, okay, I lose, I lose, make I pay the twenty," reaching for my pocket already.

That's where the strange part come in. Because before I even bring out money, the senior wave him hand, laughing.

The senior: Twenty? No no no, this one pass twenty now. We no want your money this time.

I wa confuse. I ask him, "so wetin yo want then?" And that's when the whole crowd start to gather up close, talking over each other, deciding right there for the spot what my punishment go be, and they no leave one single thing vague, they name it out loud, piece by piece, like they wa plan it whole life.

They say I must wear the mama lappa and her head-tie, full costume. They say I must carry the senior old broom for staff, hold it like some big-big chief walking stick. They say I must march from the bench, go all the way round the junction where everybody for the whole street can see, not just we small crowd. They say I must sing one full praise song for my friend, calling him "General" three time, loud, and salute him proper like soldier salute their officer. And after all that, I must kneel down for him for the ground and beg forgiveness for even thinking I could beat him.

Me: Abeg, no. Make I just pay the twenty and we done with this thing.

My friend: Ha! No way, my friend. You think na dash we want give you small way out? This one done pass money now, take your time, it's fixed.

They refuse me flat, laughing so till the small boy for the back wa rolling for the ground. No bargain, no discount, nothing. Day set for the next Saturday, same junction, same time everybody done knock off work and the whole street out anyway for market.

And my friend, when Saturday come, it happen proper, in front of the whole group them and half the junction who just wa passing and stop to see wetin going on. The mama herself wa the one to tie the lappa for my waist and put the head-tie for my head, tying it well well like she dressing me for real wedding, laughing the whole time. Somebody hand me the broom. I start to march.

I sang the praise song loud, calling my friend "General" the way they instruct me, and the small crowd wa singing back-up, clapping, some of them wa banging bucket like drum. I saluted him first time, stiff, and everybody clap. I saluted him second time, even stiffer, more clap. Then for the third salute, na there the whole thing turn to something nobody plan.

Because right that moment, one soldier man, off duty, in him plain clothes but you could still tell from how he walk, wa passing by the junction going somewhere. He see me, full lappa and head-tie, standing at attention, arm up in salute like real parade ground. And this soldier man, out of pure habit, straighten up him own self and salute me back, serious face, no smile, like we for real ceremony.

The whole street just break. My friend wa laughing so hard he sit down for the ground self, holding him stomach. The senior wa wiping him eye. Even the mama who tie my lappa wa laughing till she almost drop her water bucket. The soldier man himself, when he understand wetin dey happen, just shake him head, laugh one small laugh, and continue his way, but that one moment, that salute-for-salute, that's the thing that finish everybody that day.

After that I still had to kneel down proper and beg my friend forgiveness in front of everybody, which I did, full drama, hand on chest and all, because by then I wa already enjoying the show myself.

And my friend, that's the thing, up to today, whenever we all gather back for that same junction, somebody go bring up that story. And every time, it grow small small. Last time I heard it told, the soldier man wa a full colonel in the story, and I wa kneeling for almost one hour. Nobody correcting it either, because why? The bet is long finish, the twenty was never even paid, but that story, my friend, that story still dey collecting interest to this day.
