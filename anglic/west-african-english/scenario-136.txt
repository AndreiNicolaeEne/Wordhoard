I keep the house money inside a old Milo tin, up on top the wardrobe, small-small I di put am from the market profit so when month reach we no go suffer. Yesterday I climb take the tin down to buy rice, check am, and the money e lighter than how I leave am. I count am twice, then three time, my hand dey shake small. I check the other place wa I sometimes move am to when I no trust the tin, nothing deh there too.

I call everybody, mild small first.

Me: Somebody move the money? Maybe una borrow small thing, forget to talk, no wahala, just make yo tell me.

The elder: I no touch your money o, wetin I go do with am.

The junior: Me I no even know husay you dey keep money for this house.

The house girl: Sorry o, but na not me, sha. I no dey here everyday to know wetin dey inside tin.

Only one person wa alone for house the day the money missing, the one wa say she sick, no go school that day. So I face her direct, no more soft-soft talk this time.

Me: Na yo bi the one wa alone for house that day. Talk true, wetin happen to the money?

Her: I no touch am! I don tell you already, I no see any money for this house.

Me: Yo dey lie for my face so? Na who else go chop the money if na not yo?

Her: I no chop nothing! Why yo dey ova-vex put mouth for me like that, because na me alone remain?

Me: Take-time o, that money no fit just commot by eself. Na yo bi the only one, na yo go answer.

Her: I don talk am already, I no know! Yo fit ask the whole house, I no touch your money sef!

By now the two side dey vex well-well, voice up for the whole compound to hear, nobody backing down, nobody satisfy with wetin the other one talk. It just break off so, the money still missing, the wahala still standing there like fog wa no go clear.
