My friend,

Make I tell yo wetin happen for that auntie celebration o, since yo neva come, and na so people go dey misquote the whole thing for me if I no talk am myself make yo hear the true version.

Yo know how our aunt them dey do. She wa get she promotion for the office, and she say everybody must come, no excuse, no "I dey busy." I say okay, make I go small, greet the people them, chop small food, come back my house make I rest, because I get work the next morning self. That na wetin I wa tell myself for my mind o. But yo know how this kind thing dey go.

I reach, the food dey smell sweet-sweet, the meat plenty for the pot, and the aunt say, "no, no, take one more, yo neva even chop reach," and I say okay, one more no go kill me. Then the rice come again, and somebody say make I no shame myself, this na celebration, so I take some more. Before I know myself the drink self start to come round and round, small cup here, small cup there, and I no dey count again. Somebody put music, the whole yard start to move, and I say, well, let me dance small, I go leave soon-soon.

That "soon-soon" na the biggest lie I ever tell myself that night o. Because the next thing I sabi clear-clear na me and some people dey dance for the middle of the yard, the music loud, everybody dey shout, and after that ehn, everything just start to blur small-small. Pieces dey come to me now, like somebody wa cut the film and mix am up, but I no fit put the whole thing for order. Husay the night wa turn from fine to wild, I no fit tell yo exactly. I sabi I wa dance long time. I sabi the drink neva finish. I sabi people wa still dey call me make I come dance more when other people na go inside sleep already. Beyond that, na fog, plain fog.

So I wake the next morning, my friend, and I no go lie to yo, na so my head bi like somebody dey beat drum inside am, boom boom boom, no stop. My eye them no even want open, and when I force am open small, the light for the window bi like e wa come straight from the sun itself, e too bright, e dey pain my eye true true. Every small sound, even the bird them dey sing outside, e loud pass gun for my ear. My stomach self dey turn anyhow, just to think of food na wahala, make I no even talk of chop am. To sit up for the bed alone na work, I tell yo, like somebody wa carry heavy load put for my body.

My senior, the one wa dey stay for the house before me, she na already up, she dey move normal like nothing happen, she dey sweep the yard dey hum song small. She see me dey try sit up, my face dey show all the suffer, and she no fit hold herself, she start to laugh.

The senior: Ehn-henh, oga, so yo no fit stand up this morning?

Me: Abeg no vex me this morning o, my head no dey fine at all.

The senior: I dey see, I dey see. Yesterday yo wa shake body for the yard like person wa possess yo, today yo bi like person wa lose war. Sorry o, but I no fit lie, e funny me small.

Me: Sha, laugh, laugh, one day na yo self go dey so.

She no vex, she just dey tease me small-small, e no bi bad thing. But I lie down back for the bed and I make one serious promise for myself right there. I say, make this bi the last time, I no go ever let night carry me go so far again, I go know my measure, I go stop when I suppose stop. I even sabi for my mind sef, as I dey talk am, say na so I wa talk am before too, plenty time self, and I still dey here dey suffer again. But wetin man go do, that morning I mean am with all my heart.

The whole morning bi like nothing simple. I say make I go small walk go the shop, that short distance wey normal time no reach nothing, that morning e bi like long journey. I try do one small chore, wash small plate, and my hand them no even get strength for am. I just dey lie down, dey manage myself, dey drink water small-small, until my body start to come down small.

Around midday I force myself chop something, but nothing heavy o, just plain rice with small pepper soup, nothing rich, just make my stomach settle small. I no talk plenty, I no move plenty, I just dey sit for one corner, quiet, until the worst pass.

And as I dey lie down there quiet, the pieces from last night dey come back to me one by one, like picture dey flash. One of them, my friend, e no make me feel fine at all. I remember I wa talk something to my auntie's daughter, something wey no bi kind at all, I no even fit repeat am here plain-plain, but I sabi say I wa hurt am small with my mouth. That thing, e still dey sit for my chest, I neva even beg am pardon for am till now. I dey turn am for my head since, e dey shame me anytime I remember am. I go still need go meet am, abeg, and beg am forgive me for wetin I wa talk, I no even mean am, na the drink wa talk pass me that night.

By evening though, my friend, I na start feel like myself again. My head come down, my stomach settle, I fit sit and chop proper rice this time, I fit think straight, I fit even laugh small about the whole thing when the senior come tease me again for evening time, "so yo neva learn," she say, and I just dey laugh, wetin I go tell am.

But I go tell yo true, underneath all the laugh, something still dey sit quiet for my chest. Because I sabi myself too well o. This promise wey I wa make, na fine promise, correct promise, but na so I wa promise before, and na so I go probably promise again one day. One day again I go find myself for that same kind yard, dey ova-drink, dey ova-dance past my time, dey chop past my measure, dey wake with the same drum for my head, the same light dey pain my eye. E no easy, but that na how man bi sometimes, abi? Man go still swear am, and man go still break am, and man go still swear am again.

Anyway, na so the whole thing happen. Husay yo deh, yo for miss am true true, but I glad small say yo neva there sef, because if yo wa see me that morning, yo for laugh me pass the senior herself, yo for never let me hear the last of am.
