Time done pass midnight before the house even settle down small. Everybody wey come since morning done gone since, hand shake, hand shake, "sorry-o, sorry-o" all the way to the gate, and na just the five of us left for the front room now. The fan dey turn slow-slow overhead, the candle them still burning small by the picture, and the rice pot still warm for the kitchen because nobody never carry am comot yet.

The aunt was the one who start am, quiet, like she just thinking out loud.

The aunt: Una remember how e used to hang e cup for the same nail every evening? Not put am for table-o. Hang am for nail like say the table go run comot from am.

The eldest: Nah, aunt, e no be that nail. E be the one by the window, not the one by the door.

The aunt: (laugh small) I always get that one wrong-o. Every single time I tell the story I put am for wrong nail.

Her workmate laugh out before she even know she dey laugh, a small sharp one that catch her off guard, and she cover her mouth quick quick.

Her workmate: Sorry-o, I no mean it so loud. Is just, the cup and the nail sef.

Nobody mind am. The laugh just sit there for the room small and then pass, easy, like a breeze wey come and go.

The neighbor was the one bring the next one, leaning forward like she wa save am for the right time.

The neighbor: Una remember the thing e used to say when somebody rush something and spoil am? Every time, same words, no change.

The eldest: (says it exact, the same tone and everything) "Small small, no craze!" Just so. Word for word.

For a second the room stay quiet like the voice self visit them, like e still deh dey talk am from the corner.

Then the workmate lean back and shake her head slow.

Her workmate: Una never know this one. Before e travel that time, e wa cry small for the airport. Small small, not big, but e wa cry. E no let nobody see am. Only me see am, because I wa stand right by am.

The neighbor: Ehn?! E cry? That one na news-o, I never hear that one before tonight.

The aunt: Me too-o, I never know that.

The room settle quiet, everybody holding the news small for themselves, until the silence start to feel heavy.

The neighbor break am, standing up, reaching for the plate them again.

The neighbor: Make una eat small more, the rice still deh warm. Pass the plate make e no waste, sha.

The plate go round, hand to hand, nobody really hungry but everybody take small anyway, because the moving self na the point.

While the plate dey pass, the eldest and the aunt catch eye and something old pass between them, something that no get nothing to do with tonight at all.

The eldest: You still never send me that money for the land paper, oh.

The aunt: Tonight? You go bring that one tonight?

The eldest: I no press am. I just say am.

The aunt: (shake her head, half laugh) Not tonight, senior. Not tonight.

And they leave am there, the both of them, because some things no be for tonight, and everybody around the room know it and let am pass.

Her workmate was the one who bring back the light mood, sitting up straight like she remember the best one.

Her workmate: Make I tell una about the time e refuse to let the mechanic touch e own car. The small small radio thing wey spoil, e say na him one go fix am. Whole afternoon under that car, sweat running, hand black-black with grease, refusing everybody who come to help.

The neighbor: I remember! The mechanic wa stand right there watching am struggle, offering, and e wa wave am off every time.

Her workmate: And in the end?

The neighbor: (laughing now, full laugh) In the end the radio still no work, but e say "at least na me one spoil am, nobody else."

Everybody laugh together this time, real laugh, no edge for it at all, just the fondness sitting easy on top of the memory.

The eldest: E wa hard-head so, ya. E wa hard-head bad.

The aunt: Hard-head, yes, but not for bad reason. E just no wa like anybody doing for am wetin e feel say e fit do.

Nobody say am straight out, but everybody for the room know say that na the one thing wey go carry the name forward, the stubbornness, the refusing of help even when e wa needed, the small-small pride wey never hurt nobody but stayed anyway. That na how e go dey remembered, and that dey settle for the room like a fact wey no need voice.

Somewhere in the middle of the laughing, the aunt start to cry, quiet quiet, no sound, just water running down while she still dey sit up straight. Nobody stop the talk for am. The neighbor put a hand for her back small, and the story keep going gentle around her, the way water go round a stone for river, nobody making no show of am, nobody asking her to stop or explain.

Then, easy, the talk turn practical, the way e always do when the night reach that hour.

The eldest: Senior go handle the paper them for the office, since senior sabi that side better.

The neighbor: I go go the market early, before the sun hot, get the things them we go need.

Her workmate: I go stay with the aunt tomorrow, make she no be by herself the whole day.

The aunt: (wiping her face, small smile) Thank you, all of una. Thank you-o.

No ceremony for am, just plain talk, one thing after the next, settled the way people settle things when the crying done pass through them already and left something steadier behind.

By then only the workmate still awake proper, the others' eyes heavy, heads nodding small against the chair. She lift her cup, wetin remain of the drink inside am, and she no plan nothing, the words just come.

Her workmate: To the hard head, and the small small pride, and the cup wey still deh hang for the wrong nail in all our story them. Rest well, oh. We go carry you well.

Nobody answer with big words. Somebody just say "amen" small, somebody else knock cup against cup, soft, so the sound no go wake the ones already sleeping sitting up, and the candle them kept burning after that, the house quiet proper now, the night finally letting them alone.
