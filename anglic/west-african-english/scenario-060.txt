My friend, make I tell you the whole ting, from the time the cock crow reach the time we lock the gate for the last time, because if I no tell you well-well you go think say na small thing we do that Saturday. Na the old house we dey clear, the one wey the grandmother bin build before independence sef, before even the road bin get tar. The roof done dey leak since two rainy season, the wall dey crack for behind, and none of the pikin dem wey grow for that house want come back stay inside am. So the family sidon, senior and junior, and them agree say make we sell the place, but before anybody go sell am, everybody must come carry wetin belong to them, because una no fit sell house with people property still dey inside.

So we gather, and I mean gather well-well, not just the mother and the father dem, but auntie dem, uncle dem, cousin dem wey we no dey see reach Christmas, even the neighbour wey bin close to the grandmother come. Everybody bin sabi say this na the last time anybody go enter that gate as family property, so nobody wan miss am.

We start from the parlour because that na where the door dey open give you first. Na there the old radio dey, the big black one wey get the aerial wey bend for one side. The uncle wey senior pass everybody see am and just stand up like something catch am for chest.

The eldest uncle: Ehen. This na the radio wey the grandmother de use hear news every evening. I remember say I bin de sidon for her foot dey listen the football commentary with am, when Leone Stars bin dey play. She no go gree make anybody touch the volume, she go just say "no, no, leave am so."

He carry the radio go outside, he no let anybody help am, like say na him property alone, and nobody argue am, because everybody sabi say that radio na him own memory to hold.

For the same parlour, inside one drawer, we see one strange ting. Na small carved wooden thing, e shape like animal but nobody fit tell wetin animal e be, and e get one small hole for the middle wey nobody sabi wetin e be for.

The last born: Wetin be this one? I no ever see this thing before for this house.

Senior auntie: Sha, I no sabi am sef. Maybe na from her mother side, wey we no know well. Leave am, make we carry am go show the old people them for the compound, maybe them go know.

Nobody argue am plenty, we just pack am with the things wey we no sure of, and move go the next room.

Na the dining room bring the first proper wahala. Inside there, one big wooden sideboard dey, the kind wey get glass door and e heavy well-well, four man fit hardly lift am. Two of the cousin dem both wan claim am, one say na him mother bin promise am to him before she travel, the other say na him wey dey use am now for keep him own things.

Junior cousin: Abeg, this cabinet na my own, everybody sabi say Auntie promise me before she go abroad, is it not so?

Other cousin: Na lie. If e be your own, why the thing still dey here since three years, you no come carry am? Na me dey clean am, na me dey look after am.

The whole room quiet small, everybody de watch, until the father come stand up and settle am.

The father: Make we no dey fight for wood, abeg. The one wey dey clean am, na him get better claim, because property wey nobody de look after, e go spoil. Make the other one find something else wey belong to am well-well.

Nobody happy-happy but everybody gree, because the father talk sense and nobody wan disgrace themselves for old sideboard for front of everybody.

Then na the argument wey no settle so quick come reach: one old framed picture, black and white, of some colonial-time gathering, men dem for suit and hat standing for front of one building. One auntie say make we burn am, say e no carry good memory, say the people inside there na strangers wey nobody remember again. But the grandmother's last surviving sister, wey come specially for that day, no gree at all.

The old aunt: You no fit throway history like that, o! Even if you no know the face, na still your people time. Make you keep am, put am for one corner, no bother anybody.

Auntie: But wetin we go do with picture wey nobody sabi the person dem inside? E just go gather dust for another house now.

Them argue small-small for that one, back and forth, until nobody win outright, them just wrap the picture inside cloth and say make the eldest hold am for now, decide later. Na so some ting be, you no fit settle everything for one day.

Now, the room wey chop time pass every other room, na the store room for behind. Ah, that room, my friend, e full like say person dey pack fowl inside there. Old clothes, broken chair, plenty carton wey nobody open reach ten years, tin box, even one bicycle wheel with no bicycle. We dey sort am from morning, every person dey shout "come see this one" every five minutes, and the sun done change position sef before we finish half of am.

Na inside that same room, hidden for under one loose floorboard, the last born find something wey nobody expect. Small tin box, rusted for edge, and inside there, old naira notes wey no dey use again, and one small envelope with folded letters, the writing don fade small.

The last born: Una come see wetin I find o! Money dey inside here, but e no be money wey person fit spend again.

Everybody gather round, quiet for one minute, because nobody bin know the grandmother bin hide anything for that house. The old letters, nobody read am loud, them just pass am hand to hand, then give am to the eldest to keep safe, because that kind thing, e no be for outside talk.

By the time that store room done finish, belle done dey cry for everybody, so we take break. Somebody bin already bring pot of jollof and one big bowl of cassava leaf from the compound kitchen, and everybody sidon for the veranda, senior and junior together, eat with hand and with spoon, laugh small about the sideboard fight, laugh about the tin animal nobody sabi. Sorry word dey pass round too, sorry for the ones wey no fit come, sorry for the grandmother wey no dey again to see us gather so.

After we chop finish, na the big wardrobe from the main bedroom bring the last real wahala of the day, but this time nobody fight over am, everybody just no want am. E too heavy, e too old-fashion, the wood done soft small for one side, and nobody house get space for something like that.

Senior auntie: Who go carry this one? I no get space for my house, my own wardrobe still dey work well.

Junior uncle: Me sef, no. Take-time, if una try lift am like that the whole thing go break for una hand.

In the end, nobody claim am, and them agree make the buyer man wey go take the house, make e keep am or throw am, na him wahala now, not the family own again.

But one thing settle without any argument at all: the old sewing machine, the manual one wey get the foot pedal. Everybody just look the auntie wey still dey sew for people till today, and nobody even open mouth, them just carry am put for her side, she nod, that na that.

The last room to empty na the grandmother own bedroom, and that one nobody rush. Even though small room pass the store room, e take long time because every cloth, every shoe, every small perfume bottle bring one person or the other come stand still for some minutes, hand for chin, before them fold am put inside bag.

When the last room don empty, everybody sidon for the bare floor small, look round, no furniture, no picture, nothing again, just wall and window and the sound of person foot for the passage.

Then we divide the rest, the small small things wey never find owner yet, plates, cloth, one clock, some tool from the shed. Nobody argue, everybody just take turn call wetin them want, and if two person want the same thing them go laugh am off, say make the other one take am, "abeg, you fit have am."

Before evening reach, we lock the door one last time. The eldest hold the key, turn am, test the padlock twice, and everybody just stand for the gate quiet small, nobody sabi wetin to say again. Somebody just say make we go o, and we waka comot, the house standing there empty, dark inside, wey we go never enter again as one family.
