Make I waka you through wetin dis house be like, from the very first minute the day crack open, since you ask.

Na the mother wake first, before cock done crow sef, before any phone alarm get chance to ring. Whatever time she sleep the night before, no matter, she done up before the whole street show sign of life. And she no dey waste that quiet time for nothing. First ting, before she greet anybody, before she even open her mouth, na she go carry broom go sweep the front step and the small yard, clean clean, like say important visitor dey come that same day, every day, world without end. She no dey put light for inside, she no dey call nobody. Na only the broom sound dey move for the whole street that hour, swish swish, like clock wey nobody wind.

Na that same broom sound dey wake the father, every morning, same grumble, word for word:

The father: Wetin dis woman dey sweep for this hour, the sun never even show face self.

He go turn, he go groan, he go complain say the broom too loud for that hour, but he go still jump commot the bed fast fast, because if he slow the auntie go beat am reach the wash-room, and that na wahala wey never settle for this house, from time until now. Na him and the auntie, dem two, dey fight for who go enter first, every blessed morning, and nobody never win am proper, because whoever door reach first go hold am, and the other one go stand outside dey knock small small.

The auntie: Abeg I dey inside already o, make you go find another place.

The father: Which other place, dis na one wash-room house, you sabi say na my turn.

That one no dey settle, e just dey repeat, almost the same words, almost the same voice, like say dem rehearse am for sleep. That father, e no dey ever ready make e lose, and that auntie, she no dey ever gree, so the two of dem go dey there behind that door most mornings, like say na job wey dem dey paid for.

Once that wahala don start, na the signal make the children rouse, one after another, same order every single time. Na the eldest first, because e get sense pass, e no like make dem call am twice:

The mother: Eldest, you don wake?

Then the middle one, wey always need one more call before e move:

The mother: You, commot na bed, before I come there myself.

And last, the last born, the same words every morning, no change:

The mother: Last born, if I come meet you for that bed again, na wetin go happen you sabi am already.

Nobody dey vex proper, e just be the house own way of talking morning into existence.

Na from there the argument go start, almost every morning, close to the same words. The middle one go find say the last born don wear e shirt again, without ask, and voice go raise small:

The middle one: Na my shirt dis o, why you always dey carry my ting without tell me first?

The last born: I no see wetin dey wrong, na only shirt.

The middle one: Na so you dey talk every time, and na so you go still do am tomorrow.

Everybody sabi how e go end, nobody dey wait for the end, dem just go continue dey get ready.

The dog get e own fixed part too. E go lie quiet for e corner till the kettle start whistle, then e go jump up one time, run go stand by the kitchen door, tail dey wag, because e sabi say na from there bread go drop small for am. And if una no quick am, e go go hide one shoe, always the last born own, carry am go e corner like say na treasure e find.

Breakfast na the same, every day, e no change: bread and tea, sometimes akara if the mother wake early well well to fry am. But the father get one way wey e never vary, not one single day for as long as I sabi am: tea must reach am first, before bread, before anyting.

The father: Tea before bread, always, all of una sabi dis one already, no need remind me every day.

Every morning, one shoe go missing, almost always the last born own, and everybody sabi the three place to check without even think: under the bed, behind the door, or for the dog corner, because that dog love shoe pass anyting.

The sound wey mean say the house don wake proper, na the radio. Once the mother turn am on for the kitchen, that na the real signal, no more grumbling for bed, time don reach make everybody move body.

Na the middle one always dey run behind, every single day, no matter how early e wake sef. E go still dey find bag, e go still dey find book, while everybody else done stand for door.

The mother: Make we go, make we go, the time no dey wait for nobody o!

And na so the whole rush go start: bag dey pass from hand to hand, shoe dey find leg, somebody dey shout say dem forget something, but at the end nothing never truly missing, because the mother don pack everything the night before, she sabi say morning no get time for that kind wahala.

Sunday na the one day wey the whole rhythm change complete. No shouting, no wash-room fight, no rush for door. The house dey quiet, everybody dey move slow, dressing fine fine, and even the dog sef dey lie down quiet like e sabi say today no be like other day.

But every other morning, before anybody comot that door, na the same words the mother go say, wey she don dey say from time:

The mother: Waka good, God go carry una go, God go bring una back.

And everybody go answer am the same way, almost together, like say dem rehearse that one too.

Me: I go tell you true, I dey find the whole thing funny, small small, every single morning of my life. Same broom, same wash-room fight, same shirt argument, same shoe wey go missing, same words for door. I done see am so tay I fit close my eye tell you wetin go happen next before e even happen. I no even vex sha, na so e be, na so we grow inside am. But na that same thing make e sweet too, because you no go ever see one single morning of dis kind for your own house, and I dey here dey live am fresh every day like film wey dey repeat, and I never tire for am yet.
