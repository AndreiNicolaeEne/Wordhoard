That evening the light just go "kpai" just after dusk, no announcement, nothing. Nobody sure wetin really cause am, my elder brother say na transformer for the corner wey spark, the mother say na NEPA just dey do wetin dem like as usual. Abi na their custom self. Na third time this month sef; light bin go since afternoon the other day and nobody even talk anything, so we no too surprise, but this time e met us for the middle of film.

We start to scramble well-well. My elder brother dey shout for torchlight, the mother dey feel her way small-small to the kitchen to check where she keep candle. Everybody phone come out, but battery no dey plenty for anybody sef, so na small-small light we dey manage, no wan waste am before time reach.

Then we discover the real wahala: na only one candle stub remain for the whole house! That candle, e no reach anything sef. If we light am for sitting room, kitchen go dark; if we carry am go kitchen, parlour go dark again. So make everybody just gather for one corner, so we go all dey see the one small flame together.

That stool for corner, e don vex my father well-well that night. He knock his leg for am, one time, two times! First time he shout, second time na just hiss he hiss and sit down quiet, vex-vex.

The father: "Who put this stool here again? Una take-time o, before somebody break leg for this house."
The mother: "Sorry, sorry. E dey there since morning, abeg no vex, we go arrange the place tomorrow when light don come."

Nobody try fix anything again that night sha, we just leave everything as e be. We gather round the one candle, dey gist small, dey laugh, my last born dey ask when light go come back, and the mother just tell am, "Sleep small, love, e go come."

Light never come back that night sef. Around eleven, one by one, everybody just carry theirself go bed inside that darkness, mosquito dey sing for ear, and na the morning light wake us, shining like nothing happen.
