Let me tell you how this house we deh stand in come reach where e reach, because plenty people done pass and ask me, "unu build all this yaself?" and I di tell them, small small, with people helep, e take the whole eleven months.

The old place wa too small for us, that's how the whole thing start. When my small brother marry and bring his wife come stay, and my wife her sister pikin them come from up-country to go school for town, the room them wey we get no fit hold everybody again. Five, six people dey sleep for one room, pot dey cook for veranda because kitchen no big, and my wife she tell me straight:

The wife: This house done tight-tight for us-o. We haffi do something before the rainy season meet us so.

So me and my uncle we sidon one evening, we count the whole thing out. We say four people go fit do the work, maybe five, six months e go take if God helep us. We wa wrong-wrong for that count, but that's how everybody start, thinking small when the work big-big.

First wrong turn wa near the very start self. The mason wey we call to measure the foundation, he no check the line good, and by the time my uncle come check am with the string, we done dig the whole trench off from where e suppose to be. Two weeks work, gone, just so. We haffi break the small block them we done lay and start the measuring again from zero.

The mason: I sorry-o, this one na my fault true true. Make we start am back, no vex.

Me: Na so life be, oga. We no get time for vex, we get time for work.

After that we move small small, everybody careful now, checking the line before anybody touch cutlass or shovel. Then one Saturday morning, before cock even crow well, I wake up and hear voice for the yard. My wife brother, three men from the church, and the neighbor man, all of them done come with them own tool, nobody call them.

Me: Unu come do wetin here so early-o?

The neighbor: We hear say the work start, we say make we come helep small before we go for we own business. Una no fit do this kind work with small hand.

That day we lay more block than the whole week before. That kind help, e no get price. Community na like that for us, when they hear work dey, hand go come.

Then the rain meet us. For this Monrovia rain, when e start e no dey joke, e fit rain the whole week self, no stop. The cement no fit set, the sand wey we stack for corner wash go, and the whole compound turn to mud, nobody fit even walk near the building talk less work. We lost the better part of one week just sitting, watching sky, hoping e go clear. My uncle used to stand for veranda every morning checking cloud like doctor checking patient.

By the time the rain clear, we run short for cement. Money wey we set aside done chop small small on other things, food for the workers, transport, this and that, and the load we suppose buy no reach again. We no fit stop, so we make do. We mix the little cement we get with more sand than the book say, we use old block from the back fence wey we never break down proper, anything wey fit hold. My uncle say make we no vex about am, e say the wall go still stand, just make we mix am well well so e no weak.

My small brother wa the one wey fall. He climb the scaffold to fix one beam, the plank shift under his foot, and he come down flat for the sand pile below. Everybody run, my wife scream, but when we lift him he just dey hold his back, dey laugh small even though we fit see the fear for his eye.

The small brother: I dey fine-o, na small scare so. God don show me pity today.

We carry him go rest for two days, but nothing worse than that scare come from am, thank God.

Now the argument, e wa about the roof. My uncle say the pitch suppose steep so rain go run off quick, but the mason say if e too steep the zinc go cost more than we get, make we do am moderate. The two of them argue for almost one whole evening, voice high, hand dey wave.

The uncle: This kind low roof, when the big rain come, water go just sidon there, e go leak inside.

The mason: Chief, we no get money for that kind steep roof-o. Make we do wetin we fit afford, we fit always add gutter.

In the end we settle for something in between, and up to today the roof no give us any wahala, so maybe the two of them wa both right small small.

The bad part wa the back wall. Almost two months in, my uncle bring level, put am for the wall, and the bubble show say the whole thing lean. We call the mason back, he check am, he shake head slow.

The mason: This one no correct at all. If we leave am so, e go crack when the roof weight land on top. We haffi knock am, start fresh.

That day pain me true true, because that wall wa almost finish, we done put plenty block for am already. But we no get choice. We knock the whole thing down to the foundation and we start again, this time checking level after every three block, no more guessing.

The day the frame finally stand up, that one wa a small sweet day for us. Just the wood skeleton, no zinc yet, no wall finish for inside, but you fit stand for the middle and see the whole shape of the house for the first time, see how the rooms go sit, where the window go be. My wife bring cold drink for everybody, my small brother climb go sit for the top beam, and we all just dey look am, dey laugh for nothing.

The wife: E dey take shape now-o. E go soon done.

The last stretch, the last four night before we finish, we work past dark every night. No light for that part of the yard, so we use lantern and phone torch, my uncle holding one light while the mason di finish the door frame, me and my wife brother di paint by candle small small. Mosquito everywhere, body tired well, but nobody wan stop, because we fit smell say the end near.

And then the day come when the house stand finished, door hang proper, zinc shining for sun, floor swept clean. We call small people come, say small prayer, cut small cake, everybody wey helep dey there.

When we count the whole thing after, na eleven months from the day we break ground to the day we move in, and six different hand pass through the work start to finish, the mason wey lay the first block and come back for the last door, my uncle wey never leave, my small brother wey fall and still come back next day, my wife brother wey come the Saturday nobody call am, the neighbor man wey helep with the same surprise crowd, and the church brother wey we hire small small when money allow to finish the last wiring.

Now when I stand for inside this house, for the middle of the parlor, I fit still see all of that, the two weeks we lost for the wrong foundation, the rain wey wash we sand, the wall we broke and build again, the four night we work by lantern light. Every wall carry small story inside am. E no be big house, but na we own hand build am, and that one, nothing fit take that from us-o.
