Cock never even crow finish when the father don dey knock for una door. Na so e dey be every harvest day, e no dey wait for person to open eye well well before e start to holler say make everybody rise. The whole compound still dark, only the small lantern light dey burn for veranda, and the cold small-small dey bite because that hour before light no get mercy for anybody.

The mother don already dey up before all of us, she dey pack the basins and the cutlass dem, she dey tie wrapper for waist tight tight like say she wan fight something. She call out give the first order before we even reach the farm gate.

The mother: Make everybody wash face quick quick, we no get time to dey lele this morning. The rice no go harvest itself, abi?

We waka go the farm as the sky just dey turn from black to grey, bird dem no even start sing sef. The father, as the one wey sabi the land pass, e dey call the work, e say who go do wetin.

The father: You, the small one, make you carry the basket dem, go gather the loose ones wey done fall for ground. That work no hard, na for your size.

That work, e easy true true, but the youngest one still dey grumble small for inside, dey drag leg, because e wan show say e sef fit do the big work like the senior dem. Nobody vex for am sha, na so pikin dey be.

The heaviest part, na for the eldest and for the uncle wey come help. Dem be the strongest hand for that compound, so the cutting and the carrying of the full-full bundle dem land for their shoulder. The eldest no complain, e just tie e head-tie, spit for e palm, and start to cut, im cutlass dey sing for the stalk dem, cut, cut, cut, one time no rest.

The uncle: This na work for man wey get bone for body o! Weak person no fit stand here small time.

The eldest: Abeg no dey talk make you dey use mouth, use hand make we finish.

Everybody laugh small, because na so the two of dem dey do anytime dem work together, dey throw word for each other but the work no dey suffer.

Time the first section clear finish, the whole arrangement don change. The ones wey bin dey cut now dey bundle, the ones wey bin dey gather now dey carry go the drying place. Na so harvest day dey be, work no get fixed face, e dey shift as the ground clear.

Na for that time we notice say one of the junior ones don dey fall behind, e dey slow small small, e no dey talk, e just dey bend down dey work quiet quiet, sweat full e face. Nobody call am out for una people, nobody shame am. The mother just waka come stand near am, no big talk, she just start to help am carry the load, dey work side by side with am till e catch up small.

The mother: Take your time, we no dey rush you. Make we just finish am together.

That kind help, e no need plenty grammar, na so senior dey take care junior for that place, even person wey no be your real brother or sister, dem go still call am senior, call am junior, treat am like family sef.

Around when sun done climb reach middle sky, the smell of food start to waka come meet us before the food self reach. Somebody bring out the basin of rice and groundnut soup, garri for one side, water for calabash. Everybody drop tool, everybody wash hand, we siddon for shade under the mango tree, dey chop like say we never chop for whole week.

The uncle: This soup sweet o, whosoever cook am, make e no vex, I go chop the whole basin if una no watch me.

The eldest: Na so you dey talk anytime food come, abi na hungry dey worry you or na greed?

Everybody laugh, mouth full of rice, and small argument start over how una dey bundle the stalk dem, one person say make dem tie am tight, another person say too tight go spoil the grain. Dem argue small small, nobody vex real vex, na just how work dey go with plenty mouth around.

Before the food even finish settle for belly, the sky start to change. Cloud dey gather for the hill side, dark one, wind start to blow the leaf dem, and the father raise e eye, look up, e face change small.

The father: Rain wan come o. Make we cover the dry ones quick, carry the harvest wey don dry go inside make water no spoil am. Everybody, drop the small work, na this one important pass now.

We all rush, carry the bag dem, the basket dem, cover the heap wey still dey dry outside, tie tarpaulin for the ones we no fit carry quick. The rain threaten well well, thunder dey grumble for far, but e no fall proper, e just pass small, sprinkle small water and continue go, like say e just wan frighten us make we hurry.

Once the sky clear small, sun come out again, hot pass before, the father give word make we continue the work wey remain.

The father: E don pass, make we push, daylight no dey wait for nobody.

So we push through the afternoon, the heat dey press hard for body, but nobody complain, because harvest day no be day for complain, na day for finish wetin you start.

Na for that push time something spoil. One cutlass blade just snap for the eldest hand, e cut half way inside one big bundle and stop, blade break clean like say person bite am. The eldest hold the handle, look the broken blade, shake e head.

The eldest: This one don finish o, wahala dey.

The uncle no waste time, e bring out the spare one wey dem always carry for such case, e hand am give the eldest, dey pat e back.

The uncle: Sorry o, na so tool dey be, e go tire before man self tire. Take this one, continue.

We continue, no long grumble, no time to waste for grumble sef, because the light don start to lean, the sun don begin dey go down small small, the shadow dem dey grow long for ground.

The father: Light dey go o, make una jam am, we no wan finish this work for dark.

Everybody move quicker, hand dey work fast, nobody dey waka slow again, even the small one dey run come and go with the basket. The last section, na there everybody converge, senior and junior, strong hand and small hand, all of us for that one patch, dey cut, dey gather, dey carry, dey tie, one after another until the last stalk fall.

The father call the whole gathering make we count wetin we don bring in. Basket upon basket, bag upon bag, the rice piled high for the drying floor, groundnut for separate heap, the whole compound full with the day work.

The father: Na good harvest we get today o, God dey do wonder. Everybody don work well well, thank God make nothing spoil finish.

Once the last basket land, the whole body just release. Person siddon for ground, back dey pain, leg dey shake, sweat done dry for skin leave salt mark. Nobody talk plenty, everybody just dey breathe, dey look the heap dem wey we don gather, small small smile dey come for face even as body tire.

But before anybody fit rest proper, the mother call out one small chore before we go inside.

The mother: Make somebody go fetch water for wash leg, and make you cover the heap dem well before night meet us. Then una fit rest.

So one of the junior ones drag body go do that last small work, no grumble, because na so the day dey end, last chore before last rest, and only after that una fit siddon proper, body heavy, mind quiet, the whole harvest day done pass finish.
