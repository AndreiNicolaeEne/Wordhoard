The mother call the two of them, make them come sidon well, close to her, before she open mouth to say anything.

The mother: My pikin them, I get something for tell wuna.

She hold small silence, then she talk am plain.

The mother: Grandpa, e don die o. This morning them call me, them tell me sey e don go.

The eldest: Mama, weti e mean say person don die? Husay e di go now?

The mother: My love, when person die, e body no di work again, e no go fit breathe, e no go fit waka, e no go fit talk again. Husay e di go, nobody sef sabi finish, and I no go lie give wuna say I sabi. But wetin grandpa bi to us, the love wey e give us, that one no die. E don go, and we go miss am well-well.

The last born: Mama, e no go chop for the table again? E no go see we for house again?

The mother: No, my pikin, e no go chop with us again, e no go see us for house again. That one na the hard part, and I no go hide am from wuna.

She open her hand, pull the two of them inside, hold them tight.

The mother: Come here, my sweet one. Sorry, my pikin them, I know sey na hard news.

The eldest: Mama, we go still see am again?

The mother: We no go see am for this life again, my dear, that one na true. But we go carry am for heart, for the picture them, for the story them wey we go di tell. That kind seeing no go stop, sha.

She hold them small-small make them settle, then she talk again before she release them back.

The mother: Now make wuna hear me. Tomorrow people go start reach the house, senior for family go come help, e go noisy small-small. Weekend, we go bury am, wuna go see plenty crying, na normal thing, no make wuna ova-fear. Wuna take time o, and if any question dey wuna mind, wuna fit come meet me anytime.

Then she release them make they go back play, but she di watch them small-small.
