Chale, I dey write this one for you since morning, because I know say if I no tell you everything wey happen that day, you no go forgive me. So sit down well, make I carry you go from the very start, no skip skip, I go tell you everything as e be.

I no even sleep sef. By four the birds don already start their own noise for outside and my mind just dey race, dey race, small-small until I gave up and stood up. My sister met me for the kitchen wanting to force breakfast for me and I no fit chop, my stomach no gree receive anything, I just manage small water and half a slice of bread and that na all, up till evening. Everybody dey tell me "eat now, you no go get strength" but chale, when your whole body dey shake like that, food no go pass.

Then the wahala start. We don already dress, hair don set, everybody don near ready, when my sister shout from the room say the small handkerchief wey I bin plan to carry, the one wey belonged to our late grandmother, we no fit find am anywhere. That thing, we turn the whole house upside down for that one small handkerchief. Everybody dey run up and down, una for see us that morning, e for shock you. My mother just stood for the doorway and said, calm, calm:

The mother: Look well, it no go run comot the house. Look inside the bag wey carry the shoes.

And true true, na inside there we see am, folded small-small inside the shoe bag since the night before. I no know who put am there but I thank God, because that thing, I for don cry the whole morning if we no find am.

By the time we reach the place, both families don already gather, and chale, that moment self, seeing him people and my people standing for the same compound, greeting each other for the first time in my whole life, something catch my chest wey I no fit explain. Long time we don hear stories about their side and them don hear about ours, but to see everybody stand together, shaking hands, laughing small, that one na the first time e dey real to me say this thing dey happen.

Na my mother cry first sef, before I even enter the place, before anybody see my face. Somebody told me later say the moment the car turned into the compound and she saw me for the window, water just start dey drop, and she no even try hide am. By the time I come down, she don wipe her face quick quick and dey smile like say nothing happen, but I saw the marks.

Inside, when it reach the vows, chale, I no go lie to you, I lost my place. There was one line, small one, wey I don rehearse plenty times for mirror, but standing there in front of everybody, my mind just went blank, empty like that, and I stood there searching, searching, mouth open small, no word dey come. The whole hall quiet, everybody dey wait, and I could hear my own heart dey bang. Then somehow, from nowhere, the words just returned to me, like something remind me, and I continued as if nothing happen. Only person wey knew was the one standing beside me, holding my hand tight tight to steady me, and afterward he whispered:

Him: You had me sha, I thought you were going to cry there and not finish.

The exchange that sealed everything, when we said the words to each other proper and put the rings, that part everybody agree was the part wey settle the matter finally. No more turning back after that, na him do am, na me do am too, e don finish, e don seal.

After that na the papers. We signed, one witness standing for my side, one for his side, both of them serious like say na court case, pen dey shake small for my hand because I no fit stop smiling. The witness for my side, my senior from years back, the one wey always dey advise me even though we no be blood, she signed with her whole heart, you for see how she dey press the pen like say the paper go run away.

Then the photograph wahala start, chale, and this one, this one fit finish a person patience. The photographer wanted cluster after cluster, first this side of family, then that side, then combine them, then remove one, add another, "come stand here," "no no, you, move small," until people wey bin dey happy start to dey vex quiet quiet under their smile. My oga of an uncle kept saying "abeg small time, small time" every time somebody wanted to comot from the group, and by the third hour everybody just dey laugh forced laugh for camera, but I go tell you, some of those pictures come out beautiful sha, so maybe the suffering worth am.

Then the seating, ah, the seating. Somehow two people wey no dey see eye to eye since long time, everybody know their history, end up seated shoulder to shoulder for one table. The whole hall watching from the corner of eye to see if wahala go start, but the two of them just sat there stiff stiff, exchanging small talk like colleagues, take-time, everybody kept saying, take-time, nobody wanted trouble that day of all days.

Food, chale. The jollof, gone within minutes, I swear, before I even reach my own table people don finish serving am, empty pot standing there like say nothing bin dey inside. But that one plate of some vegetable thing my mother-in-law insisted must be on the menu, that one, nobody touch am. It sat there whole reception, untouched, and na my husband's junior brother who eventually, out of pity or mischief, carried a plate to try am, and even he no finish am.

The toasts, ah. My husband's senior stood up to speak and started strong, joking small, everybody laughing, but halfway through he mentioned our late grandmother's name and just like that, his voice broke, he couldn't continue, had to stop, wipe his face, and the whole hall went quiet with him, some people crying too, before he managed to finish with just a few more words.

One thing wey go always make me laugh when I remember that day be my husband's cousin, chale, that one never sat down the whole night. Music dey play, she dey dance, food dey serve, she dey dance small, somebody go call her to come sit, she go dance to the seat and dance back out again. By midnight everybody don tire, sitting, fanning themselves, and she still dey there in the middle of the floor, alone sef, dancing like the music dey play only for her.

And of course, somebody had to drag out the old story, the one about how my husband's father and my father first met years back, a story everybody don hear plenty times already, but that night they told it again to the whole table, adding new small-small details nobody remembered before, and everybody laughed like they were hearing am fresh, well-well laughter, the kind wey pain your stomach.

By the time the last guest left, chale, the place was just quiet. Chairs scattered anyhow, plates everywhere, decorations hanging half off, and it was just the two of us standing in the middle of all that mess, too tired to even talk plenty, just looking at each other and laughing small at how quiet everything suddenly became. We didn't clean anything that night, we just stood there, hand in hand, in the middle of everything, and that quiet, my friend, after a whole day of noise, that quiet was the sweetest part of everything.

So there you have it, the whole thing, start to finish. I wish you bin dey there o, but at least now you don hear everything, every small piece of it, as if you bin dey standing right beside me the whole day.
