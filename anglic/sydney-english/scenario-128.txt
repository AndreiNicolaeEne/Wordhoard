So this was a couple of weeks back now, on a fortnight where the weather bureau had been going on and on about a "severe storm cell" and youse just tune it out after a while because they say that every second week and nothing ever comes of it. But this one, mate. This one was different to anything I've sat through here.

It was heaps still in the arvo, that's the bit that gets me thinking back on it. Dead still, and the sky had gone this weird sort of yellowy-green colour, like a bruise. The mozzies were going off their heads too, I remember standing on the back step having a ciggie and getting eaten alive and thinking that's not normal, they don't usually swarm like that. My workmate rang about something for work and I said to her, something's coming, I can feel it in me bones, and she goes yeah heard youse are getting smashed down there, we're fine up here, and hung up all smug about it.

Within the hour the wind had picked up enough that I was out the back going for it, absolutely flat out. Esky off the deck and into the shed, the barbie cover strapped down twice because one strap's not going to cut it in that, the pot plants brought in, the kids' bikes dragged under the carport, thongs kicked off at the back door because I'd been running round in them and they were useless, slipping everywhere. The mother was inside getting the eldest to help bring in whatever was left on the line, towels and school shirts flapping round like they were trying to escape.

By the time the wind was really up we'd given up trying to save anything else and just got everyone into the hallway together, away from the windows. Me, the mother, the eldest, the youngest, even the dog was shoved in there with us, shaking like a leaf, poor thing. And that's when the power went. Just click, gone, and the whole house went dark and you could hear the youngest go a bit funny in the throat, that half-whimper kids do when they're trying not to cry because they reckon they're too old for it.

I won't lie to youse, mate, it got fair dinkum terrifying for about twenty minutes there. The wind got loud enough that talking just stopped. Not "raise your voice" loud, I mean actually stopped, because you'd open your mouth and there was no point, nobody could hear a word. We were just sitting there in a huddle holding hands and listening to this roar going over the top of the house like a train, and things banging and smashing outside that you couldn't even guess at. Then we heard a tree go down, not far off either, this massive crack and then a sort of a whump you could feel through the floor almost, and the youngest just about climbed into the mother's lap. I said something like it's alright, it's alright, she'll be right, but between you and me I had no idea if it would be, I was fair dinkum bricking it. You don't say that to the kids though, do you. You just hold on and try and look like you're not.

Then there was this almighty bang up top and a sort of tearing, ripping sound, and I knew straight off that was the roof going. Part of it just got peeled back like a sardine tin, I found out after. And not thirty seconds after that, one of the front windows cracked right across, this long jagged line straight through the middle, but it held, thank god, it held. If that had gone in on us with the wind behind it I don't know what would've happened, honestly. I've thought about that a fair bit since, more than I'd say out loud to the mother even, because there's no use dwelling on the what-ifs, but they do sit with you.

And then, just like that, it went quiet. Properly quiet, the eye or the back edge of it or whatever you call it, and we all just sort of looked at each other like, is that it, are we right now. The dog stopped shaking. The youngest asked if she could go and see her room and I said not yet, love, hang on, let's just make sure.

We waited a good while before anyone went out, because you hear about people going out too early and getting clocked by something still coming down. When we finally did open the door, mate, it was a proper mess. The tree that had come down was right across the driveway, whole thing blocking the way out completely, we weren't going anywhere in the car till that got dealt with. Big old gum, must've been there longer than the house. The roof was worse than I'd let myself think, a decent chunk of tiles just gone off the back corner, you could see straight up into the roof cavity from the yard. That's not a patch job, that's a proper repair, tradie and all, and you know how that goes trying to get anyone out here when half the suburb's ringing round doing the same thing.

Funny thing though, we went and had a squiz at the neighbour's place after, the old bloke two doors down, and his whole back fence was just flattened, completely gone, laid out flat in his yard like it'd never been standing. And honestly compared to what we copped that felt like nothing, we were almost saying to him, mate, you got off lightly, which probably wasn't the nicest thing to say to a bloke who's just lost his whole fence, but that's how it felt standing there. Perspective's a strange thing.

The mother reckons the whole street looked like a bomb had gone off, and she's not wrong. Trampolines in trees, bins down the road that weren't even ours, someone's whole gazebo wrapped round a power pole.

Anyway the days after were nothing but cleanup, absolute chaos. Mate with a chainsaw came round first thing to help get the tree cut back enough that we could get the car out, took him and another bloke the best part of a morning. Tarp over the roof till the insurance bloke could come out and have a look, which took its sweet time, and then it was weeks of back and forth with them being dodgy about what was covered and what wasn't, the usual carry-on. No power for three days so it was esky and barbie for every meal, which the kids thought was heaps good actually, treated it like camping. I was keen to just get it sorted quick but she'll be right, we got there eventually, tradies booked in, roof done properly by the end of it.

And that's the story of it, and every time I tell it now I still can't help meself, I still go "and then this tree comes down, bang, I swear to god" and "the window just went crack right in front of us" like I'm telling it for the first time, even though the mother's heard it about forty times and just rolls her eyes at me. But you don't forget a night like that in a hurry, mate. You really don't.
