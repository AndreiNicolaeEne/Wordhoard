Righto, you weren't there for any of it so I might as well tell you the whole saga properly, start to finish, because it's a good one, mate, even if I come out of it looking a bit of a goose in parts.

So you know how it is, everyone round here's been surfing since they were about four, their old man plonks them on a foam board at Freshie and off they go, and by high school they're out the back at Bondi like it's nothing. I never had a dad who surfed, grew up out west, never even saw the ocean much till I was a teenager, and it just never happened for me. Kept meaning to. Kept saying "yeah nah I'll get onto that one day." Well, one day turned into decades, and I woke up one arvo about to turn forty-one thinking, right, this is it, if I don't do this now I'm never doing it, so I signed myself up for a term of lessons down the south end like some kid on his first day of school, except everyone else in the group photo was actually a kid.

First lesson, mate, an absolute disaster. The instructor's this leathery bloke, been doing it thirty years, patient as anything but he doesn't sugarcoat it either. Had me practising pop-ups on the sand for what felt like an hour, and even there, on solid ground, not moving, I could not get my body to do the thing. Everyone else in the group's popping up smooth as you like and I'm just flailing round like a fish that's been chucked out the esky. He pulls me aside at the end, not unkind about it, just straight up.

The instructor: You'll get there, but I won't lie to you, it's gonna take you longer than it takes them. Body's not as forgiving at your age. Just gotta be right with that.

And fair enough, that's honest, but it still didn't feel great hearing it, especially with a bunch of thirteen-year-olds in the group who were reading the waves better than me by day three. Day three! I hadn't even stood up yet and these kids are cruising along like they were born out there, which I suppose in a sense they were.

The first week was carnage on my body, I won't lie. Muscles I didn't know I owned were absolutely screaming at me. Shoulders, obviously, from all the paddling, but also weird bits, like the sides of my feet, my neck, some muscle under my ribs I'm fairly sure had never fired once in its life before that week. Getting out of bed of a morning was its own event. My partner reckoned I was walking round the house like an old man with a crook back, which, fair, I was.

And then there was the fall. The big one. I still think about it. I'd actually caught a decent bit of whitewash, was up on my feet for maybe two whole seconds, felt fair dinkum brilliant, and then the board just chucked me straight over the front of it, headfirst, and the wave came down on top of me like it had a personal grudge. Got properly dumped, tumbled round under there not knowing which way was up, copped a mouthful of salt water, and when I finally surfaced I couldn't get a breath in for what felt like ages, just standing there in the shallows sort of wheezing and seeing stars a bit. Genuinely thought for a second, right, that's it, I'm done, this is a young person's game and I am not a young person, I'm getting out and I'm not coming back.

Told my mate that too, dead serious, rang her that arvo still half in shock.

Me: Nah, I'm calling it. I nearly drowned in a foot and a half of water in front of a bunch of teenagers, I'm not doing that again.

My mate: You're not calling it. You're calling me, having a whinge, and then you're going back next week, because you'll be filthy at yourself in a fortnight if you don't.

She was right, course she was, and she's the reason I dragged myself back down there the following Saturday, heart not really in it, dead keen to just watch from the sand if I'm honest. But I got back out, and that was the whole turning point really, because after that it was slow, but it was moving.

Slow doesn't even cover it though. Weeks and weeks of getting one tiny thing right after getting it wrong about forty times. Popping up on the wrong foot. Paddling too late. Paddling too early. Falling off in ways that weren't even dramatic, just quietly toppling sideways for no reason, in front of whoever happened to be out there that day, which was usually a decent little crowd bobbing round on their boards, some of them calling out something encouraging when I actually stood up for once, and a couple of the young ones clearly having a laugh at my expense, not nastily, just, you know, kids. I stopped comparing myself to them pretty early on though, because that was a mug's game, comparing yourself to someone who's been doing it since they were in nappies. The only comparison that ever did me any good was against myself a month back. Was I better than the bloke who fell off every single wave in week one? Heaps better. That was the only measuring stick worth using.

Then, must've been about the fourth month, something just clicked. Can't explain it properly even now, it wasn't anything I thought my way into, it was just one day my body knew where to put itself, knew the timing without me working it out first. Caught a wave, popped up without even a thought about it, and stayed up, all the way in, and it wasn't hard anymore. Still not easy, but not impossible, which is a different thing entirely. That feeling, mate, I reckon about it a lot even now.

Never did catch the young ones, not once, they were always going to be better than me and quicker about it, and I made my peace with that pretty comfortably in the end, because it stopped being about them. By the time the season wrapped up I was standing up most times I tried, riding a fair way in, reading a wave before it even broke, all stuff I could not have told you the first thing about a year before. And now, looking back on the whole mad business, the falls and the aching and the copping it from the young fellas, none of it sits sour in my memory at all. She'll be right, that's what I reckon whenever I think back on it. Heaps glad I did it, fair dinkum. Wouldn't change a bit of it.
