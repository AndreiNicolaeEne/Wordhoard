Mate, I'm not gunna sit here and tell you it gets easy, cause it doesn't, not really, but I wanna tell you what actually happened to me, not the version people put on a card, the real one, cause nobody sat me down and did that and I reckon it would've helped heaps.

The first few days, honestly, I don't remember feeling much of anything, and I used to think that meant something was wrong with me. It's not that. It's that there's so much going on, there's people in and out of the house from brekkie till whenever, there's the funeral home to ring, there's the celebrant, there's my sister trying to sort the flowers and me trying to sort the death certificate and Medicare and Centrelink all in the same week, and someone's always putting the kettle on, and youse are all just moving through it on autopilot together. There's no time to feel the actual thing. You're too busy being useful. I reckon that's almost merciful, at the start, even though it doesn't feel like mercy at the time, it just feels like static.

The bit that got me, properly got me, was after. Once everyone had finally gone home, once the last casserole dish had been washed up and given back and the last person had said their bit at the door and driven off, and it was just me in the house. That silence lands different to anything else, mate. It's not peaceful quiet, it's this huge empty thing, and you realise this is it now, this is the house and nobody's coming through that door later. I just stood in the kitchen for I dunno how long. Didn't cry straight away even. Just stood there.

Body went dodgy on me for weeks after that. Couldn't eat, days would go by and I'd realise I hadn't had a proper meal, just picking at toast cause someone made me. And the sleep was worse. Never a full night, just these strange little bursts, drop off for an hour, wide awake at three, drop off again for twenty minutes round dawn, up again for the day feeling like you'd been hit by a bus. Went on like that for a fair while.

The first month was the strangest bit though, cause the world just kept going, didn't it. People back at work like normal, the servo still open at the same hours, the same ads on the telly, and you're standing there thinking, how is everyone just carrying on, don't they know. Nobody's done anything wrong, it's just you're nowhere near ready and the world's already three streets ahead of you.

And then there was this one day, nothing marked on it beforehand, just an ordinary Tuesday, no anniversary, no nothing, and it undid me completely. No warning at all. I remember standing in the servo doing something dead ordinary, buying milk, and I just about hit the deck. You can brace yourself for the big days. It's the nothing days that get you.

Months on from that even, it'd still happen, and there was no pattern to it whatsoever. A smell, some random object, half the time I couldn't even tell you what set it off, and a wave would just come up out of nowhere and flatten me for a bit. Still happens now if I'm honest, less but.

The long stretch in the middle, that's the bit nobody warns you about, cause it's not dramatic, it's just this fog. Dulls everything, even the good days. You'd have a nice arvo with mates, a barbie, whatever, and there'd still be this grey film over the top of it, like you're watching your own life through a window. That went on longer than I expected, fair dinkum, way longer.

Someone said something to me in that stretch that cut way deeper than it should've, some throwaway line about how I should be "getting back to normal by now," and I know they meant nothing by it, but I whinged about it to my sister for a week after, it just sat wrong in me. And then, beside that, there was this one small thing, my workmate just turned up one arvo with nothing said, no big speech, just sat with me and put the telly on and made a cuppa, didn't try and fix anything, and that helped more than every official grief pamphlet and every well-meaning phone call put together. Funny how it's never the planned stuff that lands.

The anniversary I dreaded for weeks beforehand, properly dreaded it, counting down the fortnight like it was a court date. And then it came, and I got through it, clumsy as anything, didn't do anything special, just got through the day in one piece, and that was the whole point of it, turns out. Getting through was enough. Nobody said it had to look like anything.

Appetite and sleep came good again eventually but never in one go, more like two steps forward one back, a good week then a rough one out of nowhere, no rhyme or reason to it.

And then there was a day, months in, where I laughed, properly laughed, at something dumb someone said, and about two seconds later the guilt hit me like a tonne of bricks, like I wasn't allowed to. Took me a while to be right with that, to reckon laughing didn't mean I'd forgotten anything.

By the end of the year I was carrying it different. Lighter in spots, definitely, but never gone, and I don't reckon it's meant to go, not fair dinkum gone. You just get better at holding it.

So that's me, that's what my year looked like, but yours doesn't have to match it anywhere. It doesn't stay this hard forever, mate, it eases, but there's no set timeline on any of it, no fortnight number, no year number, nothing official. Whatever your year looks like, don't go measuring it against mine or anyone else's. She'll be right in her own time, not on a schedule. You just take it as it comes.
