G'day. Sit down a sec, because you were not there Friday and you have got to hear this one properly, hand by hand, or it's not gunna make sense why it's still doing the rounds.

Four of us round the host's kitchen table. Me, the host, the host's fella, and my flatmate. Kicked me thongs off at the door, esky already parked in the corner sweating on itself because nobody wanted a cold one till later, mozzies going off out the back so we had the flyscreen shut quick smart. Stakes agreed before a single card went out, two dollar buy in, twenty cent bumps, small enough that nobody's gunna whinge about it after, not really, not fair dinkum. The host had chucked a sickie that arvo so we started earlier than usual, which suited everyone, all of us keen on a Friday night at the cards anyway.

First four hands went by the way they always go. Nobody clearly up, nobody down, cards just moving round the table, a bit of a whinge here and there about who'd copped a rubbish deal, nothing in it. Someone had a go at the host's fella's shirt, he had a go back, standard stuff.

Then hand five turns and it's the host's fella takes it. Fair enough, happens to everyone. Hand six, same bloke again. By hand seven my flatmate's muttering "righto, righto" every time his cards come good, half joking, half not yet. Hand eight and that's four running for him, and it stops being the funny kind of streak and starts being the other kind, if you know what I mean.

Someone said something quiet about how clean the shuffle looked. I reckon it was the host, though don't hold me to that. Just a remark, not an accusation, more a "settle down" sort of comment. Laughed off straight away. Nobody's keen to make a thing of it over twenty cents, are they.

Hand nine, the host's fella deals again, and this time my flatmate's watching his hands instead of her own cards, and she clocks something proper dodgy in the way they're coming off the bottom of the deck. Different to how a normal shuffle looks, she reckoned after, and once she pointed it out I could half see it too, though I wouldn't have picked it on my own.

My flatmate: Oi. You're dealing off the bottom.

Straight out, no hedging, dead at him.

The host's fella: I am not.

Flat as that. No wobble, no "what are you on about," no laugh to buy a second. Same tone as if she'd asked him to pass the chips.

And that's where it went pear shaped. The host backed my flatmate, reckoned she wouldn't say it if she hadn't seen something. I sat a bit on the fence because fair dinkum I hadn't been watching close enough the whole run to swear to anything either way.

Me: I dunno, wasn't watching that close, but.

The host's fella: Because there's nothing to watch, that's why.

We went back over the whole four hands, arguing whether the plays even made sense, whether he'd have needed to see the bottom card to play it the way he did, whether the shuffle before hand five had looked off even at the time. Settled heaps of nothing. You try and reconstruct a hand of cards forty minutes after the fact and everyone's memory's bent a different way already, everyone remembering it to fit whatever they'd already decided.

The host's fella: She'll be right. Keep dealing.

Which, credit where it's due, is a cool way to shut down an argument you might be on the wrong side of, but nobody was walking out over twenty cent bumps, so we kept going. Ease was gone off the table from then though. Every deal after that had someone watching hands instead of watching their own cards, and it wasn't heaps of fun anymore, more like finishing off a job you'd started.

Hand ten went to the host, first thing that had gone her way all night, and there was a proper cheer for it, more relief than anything, like the room needed it.

Hand eleven, the last one, my flatmate took it clean, and there was a laugh about that too, her being the one who'd called it in the first place then walking off with the last hand herself.

Tallied it up the way we always do at the end, chips into dollars. Eleven hands played total. The host's fella finished up two dollars over whoever came second on the night, which by the time we counted was me, if being two bucks down counts as coming second at anything. So he took the whole night either way, accused or not, and there's not a lot of arguing with a tally.

And that's the bit that never gets put to bed. Every single time the four of us sit down again, someone brings it up before the cards are even out of the packet, half joking, "no funny business tonight," and the host's fella comes back with the exact same line, flat as the first time, reckons he never did a thing. My flatmate still reckons she saw what she saw and no amount of going back over it is gunna change her mind. Nobody's ever knocked back a game over it, mind you, we're all still keen on a Friday, but fair dinkum it comes up like clockwork, unsettled as the night it started, every single fortnight we get the cards out again.
