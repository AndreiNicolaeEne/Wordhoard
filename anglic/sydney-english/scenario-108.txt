Right. I know. It's been months and I keep telling you "next week, promise" and then next week turns into next fortnight and next fortnight turns into you probably thinking I've forgotten you exist over there. I haven't. I've just been useless at sitting down and actually doing it, which you know is my one flaw, my only flaw, don't argue. Tonight the house is quiet, the old man's got the footy on low in the other room, and I've made a cuppa and told myself I'm not getting up off this chair till it's done. So here it is. The proper letter. Finally.

House first, since that's what you always ask about before you even ask about people. The back fence is done. Fair dinkum, after two years of it leaning on the neighbour's lemon tree like a bloke who's had one too many at the pub, the old man got a mate from work round on a Saturday and they had it up straight by the arvo. Looks flash. Naturally the week after that the downpipe on the laundry side decided it was its turn to carry on, so now that drips every time it rains, which this winter has been most days, so that's been fun. On the list. Everything's always on the list. On the nicer side, I finally put a lemon tree in myself, out past the clothesline, little scrawny thing at the moment but the bloke at the nursery reckons it'll take off once the weather warms up. And we got a new clothesline put up too, the old one had gone dodgy in the wind, kept dumping the washing in the dirt, so that's sorted now at least.

Big news though, proper news, is the old man's job. You remember he'd been at the panel place donkey's years. Well they shut the whole yard down back in autumn, no warning really, just everyone in on a Monday and told that's it. He was spewing, I won't lie to you, spent a good week just sitting round not saying much, which for him is saying a lot. Had to go and sort himself out at Centrelink and everything, which he hated, standing in that queue with blokes half his age. But he's landed on his feet, fair dinkum. Doing courier driving now, out of a depot the other side of the bridge, up before sparrow's fart every morning, home smelling like servo pies more nights than I'd like. Mum reckons he's happier for it though, says he was going stale doing the same bench for that long, and he does seem lighter, so. She's cut back her own hours to three days now to help him with the paperwork side of things, so the house runs different to how it used to, that's for sure.

Still no one's touched the good chair since the barbie incident. You'd know exactly what I mean by that and I'm saying no more, some things stay between us.

Down the street, the family with the dog that never shut up finally moved on, out west somewhere I think, no one got the full story. New couple's in there now, keep to themselves mostly, though the mum said g'day to them over the fence last week and reckons they seem alright. And the family on the corner, the ones with the three kids already, had another one back in the cold snap. Whole street's gone round with something to drop off. Feels like there's prams everywhere you look these days.

Two people still ask after you every single time I see them, without fail. Had this exact conversation with the neighbour just last week, out the front while I was getting the bins in.

The neighbour: Any word from your sister?

Me: Yeah, she's good, flat out with work as usual.

The neighbour: Tell her I said g'day. Tell her the lemon tree's finally standing up straight without her.

Me: Will do.

And your old workmate from the servo job, ran into her at the shops, same thing every time.

Your old workmate: You still owe me her number, you know that.

Me: I know, I know, I'll sort it, no dramas.

Your old workmate: She better not have gone and forgotten us all over there.

Me: She hasn't. Reckon she thinks about this place more than she lets on.

Now the thing I wouldn't ever say to your face on the phone because we'd both go to water and it'd be awkward for a week. I miss you heaps more than I let on when we're talking, more than the calls would ever show. I go into your old room sometimes just to sit, not even doing anything, just sitting there like a goose. Haven't told mum that. Telling you instead, in writing, where it's safer somehow.

Season's been a shocker, if I'm honest. Rain that wouldn't let up for what felt like a solid fortnight straight, the sort that gets into everything, mould starting up in the laundry, everyone's washing never drying properly, hanging round the house like ghosts. Then the heater carked it right in the coldest week, of course, typical timing, and we were all rugging up in front of the telly with blankets like a pack of bogans till the old man got someone in to fix it. She'll be right now though. Always is eventually.

Tell us a few things, would you. What's the weather actually like where you are this time of year, proper answer, not the postcard version you give mum. Are you keen on the job still or is it wearing thin on you now the new's worn off. And have you found yourself a decent local, somewhere you can just rock up on your own of an evening and not feel like a stranger, because I worry about that, whether you've got your people over there yet or whether you're still doing it all solo.

Anyway. I'll get that coat of yours in the post this week, the one you left behind in the wardrobe, the one you keep asking about and I keep saying I'll send and then don't. This time for real, I promise, no more excuses. Write back when you can, doesn't have to be some big thing, even just a few lines. Miss you heaps. Mum sends her love and so does everyone who keeps asking after you, and so do I, more than I'll ever properly say out loud.
