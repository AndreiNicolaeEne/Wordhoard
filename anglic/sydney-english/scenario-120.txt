Me: So you never heard the marathon story. Righto, get us another one out the esky and I'll tell it properly, but.

My friend: Go on then. I only know you did it, I don't know how you did it.

Me: Well that's the thing, nobody did, that's half the problem. I opened me big mouth at a barbie, must've been eight months out, dead sober too, that's the worst part, and announced to the whole yard I was doing the marathon. Hadn't run further than the servo and back in about six years. But I'd said it in front of me old man, the sister, half the street, so there was no quiet way of taking it back after. Soon as you say it out loud like that you're locked in, everyone's asking how the training's going before you've even bought the shoes.

My friend: Classic you.

Me: I know, I know. So first coupla weeks, mate, I will not lie to you, it was heaps worse than I reckoned it'd be. I thought I was reasonably fit, walk to work and that, but the body was not having a bar of it. Every session left me more wrecked than the one before, like it was compounding. I'd get up the next arvo, sorry, next morning, felt like arvo the way I was moving, and everything from the hips down was just cactus. I was hobbling round the office like an old bloke, and everyone havin a go, "how's the marathon training, champ," youse know how it is, they love it when you've bitten off more than you can chew.

My friend: Did you keep at it though.

Me: Settled into it after a bit, yeah. Got meself a proper routine, out the door same time most days whatever the weather was doing, didn't matter if it was bucketing down or I was in a shocking mood, you just go, cause if you let yourself off once you let yourself off forever. That's the bit nobody tells you, it's not really about the legs after the first month, it's about not giving yourself an excuse.

Then round about week nine, ten, I copped a real setback. Knee just went on me halfway through a long run, felt like something tore, and I ended up not running for the better part of a month. Proper scared me, not gunna lie, I thought that's it, season's over before it started, and everyone's gunna ask what happened to the marathon. Had to go the physio, wasn't bulk billed either, so that was rego money each visit till I dug the health care card out, thank Christ for the HCC or I'd have been whinging about that on top of everything else.

My friend: So how'd you get back into it.

Me: Cut the whole plan right back once I was cleared. Way more careful than I'd been. Didn't chase the numbers I'd had before the knee, just built it up slow, extra rest days, the physio drummin it into me. And that's when a bloke from work started coming out with me, we'd sorta fallen into it, he was keen to do a half at the same event so it lined up. Having him there made a real difference, not every session, but the ones where you truly cannot be bothered, having someone waiting on the corner means you actually turn up. He wasn't gunna let me chuck a sickie on a training day and I wasn't gunna let him either.

My friend: Fair enough.

Me: Right, now the one day. There's always one day, and this was it. Long run, meant to be the big one before the taper started, and it all went pear shaped at once. Body just switched off round the eighteen k mark, legs turned to lead, and the head went with it, I was ready to sit down on the nature strip and ring for a lift home. Nearly ended the whole attempt right there, fair dinkum, if the training partner hadn't been beside me telling me to just get to the next tree, next lamp post, whatever. Never come that close to chucking it in before or since.

And the doubt, mate, that hit worst somewhere round the middle of the whole season, not even on a bad run, just generally. The actual event still felt so far off it didn't seem real, like I was training for something that was never actually gunna happen, just training to keep training. You start reckoning maybe you're not the sort of person who does this sort of thing.

My friend: What turned it round.

Me: Small thing really. Bit past the middle there was one session that just went better than any before it, nothing flash, just felt easy for once, like the legs finally agreed to the arrangement. Wasn't dramatic, but it put the wind back in it. After that I tightened everything up heaps, watching the food properly, actually resting on the rest days instead of just not running, taking the whole caper serious. And then final week, and this near killed me cause every instinct says train harder, I backed right off instead. Barely ran at all. Had to trust that the work was already done, months of it, nothing left to prove in the last few days.

Night before, didn't sleep worth a damn. Lay there going over the whole thing hours before there was any need to, checking the alarm about six times, running through the start in me head like that was gunna help.

My friend: And the day itself.

Me: Fine for ages, honestly heaps better than I expected for the first three quarters, felt like I had it in hand. Then the last stretch, the last five or six k, that's when it properly turned on me. Everything in me wanted to stop, dead set everything, and I mean walk, sit down, ring someone, anything but keep going. Body was done arguing politely and just started screaming. And you keep going anyway, don't know how exactly, you just don't let the legs stop even when they've voted on it themselves.

Crossing the line was nothing like what I'd pictured. I'd built up this whole scene in me head, arms up, big grin, the lot. Reality was I just sorta stopped moving and near went down, some bloke in a hi vis vest caught me under the arm, and I didn't feel proud or emotional or any of that, I just felt hollowed right out, like there was nothing left in the tank to feel anything with. Took a good hour before it even started to feel like an achievement rather than just an ordeal that had finished.

My friend: And after.

Me: Body took the best part of a week to feel like mine again. Stairs were a joke for days, going down worse than up, had to go down sideways like an old dear. But mate, honestly, whole season changed what I reckon I can do. Not just running, just generally. You find out the body'll do heaps more than you give it credit for if you don't let the head talk you out of it first. She'll be right, that's about the size of it. I still can't quite believe I backed meself into it at a barbie, but I'm heaps glad I did.

My friend: No dramas. Get us another one out the esky then.

Me: Yeah, no worries.
