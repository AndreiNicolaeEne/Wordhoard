Oh mate, don't even get me started, but since you asked I'll give you the whole saga, start to finish, because someone has to know what I went through.

So it was my turn this year, right. Everyone else has hosted at some point, my sister's done it, the in-laws have done it twice, and I kept dodging it every year with some excuse, and this time I just went, nah, righto, I'll do it, how hard can it be. Famous last words. I reckon I was fine about it for about a week and then it hit me properly, like three in the morning, wide awake, going what have I actually agreed to here. I've never fed more than four people in my life and suddenly I'm meant to be running the whole show.

And of course I couldn't just do something simple, could I. No, I sat down with a cuppa and drew up this menu that in hindsight was completely deranged. I had the bird, I had a ham on top of that, I had about four different sides, a pav, a trifle, some fancy potato thing I saw some bloke do on the telly that apparently takes three hours and about a kilo of butter. Heaps ambitious, way more than sensible, and I knew it even while I was writing it down but I just kept adding things. My workmate said to me at the time,

My workmate: You do know you don't have to cook everything you've ever seen in your life in one sitting.

Me: Yeah nah it'll be right, I'm keen to give it a go.

Should've listened to her honestly.

Then the numbers started creeping. Started as eight of us, cosy, manageable. Then my sister goes can she bring her partner's parents because they'd otherwise be on their own, and fair enough, so that's ten. Then the neighbour's rocking around by himself so he gets roped in, that's eleven. Then someone's cousin's in town. By the end I'm catering for sixteen off a menu I wrote for eight, and I did not adjust the quantities properly because I was in denial the whole time.

Shopping trip was its own disaster. Out to the shops with a list longer than my arm, and everything's fine until I'm looking for one specific thing, this one particular sauce, and it is nowhere. Three different shops, up and down every aisle, asked the bloke stacking shelves and he just shrugged, asked at the next place and same deal, everyone's cleaned out because of course everyone's doing the same shop as me that week. Ended up ringing round family half defeated asking if anyone had a spare bottle sitting in a cupboard somewhere, fair dinkum I was that desperate over one sauce.

Night before, I was up till stupid o'clock prepping. Peeling, chopping, marinating, trying to get ahead of myself because I'd read all these tips about doing the work the night before so the day itself is easy. Went to bed way too late, mind absolutely racing, and I actually wrote out a proper timed schedule for the morning, like an actual bit of paper, ten fifteen do this, ten forty-five do that, oven on at this time, very organised, very proud of myself.

Didn't survive contact with the actual morning. Not even close. First thing, I slept through my alarm because I'd gone to bed so late, so the whole schedule's already out the window before it's even started. Then I'm trying to do three things at once and forgetting steps, and the kitchen's chaos within about twenty minutes, benches covered, every pot I own dirty already and nothing's even cooked yet.

Then the first proper disaster hits. I'd got the potato thing in early because it needed the longest, got distracted dealing with the ham, and by the time I remembered it, it's ruined, black on top, stuck to the tray, an absolute write-off. I just stood there for a second going oh no, actual proper panic, because that was meant to be a hero dish and now it's bin material.

Scrambled straight into fix-it mode. No time to start it again from scratch so I threw together a much simpler version with what I had left, half the ambition, none of the fancy bits, just got it done and moving. Told myself she'll be right, nobody's going to remember the original plan anyway, they're only eating what turns up.

Right in the middle of that chaos the doorbell goes and it's my mother-in-law, dead early, way before anyone else was meant to show, and normally I'd be embarrassed being caught mid-disaster but honestly I just handed her an apron and put her straight on peeling duty. She didn't even blink, just rolled the sleeves up.

Mother-in-law: Well I'm here now, might as well make myself useful, love.

Best thing that happened all morning, fair dinkum.

Then it's the oven and stove both absolutely maxed out, everything wanting the same twenty minutes at the same temperature, dishes literally queuing, one thing coming out so something else can go in, me doing this ridiculous juggling act shuffling trays around, cursing that I don't have a second oven, genuinely considering whether the barbie out back could take an overflow dish, which it actually did in the end and saved me.

Funny thing though, the dish I was least fussed about, this side I threw together almost as an afterthought because I had spare veggies going soft in the fridge, came out heaps better than I expected. Better than half the things I'd stressed over for a fortnight.

Meal ended up starting a good hour and a half later than I'd planned, everyone standing round with drinks going hungry, but nobody seemed to mind much, that's how it always goes I reckon.

First bites and you get the honest reactions straight away, don't you. Bit of quiet, a few nods, someone going oh that's lovely, someone else with their mouth full giving a thumbs up. Then the blunt one came, my brother, no filter whatsoever, straight up with the ham.

The eldest: Yeah nah this one's a bit dry, mate, not gonna lie.

No sugar coating it at all, and everyone just laughed because he's right, it was a bit dry, I'd overcooked it a touch trying to keep it warm through the delays.

But that little afterthought veggie dish, the one I nearly didn't even bother making, that was gone in about ten minutes flat, people going back for seconds and thirds, loudest praise of the whole table.

My sister: Whoever made this needs to make it every single time from now on, forget the fancy stuff.

By the end of it everyone's slumped round full, esky's empty, plates everywhere, and someone asks would I do it again next year. Half joking I said absolutely never again, not a chance, and everyone laughed because they know full well I'll be back at it in twelve months doing exactly the same thing to myself. She'll be right though, that's hosting for you, heaps of stress and somehow you'd do it all again.
