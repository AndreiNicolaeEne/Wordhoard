The tin lives on top the fridge behind the Vegemite, has done for years, and I go for it Tuesday arvo like always to sort the fortnight's shop and it's light. Straight off, before I've even counted, you can feel it's light in your hand. Count it anyway. Count it twice. Then check under the telly where a twenty ends up sometimes when someone's grabbed it for the servo and meant to put it back with a note, no dramas. Not there either.

So I start asking round the house, easy at first.

Me: Anyone been in the tin? Not having a go, just asking, someone might've borrowed it meaning to say.

The eldest: Wasn't me.

The middle one: Nuh, haven't touched it, swear.

The youngest: Wasn't me, Mum, fair dinkum.

Flat as, the lot of them, not one looking up proper off the phone.

Then it comes to me the eldest was home alone Thursday, everyone else at work or uni, and the money was right Wednesday night, I'd counted it myself.

Me: You were here by yourself Thursday. What happened to it.

The eldest: I already told you, it wasn't me.

Me: I'm not mucking round now, mate. There's near two hundred gone out of that tin and you're the only one was in this house all day.

The eldest: And I'm telling you I never went near your tin, so don't reckon you can just pin it on me 'cause I was home alone, that's dodgy as!

Me: Who else was it meant to be, it doesn't walk out the door itself!

The eldest: I dunno, maybe you miscounted, maybe you spent it and forgot, but it wasn't me, I'm not lying to you!

Me: Don't you dare turn this round on me, I know what I bloody counted.

The eldest: I don't care what you reckon you counted, I never touched it, and I'm sick of being blamed for youse losing track of your own money!

Nobody backed down an inch, and it just sort of sat there in the kitchen after, the both of us not looking at the other one.
