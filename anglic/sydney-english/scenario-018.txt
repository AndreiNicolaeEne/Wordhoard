Righto, righto, settle down, youse lot, before the esky runs dry and nobody can hear a word of it anyway. My partner's told me just to get up and tell it properly since apparently I tell it better, which is rich, but here we are.

So. How we met. None of this online-dating carry-on, none of that. It was a mate of mine's barbie, out the back of his place, must be, gees, it's a long time ago now. I'm on snag duty because nobody trusts me with anything harder, and I put the snags on before the prawns went on, and this one marches over from the esky with a stubby in hand and has a go at me right there in front of everyone.

Me: What, I've done something wrong now?

My partner: Mate, prawns go on first. You put snags down before the prawns, that's a hanging offence round here.

That's it. That's the whole thing that started it. I still don't reckon there's an actual rule about it, I think it was made up on the spot, but it's stuck with me twenty-odd years and it'll stick with me twenty more. Told with that dead straight face like I'd committed an actual crime.

Now I've got to admit, my partner wasn't keen straight off, and fair enough too. Had just come out the other side of a pretty dodgy relationship not long before, one of those ones that drags on longer than it should, and wasn't looking to jump into anything, kept saying as much to anyone who'd listen. So for a good few weeks it was just running into each other at that same mate's place, bit of texting, nothing in it, or nothing anyone was calling anything. What changed it, far as I can work out, was I just kept turning up. Didn't push, didn't carry on about it, just kept being around, same as always, and I reckon that's what wore it down in the end, not some big grand gesture, just consistency. My partner still says that's the whole trick with me, more persistent than persuasive.

First proper time we spent time together, just the two of us, not by accident, that took a while to organise, because my partner was doing shift work at the time, nights mostly, and I was your standard nine-to-five bloke, so our days were like ships passing. I'd be getting up when this one was getting in, half dead on the feet. Took us weeks to line up something that wasn't a five-minute chat at a servo on the way to somewhere else. In the end we gave up on dinner altogether and just did brekkie. I'd swing past after the nightshift finished and we'd get a coffee and something greasy and just sit there, knackered, and that became our thing. Still is, if I'm honest, we still do a servo brekkie most Sundays even now.

Which brings me to the thong. Youse have all heard about the thong, I know, but it's not a proper telling without it. Same night as the snags business, actually, my partner walked home from that barbie with one thong on, having lost the other one somewhere in the mate's backyard, never found it. So every anniversary since, without fail, I give this one a single thong, wrapped up all proper, and every year the joke is we're still looking for the pair. Got a drawer full of odd thongs at home now. It's a dumb joke and it's never once stopped being funny to either of us.

The moment I knew it'd gone serious, my partner came down with the worst dose of gastro going round, proper flattened by it, and I turned up with soup and chemist stuff and just stayed on the couch that night in case anything was needed. Nothing to it really, just what you'd do. But my partner rang the mother the next day to say so, and by the time I heard about it secondhand, half the family already knew before either of us had said a word to each other about where this was actually heading. That's how news travels in this family, mind you, always has.

The mother: She rang me up going on about soup, of all things. I said to her, well, that's it then, isn't it.

The proposal wasn't anything flash, and I won't pretend it was. Home on the lounge room floor of a Sunday arvo, footy on the telly, nothing booked, nothing planned, ring bought a fortnight before and sitting in my jacket pocket that whole time going soft with sweat because I couldn't work out the moment. In the end there wasn't a moment, I just turned the telly down and asked. My partner reckons I didn't even get down on one knee properly, more of a half-crouch, and that's fair, I probably didn't.

About a year on from that we were married. Didn't see the sense in a long engagement, we'd already done the waiting around bit early on.

Families took it different ways. Mine were thrilled from the off, no worries there. My partner's father, though, took his time coming round, nothing said outright, just a bit cool about it for a good while, I reckon on account of what came before, wanting to make sure this one wasn't walking into something the same again. Took him the best part of that year, honestly, before he'd properly warmed up, but he got there, and he's the one who cried the most on the day, which nobody lets him forget.

The thing from the wedding day itself that no photo ever caught, the venue's dog, some old kelpie belonging to whoever ran the place, wandered straight up the aisle halfway through the vows and sat down between the two of us like it had every right to be there, and the celebrant just kept going like nothing had happened. Everyone was laughing too hard to think about a camera. Best part of the whole day, and there's not a single photo of it anywhere.

We've had a rough trot since, same as anyone does. There was a stretch a while back where we were both out of work the same fortnight, mortgage still due regardless, and it was heaps stressful for a good long while, proper dodgy patch. What got us through it was mates and family pitching in without making a big song and dance of it, and the two of us just knuckling down and getting on with it, she'll be right and all that, even when it didn't feel right at all. We came out the other side of it, anyway. Still here.

What hasn't changed one bit since that barbie all those years back, my partner still can't let a barbecue go past without checking I've done the prawns first, and I still cop it if I haven't. Still bickers with me over nothing, still laughs at the same terrible jokes, still keen on the same daggy stuff we were keen on back then. Fair dinkum, some things just don't move.

So, if youse would all get your glasses up. To this one, for putting up with me, for the snags, for the thongs, the lot of it.

Cheers.
