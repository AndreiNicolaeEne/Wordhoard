Me: Mum, can I tell you something, and can you just let me get right through it before you say anything.

Mother: That sounds ominous. Go on then.

Me: It's about the leg. The scar. You know the one, everyone at every barbie for twenty-odd years has asked about it and I've told the same story every single time, and it's not true. None of it. I'm going to tell you the real one now because I reckon I've carried it long enough and it's starting to feel dodgy, if that makes sense, lying to you about something this small for this long. So here it is.

It wasn't the bike. There was no gravel, there was no falling off round the back of the servo like I always say. What actually happened was we were down at the usual spot, the creek past the reserve, the one with the big fig hanging half over the water, and there was four of us that arvo, stinking hot, mozzies already starting up even though the sun hadn't properly gone down. And the dare was the tree. Climb as high as you could stand and jump.

I'd backed out of it once before, maybe a month earlier, gone halfway up and just frozen and come back down, and they never let it go. Every time after that it was "remember when you chickened it", "remember when you couldn't even do the low branch", that sort of thing, and kids don't forget stuff like that, they just don't, they bring it up for years if you let them. So this time when the eldest of us said do it or don't ever come down here again, I went. And because I was that keen to prove I wasn't the one who backs out, I didn't stop where I meant to. I kept going past the branch I'd picked out in my head and I went right up near the top, way higher than any of them had actually done, higher than I should've, just to shut them all up once and for all.

Mother: Oh, love.

Me: I know. I know. So I jumped, and I must have leant back a bit or something because I hit the water wrong, flat, and it knocked every bit of wind out of me straight off, like someone had sat on my chest. And under the water there was something, an old bit of fencing wire or a tin sheet or god knows, nobody ever went looking to find out what it actually was, but it opened my leg right up, and I came up not even able to yell properly because I still had no breath in me, just this sort of horrible quiet thrashing.

The others panicked. Properly panicked, dragged me out onto the bank, and one of them was crying, and we're all just sitting there looking at this cut that's gone straight through, blood everywhere, and someone said we can't say what happened, our mums'll never let us near the creek again, and someone else said the bikes, say it was the bikes, we were mucking around on the bikes near the gravel bit by the shops. And I agreed to it. Straight away. Soaked through, shaking, bleeding, and I agreed to lie to you before I'd even got my breath back properly.

So I came home telling you I'd come off my bike, and you believed me, why wouldn't you, and you drove me to the bulk-billing doctor because we had to flash the HCC card, otherwise it would've cost a fortune we didn't have back then, and I sat there and told the doctor the same story with a straight face while you held my hand, and he stitched it up not knowing any better either, and that was that. Story set. And I've told it that way to every partner, every workmate, every random at a pool who's gone "how'd you get that", for twenty-something years now. Never once wobbled on it. Fair dinkum, I nearly believe it myself some days.

Mother: Why now though. After all this time, why's it coming out now.

Me: Because a fortnight ago it was my own kid, down near the water with a couple of mates, and one of the older ones dared them to jump off the rocks, and I heard myself saying don't you dare do anything like that, and my kid looked at me and said but you did stuff like that, everyone says you were mad for it, and I nearly told the bike story again, right there, on autopilot, and something about doing that to my own kid made me feel proper sick. Lying to protect a story that was never about protecting anyone, it was just about not looking like I'd stuffed up. And I thought about you finding out one day after I'm gone, from someone else, one of that lot from back then, at a funeral or whatever, some bloke going oh yeah, remember the tree, and you never knowing, all these years, and I couldn't sit with that.

So that's the apology, really, that's what this is. I'm sorry I lied to you when I was a kid and scared and didn't want to get in trouble, I get that, I was young, no dramas, that's kid stuff. But I'm properly sorry I kept it going for twenty years after, when I was old enough to know better, when I could've told you at any barbie, any Christmas, any time you asked and I gave you the same rubbish answer with a straight face. You believed me because you trusted me, and I let that trust do the work of covering up something that honestly wasn't even that bad once you know the truth of it, it's not like I robbed a servo, I jumped off a tree like an idiot because some other idiots dared me to.

Mother: You always were one for taking a dare too far.

Me: I know. I know I was. And I'm sorry, Mum. Properly sorry, not the sorry you say to get out of trouble, the real one. You didn't do anything wrong, you just believed your kid, and that's on me, not you, that ever needed fixing up in the first place.

Mother: Well. She'll be right. I'm just glad you're telling me now and not, I don't know, at my funeral like you said.

Me: Yeah. Me too. Heaps glad, actually. I've been keen to get this off my chest for longer than I probably realised.

Mother: Next time one of your lot dares your kid into something, you tell them straight, none of this proving yourself business. It's not worth a scar and twenty years of fibbing to your mother over it.

Me: Yeah, fair enough. I reckon you're right. No arguments there.
