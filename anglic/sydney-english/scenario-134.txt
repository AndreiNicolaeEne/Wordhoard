You could tell early doors this was gunna be the one. By September the vines were already going off, and my old man reckoned we'd smash the fortnight target before we'd even properly started. Come the end of it we'd pulled in near double what we normally do, no worries, and everyone down the road was saying the same about their own patches.

But the pumpkins, mate, the pumpkins were something else. We're talking heaps past what that paddock usually gives us, way past. Last time anyone come close was donkey's years back, when the eldest reckoned they'd had a big one too, but even that got beaten by a mile this time round.

Had to get extra hands in for the last push, couple of blokes from up the road and a mate's kid who'd chucked a sickie off his real job just to help us get it in before it turned. Long days, brekkie at sparrow's and still going come arvo smoko, thongs off and gumboots on the whole week, no complaints though.

Once we'd covered what the family needed and what was owed round the neighbourhood, there was still heaps left over. Some went in the shed to store, rest we traded off at the servo noticeboard and down the markets, whatever people were keen on.

The eldest grower on our road, been at it longer than anyone, stood there at the end of it shaking his head.

The eldest: Fair dinkum, that's the best I've seen it, and I've seen a fair few.

Me: Reckon it's the rain, coming right when we needed it?

The eldest: That, and a mild stretch right through the season. Everything just lined up, mate. Different to any year I can remember, but.

Still now, any bad season, someone'll say it's dodgy compared to that year, and everyone knows exactly which one they mean without you having to say another word, fair dinkum.
