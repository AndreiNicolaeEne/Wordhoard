Eleven days straight it held, no break in it at all, and I still can't get over how bad it got. First morning of the lot the frost was already thick as anything on the car, heaps colder than what they reckoned on the forecast, and we knew straight off this wasn't going to be a normal one.

Had the heater going round the clock the whole stretch, morning to night, because the second you let it drop the whole house went to ice in about ten minutes. Chucked another load on before bed every single night, no exceptions, and still woke up with your breath showing in the kitchen.

Outside tap froze solid on the fourth day, first time that's ever happened here, never in all the years we've been in this place. Pipe just gave up on itself, cracked clean through when the old man went out to have a go at it, dodgy old thing anyway but it never done that before.

The eldest reckoned she'd seen nothing like it since a cold snap back in the seventies, always whinges on about that one, said they lost half the tank of tomatoes that time too.

Me: This one's worse than the one you always go on about though, isn't it.
The eldest: Worse, love, way worse. That one never held on this long, but.

Worst of it was night eight, coldest by a mile, the whole lot of us rugged up on one couch in front of the heater because no one was keen to sleep in their own room that night.

Cost us the tomato plants out back, whole lot gone black and done for by morning, right when they were coming good and all.

Then after them eleven days it just broke. Sun came out proper, heaps warmer straight off, and everyone was out the back like it was the best arvo going. The eldest reckoned she'd never been so glad to see the sun in her life, and fair dinkum, none of us were about to argue with her on that.
