Righto, I'll tell you the whole thing, because you love this idiot as much as I do and you'll want it in order, not the short version.

By the end of the night my friend was cooked. Properly cooked, not just tipsy-and-giggly cooked, the full production, eyes gone half shut, head lolling a bit on every second sentence. Started out heaps good, I'll give it that, the arvo was all beers in the sun and someone's dodgy playlist and everyone reckoning it was going to be a top night. Then it just tipped over somewhere after the third venue and nobody clocked exactly when.

First clear sign it had gone too far, my friend stood up off the stool to go to the loo and just didn't. Like the legs put in a formal resignation. Grabbed the bar, missed, grabbed it again, and I'm across the table going hang on hang on, and of course the reply comes back instant.

My friend: Nah I'm fine, I'm fine, mate, I'm heaps fine.

Me: Mate you can't stand up.

My friend: I'm STANDING.

Wasn't. Was doing a sort of slow lean into the bar like it owed money. And the words were coming out soft at the edges, all the consonants gone AWOL, so "fine" came out more like "fiiine" with an extra syllable nobody asked for. That's when you know, right, when they insist and their own body's out there contradicting them mid-sentence.

Getting my friend to actually leave took some doing. There's always the argument, one more, just one more, and I'm not having a bar of it by this point, but my friend's not keen on leaving a night early, ever, on principle. Had to do the whole routine, promise the servo run for chips on the way, promise it wasn't the end of the night just a change of venue, the usual fibs you tell a drunk person to get them vertical and moving. Eventually got an arm under one arm and hauled, and my friend went from dead against it to full cooperative in about four seconds flat, which is also how it goes.

The walk home I had an arm round my friend the whole way, no exceptions, because the footpath was doing that thing where it seemed to lean. We didn't get far before there was a stop, right on someone's front wall, my friend just folding down onto the low bit of brick to, quote, "have a bit of a sit," which lasted till it turned into being sick into the garden bed behind it. Held the hair back, said all the useless things you say, there ya go, get it all up, you're right, you're right. Someone walking a dog past us at that exact moment gave us the full up-and-down.

The one with the dog: Big night, is it.

Me: You have no idea, mate.

The one with the dog: Good luck to youse.

Fair enough reaction, to be honest, we must've looked a state, one of us upright and apologetic and the other one draped over a stranger's fence.

Got moving again after that, and this is where the wrong turn happened, because in the confusion of holding someone up and trying to remember which street we actually needed, I went straight past the turn-off and we ended up a good block out of the way before my friend, of all people, in the state my friend was in, went hang on this isn't right. Which was almost impressive. Doubled back, lost another ten minutes we didn't have, my friend's shoes basically dragging by that point.

Tried to get water into my friend twice along the way, had a bottle in my bag the whole time like a sensible person, and got knocked back flat both times.

My friend: Don't want it. Don't need it. I'm fine.

Me: Just have a sip.

My friend: NO.

Third time's the charm though, stopped, shoved it at my friend's mouth basically, and that time it went down, a few big gulps, dribbling half down the chin, but in it went, and I reckon that was the difference between a rough morning and a properly shocking one.

Somewhere in amongst all that is when the maudlin bit came out, the kind of thing that only ever surfaces at this level of drunk and never at any other time, my friend grabbing my arm dead serious.

My friend: You know I love you though. Like actually. Like you're the best. I don't tell you enough.

Me: I know, mate. Love you too. Keep walking.

My friend: No but I MEAN it.

Which, sober, my friend would rather die than say out loud, and we both know it, and that's half of why it's funny and half of why it's actually a bit lovely.

Got to the door eventually and that was its own whole saga, the key would not go in, my friend's key, my friend's hand, absolutely refusing to cooperate, jabbing at the lock like it had personally wronged them. Had to take over, get it open myself while holding my friend up with the other arm, which is a skill nobody teaches you and you only learn on nights like this one.

Once we were in, walked my friend straight to the couch because the bedroom felt like a bridge too far, and lowered my friend down onto it more than sat, and got the shoes off, and a bucket next to the couch just in case, learned that lesson the hard way once before and never again without a bucket handy.

Last thing before my friend's eyes properly shut for the count was the big promise, the vow, dead solemn.

My friend: Never again. I'm serious. Never drinking like that again. This is it. Done.

Me: Yeah, righto, mate.

Didn't believe a word of it even as it was coming out, and I don't reckon my friend believed it either, not really, it's just the thing you say, the ceremonial bit at the end, everyone knows the script.

Checked in the next arvo, texted first because ringing felt cruel, and got back one word, dead. Went round with a bacon and egg roll because that's the actual cure, no matter what anyone tells you about hangover shakes and electrolyte drinks, it's a roll and a Powerade or nothing. My friend remembered bits, patchy, remembered the first venue clear as day, remembered almost nothing after the third, no memory of the fence, no memory of the bloke with the dog, absolutely no memory of the maudlin declaration of love, which I did not let slide, told it back word for word while my friend went red and groaned into the roll. Filled in the rest for them, the wrong turn, the door, the whole production, and by the end my friend was half laughing half wanting to disappear, saying never again, mate, fair dinkum this time, and I just said yeah, heard that one before, and we both knew, and she'll be right anyway, she always is.
