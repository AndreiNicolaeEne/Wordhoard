Righto, you want to hear the worst trip I ever done? Sit down, this one's a beauty. I still can't believe we lived through it, honestly, but here we are, so.

It was meant to be dead simple. Booked it weeks out, coach down to Melbourne for a mate's wedding, seats sorted, esky packed with a few tinnies and some sandwiches for the road, the whole show planned to the last detail. Get on about six, sleep most the way, roll in fresh as anything for the ceremony the next arvo. That was the plan, anyway.

First delay and we hadn't even left the terminal, mind you. Six o'clock comes and goes, quarter past, half past, still no coach in the bay. Turns out the one that was meant to come down from up north and turn straight round had some dramas of its own, flat tyre or something, so there's the whole lot of us just standing round with our bags going, right, here we go then, beautiful start.

Finally get moving must've been gone seven, and I remember thinking, no worries, we'll make that up easy on the road. Ha. Should've known better then and there, but.

So we're maybe two, three hours in, out past Yass somewhere, middle of nowhere really, and the whole coach starts up with this shuddering noise like something's dying underneath us, and next thing the driver's pulling in on the shoulder saying he reckons it's the gearbox, we're not going another inch. Dodgy old thing anyway, should've known.

So there's the lot of us, whole busload, standing round on the side of the highway with our bags and the esky, and it just keeps growing, because turns out there's another coach broke down not far up the road as well, some poor other mob in the same boat, so everyone funnels in together into this one big crowd standing round not having a clue what's what. Somebody's got a radio going, somebody's whinging about missing their connection, kids running round, it was a whole scene.

And the wait just stretched and stretched. Past three hours, easy, and nobody could tell us a thing, next coach coming from Sydney, no eta, next coach coming from Albury, no eta, drivers on the phone looking more and more stressed every time you asked them. And on top of all that the weather turned, a southerly came through and the temp just dropped off a cliff, started spitting, then properly bucketing down, and the whole crowd's crammed under this one tin awning at the servo trying to keep dry, mozzies somehow still going for you even in the rain, I don't know how they manage it.

And this is where it got properly hairy, because my mate goes off in all that to find where they'd shifted our bags to, drivers had moved them under tarps somewhere out of the wet, and just does not come back. Twenty minutes, near enough, and I'm standing there in the rain with everyone else thinking, right, lost my mate in a crowd of two hundred stranded strangers on the side of the Hume Highway, brilliant, this is going heaps well. Properly panicking by the end of it, going round asking people had they seen them, before they just wander back round the other side of the building, bags in hand, wondering what I was so worked up about.

Now here's the bit I've never forgotten, and it's the one good thing out of the whole day. This bloke, truckie by the looks, pulled his rig in at the servo seeing the mob of us standing there drowned, and just started handing round a stack of these little foil blankets he had in the cab, and let a good dozen of us charge our phones off his truck battery while we waited. Didn't want a thing for it, just said something like it was a shocker of a day to be stuck out here and good luck getting where you're going, and off he went. Fair dinkum, I think about that bloke every time I drive past a truck stop. Restores your faith a bit.

Anyway, by this point we're soaked, cold, and going nowhere, so we end up forking out for a cab the rest of the way into the next town, the two of us plus another couple off the coach splitting it, and even split it come to near fifty bucks each, money that was never in the plan, not a cent of it. And that's when the argument kicked off proper, because we're standing in this pub car park trying to work out what to do next.

Me: I reckon we just wait here, get the replacement coach when it comes, it's got to come eventually.

My mate: Wait for what, mate, we've been waiting three hours already, they've got no idea when anything's coming. I say we hire a car and just drive it ourselves.

Me: With what money. We just spent fifty bucks on a cab we didn't plan for.

My mate: Better than sitting here missing the whole wedding, isn't it. She'll be right, we'll sort it.

It got heated, proper heated, the kind of argument you only have with someone when you're both cold and wet and exhausted and neither one's actually wrong. In the end we hired a car off this dodgy little place in town, bloke behind the counter looked like he'd rather be anywhere else, and off we go, finally moving under our own steam.

Except my mate's driving and reckons they know a short cut round the back way that saves twenty minutes, and instead we're driving down this dirt road in the pitch dark and the rain going nowhere near Melbourne at all, for two solid hours before we work out we're basically back where we started. That was the low point for me, no question. Sitting in a hire car at midnight, soaked through, no phone reception, both of us too tired to even argue anymore, just sitting there in silence like two stunned mullets wondering how any of this had gone so wrong.

And then, out of absolutely nowhere, the stroke of luck. Rain finally eases off, one bar of reception comes back on the phone, and there's a message from another mate who was driving down separately and was about an hour ahead of us, saying they'd stopped for the night at a motel just up the road and had a spare bed going. So we roll in there, get a couple hours' actual sleep, and then it's up at dawn and straight down the highway, no more short cuts, no more dodgy back roads, just straight down the middle the whole way.

We rolled into Melbourne a full nine hours after we were ever meant to. Missed the ceremony completely, walked into the reception looking like we'd been dragged through a hedge backwards, hair everywhere, still half in yesterday's clothes because the bags never really dried out properly. The groom took one look at us and just started laughing, didn't even need the story yet, the state of us told it for him.

But we made it, and heaps of people at that reception reckon it's still the best story from the whole weekend, better than the wedding itself if you ask some of them. And here's the thing, at the time it was an absolute nightmare, proper miserable, I could've cried standing in that rain. But now, telling it, I can't stop laughing the whole way through. Funny how that works, but.
