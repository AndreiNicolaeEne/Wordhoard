Righto, I owe you the full rundown since you missed it, so here it is, warts and all.

So it was my sister's thing, the big one, twenty years married, and she'd gone all out, esky's full, the barbie going since about two in the arvo, half the street rocked up. I said I'd just have a couple and head off early because I had stuff on the next day, you know how I always say that. Well. That lasted about as long as it usually does. My brother-in-law's up there doing his toast and someone's already shoved another snag and a lamb chop on my plate before I've even said yes please, and then it's "go on, one more, it's a celebration, don't be soft," and I'm not one to knock back food I never am, so that's that gone. Then it's the drinks, and everyone's topping you up before your glass even looks empty, and you're not really counting anymore because it's not that kind of night, it's the kind where someone's mum is doing the running man on the back deck and you think well I can't just be standing here.

Last proper thing I remember clear as day is being out near the esky with a couple of the cousins, having a whinge about work like you do, and someone had put the speaker on and we were all singing along dead serious like it meant something. After that it goes patchy. I know there was dancing because apparently I was still going at it long after most people had thongs off and were sitting down having a lie in the grass. I'd told myself heaps of times through the night, right, this is the last one, I'm calling it after this song, and then somehow there's another one on and you're keen for just one more go on the dance floor and that's you sorted for another hour.

Woke up the next morning and mate, it hit like a tonne of bricks. First thing, head absolutely pounding, like something's trying to get out from the inside. Rolled over and even the thought of brekkie, any of it, eggs, toast, didn't matter what, my stomach just turned right over. The light through the curtains felt like it had it in for me personally, and my flatmate walking down the hall in thongs sounded like someone dropping pots and pans. Just sitting myself up in bed took a proper effort, like my whole body had put in a complaint overnight and was refusing to cooperate till it got heard.

My flatmate was up before me, moving round normal as anything, cup of tea in hand, and copped an eyeful of me shuffling out looking like death warmed up.

The flatmate: Oh here she is. You right there?

Me: Don't.

The flatmate: I'm not saying anything. I'm just saying you were still out there going for it when I called it, and that was, what, three hours before you.

Me: I said don't.

The flatmate: No dramas, no dramas. Reckon there's a bit of a sickie in your future today.

Me: Reckon you're not wrong.

And lying there right after that I made myself the promise, the same one, hand on heart this time, never again, never letting a night run away on me like that again, and I know full well I've said it before, probably word for word, and I'll probably say it again some day down the track, but in the moment you mean it, dead serious, every time.

Whole morning was write-off. A short walk down to the servo for a bottle of water felt like a proper expedition, legs going but the rest of me not fully signed up for it. Even a small chore, like just putting a load of washing on, sat there on the list looking like a mountain. I basically camped on the couch, curtains half drawn, telly on low, not really watching it, just needing the noise to be somewhere else other than in my head. Managed some dry toast round lunchtime, nothing more than that, no way I was going near anything with sauce or spice on it, just enough to settle things down without setting them off again. Kept quiet, kept still, let the day do its thing to me rather than the other way round.

And in between all that, bits of the night kept surfacing, little flashes, like someone flicking through photos too fast to really look at any of them properly. Most of it was harmless, us all singing badly, someone's dog getting into the esky, that sort of thing. But there's one bit that keeps coming back and it's not a good one. Pretty sure, more than pretty sure actually, I had a real go at my sister's workmate about something, can't even fully remember the ins and outs of it now, just that I was heaps loud about it and it wasn't kind, and everyone sort of went quiet for a second the way people do when they're deciding whether to say something. I owe that one a proper apology, and I know it, and I've been putting off the message because it's embarrassing as, sitting there thinking through what I'm even going to say, sorry doesn't quite cover being a bit of a bogan about it in front of half the family. Keep turning it over and it doesn't get any better the more I look at it.

By that evening though, finally started feeling like a person again. Had a proper feed, none of your dry toast this time, actual dinner, and could string a full thought together without it sliding off somewhere. Felt almost normal, sitting up, telly actually making sense, not just noise.

So there it is, the whole sorry saga. She'll be right in the end I reckon, I'll send the apology, she'll probably say no worries and mean it more than I deserve, and everyone will have a laugh about it at the next barbie. But between you and me, and this is the bit I keep sitting with, I know I'll do the exact same thing again next time someone close throws one of these on. Doesn't matter how many promises I make lying in bed feeling like absolute rubbish, next celebration that comes round I'll be first up dancing and last one still going, and I already reckon I know it.

Anyway. That's your lot. Say g'day to your lot for us, and don't let me near an esky again without someone reminding me what happened last time.
