So you weren't there for it, righto, I'll take you through the whole day, start to finish, cos it was a big one.

Reason we were even doing it is Nan's gone into the home permanent now, the hip thing plus she's not safe on her own no more, and the money from the house has to go toward the fees, PBS only covers so much and Centrelink's not exactly generous when it comes to aged care top-ups. So it's getting sold, and everyone agreed, no point letting it sit there empty racking up rates and God knows what. We got the whole clan in for it though, not just us, the aunties and uncles and a couple of the cousins who hadn't set foot in the place in donkey's years. Figured many hands, less heartbreak, that was the theory anyway.

We started in the good room out the front, cos that's the one nobody actually used except Christmas, so it was the least confronting to open up first. My auntie's going through the cabinet and she pulls out this old ashtray, resin, shaped like a boomerang, chipped on one end.

Auntie: oh my God. This was on the coffee table my whole childhood. Every barbie we ever had, this thing was sitting there full of butts.

She went a bit quiet after that, just holding it, and nobody rushed her, we all just kept going round her.

Next thing turns up in the same cabinet, bottom drawer, a little brass key on a bit of ribbon gone all faded. Nobody had a clue.

The eldest: what's this open then.

Uncle: no idea. Doesn't look like the front door key, doesn't look like the shed neither.

Cousin: might be a diary lock, one of them little ones.

We tried it on every lock in the house before the day was out and it fit nothing. Still sitting in someone's pocket now far as I know, fair dinkum mystery, never got sorted.

Then it was the dresser in the hallway that caused the first real barney. Solid old thing, dark wood, been in the family since before any of us were born. My auntie reckoned she should get it cos she's the eldest daughter, the eldest reckoned she should get it cos she's got the space and everyone else is in units. Went back and forth a fair while, bit of a whinge from both sides, until the uncle just said give it to whoever's actually got a house with a hallway wide enough, and that settled it, cos when you put it like that there was only one answer really. She'll be right, everyone shook hands on it more or less and moved on.

Bigger stoush was over the garden gnome. Ugly little bloke, chipped nose, been out the front since forever. Half the room wanted him gone straight in the skip, dodgy old thing, and my cousin was dead against chucking him, reckoned he was basically part of the family at this point. That one never really got settled, to be honest, he just sort of ended up in someone's boot at the end of the day without anyone officially agreeing to anything.

Bathroom and the little box room went quick enough, nothing much in either, bit of dust, some old towels nobody was keen to keep.

Laundry's where things got weird. Behind the water tank, wedged up under the floor join, the uncle found an old biscuit tin wrapped in a plastic bag, and inside it was a wad of cash, old notes, the ones before decimal even, plus a couple of war medals nobody knew Nan's father had. Whole room went dead silent for a second.

The mother: nobody knew about this.

Uncle: not a soul. She never said a word.

Youngest: reckon she forgot it was even there.

Nobody had an answer for that either, fair dinkum, it just went into a bag to sort out properly later, cash and all.

The room that ate the whole afternoon though was the garage. Absolute disaster zone, tools going back decades, half of them rusted solid, tins of paint from before any of us were even born, an old esky with the lid gone brittle, boxes on boxes nobody had touched since God was a boy. Took us longer than every other room combined, honestly, we thought it'd be an hour and it ended up being most of the arvo.

We stopped around one for a feed, everyone starving by then. Someone had brought the good esky, proper cold beers and soft drinks packed in ice, and the uncle got a barbie going out the back on the old plate, snags and onions, bit of bread, real basic but heaps good when you're that hungry. Everyone sat wherever, on the back step, on upturned crates, thongs kicked off, just having a laugh in between mouthfuls about stuff that had turned up so far.

The Youngest: reckon Nan had more hidden round this place we haven't even found.

The mother: wouldn't surprise me one bit.

After lunch it was the piano in the back room nobody wanted to deal with. Massive thing, out of tune for years, nobody plays, and moving it was going to cost more than it was worth to anyone. Went round the whole group asking, keen on it, anyone, dead silence every time. In the end it went on the hard rubbish list for the removalist blokes to deal with, bit sad watching it go out the door sideways but nobody was going to lug that thing home.

One thing everyone agreed on straight off, no argument at all, was the photo albums. Soon as someone found the box of them the eldest just said I'll take these, keep them safe, and every single person in that house went yeah, course, that's exactly right, no dramas whatsoever. Easiest call of the whole day.

Last room to go empty was the main bedroom, Nan's room, and that one felt different to all the rest, heavier somehow. Wardrobe, the bed, her good coat still hanging there. Took a while but eventually it was just bare walls and carpet marks where the furniture used to sit.

Once everything was sorted into piles, we just went round and divvied up what was left, bit of crockery here, some linen there, the youngest ended up with an old record player nobody else was fussed about, my cousin took a box of tools despite the state of them, someone grabbed the last of the good glasses. No real fuss by that stage, everyone was knackered and just wanted it done.

By the time we locked up it was getting on for evening, mozzies starting up outside, and the mother did one last walk through every empty room by herself before we shut the door. Nobody said much on the front step after, just stood there a minute, then someone said righto, that's that then, and we all headed off to our cars. She'll be right, someone said, and that was about the size of it.
