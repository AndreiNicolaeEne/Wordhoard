Me: How's yourself, the whole of ye. Sit in there now and give me a minute, I'll tell it to ye plain, the way it went, and then yiz'll have it same as I have it.

It was only the two of us above in the room, near the end, fierce quiet only for the machine ticking away. I was that knackered by then I could hardly see straight, but I sat with him regardless. The first thing he said wasn't a bit about dying, no. He looked out the window and he said it.

The father: There's a grand stretch in the evenings now, isn't there.

Me: There is, I said. Sure it'll be bright near enough till nine before long.

That eased him into it, like. Then the look changed on him, the sharp look, and out it came.

The father: You'll see to that shed roof before the winter's on top of ye, and don't go making a hames of the guttering while you're at it. I'm not codding you now.

Me: I will, I said. I will so.

He settled again with that. Then he used the word, the one he'd always had for me, since I was no size at all.

The father: Good girl, pet.

Me: I near went, hearing it once more, after all them years of thinking that word was behind us.

Then he asked me a thing I never once thought I'd hear off him, not a man who asked anything of anyone.

The father: Were you happy, in it all, with me?

Me: I was, I told him, and I meant that more than I ever meant a word in my life. Amn't I after telling ye already, it was no small thing to say back to him.

The last whole sentence he got out was, "Mind the others now, won't ye," and I've had it word for word ever since, turned it over that often it's worn smooth as a stone.

After that there was no more talk in it at all. Only the quiet, and himself going smaller in the bed by the minute, and my hand in his the whole time, holding on to him till he drifted off, easy enough in the end, no fuss to it.

That's the whole of it now, plain as I have it. I wanted yiz to have it too.
