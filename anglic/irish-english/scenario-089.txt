How's yourself. I'm only after remembering I never gave you the full story of the skating, so gather round and I'll tell it to you straight, the whole humiliating stretch of it, start to finish, because you missed every bit of it and it'd be a crime not to pass it on.

Right, so. I was thirty-four when I decided, out of nowhere, that this was the year I'd learn to skate. Everyone I know was doing it since they were four or five, birthday parties above in the rink, falling on their little arses at an age when it costs you nothing to fall, and here's me going in the door at thirty-four like I'd something to prove. I couldn't tell you what started it exactly. Maybe it was the nieces flying round like they were born to it and me thinking, sure, if a five-year-old can do that, amn't I well able myself. Turns out that logic doesn't hold up half as well as you'd think.

First lesson. Disaster from the off. Standing there holding the wall like it owed me money, and the instructor, a young one who I'd say wasn't that long out of school herself, looks over and says,

Instructor: Right, let go of the wall there and try to glide.

Me: I will, so.

And I did not glide. I went down like a sack of spuds off the back of a lorry. She was decent about it, fair play, no slagging, just,

Instructor: You'll find it harder at your age, to be honest with you. The body doesn't take to it as quick.

Which, grand, I appreciated the honesty, but it wasn't what I wanted to hear with my knee already roaring. Within about three lessons I had eight-year-olds overtaking me, actual eight-year-olds, going backwards past me like it was nothing at all to them, and I'm not ashamed to say that stung more than I expected. You'd think you'd be well able for a bit of humility at my age, but no, it needled me something fierce.

The whole of that first week I was kilt. Banjaxed altogether. Muscles aching that I didn't know I had, in places you wouldn't think an ache could reach, and I does be sore just thinking back on it now. I couldn't even do the messages the Tuesday after, the legs wouldn't carry me the length of the shop. Himself at home was no help, laughing his head off every time I went to sit down and let a groan out of me.

Himself: Would you listen to yourself, you sound like a woman twice your age.

Me: I am after doing more physical activity in one hour than you've done in a year, so leave off.

But there was one fall that put the heart crossways in me altogether. I'd gotten cocky, or as cocky as you can get after a week of being an eejit on ice, and I went for a bit of speed I'd no business going for, and down I went, hard, flat on my back, and it knocked every bit of breath clean out of me. I lay there on the ice for what felt like an hour but was probably four seconds, staring up at the lights, thinking, that's it now, that's me finished with the whole notion. A few of the class gathered round, some of them concerned, God love them, and one young lad, couldn't have been more than ten, looking down at me going,

The young lad: Are you all right there?

Me: I am.

Which was a lie, but sure what else would you say to a child.

Yer man beside him only laughed and said,

The young lad's friend: Dat's some fall, missus.

Tanks very much, I thought, delighted for you.

I told the instructor after that I was packing it in, that I'd made a right hames of the whole enterprise and there was no shame in a woman my age admitting she'd no business on a rink. She wasn't having it, and neither was a friend of mine who'd talked me into signing up in the first place.

The friend: You're not giving up over one fall. Sure everyone falls. Come back the once more, and if it's still desperate after that, fair enough, we'll say no more about it.

Me: I don't know. I'm after making an awful show of myself.

The friend: You are not. Come back the once more.

So I did, more out of not wanting to give her the satisfaction of being right about me quitting than any great courage on my part. And progress after that was the slowest, most humiliating thing you can imagine. Weeks of getting one small thing right after getting it wrong a hundred times, a whole small crowd of parents and young ones at the barrier watching me try and fail to do a stop that a five-year-old would have down in an afternoon. Some of them were sound about it, giving me a bit of encouragement, and some of the young lads were clearly delighted with themselves, acting the maggot behind me, having the craic at my expense, and the instructor would give out to the bold ones fast enough, but I couldn't even blame them, the state of me.

The only thing that kept me going back was I stopped measuring myself against the young wans flying round backwards and started measuring myself against the eejit I'd been a month before. That fella couldn't stand up without the wall. This fella could, some days, get halfway across the rink without landing on his arse, and there was one evening a couple of the class went on about how far I'd come and yer wan at the desk who does the skate hire said it too, and I'll admit I went home fierce delighted with myself, notions and all.

And then, out of nowhere, around the fourth month, it clicked. I couldn't tell you the day exactly, but I remember the feeling of it more than anything, a sort of looseness coming into the hips and knees that hadn't been there before, like the ice stopped being the enemy and started being just a thing I was standing on. It wasn't in my head at all, it was in the body, some switch flicked over without asking my permission. The instructor was there that evening and said it plain,

Instructor: There you go now, you have it.

Me: Do I?

Instructor: You do.

And I did. Not well, mind you, never savage at it, I never caught up to the fastest of them, and never will, some crowd are just built for it or got the years of childhood practice I never had. But I made my peace with that. By the end I could do things without a thought in my head that I couldn't have imagined doing without going down flat on my back six months before. Stopping, turning, a bit of backwards work even, badly, but doing it.

Looking back on the whole desperate season of it now, and honest to God it was desperate, the falls and the soreness and being laughed at by children barely up to my waist, I only think fondly of it. There's not a sour note left in it at all, it's gone soft and grand in my head the way these things do once the bruises are well healed and put away, like everything else, in the press at the back of your mind. I'm glad I made an eejit of myself for a year. I'd do the whole hames of it again tomorrow if you asked me.

So there you have it, the full saga, the one you missed. How's yourself anyway, and are yous coming down to see me have a go at it yourselves sometime, because I will so, and yiz can have the laugh at me that everyone else already had.
