My friend: How's yourself. Go on then, out with it, I could hear the two of ye above in it Tuesday night through the wall, roaring like something off the telly. What in the name of God was going on?

Me: Ah stop, I'm mortified, I am. Sure it was nothing at all when it started, that's the worst of it.

My friend: Go on so.

Me: Right. I'd asked himself Tuesday morning would he do the messages on the way home, there was shag all in the fridge and I was working till half six. And he says he will, no bother, off he goes to work. And doesn't he land home at seven with nothing only a paper and a bag of rolls. No messages done, no dinner in the house, and I'm above in the kitchen starving after a long day and knackered out of it and all.

My friend: Ah here.

Me: I'll give it to you straight, I roared at him first, there's no getting away from that. I says to him, "I asked you one thing, one thing, to do the messages, and you couldn't even manage that." And fair enough, that's a fair enough thing to be cross about, but it was the way I said it, roaring above in the kitchen with the wooden spoon still in me hand like a right gobshite.

Himself: Would you calm down, I forgot, it's not the end of the world.

Me: And that set me off worse again, because forgot wasn't good enough for me at that point. So I says something I'd no business saying, I says, "You never do a single thing in this house, not a thing, I'm sick doing it all on me own." Which isn't even true, and I knew it wasn't true the second it was out of me mouth, but sure once it's said it's said.

My friend: We've all said that one.

Me: And himself, fair play, he didn't let that one go by him either. He got the height of it then, and says back, "Well if we're saying things, don't be lecturing me about pulling my weight when you're still handing money to your sister every month behind my back, don't think I don't know." Which, that's an old one, that's a thing that's usually left well alone between us, and he only dragged it up to hurt me, and it worked and all.

My friend: Jaysus.

Me: So then I'm raging altogether, and I says I'm entitled to help my own sister with her own business and it's nothing to do with him, and he says something smart back about that being rich coming from me, and it went on like that a good while, the pair of us above in it, roaring back and forth, and at some stage he went and banged the bedroom door so hard the picture of the two of us off the landing wall came down off its hook and the glass in it banjaxed on the floor.

My friend: I heard that bit and all, the bang of it.

Me: I'd say the whole road heard it, honestly. Anyway after that I just stopped. Didn't say another word to him the whole rest of the night, went in and sat with the telly on and not a word out of me, and he stayed in the good room the whole time on his phone, acting the maggot on it for show more than anything. We went to bed the two of us not speaking, and it carried on into the Wednesday and all, him in the good room half the day, me above doing the ironing and saying nothing, only "will I put the kettle on" the odd time out of pure habit, and not much else besides. Guts of a full day that was, the two of us in the one house like strangers, and we were both kilt after it if I'm honest, that sort of thing takes it out of you worse than the row itself.

My friend: Who cracked first.

Me: Himself did, God love him. He came up the stairs about seven that evening with a mug of tea for me, and he says, "I made you that ages ago, it's probably stone cold by now, will you drink it anyway." Just that, doing an oul funny voice going up the stairs with it and all. Nothing about the row at all at first, just the tea.

My friend: Ah that's sweet really.

Me: It was, though I didn't let on straight away. But then he sat down on the end of the bed and he says, "I'm sorry for what I said about your sister, that's between yourself and her, it's none of my business what you do for her and I'd no right dragging it into it. I know dat now, and I'm sorry." And that was the real apology, that was the one that mattered, because he named the actual thing, he didn't just say sorry for the row in general and leave it at that.

My friend: And did you say your piece and all?

Me: I did so. I told him I was sorry for saying he never does anything, because he does, he's no eejit, he does plenty, only I was tired and starving and I took it out on him far worse than it deserved. And I said sorry for roaring first and all, because I know rightly I started that end of it.

My friend: And?

Me: And then he calls me pet. First time in about four days, because we do be very formal with each other when we're vexed, no pet, no love, nothing, just the bare minimum required. So the second he says "ah here, pet, would you not eat something," I knew we were grand again, that was the tell, straight away I knew it.

My friend: What sealed it though.

Me: He went off then below and put the immersion on for me, without me asking him at all, so I could have a proper bath, and himself going on for years about the leccy bill and the immersion left on and here he was putting it on for me off his own bat. That's how I knew it was properly mended, because that man wouldn't put the immersion on for the Pope himself normally, and I told him so and all, and he only laughed.

My friend: Ha! Notions now, are we, with the baths. And are ye grand now, the two of ye?

Me: We are. Great gas about it now if anything. We do be saying it to each other, "mind you don't be doing a hames of the messages again," and having a laugh over it.

My friend: I'll tell you what though, I'll never let it drop that I heard the whole thing through that wall.

Me: Go on so, get your laugh out of it, but sure you're only codding, you'd have said nothing if the two of us above were roaring the odds over something worse. It was a small enough thing in the end, a stupid row over the messages that grew a head of steam on it it never deserved. But sure that's the way with the pair of us, fierce dramatic over nothing and grand again by teatime the next day.

My friend: Amn't I only delighted to hear it. Sure yous were always desperate for the drama, one a culchie and one a jackeen, no wonder ye go at it.

Me: We were so. But there's a grand stretch in the evenings now anyway, and we do be out the two of us walking after our tea most nights, so no harm done in the end of it all. Only don't be telling yer man next door I said that, or he'll be over asking after the immersion and all.
