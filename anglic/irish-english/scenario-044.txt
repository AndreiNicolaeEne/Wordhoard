The foreman had the trowel loaded and the block set before the young fella even had his gloves sorted, showing him the whole of it in the one go: mortar buttered on thin enough to skin the block, no more, set down easy and tapped level with the handle.

The foreman: Right, watch me now. You load her like that, just a skim, and ye set the block down nice and slow and tap her home. Level, plumb, that's it. It does be awkward the first go, but you'll get the feel of it.

The new hand loads the trowel heavy and slaps the mortar on thick, half of it running down the face of the block.

The foreman: Ah, that's too much now. You want a skim off her, not a load. Try it again, easy this time.

The new hand: I will so. Are we doing the corners first or the whole side?

The foreman: Didn't I only show ye that. It's left to right we're going, the whole course, I said that already. Skim the mortar, like I showed ye, that's the whole of it.

The new hand loads the trowel heavy again, and this time the block slides in the wet mortar and goes crooked, cracked clean across.

The foreman: Jesus, would ye look at dat, that's the same thing again. That's your second one banjaxed on me now. Are you even watching what I'm at, or are you acting the maggot?

The new hand: I am doing it the way you said, I swear, me hand's just not doing what your hand's doing.

The foreman: Don't be getting notions with me and don't be codding me either. Right. Forget the trowel a second. Come here to me and watch the hand only, not the block. See the turn of it, like closing a door easy, never slamming it.

The new hand: Like that?

The foreman: That's it. Look at yer man now. Amn't I after telling ye that's all it was.

The new hand: I have it, have I?

The foreman: You have. Sure that's grand altogether. Don't go making a hames of the next one now and we'll get on fierce well, the two of us.
