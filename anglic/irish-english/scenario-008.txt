The child: Ma, will you not just get me the phone, I'm after asking you about it a hundred times now.

The mother: You are, and I'm after telling you a hundred times, amn't I, I haven't the money for it. Between the messages and the leccy and this immersion ating money for the last two weeks, there's nothing spare going on a phone for you. That's the plain truth of it, love, no dodging round it.

The child: I'll do the bins every week for a year if you get it for me.

The mother: You'll do the bins anyway, that's mine to ask, not yours to trade with.

The child: What about for my birthday then, would you get it then.

The mother: We'll see nearer the time. If your da's hours pick up after the summer, I'd say we'd look at it again then, properly look, not just say it to shut you up. I'm not promising it now though.

The child: Will you think about it at least.

The mother: I will so. But thinking about it isn't saying yes, do you hear me.

The child: I do. ...Yer one up the road has one already and she does be only eight.

The mother: I don't care what yer one has, sure I'm not her mother, and what her crowd can or can't afford is neither here nor there to what goes on in this house. Don't be giving out to me over what's in someone else's house.

The child: It's not fair.

The mother: Life's not fair, pet, you'll learn that soon enough and it'll be no harm to you. Here, I'll tell you what I'll do instead. I'll put a few euro by every week towards it, and whatever you get off your granny goes in on top, and we'll see where we are come the autumn. That or nothing.

The child: ...Grand, I suppose.

The mother: Good girl. Now go on and do your homework before I lose the patience with you altogether.
