Me: How's yourself.

The neighbour: Grand, grand, come in out of that, you're perished. What's wrong with you, you've a face on you.

Me: I'm after coming from below, the roof's at me again. It's over the back bedroom it's worst, the corner above the wardrobe, and it does be dripping down the wall something shocking when it rains hard. The plaster's gone soft as a sponge behind the paper.

The neighbour: Months this is going on now, is it?

Me: Months, aye, since before the summer. Amn't I after asking yer man twice already and not a thing done.

The neighbour: What did he say the first time you raised it with him?

Me: He said he'd send a fella out to have a look, no bother at all, he'd have it sorted inside a fortnight. That fortnight's long gone. I rang him again a few weeks after and it was the very same story, he'd get onto it, he's fierce busy, sure.

The neighbour: And how long ago was that second time?

Me: A month now, near enough. Nothing since.

The neighbour: Right. You've a few roads you could go. Put it to him in writing, dated, that's your proof after if it comes to a tribunal, but you'd be sitting under a bucket the while it takes. Or get the council man out to inspect it, only then yer man might take the notions that you're the one causing him bother and try it on to put you out. Or you fix it yourself and take it off the rent, but he could turn round and say you never asked him proper, and make a right hames of that against you.

Me: God, I never thought he'd try that.

The neighbour: I had a landlord below in the old flat did the very same, promised me and my sister the gutter twice and was only codding us the whole time, no intention in the world. I wrote him the letter in the end, kept a copy for meself, and didn't he have a fella out within the week.

Me: So you'd write it down, so.

The neighbour: I would. Get the date and the corner and the whole of it down plain, send it registered, and if there's nothing after a fortnight we'll go to the council together.

Me: I will so.
