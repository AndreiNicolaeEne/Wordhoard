Me: Howya. I know well you couldn't get over for it, God love you, stuck where you are, so I'm going to give you the whole of it now start to finish, and you'll have it as good as if you'd stood there yourself, so you will.

The word went round fierce fast, faster than you'd credit a bit of news like that travelling. I'd say be the Tuesday evening near everyone we knew was after hearing it, and the phone in the hall was hopping off the hook for the two days after, never a minute's peace got in that house. Amn't I after saying to the mother, would you ever let it ring out the once, and she wouldn't, of course, sure how could she, it might be someone with news of a flight or a Mass card or God knows what.

Two mad days we had of it after that, scrambling, getting the house right and the removal sorted and the priest squared away, feck all sleep got by anyone belonging to us. My aunt over in Boston was on a flight within the hour of hearing, a full day of travelling on her, planes and cars and more planes again, and didn't she land in the door with barely the time to change her clothes before the house filled right up on top of her. Fierce woman altogether, that one. How's yourself, she said to me on the step, like it was any old Tuesday and not what it was, and I nearly went again meself hearing it.

By first light Thursday the house was already thick with people, and the food coming in the door on top of it, ham and brack and casseroles off women we hadn't clapped eyes on in years, a savage spread altogether, more than anyone had asked for or could eat in a month, plates on every surface there was in the place. You'd think we had notions above ourselves with the half of it. Someone had to run and do the messages twice over just for milk and bread to keep the tea going, the cups that went out of that kitchen would frighten you. And someone roaring from the hall to mind the immersion, with that many staying over there'd be no hot water left going for anyone by the Friday.

Everyone that came was in the dark clothes, the plain stuff, nothing needing saying about that, you know yourself how it goes.

Before we walked out we all gathered into the one room, the whole crowd of us packed in tight, and I'll tell you it went fierce quiet, quieter than you'd ever believe a room that full could go. Not a cough out of anyone, not a chair creaking. A few of us carried him out together, meself and the eldest and two of the cousins, and Lord it's a heavier weight a person is than you'd think, or maybe it's only the weight of the whole thing that's in it by then.

Partway through, the youngest broke down all of a sudden, no warning on it at all, just went, and nobody moved to stop her or hush her, we only let it happen, sure what else would you do with a thing like that. It passed off her after a minute or two and she was grand, she said after, mortified, and nobody minded a bit.

The priest said his piece, and then the neighbour from two doors up got to her feet and said a few words after him, and there's a line out of it that's gone round everyone since, repeated to me now three or four times by people that barely knew him at all.

The neighbour: He never left a room worse than he found it.

Deadly line, that. I've had it said back to me at the shop and below at the church gate and once by yer man that does the messages for the old ones on the road, and every time it near catches me again.

And then, in the middle of all that seriousness, somebody brought up the time himself made a complete hames of painting the front gate, put the paint on with it half open and painted the thing shut so it wouldn't budge for a week, and the laugh that went round the room took everyone by surprise, including the one that started it, she near choked trying to swallow it back down. It was only the two seconds of it, and it felt mad, laughing like that in a room like that, but sure it was exactly the sort of eejit carry-on he'd be at, and I'd say he'd have been raging altogether if not one of us had laughed.

The walk out to the resting place felt fierce long, longer than I remember it being the other times we've walked that road, and I have walked it plenty. There was a grand stretch in the evening for it if you can believe I was noticing a thing like that at a time like that, the light still soft and holding over the road the whole way.

The words said there were short and plain, nothing dressed up about them, and a handful of the clay was let go into it, and that was that, done, no more to it.

The walk back was shorter, it always is, isn't it, whatever way that works. And the meal after was put together by whoever was standing closest to the mother when the job needed doing, no plan to it whatsoever, just people falling into it, and there was enough of it after to feed the whole crowd twice over and then some, ham sandwiches going out the door for days after.

Yer man from two doors down that nobody thought would show his face at all, given the falling out years back over the wall between the two gardens, he showed up anyway, stood at the back of it, said nothing much only, "I'm sorry for your trouble," plain as that, and it meant more than anyone let on, I'll tell you that much. Herself just squeezed his hand and that was the whole of it between the two of them, no more needed saying, none at all.

By the evening the house had emptied right out, the last stragglers dribbling off with their tins and their dishes to be collected another day, and the quiet came down on the place all at once, heavier than any of the noise had been the two days before it. Herself sat there below in the good chair, kilt with the whole of it, banjaxed entirely, and I sat with her a while and neither of us said a thing, there wasn't a thing needed saying by then.

I wish you'd been there for the laugh more than anything else in the whole of it, if I'm honest with you, the two seconds of it in the middle of all that seriousness, because that was him to the life, that was, and you'd have known in an instant why the lot of us were creased over a gate that got painted shut fifteen years ago and never fully opened right since. That's the bit I wanted you to have most of all, out of everything I've told you.
