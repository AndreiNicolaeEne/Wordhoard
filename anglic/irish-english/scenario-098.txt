We weren't from here, not really, not four generations back. The people before us were off a small bit of land down west, a few acres of nothing much, more stone than soil, and there were too many of them for it. That's the whole of what needs saying about the old place, there's no call going on about it beyond that: a small farm, a big family, and not enough ground under it to keep the lot of them fed past March. When it came down to it there was one going to have to go, and it was the great-grand uncle that went, him being the youngest with the legs able for it.

Grandmother: He walked it, love, don't be thinking it was any other way. Walked it and slept where he could get shelter and arrived into this place with the shoes near worn off him and not a soul in it that knew his name. He hadn't a bob to his name when he got here and he hadn't a bed of his own either. There was a family already settled here that had a bit of a connection to us, third cousins or the like, and they took him in above in the corner of a room that wasn't rightly theirs to be giving away, if it comes to that, but sure that's how it was done then, you didn't own a roof, you borrowed the corner of somebody else's and you were glad of it. There was no steady start to it. Me: Did he ever think of going back?

Grandmother: He did not. Sure what was there below to go back to, only stones.

Grandmother: Two years it was, and a bit over. He'd have you believe it was no time at all when he told it himself, but sure the mother always said it was the longest two years she ever put down, waiting on a letter and not knowing was he dead or living half the time.

Mother: She does be telling it different every time, mind. It's a bit longer in the telling every winter.

They arrived, the rest of them, with next to nothing, same as he had. Yer man was there below at the docks to meet them, and how's yourself, he says to the mother, like they'd only seen each other the week before and not two years and a bit gone, and that was the whole of the greeting, and it was enough.

Mother: You don't touch that box. I don't care is it only an old tin, you leave it where it is.

Grandmother: It was. But sure it was theirs by then, and that's a different kind of hard to carry.
