Right, so. Yous wanted the whole story of the week I was minding the house, and I'll give it to ye start to finish, because by God it was a week and a half in seven days.

It started grand. Herself dropped the keys round the Sunday evening, went through the whole list, the bins on a Tuesday, the cat's not to go out after dark, there's a spare fob for the gate in the press under the stairs, don't mind the immersion, it does be a bit temperamental. I said sure it'll be grand, I'm only up the road, what's the worst that could happen. I was only codding meself, as it turned out.

First night and I couldn't settle at all. Something wasn't right about the place, though I couldn't have told you what. The heating was clicking on and off be itself, and there was a smell off the kitchen I didn't like, and I lay there thinking, I'm after locking every door twice and still I'm listening for something. Slept about four hours in the end and woke feeling like I'd been dragged through a hedge.

Second morning, disaster number one proper. I went out the back to put the bin out and didn't the dog shoot past me legs and off up the road like he was after winning the Lotto. I'm standing there in me pyjama bottoms roaring his name down a housing estate at half eight in the morning, feeling like an awful eejit. A neighbour two doors down came out in her dressing gown.

The neighbour: Is that your dog, love?

Me: He's not mine, I'm only minding him. Did you see where he went?

The neighbour: He went round be the green there, chasing something I'd say.

Took the guts of an hour, up and down, calling him, shaking the biscuit box like a gobshite outside every gate on the road, before I spotted him sitting as good as gold outside the chipper like he owned the place. Got the lead on him and he trotted home beside me like butter wouldn't melt. Herself would've had a canary if she'd seen that.

The garden was the next thing crept up on me, slow enough that I near didn't notice till it was too late. There was tomato plants out the back, lovely they were the first day, and I was meant to be watering them of an evening. I did it the first two days and then forgot, and forgot again, and by the Thursday half of them were gone yellow and drooping like they'd given up on life altogether. I gave them a good soaking then, desperate late, and some of them came back a small bit, but there was two of them beyond saving. I said nothing, only pulled the worst of them out and hoped for the best.

The washing machine went on the Wednesday. I'd a load in, nothing mad, just towels, and I heard this awful thumping noise like someone was after putting bricks in with the wash. Went out and there's water creeping across the utility floor and the machine banjaxed altogether, stuck mid cycle, door locked shut with me good towels inside it. I did give out to it, I can tell you, standing there in me socks in a puddle. Ended up mopping the floor with half the good tea towels because I couldn't find where they kept the mop.

The Thursday a fella showed up at the door with no word of warning, big build on him, said he was the brother.

The brother: Alright. Is she about?

Me: She's away for the week, I'm only minding the place.

The brother: Ah right, nobody told me that. Sure I'll come in and wait a while so, if that's alright with you.

Sat in the kitchen for an hour drinking tea and telling me stories about himself and herself when they were young, and I'm there thinking, I don't know this man from Adam and he's after making himself at home in someone else's kitchen. Turned out to be sound as a pound in the end, only I nearly had a heart attack when the doorbell went.

Then the Friday came the real jolt. She'd left her good ring on the windowsill above the sink, told me to mind it, and I'd taken it off meself doing the washing up one evening and set it down somewhere, and Friday I went looking for it and it was gone. I searched that kitchen like a mad thing, under the mats, down the back of the press, in me own pockets three times over. I was sure I'd lost it for good, sure of it, and I could feel the sweat coming up on me thinking how I'd tell her. Found it in the end wedged down in the plug hole of the sink, God knows how, fished it out with a fork shaking like a leaf. I'm after having the fright of me life over that ring, I said to no one, and put it straight in the press, out of harm's way.

The Saturday I went out to do the messages, quick enough, only a few bits, and didn't I pull the door shut behind me out of habit and the keys sitting on the hall table looking back at me through the glass. Stood there like an eejit for a minute before it hit me proper. Rang round, no answer from the neighbour, the spare fob was inside as well, of course it was. Ended up sitting on the wall for the guts of three hours waiting on a locksmith, freezing, watching the world go by, feeling like the whole road was looking at me and thinking, would ye look at the state of yer man, locked out of a house that's not even his own.

The Sunday it lashed rain all afternoon, proper stair rods, and I only realised at teatime that I'd left the box room window open a crack for the air the night before. The carpet in there was destroyed, soaked through, and there was a damp patch spreading up the wall that I didn't like the look of at all. I got every towel I could find down on the floor and a fan heater going and prayed it'd be dry before Saturday.

The night before that, actually, I'd a fright of a different sort. Woke up around three to a banging out the back, sure someone was after breaking into the shed. Went down in me bare feet with the poker from the fire, heart going ninety, and it was only the gate come loose off its latch, banging in the wind. Stood out there in the cold in me pyjamas feeling like the biggest eejit going, latching it shut with a bit of twine.

That gate, and the immersion besides, had been the thing plaguing me the whole week, on and off, tripping and banging and giving me no peace. The Friday before last I finally sat down and sorted the both of them proper, found the fuse box and worked out which switch was which, and fixed the latch with a proper bolt I got in the hardware shop. First bit of peace I had all week.

The last day was pure panic, tidying like a mad thing, hoovering, fixing cushions, scrubbing the sink where the ring had been, throwing out the two dead tomato plants before anyone could ask questions, praying the carpet in the box room had dried out enough that nobody would notice. I was knackered by the time they pulled up.

Herself came in, looked round, delighted with herself.

Herself: Well, how did he behave for you?

Me: Grand, no bother at all. He got a small bit of a run on Tuesday but sure he was back within the hour.

Herself: And how was everything else, house alright?

Me: Ah, grand. The immersion was acting the maggot for a while but I got it sorted. Quiet enough week altogether.

I didn't mention the ring, or the washing machine, or the state I nearly had meself in over that gate at three in the morning. Some things are better left where they are. She said she'd have me back to mind it again sometime, and I said I would so, God help me, though I don't know would I be able for it twice.
