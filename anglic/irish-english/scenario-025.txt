Me: I'm banjaxed altogether. Are we near the roundabout yet?

Himself: We are.

Me: Your mother, God love her. "Isn't it gas the way you do the potatoes, love, sure I always do mine a different way." That's not gas, that's your mother telling me my potatoes are only shocking.

Himself: She didn't mean anything by it.

Me: She meant plenty by it. Amn't I only saying it like it is. And don't be defending her, you weren't even listening, you does be off in your own head half the visit.

Himself: I was listening.

Me: Were you listening to your father telling the one about the tractor and the river again? Third time this year I've heard that story, and it's longer every time he tells it.

Himself: He does love that story alright, in fairness. And yer man from up the road gets a bigger part in it every time and all.

Me: I'm after hearing it that many times I could tell it meself, in his voice and all. And your mother still going on about the immersion being left on above in the good room, like it's the crime of the century.

Himself: Ah he's an aul lad, let him have his story.

Me: And then the door. Forty-five minutes at the door, Himself. "Will yous not stay for one more cup," "sure the road'll still be there in an hour," and putting the leftover spuds in the press for us before you'd your coat half on.

Himself: ...

Me: Are you laughing?

Himself: I am.

Me: So you admit it.

Himself: I do so. She does go on something desperate at that door, God help us. And pushing the tea on us after we'd said we'd go three times, that's not manners, that's notions.

Me: Now you're at it.

Himself: I am. And the gravy comment, that was a hames of a compliment if ever I heard one.

Me: There you go.

Himself: Sure it's only the two of us in the car, we're grand to say it.

Me: We are so.

Himself: Fierce woman, my mother, God bless her, but fierce.

Me: She's a savage for it, no doubt about that.
