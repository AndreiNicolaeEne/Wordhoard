Me: Is it the fuse or is it the whole road?
Himself: How would I know, amn't I stood here in the dark same as you.

The eldest went rooting in the press under the stairs for the torch, and there wasn't a battery in it that worked, not one. The mother does be great in a crisis, fair play to her, she had the drawer turned out onto the table looking for the matches while giving out to the young fella for eating the last of the birthday candles off a cake box for the craic months back.

There was one stub of a candle going spare and that was it, the whole lot of us huddled round the one flame at the kitchen table like something out of the famine. Yer man the youngest kept knocking into the same press door in the dark, twice, and the second time he let a roar that he'd kilt his toe entirely.

The mother: Would you ever look where you're going.
The youngest: I am looking, sure I can't see a bloody thing.

Nobody was bothering fixing the fuse box that hour of the night, not with the torch dead and the rain lashing outside, so we just left it. We sat in around the one bit of light playing cards on the table, having the craic, blaming the ESB, blaming the immersion, blaming everything only ourselves. It never came back on. We went up to bed in the pitch dark in the end, feeling our way up the stairs, and it was only the morning brought the light back, grand as you like, like nothing had happened at all.
