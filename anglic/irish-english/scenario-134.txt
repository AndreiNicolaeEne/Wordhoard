Honest to God I'm after telling this story about four times already this week and I'll tell it again because it's mad altogether, so it is. The harvest below on the home place this year was the best anyone going has ever clapped eyes on. It was clear early on this one was going to leave the rest standing, in at nearly double what you'd get most years, and I'm not codding you for the craic, that's the truth of it.

It's the barley that walked away with it altogether. It came in miles past what it usually gives, and miles past the best they'd ever had before that, and that was years back, before himself even took over the running of it off his own father. Herself said she does be out in that field every year of her life and she never once saw it looking the way it looked this year.

They'd to bring in extra hands just to get it in before it turned on them, half the parish above in the field at one stage, some of them young lads acting the maggot and getting given out to for it, but sure they got there. Once the usual need was seen to, what was over, some of it went into the shed for the winter, and the rest got sold on to the neighbours that came up short.

The eldest that does be out there every season, he set this one against every harvest he's seen back to when he was only a young fella, and he said, straight out, this was the best of the whole lot. Are you sure of that now? I am, he said, and what got the credit was the rain landing exactly when it was wanted, and a grand mild stretch right through the summer that never once let up.

It's still the one they measure everything else against below. Any middling year since, someone will say sure that was no harvest at all next to that year, and everyone knows well the one they mean.
