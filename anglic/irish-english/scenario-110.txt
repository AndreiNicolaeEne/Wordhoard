The case is barely in the hall when the whole kitchen turns round on me like I'm after walking into an interview.

The mother: Sit down there and put that bag in the press, you'll trip over it. Himself's within getting himself sorted, he'll be out now. Go on so, tell us, what's it actually like over there.

Me: Ah it's grand, Mam. Big. Bigger than you'd think even after I'm after telling you about it a hundred times on the phone.

Auntie: Bigger than here though, sure everywhere's bigger than here.

Me: No I mean proper big. You could grow up your whole life in it and never see the half of it. Here you'd walk from one end of town to the other in twenty minutes moaning the whole way about the twenty minutes. Over there it does be the better part of an hour some days just crossing from one side to the other, and that's not even leaving the one part of it.

Da: (arriving in, sitting down heavy) An hour, is it. Tanks be to God I never have to do dat. How's yourself anyway.

Me: How's yourself. Grand, only wrecked after the flight.

Da: Kilt with the travelling I'd say.

Me: I am, a bit.

The eldest: And the noise of it, is it worse than here at all?

Me: It's a different class of noise altogether. Here you'd hear the guards' siren of a Saturday night going down the road and that's your excitement for the week. Over there it's sirens the whole night, and the trains below in the ground shaking the cups on the shelf, and lads roaring at each other out the window like it's the most natural thing going. It's shook I was the first few weeks, walking round like an eejit with the phone stuck to me face the whole time trying to work out was I even going the right way.

Auntie: God love you.

The mother: And where are you actually living, tell us properly this time, not just "an apartment", where.

Me: It's small, Mam, smaller than you're picturing I'd say. You're picturing yer wan's gaff below with the extension on it, and it's nothing like that at all, it's more the size of the box room here only the whole kitchen and the bed's in the one room with it. The jacks is the size of a wardrobe.

Da: And what's dat costing you.

Me: Triple what you'd pay here for something twice the size, near enough. I told a fella over there what the rent was going for round here and he looked at me like I'd said it was free.

Da: Fierce dear it is, dat place, for a box.

The eldest: Are the people friendly at all over there, or are they all notions and no time for you?

Me: That's not really a yes or no one, if I'm honest. They'd help you quicker than you'd get help here half the time, someone'd have you sorted with directions and gone again before you'd even said thanks properly. But going in and out of the shop every day, nobody's asking after your mother. It's friendly in the emergency and not that friendly in the ordinary, if that makes any sense to yiz.

The eldest: Sure that's mad. Worse than here for the aul chat so, and better than here if you're in bits on the ground.

Me: That's it exactly.

Auntie: And where do yiz do the messages, is there a market at all or is it all these big shops?

Me: There's a market, a good one, a few streets of stalls, fruit and veg and lads roaring the price at you same as anywhere, deadly craic of a Saturday morning. But for the actual messages, the milk and the bread and that, it's the corner shop, open the whole night. Yer man that runs mine below knows me order at this stage. You'd pay through the nose for a pint of milk at three in the morning but sure you'd want it by then.

Da: And the work. How does a fella get himself sorted for work over there at all?

Me: Different game altogether, Da. It's not walking in with the CV under your oxter anymore, it's who you know, and emails, and some crowd ringing you up out of the blue because a fella you had one coffee with six months back passed your name on. I made an awful hames of the whole thing the first few months, hadn't a clue how any of it worked, doing everything arseways. There was one gobshite of a manager giving out over nothing the whole time in me first job, and I nearly packed it in.

Auntie: Would you go back and do it different if you were starting again?

Me: I would, so.

The eldest: Were you lost a lot, finding your feet like?

Me: Lost is right. Actually lost, three or four times the first month, proper turned around, not knowing was I going north or south of meself. I'm after learning the whole layout of the place now the hard way, one wrong train at a time. There's bits of it I could nearly walk with me eyes shut at this stage.

The mother: And the food, how's the food compared to here?

Me: Better in some ways and desperate in others, honestly. You'd get any country's dinner you fancy any hour of the day or night, it's savage for that. But you go looking for a decent bit of brown bread or a proper rasher and you'd be walking an hour to find it, and even then it's not right, it's not the same at all.

Da: Are you managing alright for yourself over there though, are you eating properly.

Me: I am, Da, don't be worrying.

The mother: (calling through) Don't be running the immersion the whole time you're above getting sorted, I only put it on the half hour ago.

Me: I won't, Mam.

Auntie: So is it better than here so, all told, when you put the whole of it together?

Me: Ah now, that's not a fair one to be asking a fella.

Da: Go on, is it better or worse.

Me: It's different, Da. Savage in some ways and fierce hard in others, and I wouldn't put a better or a worse on the two of them, I'd say different, and leave it there.

The eldest: You're only codding us now, you must think one's the better.

Me: I amn't codding at all, I'm giving yiz the honest answer. It's different, the two of them, that's all I'll say on it.

Da: Are you acting the maggot with me, is that a real answer at all.

Me: It is, Da. It is so.

There's a knock and the neighbour puts the head round the door without waiting to be let in properly.

The neighbour: How's yourself! Sure I heard you were home and I couldn't not call in and see were you grown two heads over there yet.

Me: How's yourself. I'm grand, no heads gained, don't worry.

The neighbour: Tanks very much, I'll only stop the minute, don't mind me. Don't be getting notions off them over there now, coming home thinking you're above the rest of us.

Me: Not a chance of that.

The neighbour: Sure we're only culchies to you now anyway, above beyond in the big place.

Me: Yiz are not, don't start.

The mother: Will you come home though, in the end. For good, I mean.

Me: (long pause) I will, so. Someday. I couldn't tell you is it next year or is it ten years, but I will.

Da: Sure dat's grand so.

The mother: Grand stretch in the evening anyway while you're sitting there, we'll say no more about it tonight. Put the kettle on there like a good one and give us a bit of peace from the whole inquisition.

The eldest: Ye'd want your head examined moving somewhere that dear anyway, and that's the last I'll say on it too.

Da: Feck it, sure I don't know how yiz manage at all over there, and dat's the truth of it.

Me: You'd manage grand yourself, Da, you'd have the whole place sorted in a week.

Da: I would in me eye. I'd be banjaxed inside a day.
