I was above in the back garden hanging out the wash when I seen it first, only a smudge of grey away off over the mountains, and I says to the neighbour, look at that now, and she goes, sure it's only gorse, they do be burning it this time of year. And we stood there the two of us watching it like it was nothing to do with us at all, cup of tea in me hand, grand and easy, the early afternoon sun still splitting the stones.

Within the hour though it wasn't grand at all. The wind turned and pushed the whole lot of it down towards us and the smoke started stinging me eyes something desperate, and I could taste it at the back of me throat before I could even see the flames properly.

Himself came out and says, we're going, and I says, going where, and he says, anywhere but here, get the child and don't be minding the rest of it.

I was out of that house in twenty minutes, I swear to God, only the child and the dog and me bag, the press left open and everything in it forgotten. Me throat was raw as sandpaper the whole way down the road and the heat of it pressing on the back of me neck like an open oven door. I looked back once at the light of it going up over the roofs, fierce orange against the sky, and I said a prayer and kept walking.

Two nights we were above at the sister's, not knowing was there a house to go back to at all, glued to the phone for any word. Are they saying anything, she'd ask me, and I'd say, they are not, not a thing.

But when we went back the place was grand, spared entirely, not a mark on it. I nearly cried at the gate.
