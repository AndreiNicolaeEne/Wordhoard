The aunt: How's yourself, love. I was above in the cousin's below in town and she was going on about yer man that does be working above in Finglas, the ESB fella, and honest to God she couldn't get over him.

The niece: Is that right.

The aunt: It is, it is. Three things I'll give you now and you can do what you like with them after. He's a fierce hard worker, first of all, never out of a job since he left the school, and he's saving for a place of his own already. He's the gentlest fella you ever met, wouldn't say boo to a goose, none of your acting the maggot in the pub of a Friday night. And the family he comes from, sure they're lovely, good culchie people below in Kildare, none of your jackeen notions, herself reared them all lovely and himself never missed a day's work in his life. What more would you want, tell me.

The niece: I don't know, I amn't looking for anything at the minute, I'm banjaxed with the job as it is.

The aunt: Are you not the least bit interested? Tell me straight, is there something wrong with him at all?

The niece: There's nothin wrong wit him, sure I never even met him properly. I'm only after starting the new position and I haven't the head for it, that's all that's in it.

The aunt: You haven't the head for it. Well let me tell you, your own cousin said the very same to my face, I amn't ready, I'm too busy, and look at her now, three lovely children and a house of her own, and doesn't she say to me only last week she'd have been an eejit to have let him go.

The niece: I'm delighted for her, I am, but that's her business and this is mine.

The aunt: Fair enough, fair enough, I'm only saying it for your own good, I amn't trying to be giving out to you over it, ye're old enough to know your own mind.

The niece: I do know it, and I'm grateful to ye for thinking of me.

The aunt: Well the offer's there whenever ye want it, he'll still be a grand catch in six months or six years, and yous can tell me any time ye change your mind. Sure I'll say no more about it now.
