I owe you this letter the guts of a year now and I'm only getting to it, so I am. Don't be asking me where the time went, I couldn't tell you, only that it went, and here I am of a Sunday evening with the tea gone cold beside me and the whole of it to catch you up on, so bear with me, it'll be a long one.

Start with the house so, since you'll want to know. The gutter is done at last, after two summers of me saying it and himself saying it and neither of us lifting a finger, till a fella came out in March and had it sorted in the one afternoon, and weren't we only mortified we hadn't rung him years back. Course now it's the immersion gone on us, the heating element or whatever's in it, herself does be giving out about the cold showers every morning and himself says he'll look at it at the weekend, which you know rightly means some weekend that hasn't arrived yet and mightn't ever. We put in a raised bed out the back where the old shed used to stand, rhubarb and spuds and whatever cuttings herself got off the neighbour, and it's doing grand, better than any of us amateurs had a right to expect.

The big news, and you'll have heard some version of it already off the mother in her own sideways way of telling things, is that the sister is after getting made permanent below in the Cork office, after the two years of them stringing her along on the contracts one after another. She's moved down properly now, got herself a place out past the college with a girl from the work, and it happened quick enough in the end, one Friday she got the call and by the following month the van was loaded and gone. Himself wasn't a bit happy about it, though he'd never say it straight out, only you'd know it by the quiet that sat over the dinner table the week she told him.

The mother: Sure isn't Cork only up the road.
Me: It is, in the way anywhere's only up the road when you don't want to say you'll miss someone fierce.

She laughed at that and gave out to me for being smart, and that was the end of it, the way these things always end here, a joke thrown over the top of it and everyone moving on like it wasn't said at all.

The good delph is still above in the press, still saving itself for nobody, in case you were worrying about that.

Neighbours now. Below at the corner, the ones with the dog that never once in its life stopped, they're gone, sold up in the spring, and a young couple's after moving in with a small one already on the way by the look of her when I passed her at the bins. Across from us, the pair that had their first the same year as yourself, they're after having a second, roaring the place down by all accounts, though sure amn't I only going on what herself reports back over the wall of an evening, she has the whole road's business before the postman does.

Two of them ask after you without fail. The aunt below, first thing out of her mouth every time I see her is how's the wanderer keeping, is the job good to you, and are you eating right, as if I'd have any way of knowing that last one. And yer man you used to be above in the warehouse with, he stopped me outside the shop only the other week, wanted to know were you settled, was the place half decent, the usual, and stood there a good ten minutes at it, delaying me from the messages I was meant to be doing.

I'll tell you something now I wouldn't say to your face, only it comes easier in the writing: the week after you went I went up and sat a while on the end of your bed, one evening, didn't touch a thing, didn't rightly know what I was at, only that the room felt wrong being that tidy and that quiet with nobody making a hames of it. I haven't told a soul that, so keep it between us, so.

The winter that's just gone was desperate, wet the whole way through it, the road below flooded twice and we'd to go round by the school both times, cursing the whole county. This last stretch of spring made up for it fierce though, the garden never looked better and there were a good few evenings you'd call a grand stretch in the evening, out in the light till near ten with the neighbours all out doing the same as us, standing around doing nothing in particular and delighted with themselves for it.

Himself is the same as ever, still calling anyone under thirty an eejit for one reason or another, still acting the maggot himself half the time he's giving out about it in others. Herself does be at the radio every morning same as always, and I will say the house does be quieter without you roaring back at whatever eejit was on it, we've nobody to do the shouting now only me, and I haven't the same knack for it.

Tell us now, is the job still good to you, are you well set with the money after the rent and the whatnot's taken out of it? Is the weather half as bad as you make out in your texts or are you only codding us for the sympathy? And are yous making friends over there at all, going out, seeing a bit of the place, or is it still yourself and the four walls of an evening? Don't be giving me one line answers either, I want the whole of it back, same as I've given you here.

I know I say it every single letter and never once follow through, but I'll get that parcel out to you before the month is out, the tea and the good biscuits and whatever else herself throws in when she hears I'm sending, I will so, I swear it this time. Write back when you get the chance, and mind yourself over there, and don't be leaving it a year before I hear from you either, the pair of us are as bad as each other for it.

Your brother
