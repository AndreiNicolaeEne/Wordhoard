The licht gaed oot jist efter dark, nae warnin, jist a clatter fae somewye in the waa an syne aathing black. We thocht it wis the fuse box, or mebbe the hale street, for there wisna a licht shinin onywhere doon the road aither.

Faither: It's no jist us, look, the hale street's oot.

Mother: Weel that's nae odds tae us the nicht, we're still sittin in the dark.

He wis through at the fuse box in a meenit, flickin the switches up an doon, but naethin wid tak.

We gaed scramblin for onything tae gie a bit licht. The mother wis sure there wis a torch in the drawer, ye ken the ane, but the battery in it wis deid as a steen. The eldest fund a wee stump o a candle ahin the press, nae mair nor twa inch o it, an that wis aa there wis in the hale hoose forbye. Nae mair candles, nae spare matches tae licht anither even gin there hid been ane.

So the ae candle hid tae dae for the hale lot o us, passed fae haund tae haund whaur it wis needit maist. The wee one chapped her shin aff the same stool twice runnin, cursin awa in the dark.

The eldest: That's you again! Are ye no learnt yet?

The wee one: I canna see the thing, can I.

We aa hid a lauch at that, even her, though she wis rubbin at her shin near greetin.

Naebody wis for fixin the fuse that nicht, ower dark an ower fashed tae bother wi it. We jist sat in aboot the table, the ae flame gaun atween us, spikkin awa aboot naethin much, the claik o the day, wha said fit tae wha. It wis affa cosy in a wye, aabody close for aince, nae tellyvision, nae naethin tae dae but spik.

The licht never cam back that nicht. We gaed up tae wir beds in the pitch mirk, feelin wir wye up the stairs, an left the fixin o it for the morn.
