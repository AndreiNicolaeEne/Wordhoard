Fit like. Sit yersel doon a meenit, I'm fair forfochen, but I hiv tae tell ye foo the day gaed, cause I've nivver seen the like o' it on this street afore.

Ye ken the auld tree at the tap, the muckle een that's been leanin ower the waa since afore ony o' us bade here? It wis aboot hairst time, nichts drawin in, and it cam doon in the nicht wi aa that wind, richt across the road and half intae the back greens forbye, takkin the fence wi it, a fair puckle o' the steen dyke tae, and flattenin the hale row o' neeps in the corner plot forbye. Naebody kent fit wye it gaed doon that nicht wi nae warnin. There wis nae caur gettin oot, the bins lorry widna get near, and there wis nae wye een hoose wis gonnae shift that itsel. Affa muckle job, that.

It wis the mannie fae ower the road that first cried oot, staunin in the street in his sleeves at seven in the mornin, roarin up and doon, "Is there onybody gaun tae help me wi this or are we aa jist gonnae staun aboot?" And ye ken foo it is on this street, een door opens and the word's awa afore ye've hid yer tea. A chap on a windae here, a text there, somebody's dog barkin at the stramash, and afore lang the hale street kent.

We thocht there'd be sax or seiven o's, that's aa. There wis nearer thirty, weel ower a dizzen mair than onybody hid coonted on. Fowk we'd nivver seen liftin so much as a spade cam oot wi wellies on and gloves that hidna been used since the last time. Aabody got a job. Since it wis a Saturday and nae skweel, the loons and quines were sent tae gaither sticks and wee brainches intae a pile for the fire, and they were richt prood o' theirsels, rinnin back and fore wi armfus, ane o' the loonies fair shoutin he'd fund the biggest stick on the street, and a wee quinie no faur ahin him wi her ain pile near as muckle.

The heaviest o' it fell tae the big chiel that dis the scaffoldin, he's affa strong, and he wis on that saw fae the meenit it stertit tae the meenit it feenished, nae a blink o' a rest, sweatin through his jaicket even wi the caul that wis on. He took the trunk in bits, and ithers hauled the rounds awa tae the side, and it wis heavy, heavy work, aabody's back'll ken it the morn, nae doot.

Aboot midday there wis a richt fleg. A brainch that naebody thocht wis under ony pressure swung back fan the rope wis pulled and it clattered the mannie fae the corner hoose richt across the shooder. He gaed doon, and for a meenit naebody said a wird, we were aa feart it wis waar than it wis. But he wis up again in nae time, cursin awa tae himsel, jist a sair shooder and his pride mair hurt than onythin.

That's fan the argy-bargy stertit, ower foo tae cut the last bit sae it widna dee that again.

The mannie: Ye're gaun aboot it aa vrang, ye should be cuttin fae the ither side, that's fit's gonnae bring it doon on somebody's heid.

The chiel wi the saw: Weel fit dae ye want me tae dee, staun here aa day? Ye come and dee it yersel if ye ken sae muckle aboot it.

The mannie: I'm jist sayin, dinna fash, I'm nae hivin a go at ye.

The wifie fae twa doors doon: The baith o' ye, wheesht. Naebody's deid, naebody's gonnae be deid, jist tak yer time and dee it richt.

And that wis that, sortit in twa meenits, back tae work, nae mair said aboot it aside a bit o' claik later ower the soup.

Cause aye, the soup cam oot, twa hooses brocht their ain pots doon and set them on a table somebody'd dragged fae a back green, and rolls forbye, and a flask or twa o' something stronger for them that wanted it. Fowk were in and oot aa day wi tea, biscuits, a plate o' stovies fae somebody's mither, naebody gaun hungry, that's for sure.

The auldest een there, she maun be past auchty, wis telt tae sit doon aa day, and she wid hae nane o' it. "I'm nae sittin here like a stookie fan there's work tae dee," she says, and she took hersel a job stackin the logs intae a neat pile against the waa, and naebody could talk her oot o't. She's aye been the same, that een, nae sittin doon for onybody, fae mornin tae nicht, a wee wifie wi mair birr in her than half the young eens there.

The moment aabody laucht the same, richt thegither, wis fan yon vratch o' a dog fae the end hoose ran aff wi the big chiel's bunnet richt intae the middle o' the muddiest bit o' the ditch, and him chasin it in his good boots, and the dog thinkin it wis aa a rare game, dartin aboot wi the bunnet in its mou like a prize, and the chiel skiddin and near gaun doon himsel forbye. The hale street wis roarin, tears comin doon fowk's faces, and even the dog's ain wifie couldna get a wird oot for lauchin.

We thocht we'd aa be hame by fower. It wis near seiven, the meen near up already, afore the last o' it wis deen, three hoors mair than onybody hid coonted on. But by the end the road wis clear, the fence wis up again, weel, up eneuch tae hold, the trunk wis in bits and the logs stacked and shared oot amang the hooses for the winter, nae idea far we were gonnae pit them aa, but we managed, and aathing that needed deen wis deen. Somebody gied a cheer fan the last bit wis lifted clear, and it gaed richt up and doon the street, aabody joinin in, even them that hidna deen a hand's turn aa day.

And the street efter that, weel, it wisna the same. Fowk sat oot langer than they'd hae deen, naebody wantin tae gang in first, the bairns still rinnin aboot in the daurk, and fowk that hidna spoken in months staunin claikin like they were auld freends, and fowk'll spik aboot this for a while yet, I'd wager. Jist for the ae nicht, mind, but it wis there, ye could feel it. The auld wifie gied the big chiel a bosie afore she gaed in, her een fair shinin, and he didna ken far tae look, and that set aabody aff again, lauchin.

It's been a gweed day, richt eneuch, for aa the sair backs and the sair heids. Ye canna get that on ony ither nicht. But we got it the day. And that's fit like it wis.
