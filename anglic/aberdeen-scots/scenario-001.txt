Iss aa stertit on the evenin, aboot seiven o'clock, jist a dull ache doon my back that I thocht wis naething ava, jist a lang day on my feet or the tea sittin wrang. I didna say muckle, jist bode aboot the hoose pittin things by, thinkin it wid gang awa gin the morn.

But by nine it hidna gaun awa, it wis comin in waves noo, and I kent fine, this wis it stertin richt.

Me: Iss is it, I think.
Him: Are ye shair?
Me: Aye, I'm shair.

Syne it wis a lang, lang wait. We bode the hoose a fair while, me pacing the flair, him haudin my haun and sayin nae muckle, jist there aside me. My eldest wis awa at her granny's for the nicht, sleepin soond, nae notion fit wis gaun on at hame. Sae it wis jist the twa o us, ower the lang hoors.

Past the middle o the nicht it turned affa sair, hard and feart-like, and I mind greetin oot wi it, thinkin I couldna dae iss ony langer. That wis fan the midwife cam, no fae the stert, she'd been at anither hoose afore, but she cam in aboot twa in the mornin and took a haud o things richt awa.

Midwife: Yer daein fine, quinie, dinna fash, we're nae far aff noo.

That last stretch wis the sairest, aa my strength intil it, and syne, naething. A fyow seconds, mebbe five or six, but it felt like an oor, nae a soun in the room, and I mind lookin up at my man's face and it wis fair white.

Syne the cry cam, a richt roar o a thing, and afore a meenit wis by I had him, my ain loonie, my second, warm and roarin intil my airms, and aathing else jist fell awa.
