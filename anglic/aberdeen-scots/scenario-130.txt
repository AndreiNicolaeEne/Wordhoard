Watter wis at the door first thing, nae mair than ankle deep, bit it wis there aa the same, in aneath the door afore the licht wis richt up. Checked again an oor efter an it hid come up affa, ower ma sliphers noo, sae we startit liftin fit we could aff the flair while there wis still time tae dae it.

The eldest: Fit dae we tak up first?

Me: The books an the wee heater, quick, afore it's ower late.

We got the boxes fae unner the bed up ontae the table, the press, onything that stood heich aneuch. By midmornin it wis up tae oor knees, cauld richt through ye, an we wis wadin aboot pittin the last o it ontae the coonter, the windae sill, onything ava. The mannie fae next door cam through the watter in his wellies tae see we wis aaright.

The mannie: Fit like?

Me: No affa gweed, tae be honest wi ye. Still comin up, an I canna say fit wye.

It keepit comin aa the same, past onything we'd managed tae raise, past the coonter tap, an by early efternoon it wis heicher than it hid ivver been in ma time bidin here. Naething left tae dae bit stand up the stair an listen tae it slap aff the waas. Feart, aye, though naebody said the word oot loud. We didna spik much, jist listened tae it fittin intae ilka corner o the hoose, and I thocht tae masel, this is gey vrang, watter shouldna rise like this in a body's ain hame.

Several oors efter it peaked it startit tae turn, gaen doon that slow ye couldna tell at first, bit by the nicht there wis nae mistakin it wis awa doon.

The morn I gaed doon tae see fit it hid left ahin. A broon line richt roon the waas, heicher than I'd ivver hae guessed watter could get. Stood there a meenit, naething tae say, jist lookin at it.
