Fit like? Sit yersel doon and I'll tell ye the hale thing, cause naebody that wisna there believes it fully, and you werena there, so here's yer chance.

It started ower a game o pool doon the local, on a Setterday, nae different fae ony ither Setterday, till it wisna. There's a scrutty aul table in the back room, cushions gone saft on the een side, and me and my mate wis knockin balls aboot afore the darts fowk even got there. Naebody wis plannin a game right, we wis jist muckin aboot, and then he says, affa casual like, leanin on his cue,

Him: A fiver says I tak ye.

Me: Awa ye go. A fiver? Ye couldna beat yer ain granny at this.

Him: Pit yer money far yer mooth is then.

And that wis it, that wis aa it took, cause by then the ithers had stopped their ain spik and wis gaithered roon, and ye ken fit like fowk get, aabody wants a bit o it, naebody can jist watch.

A quine fae the bar end: A fiver's naething, mak it a tenner.

The big chiel fae the darts: Naw, twinty. Gie it some weight.

And afore either o us had chalked a cue proper, it wis twinty pund, agreed, shakit hands on it, nae takkin it back. Twinty's nae a sum that'll skin ye, jist enough that ye'd feel it, jist enough tae mak ye play different fae hoo ye'd play for naething.

So we played. And I'll tell ye, it went back and fore a gweed while, neither o us clear ahead, him takkin the middle balls affa clean, me clawin back on the colours, the wee crood roon aboot gettin looder every time a shot went in, roarin like the hale skweel wis in, egging it on, "come on now," "he's got ye now," "watch that een." Affa noisy for a Setterday efternoon in a back room wi nae mair than eicht fowk in it.

Then it turned. I widna even say I played better, I'd say he jist gied it awa, cued up for a straightforward pot on the black, and instead pit the white doon efter it, foul shot, gemme tae me there and then, though there wis still balls sittin on the table we baith kent didna maiter noo. He kent it afore I did, tae be fair, and he wisna feart tae say it. He stood back fae the table, pit the cue doon flat, and said,

Him: Aat's it, I've lost.

Nae greetin aboot it, nae argyin the toss, jist said it plain, cause fit else can ye dae when it's aat clear. Aabody roared like the roof wid come aff.

Noo here's far it goes strange, and this is the bit naebody believes till I tell them proper. The twinty pund niver got peyed. Naewhere near it. The crood, still stannin roon, decided amang themsels, afore he'd even got his breath back tae normal, that the peyment widna be money at aa. It wid be a performance, richt there in front o aabody, and they didna leave a single bit o it vague, they laid it aa oot on the spot.

The big chiel: Ye're gyan tae sing the hale o "The Northern Lights" up on this table here, stannin, nae sittin doon.

The quine fae the bar end: And ye're deein it wi the pinny fae ahin the bar tied roon ye like a kilt.

The aul mannie in the corner, that hidna said a word aa nicht: And ye hiv tae dae the actions. The full richt airm up at "grey toon" and aa, nae half-hertit aboot it.

He tried tae bargain his wye oot, mind, laughin but ye could tell he meant it a bittie.

Him: Look, I'll jist gie ye the twinty the morn, we'll caa it quits, nae need for aa this palaver.

Nae chance. They wis aa laughin at him even askin.

The big chiel: Ower late for aat, chiel. Ye said the wager, we're haudin ye tae it. Ye've got it aa vrang if ye think there's a wye oot noo.

Sae a day wis set, the followin Friday, same back room, and word got roon aat week like claik does, aabody that hidna even been there the Setterday kent aboot it by the Wednesday, and by the Friday the back room wis fuller than it iver is, aa there jist for this, ilka een o them expectin a laugh.

And he did it. He got up on that table, pinny tied roon his waist like the daftest kilt ye iver saw, and he sang the hale thing, every verse, nae skippin een, wi the actions and aa, airm up at "grey toon," the ither airm oot for the "lassies" line, the hale performance, nae haudin back, cause by then he'd decided if he wis deein it he wis deein it richt.

But here's the moment, the een that gets brought up every single time this story gets telt again. When he got tae the bit aboot the lassies, he pit his airm oot for the actions and forgot the table wisna as lang as he thocht, and he stepped back richt intae a full pint sittin on the edge, and it went ower him, doon the front o the pinny, and he didna even stop singin. Nae a flicker. Kept the note gyan, soakin wet, pint runnin doon his leg, and jist sang richer for it, like it wis pairt o the act aa alang. The place went daft. Fowk greetin laughin, the big chiel bent double haudin the table, somebody nearly fell aff their chair, the aul mannie in the corner laughin that hard he'd tae tak his glesses aff. That's the bit, every time, aat's the bit that sets aabody gyan again jist hearin it telt.

And that's the thing aboot it noo, mair than the twinty pund iver wis. Whenever we're aa thegither since, doesna maiter far, doesna maiter fa's there, somebody'll bring it up, "mind the pool table," and it gets telt again, and every time it gets telt a bittie mair gets pit on tae it, the pint gets bigger, the note gets higher, somebody swears he did a full turn on the spot at the end noo though naebody mindit aat the first time roon. Gie it anither year or twa and he'll hiv sung the hale thing standin on een leg wi the pinny on fire. That's hoo it goes. The quine fae the bar end still gies him a bosie every time she sees him ower it, jist for makkin the winter worth talkin aboot. The wager's lang forgotten, naebody iver saw the twinty pund and naebody ever will, but the story, that jist keeps gyan, growin a bittie mair theater on it every single time it's telt.
