Fit like the journey wis? Dinna even ask, min. Sit yersel doon, cause I'll tell ye aboot the warst journey I ever took in aa ma days, and I'm lauchin noo tellin ye, though I wasna lauchin a peep o it at the time, no a bit, I'll tell ye that for naethin.

It wis aboot hairst time, and it wis aa planned oot weeks in advance, mind, nae jist a slapdash thing thrown thegither the nicht afore. Tickets bookit, the case packed twa days early, forecast checkit three separate times jist tae mak sure. We wis heidin doon for the cousin's waddin, ma pal and me, and aabody wis sayin it wid gang like clockwark, fine day for it, plenty o time, naething tae fash aboot.

First delay, and we hidna even got prapperly stertit oot the door. The taxi we'd bookit tae tak us tae the station nivir cam, jist nivir cam, and there's us staunin at the close wi the cases, phonin the firm, and some quinie on the ither end tellin us it's "on its wye" for the best pairt o forty meenits, and it wisna on ony wye at aa, it wis sittin in a depot somewye. Fit wye it nivir turned up in the first place, we nivir did find oot. So we're aff on foot, half rinnin wi the cases dirlin aff wir legs, and we jist aboot got the train, twa meenits tae spare, and I mind thinkin, weel, that's the worst o it ower, surely. Fit a thing tae think, foo wrang I wis.

We wisna even an hour intae the journey afore the train grinds tae a stop in the middle o naewhere, nae idea faur we wis, jist fields, and the driver comin ower the speaker sayin there's a fault wi the engine and they're lookin intae it. Half an hour o that, then they tell us we've aa tae get aff and wait for a replacement bus, and by that time there's a fair crowd o us gaithered, folk fae wir train and folk fae ither trains that wis cancelled forbye, aabody staunin aboot wi their cases, a loonie somewye greetin his hert oot, and naebody kennin fit wis gaun on. The wait stretched past three hoors, easy, mair like fower afore onything muived, and every time ye asked somebody in a uniform fit like wis gaun on, they'd jist shrug and say they widna ken, naebody wid tell ye onything, ye jist hid tae bide.

And on tap o that the weather turned. It hid been fine that mornin, nae a cloud, but by the time we're staunin ootside this wee station in the back o beyond, the sky's gone black and it's comin doon in sheets, and there's nae shelter tae speak o, jist a wee bit awning that half the crowd's crammed under, and the rest o us jist gettin drookit tae the bone and cauld as a neep.

That wis when the bags got mixed up in the stramash, folk pushin forward aa at once when the bus finally cam, and I turned roond and ma pal wisna there. Jist gone. I'm shoutin ower the heids o folk, and naebody's payin ony heed, and for a gweed twenty meenits I'm feart somethin, thinkin they've been left ahin athegither, cases and aa, till I finally spot them ower at the ither end o the platform, swept up wi a different bit o the crowd and cudna get back through. Nivir been sae relieved tae see a body's face in ma life.

There wis a mannie, though, an aulder chiel, that I've thocht aboot mony a time since. He'd seen the state o us, drenched and fair frazzled, and he jist quietly handit ower a spare umbrella he hid, said he hid anither ane at hame onywye, dinna fash aboot it. His een were that kind, and he didna even wait for a thank ye prapperly, jist gaed on his wye. I've nivir forgot that, a stranger's wee kindness like that when aathing else wis gaun vrang.

By the time the replacement bus got us onywye near, we'd tae get a taxi the rest o the wye, and that wis anither fifty poun we'd nivir budgeted for, nae a penny o it planned, and I mind staunin there wi the wallet oot thinkin this waddin wis costin us a fair bit mair than we thocht.

And that's when the argument stertit, me and ma pal, ower fit tae dae next.

Me: We should jist wait, there'll be anither train.

My pal: Wait for fit, anither three hoors? Or are we waitin for the morn's train an aa? We're better aff gettin a taxi the rest o the wye, we'll miss the ceremony aathegither.

Me: And fa's peyin for that, eh? We've spent a fortune awready.

My pal: I dinna care fit it costs, we canna jist bide here aa nicht.

It got affa sharp for a wee while, the twa o us fair snappin at ilka ither, tired and drookit and nae in the mood for reason wi ony body. But ma pal won oot, richt or vrang, and we got the taxi.

And of course, the driver, tryin tae outsmart the traffic, took a vrang turn aff the main road and doon some back wye he wis sure wid be quicker, and it wisna, it added a gweed twa hoors on tap o aathing else, roond and roond wee country roads in the daurk and the rain, nae licht tae see by bar the heidlights and a bit meen keekin through the cloud noo and again, wi the meter runnin and naebody spikkin a word.

By that point I wis past carin. That wis the low o it, richt there, sittin in the back o that taxi, soakin, starvin, nae a wink o sleep in us, sittin hard as a steen wi the cauld and the tiredness, cudna keep my een open, thinkin we'd nivir get there and it wid aa hae been for naethin.

Then, jist as we're aboot tae gie up hope aathegither, the driver spots a sign for a wee back road that actually did link up tae the main route, pure luck, and fae there it wis a straight run, nae mair hold-ups, nae mair vrang turns.

We got tae the waddin nine hours later than we wis ivver meant tae, missed the ceremony aathegither, missed maist o the meal forbye, walked in jist as the speeches wis stertin, the twa o us like drooned rats, cases half fawin apart, hair stuck tae wir heids, nae a dry stitch atween us. The cousin near swallowed us up in a bosie at the door, drookit as we wis, didna care a bit, and I'm sure the state o us gied the photies a richt fleg for years tae come.

But we got there. And noo, months efter, I can sit here and lauch aboot the hale thing, the bus, the rain, the argument, the vrang turn, aathing, though I'll tell ye, at the time, there wisna a single peerie bit o it that felt lauchable ava. It's still the claik o the hale family tae this day, and I'm nae sure it'll ivver stop bein the spik o us at ony gaitherin, no as lang as we baith live.
