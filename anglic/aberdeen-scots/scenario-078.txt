Right, I'll tell ye the hale thing fae the start, cos you'll get a laugh oot it even though at the time it wisna funny ava.

By the end o the night my friend wis steamin. Nae just merry, nae just a bit fu, but proper steamin, the kind far the een go aa gley and the heid's tippin forward like it's ower heavy for the neck tae haud up. We'd been in fae early doors, and it hid crept up on the baith o us the wye it dis, ye dinna clock it till it's ower late and the chiel's swaying at the bar tryin tae order anither ane.

The first sign it hid gone properly wrang wis the legs. My friend went tae staun up aff the stool and juist folded, nae warnin, doon like a puppet wi the strings cut, and I hid tae grab an airm afore they went richt intae the flair.

Me: Fit like, are you aaright?

My friend: Aye aye, I'm fine, I'm affa fine, dinna fash yersel.

But the words come oot aa run thegither, nae proper shape tae them ava, and they were haudin ontae the bar like it wis the only thing keepin the hale warld fae spinnin awa fae them. I kent richt then it wis time tae get them hame, but tryin tae tell a steamin chiel it's time tae leave is a battle in itsel.

My friend: Naw, naw, I'm nae leavin, the nicht's young, we'll hae anither ane, c'mon.

Me: You can barely staun, we're gaun.

My friend: I can staun fine, look...

And doon they went again intae the same stool, missed it, near went ower backarts. It took a good ten meenits o coaxin, half haudin them up, half arguin, afore they gied in and let me get them tae the door, grumblin the hale time aboot foo I wis spylin a gweed nicht.

Ance we were oot, I got an airm roond them and kept it there the hale wye hame, cos there wisna a chance o them makin it on their ain twa feet. They were leanin their full wecht intae me, and I could feel them stoatin fae side tae side even wi me haudin on. We didna get faur afore they hid tae stop, bent ower against a waa, bein sick intae the gutter while I stood haudin their shooders and tellin them tae juist let it aa oot, it'd help. They sat doon richt there on the curb efter, heid in their hands, sayin they juist needed a meenit.

A wifie comin the ither wye wi her messages gied us a look ye widna believe, hauf disgust and hauf peety, and muttered somethin under her breath aboot the state o young fowk the day as she gaed by, giein us a gey wide berth. I juist gied her a wee nod, nae the time tae explain yersel tae a stranger at hauf twelve at nicht wi a sick chiel on the pavement.

We got them back up efter a whily and set aff again, but I'll admit, wi my ain heid nae exactly clear either, we took a wrang turn doon a street that wisna the wye hame ava, and it wis a good five meenits afore I clocked it and we hid tae double back, my friend aa the while askin far we were gaun and me nae haein a straight answer.

Fin we finally got near hame I tried tae get some watter intae them.

Me: Here, drink this, you need it.

My friend: I dinna want watter, get it awa fae me.

Me: You're haein it whether ye like it or nae.

My friend: Nae, nae, I'm fine...

But efter a bit mair pushin they gied in and took a few gulps, coughin hauf o it back up, but it wis in them at least. It wis somewhere aboot there, sittin on a low waa catchin their breath, that they turned tae me aa sudden and serious, and said the ane thing that only comes oot a body fin they're that fu.

My friend: D'ye ken, you're the best chiel I've ever hid in my hale life, I mean that, I'd dae onything for you, onything ava.

Me: Aye, I ken, come on, up ye get.

My friend: Naw but I mean it, I really really mean it...

And greetin a wee bit at that, richt there on the waa, ower nothing and ower everything aa at ance, the wye they get.

We got them up again and made it tae the door at last, and that wis a battle o its ain, the key widna gang in the lock richt cos my friend wis leanin on me and I wis tryin tae haud them up and fin the keyhole aa at the same time in the dark, and there wis a fair bit o fumblin and swearin afore it finally turned. Got them ower the doorstep, near went doon again on the mat, but I got them through tae the livin room and doon ontae the couch, and they were oot near enough afore their heid hit the cushion.

Afore they went richt under they grabbed my airm and said it, the promise, the ane they aye say.

My friend: I'm never drinkin like that again, never again, I mean it this time.

Me: Aye, richt you are.

And I didna believe a word o it even as it wis comin oot their mooth, cos we baith kent fine it wisna the first time we'd hid this conversation and it widna be the last either. But ye let them hae it onywey, cos it's kindly meant even if it's a lee.

The morn I gaed roond tae check on them, and they were sittin up wi their heid in their hands, lookin like death, askin fit happened.

My friend: Fit like, foo bad wis I?

Me: Affa bad. D'ye nae mind ony o it?

My friend: I mind bein at the bar, and I mind... did I greet aboot somethin?

Me: Aye, you telt me I wis the best chiel you'd ever kent in your hale life.

My friend: Oh God. Did I fa ower?

Me: Aye, mair than ance.

My friend: Wis there a wifie...

Me: Aye, she gied us a look that wid curdle milk.

And bit by bit I filled it in for them, the wrang turn, the bein sick, the door, aa o it, and they sat there wi their heid shakin, half laughin and half moaning, sayin never again, and I juist smiled, cos we baith ken fine that's a promise made ower and ower, and it'll be made again, and I'll be there again tae get them hame, cos that's fit ye dae for the fowk ye love.
