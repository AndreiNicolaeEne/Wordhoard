Me: Fit like? Div ye hae a meenit? I'm fair forfochen wi this roof o mine.

The neighbour: Aye, come awa in, sit yersel doon. Fit's adae?

Me: It's the corner abeen the back bedroom, ower the chimney breist. Been drippin since the back end o the hairst, and noo it's fair rinnin doon the wa when it's coorse ootside. I've asked the landlord twice already, and the first time he says he'd get a mannie oot the morn, nae bather, he says. Naethin happened.

The neighbour: And the second time?

Me: Same spik. "I'll get somebody tae look at it," he says, "dinna fash yersel." That's near three month back noo and nae a soul's been near the place.

The neighbour: Three month, ye say, and twa promises wi naethin ahin them? That's affa lang tae thole a leak. Richt, ye've a wheen o wyes tae gyang aboot it. Ye could pit it aa doon in writin tae him, gie him a date, and keep a copy for yersel. Or ye could phone the cooncil, get the environmental health mannie oot tae hae a look, though that can tak a while and he'll no thank ye for it. Or ye could juist withhaud the rent till it's mendit, but that's a risky ploy, he could turn roond and pit ye oot.

Me: Aye, I'm feart o that ane.

The neighbour: I ken fine fit ye mean. I had a landlord masel, years back, that did the vera same thing tae me, a leak abeen the kitchen, and he strung me alang for near a year afore onything got dune. It wis only when I got it aa in writin, wi a date on it, that he moved.

Me: Richt then. I'll pit it doon on paper the nicht, tell him a date, and keep it for masel in case it comes tae mair.

The neighbour: Aye, that's the ane tae try first. Dinna fash, we'll see foo he answers that.
