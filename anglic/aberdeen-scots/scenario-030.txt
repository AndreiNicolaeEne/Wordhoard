Me: Richt, come on then, ye're nae gettin oot o't this time. Gie's the recipe afore it's lost athegither.

The aunt: Oh dinna fash me, quine, A'm nae a book, A'm jist an aul wifie. Richt, ye start wi the flour. A pound, near eneuch. Mebbe a pound and a bittie mair, ma mither aye said a pound but she aye pit in mair than that hersel.

Me: And fit else gings in?

The aunt: The butter neist... na, wait, A'm gettin ahead o masel again. Ye rub the sugar intae the flour first, syne the butter efter, cauld fae the press, aboot half a pound o that.

Me: Fit aboot the eggs? Ye near forgot the eggs the last time ye telt is this.

The aunt: Aye, richt eneuch, the eggs, near forgot them athegither! Twa o them. Ma grunnie aye said if ye leave oot the egg it winna haud thegither ava, sae dinna you dae that.

Me: And syne?

The aunt: Syne ye fold in a gweed handfu o currants, nae mizzled oot, jist a fistfu, and wark it aa thegither, but nae ower lang mind, jist eneuch tae bind it, then intae the tin wi't.

Me: Is there a bit ye canna rush?

The aunt: The ristin. That's the ane ma grunnie aye warned is never tae rush, she'd say leave it be, dinna keek in on it every twa meenits, it kens its ain time and it'll tell ye when.

Me: How dae ye ken it's deen, but?

The aunt: Naething tae dae wi a clock. It comes awa fae the sides o the tin itsel, and the hale hoose smells o't, that's aa the tellin ye need.

Me: And the sugar, foo muckle exactly?

The aunt: Never measured it in ma days, jist a fistfu, judged by e'e, same as ma mither aye did.

Me: Could ye pit in raisins insteid o currants?

The aunt: Aye, ye could richt eneuch, jist mind they're bigger, cut them doon a bittie, but aye, that wid dae fine.
