Ah'll tell ye fit happened, richt fae the start, cause it's a queer story an it's aa mendit noo, but my god it wisnae aye that wye.

It wis a wild nicht, the wind fair roarin doon aff the hill, rattlin the slates, the meen naewye tae be seen ahint aa that cloud. Ah mind lyin there thinkin, that's some nicht, an gled Ah wisnae oot in it. Come the morn Ah gets up, pits the kettle on, an gings oot the back tae see fit like the gairden wis efter aa that carry-on.

An there it wis. The fence, the hail run o it, doon flat, splintered like a box o matches, ma neeps aa squashed intae the ground an aa, an this muckle great tree lyin richt across the middle o it, roots up in the air like some affa great hand. Ah just stood there in ma sleepers, no even a jaicket on, gawpin at it.

Noo the first thing ye dae is work oot fit tree it even wis, cause it wisnae growin richt in oor gairden, an it wisnae richt in theirs aither - it wis sittin near enough on the line atween the twa, but leanin, if ye looked at it richt, gey clear ower intae oor side. Been there years, that tree, langer than either o us had bidden there, Ah'd say.

So Ah gings roon tae the neighbour's door, chappit on it, an oot comes the neighbour, still in the dressin goon.

Me: Ye'll hae seen the state o things then.

The neighbour: Aye, terrible nicht that. But fit can ye dae, it wis the wind, nathin tae be duin aboot the wind.

Me: The wind didna plant the tree richt on the line an let it lean ower ma fence for the past ten year though, did it.

The neighbour: It's a tree. It's nae a fault. Things faa doon in a storm like that, it's naebody's fault.

An that wis the start o it richt there, cause Ah wisnae huvin that. Ah minded fine that tree had been leanin for a gweed while, an it wisnae juist me that thocht so aither. The aal wifie twa doors up, she'd said it tae me hersel a couple year back, ower the dyke.

The aal wifie: Ah tellt them, ye ken. Ah tellt them fine that tree wis vrang, leanin the wye it wis, an naebody listened tae me.

So Ah went back roon, an Ah tellt the neighbour it wisnae juist the wind, that tree had been leanin for years, an they'd been tellt aboot it an aa. The neighbour got affa crabbit at that, said Ah wis makkin oot like it wis their fault entire, an Ah said weel it's no aa my fault aither, an so it gaed on, back an fore, neither o us wantin tae be the een that gied in first. Days o it, near enough. No shoutin exactly, but that stiff wye o spikkin tae somebody ower a fence, aa polite on the surface an affa cold aneath it. Ah'll admit Ah wis a bittie feart tae even gang oot the back in case Ah saw them there an hid tae spik. The claik gaed roon the street an aa, ye ken fit it's like, aabody kennin aabody's business an naebody sayin a word tae yer face aboot it.

It wis an aal mannie fae further doon, naethin tae dae wi either o us really, jist a chiel that had bidden on that street langer than onybody, that cam ower ae day, unasked, mind, naebody had said a word tae him aboot it.

The aal mannie: Noo then, dinna fash yersels. Ah'm no here tae say fa's richt an fa's vrang, cause it's a bittie o baith an a bittie o naither, an that's aye the wye it is wi a tree an a fence. But it's a shared fence, an it wis a shared tree near enough, growin on the line the wye it wis, so it's a shared cost, split doon the middle, an neither o ye has tae say ye were vrang tae dae that. Ye're jist bein fair, the twa o ye thegither.

An it wis queer, but that wis aa it took. Naebody had tae back doon, cause naebody wis askit tae admit onything, jist tae split it, an that wye baith o us could dae it an still haud oor heids up. The neighbour said aye tae that affa quick, an so did Ah, an Ah think baith o us wis relieved tae hae a wye oot o it that didna feel like losin.

Aince the money wis settled, the actual work o it hid tae be duin, an that got split an aa. The neighbour got a chiel in wi a chainsaw tae cut up the tree, an Ah did the clearin on oor side, an we agreed tae split the timmer for the new posts atween us. First mornin o it wis affa stiff, Ah'll tell ye that, the twa o us workin awa no ten feet apart an hardly a word passin, jist "richt" an "aye" an "pass us that ower," naethin mair than that. Ye could feel it still sittin there atween us, aa that days o argy-bargyin, even though the money wis settled fair an square.

But it wis the neighbour's wife that eased it, in the end, mair than onything the mannies said tae each ither. She cam oot mid mornin wi a tray o tea an pieces, didna even ask, jist set it doon on the wa an said help yersels. Ye canna keep bein stiff wi somebody that's handit ye a bacon roll ower a broken fence. An later on, Ah handit the neighbour ower ma ain saw when his blade gaed blunt, nae word said aboot it, jist handit it ower, an he took it the same wye, an fae there on it wis easy. Their quinie an loonie were aff the skweel that day an aa, roon helpin cairry posts an haudin the wire straicht, an it didna feel like work onymair, jist twa hooses gettin a fence back up thegither.

Took the feck o a week, aa telt, but the fence that's up noo is a sicht sturdier than the aal een ever wis - proper posts, deep in, gweed strong wire, no like the aal thing that wis half rotten onywye afore the tree ever touched it.

An noo, ken, things sit better atween the twa hooses than they did afore aa this stairtit. We spik ower the fence maist days, she brings roon a bit hairst veg fae the gairden when there's ower much o it, Ah gie a bosie tae the bairns when Ah see them comin hame fae the skweel. It took a bad nicht an a fair bit o fashin tae get there, but Ah widna say it wis a bad thing, no in the end. Ye ken fit like it is - ye can bide next tae somebody twinty year an hardly ken them, an it taks a doon fence tae mak ye actual neighbours.

That's the hail o it, onywye.
