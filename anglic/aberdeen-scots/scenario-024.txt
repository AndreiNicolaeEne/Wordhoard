The bride: Richt, we need tae get this leet doon afore the morn. We're affa ower, thirty ower fit the place can even seat.

The groom: Thirty? Foo did it get tae thirty, I thocht we'd trimmed it aaready.

The bride: Ken fine foo, yer mither's side jist keeps growin every time we look at it. Start wi the far-oot anes, yer cousin's loon we've nivver spoken tae in oor life, the workmates neither o's spiks tae fae ae year tae the neist. Cut aa that and it's doon by fifteen, saxteen easy.

The groom: Aye, dae that then. But dinna touch the aunt. She's stayin on the leet nae matter fit onybody says.

The bride: The aunt that cried ma mither a vratch at yer sister's christenin?

The groom: She's faimly. She's stayin, that's the end o it.

The bride: Weel if she's stayin, somebody's comin aff your side tae even it up. It's aye the wey, your side aye gets mair seats than mine, aabody kens it, it's aye been vrang and it's aye me that has tae say it.

The groom: No this again.

The bride: It's true though, and ye ken it's true.

The groom: Fit if we jist hae a smaa do efter, for the anes that dinna fit, a bit tea the morn's morn, nathing fancy?

The bride: Naw, naebody wants tae be telt they're the second-best invite. That's waur than leavin them aff aathegither.

The groom: Fine, fine, dinna fash yersel. Coont it again.

The groom: ...still a dizzen ower.

The bride: Richt. Take the twa chiels fae yer work night oot, that's ten.

The groom: Aye, ten it is. We'll spik on the rest the morn.
