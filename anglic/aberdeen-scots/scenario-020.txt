The grieving friend: Fit like. Sorry, I ken it's late, I jist couldna sit in the hoose masel ony langer.

The listening friend: Come in, come in, sit yersel doon. Fit's happened?

The grieving friend: He's awa. Packed the bag Setterday nicht, said "this isna workin" and that wis that, oot the door afore I'd even got a word in.

The listening friend: Mm.

The grieving friend: I keep gaun ower it in my heid. Fit I said, fit I didna say. Maybe I wis ower thrang wi work, nivver hame in time, maybe I wisna affa much fun tae bide wi ony mair.

The listening friend: Aye.

The grieving friend: Maybe if I'd tried harder, gaun oot mair wi him, I dinna ken, maybe it wis jist me.

The listening friend: Naw. Stop that richt noo, dae ye hear me. That's nae fit happened ava. He wis seein that lass fae his work months afore he ever said a word, aabody kent it but yersel. That's the vrang o it, nae you, nae yer work, nae yer nichts in wi a book. Him. Aa him.

The grieving friend: ...

The listening friend: And it disna say a single thing aboot fit kind o quinie you are. Ye hear? Nae a thing. You're jist the same as ye ivver wis.

The grieving friend: I'm feart o the hoose the nicht, I dinna even ken fit I'm feart o.

The listening friend: Weel ye're nae bidin in it thinkin yersel roond in circles. I'm stayin the night, nae argument, or the morn we get you oot athegither, up by the hairst fields, get some air intae ye, awa fae aa they wa's.

The grieving friend: Ye dinna hiv tae dae aa that.

The listening friend: Dinna fash yersel, quinie, I'm nae gaun onywhere. Come here.

The grieving friend: Yer an affa saft chiel yersel, ken that.

The listening friend: Aye, weel. Bosie first, greetin efter, that's the rule.
