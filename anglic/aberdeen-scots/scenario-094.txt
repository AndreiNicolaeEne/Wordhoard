The mother: Right, come here aside me, the pair o ye. Sit doon on the settee, nae runnin aff, I want ye baith close.

The eldest: Fit's wrang? Ye've got yer sair face on.

The mother: I've something tae tell ye, and I want ye tae listen richt. Yer granda's deid. He passed awa this mornin, affa peaceful, in his bed.

The eldest: But fit dis that mean though? Like... fit happens tae him noo, where's he gone?

The mother: It means his body's stoppit workin, pet. His hert's just stoppit, and naethin can start it again. He's nae in pain, nae feart o onything ony mair. He's gone, and we'll nae see him walkin aboot or hear his claik at the door again. It's a sair, sair thing, and it's aaright tae greet, cause I'm greetin masel.

The youngest: Is he sleepin?

The mother: Na, quinie, he's nae sleepin. Sleepin ye wake up fae. This isna that. This is the end o his life, and he'll nae wake again. I ken that's an affa hard thing tae hear, but I widna tell ye onything but the truth.

The mother: Come here, gie's a bosie, the baith o ye.

The mother: There noo, my wee quinie, in ye come, richt in aside me.

The youngest: Will we see him again?

The mother: Nae here, no the wye we're used tae, nae sittin in his chair wi his tea, nae at the door wi his aal jokes. But he'll aye be pairt o us, in fit we mind o him, and naebody can tak that fae us. I'm nae gonna dodge it, love, he's nae comin back.

The mother: Richt, listen tae me noo. Ower the next twa-three days there'll be fowk in and oot the hoose, yer auntie, yer uncle. We'll gyaan through and see him afore the funeral, if ye want tae. There'll be a service, and fowk will spik aboot him and mind the gweed things. It'll be a sair time, but we'll get through it thegither, aa o us. Awa an play noo, I'm richt here.
