I'd been pittin it aff for weeks, but the gas bill wis due and I wisna gaun tae let it lie nae mair. We met ootside the shop like ony ither day, him wi his messages under his airm, and he says fit like as if aathing wis fine atween us.

Me: Fit like yersel. Listen, I dinna wint tae mak a thing o it, but it's near three month noo since I lent ye the eighty pound. For the caur, mind.

The friend: Aye, aye, I ken, I ken. Things hae juist been affa ticht this month, the wifie's been on at me aboot the leccy and aa that.

Me: Ye said that last month an aa. And ye promised me it by the hairst, ye said it yersel, sittin richt there in the pub. That's come and gaen.

The friend: Fit dae ye wint fae me, bleed fae a stane? I'm no made o siller, chiel.

Me: I'm no askin for siller ye dinna hae, I'm askin for the siller ye promised. There's a difference.

The friend: Ye're gey quick tae mind a date fan it suits ye. I've done plenty for ye an aa, I dinna gyang aboot the claik on that.

Me: This isna aboot fit ye've done for me, it's aboot eighty pound that's been sittin unpaid since the spring.

He gaed a bit quiet then, luikin doon at his beets, and I could see he wis feart I wis gaun tae mak a scene o it richt there on the street.

The friend: Dinna cry me a vratch ower a few bob. I've aye peyed ye back afore.

Me: No aye on time though.

The friend: Look, I've thirty on me the noo, tak that, and I'll hae the lave tae ye by the morn, I swear it.

Me: Aye. Weel. We'll see fit the morn brings this time.

He handed ower the notes withoot lookin at me, and we baith kent fine it wisna sorted, juist pit aff again, like aa the ither times afore it.
