Right, come awa in aside me and I'll shew ye the wye o things, for naebody's ever vrote it doon bit aabody kens it jist the same.

See the motor, ye leave it up by the wa there, nae bother, aabody dis that and naebody thinks twice. Bit dinna even think on pittin a cairt or a motor doon by the corner hoose, even though it sits toom fae mornin tae nicht wi nae sign nor naethin - that's the auld mannie's bit, has been for years, and he'll be oot in his sliders gi'in ye the ee afore yer handbrake's even on.

See that strip o gress atween the twa fences, naethin merks it, nae dyke, nae string, bit ye dinna set a fit on it aa the same. That's the wifie's ground, and aabody kens far it stops, though she's never said a vord aboot it in her life.

Come the nicht, aifter aboot nine, the hale street gings affa quiet, nae radios, nae doors bangin, nae loons oot kickin a ba. Naebody pit up a sign for it, it's jist the wye it's aye been.

Fan ye're passin folk on the pavement, the aulder een get the nod first, aye, afore the young eens, afore the bairns even. It's jist mainners, ye ken, and ye'll learn it quick eneuch.

Noo, borrowin - if ye need a spade or a cup o sugar or the like, ye jist chap the door and ask, nae bother, folk are affa gweed aboot it here. Bit mind this: if a thing disna come back, naebody says a vord tae yer face, nae claik gings roon aboot it either - bit ye'll no be asked in again in a hurry, and that's the hale o it, quiet as ye like.

And the one rule? Aabody pits their bins oot the nicht afore, though we aa ken fine ye're no sposed tae. Dinna fash yersel aboot it, jist dinna be the last een tae dae it.
