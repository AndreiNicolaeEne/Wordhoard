Richt, sit doon aa o ye, I'll gie ye the hale o it fae the stert.

Me: I wis up at some ungodly oor, nae even needin tae be, jist lyin there wi ma stomach in knots, thinkin aboot fit wye I'd ivver said aye tae this job in the first place. Oot the door affa early, walkin doon in the caul wi ma heid fu o aa the things that could gyang vrang.

The mother: Ye aye dae that tae yersel, ye ken.

Me: Aye, weel, I got there and there wis a mannie waitin, the one in chairge o's, and he's jist "fit like?" tae me, canny as onything, like I'd been there years. Shawed's roond, gied's ower tae the fower or five ithers, and then handed me the first job, which wis nae half the size o fit I'd been bracin masel for aa nicht. I thocht it wid be some muckle complicated thing and it wis jist sortin and stackin, dead simple.

The eldest: Did ye manage aa richt?

Me: Mair or less. I fair made a richt cyaak o masel aboot denner time, drapped a hale load o the stuff richt in front o them aa, and jist stood there like a feart quinie, face reid as onything. Naebody said much, jist got on wi it.

At the break I wis sittin sizin them aa up, the wifies and the loons, tryin tae wark oot fa wis fit. There wis this ae mannie, aulder chiel, didna say muckle, but he cam ower withoot me even askin and shawed's the richt wye tae dae it sae it disna aa fa doon again. Affa gweed o him.

The pace wis quicker than I'd thocht, nae time tae stand aboot, and the nine oors fair flew in a wye I wisna expectin.

Walkin hame I wis ower it aa again in ma heid, every bit o it.

The mother: Weel, fit's the verdict then?

Me: Nae bad ava. Gie's a bosie, I'm shattered.
