Me: Fit like? Richt, fit aboot the nicht then, dae we say aicht at the quarry bar, aabody there?

My friend: Aicht's nae gweed for me, I'm working till hauf seven, widna get doon in time, ye ken fit the buses are like.

Me: Aye fair eneuch. Nine dae ye then?

My friend: Nine's better aye. Richt, fa are we checking wi? Gaun doon the list.

Me: Yer sister first, is she in?

My friend: She's in, said she's free aa nicht, naethin on for her.

Me: Fit aboot the chiel fae yer work, the ane that's aye late?

My friend: Him, aye he's in an aa, said he'll come straucht fae his work.

Me: The wifie fae the flat next tae mine?

My friend: Naw, she canna come, she's got the bairn the nicht, naebody tae watch him, so that's her oot.

Me: Ah richt, that's fair eneuch, dinna fash aboot it, canna be helpit.

My friend: Fit aboot the ither twa quines?

Me: Baith in, I spoke tae them aforehaun.

My friend: Nine's still a bittie early for me tae be honest, could we nae push tae hauf nine?

Me: Hauf nine's affa late for some o them though, they've their work the morn, mind.

My friend: Aye true eneuch, I widna want them aa deid on their feet the morn's morn either.

Me: Nine it is then, that'll dae fine for maist o them onywye.

My friend: Richt, quarry bar, nine o'clock.

Me: I'll pass the wird tae the lave o them, ye dinna need tae dae it aa yersel.

My friend: Cheers for that. Fit dae we think, sax or seiven o us aathegither?

Me: Aboot that aye, mebbe seiven if the chiel brings somebody, but dinna haud me tae it, could be sax.

My friend: Fine, that'll dae rare. See ye the nicht then.

Me: Aye, see ye there, dinna be late!
