Richt, I'll tell ye foo the day gaed, seein as ye werna there yersel.

It wis the auld hoose we were clearin, the grandmither's, the ane she'd been in since she wis a quinie hersel near enough. She's in the hame noo, the puir sowel, and canna manage stairs nor onything else, so there wis nae sense keepin the place on, teem as it wis and naebody tae bide in it. The rates alone wid hae done for us. So the hale crowd cam, no jist the closest o us but aunties and cousins and a chiel that mairrit in tae the faimly years back and still turns up for things like this, fit like he is. Aabody wi a van or a bit o rope in the boot.

We startit at the front door and jist gaed roon, room by room, so's naethin got missed or duplicated.

The lobby wisna much, coats and buits and an umbrella wi nae spokes left in it, intae the skip wi that. But the front room wis a different maitter. My auntie wis in there afore the rest o us, on her knees at the auld sideboard, and she gied a wee cry that brought us aa through.

The auntie: Losh, look at this. That's her and my faither, afore he even gaed tae the skweel, doon the beach at Stonehaven. I mind her tellin me aboot this verra photie, the day it wis teen.

She sat on the flair wi it a good ten meenits, no sayin much mair, jist lookin.

Then there wis the ither thing, oot the same drawer, a wee tin box wi a kind o metal disc inside it, nae writin on it that onybody could mak oot, jist a pattern like a flooer. Naebody kent fit it wis.

The eldest: Fit is that even for? Is that fae the war, div ye think?

The cousin: Could be onything. Some kind o badge, mibbe.

Nae idea. It gaed in a bag marked "keep, ask her," though we aa kent fine she'd likely no mind either, her memory bein fit it is noo.

The sittin room wis the dresser argument. Big auld thing, gweed wood, been in the faimly since afore ony o us wis born, and three fowk wantit it and nae room in ony o their hooses for it.

The mother: I've the wall for it, I hae. And I'm the ane wis here every Sunday for the past twinty year, so.

The auntie: Aye, weel, I'm no sayin naethin aboot that, but it wis oor mither's mither's afore it wis hers.

It gaed on a fair while, back and forrit, till the chiel that mairrit in said jist measure the twa walls and see fit fits best, and that wis that, settled wi a tape measure raither than ony mair claik.

Fit didna settle sae easy wis the stag's heid ower the fireplace, an ugly thing wi glesswork een that hud aye gied me the willies as a bairn.

The loon (grown noo, but still cried that): That thing's gaun straight in the skip, I dinna care fa says fit.

The eldest: Ye canna jist bin it, it wis her faither's, it's been on that wall langer than you've been alive.

The loon: Aye, and it's hideous, and it's affa heavy, and naebody's pittin it up in their ain hoose, so fit's the point keepin it?

Naebody won that ane richt oot. It got pit tae the side, "tae think on," which meant it sat in the hall for the rest o the day while fowk stepped roon it.

Ben the passage, ahin a loose bit o skirtin board that the eldest happened tae catch wi her fit, we fund a wee bundle o letters, tied wi string, fae a chiel nane o us had ever heard tell o. Naebody kent fa he wis or fit he'd been tae her. The auntie gaed affa quiet readin the first ane, and jist said, "weel, there's a story there we'll never ken noo," and pit them in her bag tae read hersel, later, quiet.

The kitchen wis the room that teuk the langest, by a lang wye. Ye'd think a kitchen wid be quick, but naw, it wis fu tae the gunnels, tins o this and that gaun back tae fan wi dinna ken, jars o buttons, a drawer o string that widna throw awa, pots and pans for every size o pot and pan there ever wis. We were in there near three oor, sortin and stackin and haudin things up sayin "keep or binned?" ower and ower till the word lost aa meanin.

Aboot the middle o the day we stoppit for wir denner, aabody roon the ae table that wis left, soup fae a flask and pieces and a cake somebody's wife had sent doon. Naebody said much aboot the hoose for a fair while, jist ate, and the chiel telt a story aboot fan he first cam tae the faimly and didna understand a word onybody said tae him, and we aa lauched, and it wis gweed tae sit doon efter the mornin we'd had.

The piano wis the big ane naebody wantit. Nae room fits it, nae ane o us plays it richt onywye, and the chiel wi the van said even gettin it doon the stair wid tak three men and likely tak the wa wi it. In the end it gaed tae a man that cams for such things, teuk it awa for naethin, and there wis a queer moment aabody stood in the empty corner o the room fan it wis oot, no sayin onything.

The bible, though, naebody argued ower that at aa. It jist gaed, wioot a word said, tae the eldest, since she wis the ane that read fae it at the grandfaither's funeral, and that wis richt and aabody kent it wis richt.

By late efternin the last room, the wee back bedroom, wis teem, jist the bare flair and the mark on the waa fae far the bed heid had stood aa thae years, and it wis a queer thing, staunin in an empty hoose that used tae be sae fu.

Fit wis left ower, the odds and ends, plates that didna match onything, a claik o spoons, a rug naebody had claimed, we jist divided it oot atween us there and then, aabody teckin fit they liked the look o, nae fechtin left in onybody by that time o day.

Syne we locked the door for the last time, aabody staunin on the path a meenit, and naebody said much, jist "richt, that's that then," and gaed wir different wyes hame.
