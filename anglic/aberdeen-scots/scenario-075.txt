Me: Haud on... is that you? Naw, it cannae be!

The auld pal: Losh, it is! Fit like, ye chiel, fit like! I nivver thocht I'd see your face here o aa places!

Me: I ken, I near walked richt past ye, I did! Fit are ye deein doon this wye?

The auld pal: Jist ower for a day or twa, business like. Foo lang's it actually been noo, dae ye mind?

Me: Mair nor ten year, easy. Since skweel, I'm thinkin. Must be eleiven, twal even.

The auld pal: Aye, since we were jist a loon and a quine runnin aboot the place gettin fasht for aathing. So fit's been gaun on wi you aa this time?

Me: Workin awa, same auld same auld. Mairit noo, twa bairns, a loonie and a quinie. Yersel?

The auld pal: Nae mairit yet, bit courtin. Working up in the toon still, same trade. Here, mind fin we bade oot in the neep park till the fairmer chased us, and you tore your breeks on the fence and grat aa the wye hame?

Me: Ha! Dinna! I'd forgotten that, ye rotten mannie, I was feart tae go hame in case my mither saw the state o me!

The auld pal: Your face though, I laugh yet thinkin on it.

Me: Fit aboot the ither een, dae ye ever hear fae her? Naething fae me in years noo.

The auld pal: Nae a cheep. Naebody kens far she bides noo, she's jist away, disappeared aff the face o the earth.

Me: Affa shame that. Weel, gie's your number this time, we'll nae let it be anither ten year.

The auld pal: Aye, richt enough, here. And I mean it this time, nae jist claik, I'll actually spik tae ye, the morn even, I promise.

Me: Aye ye said that last time an aa!

The auld pal: Weel this time I mean it. Bosie afore ye go?

Me: Aye, come here ye.
