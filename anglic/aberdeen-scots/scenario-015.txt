Love, I need tae say this plain afore I say onything else: I love ye, richt tae the bone o me, and I hivna the wirds tae mak it fancier than that.

I mind fine the day it turned serious. It wisna a big occasion, jist a wik nicht in, you at the sink wi yer sleeves rowed up, singin awa tae yersel some sang aff the radio, nae even kennin I wis watchin. I stood in the door wi the kettle bilin ahint me and thocht, that's it, that's the een I'm bidin wi. Naethin happened. Naebody said a wird. But I kent fae that meenit on, and I've aye kent it since.

There's a thing aboot ye naebody else wid think tae mention, the wee scar abeen yer left ee, fae fawin aff yer bike as a loonie, and hou ye rub it withoot kennin when yer thinkin hard. I've watched ye dae it a thoosand times and I dinna think ye've ever kent I notice.

My days are a different shape wi ye in them. The hame feels like a hame noo, nae jist a hoose I bide in. And I'll say this plain, the days we're apairt hiv got harder, nae easier, the langer on. I used tae manage fine my lane. Noo the flat's affa quiet athoot yer claik and yer daft sangs, and even a bosie fae ye wid set the hale day richt.

I'm nae great at spikkin aboot this kind o thing, but here's the thing I've never said oot loud: I'm feart, sometimes, that I'll nae be eneuch for ye, that ye'll wauken up ae day and wint mair than fit I've got tae gie. I dinna say it cause I dinna wint ye fashin yersel ower it. But it's there, and this bit o paper's the only place it gets tae come oot.

So here's my promise, and I mean it: I'll nae let ye face onything my lane. Whativer comes, the morn and aa the morns efter, I'll aye be bidin richt aside ye.

Aa my love, my ain quinie.
