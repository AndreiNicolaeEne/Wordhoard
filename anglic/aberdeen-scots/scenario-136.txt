I keeked in the tin ahin the press, whaur the month's siller sits by, and it wisna richt, affa lighter than it should be. Coonted it aince, coonted it twice, still short a fair bit aff the low hunners. Checked the ither place forby, the wee poke in the drawer whaur it gets shiftit sometimes, but naethin there aither.

So I gaed roon the hoose, saft-like tae stert wi.

Me: Has onybody moved the siller fae the tin? Nae bother if ye did, jist say.

The wife: No me, I've no touched it.

The eldest: Fit are ye on aboot? I dinna ken naethin aboot ony siller.

The loonie: Nae me either, I've been at the skweel aa day.

Naebody kent naethin, aa denyin it flat, ilka een the same. Syne it come tae me - the eldest wis the ane that bade in the hoose her lane the day it maun hae gone, aabody else oot at wirk or skweel. So I speirt again, nae saft this time.

Me: You were her lane in this hoose the day it went. Naebody else here aa day. So fit happened tae the siller?

The eldest: I telt ye, I dinna ken! I widna touch yer siller, I sweir tae ye.

Me: Dinna gie's that, quinie, ye're the only een that could hae. Ye're sittin there tellin me lees tae my face.

The eldest: I'm no lein! Yer aye thinkin the warst o me, it's affa vrang, so it is, and I canna believe ye're sayin this!

Me: Somebody's lein, and it's no me!

The eldest: Weel it's no me either, ye vratch, and ye can think fit ye like!

It jist stapped there, naebody backin doon, naebody believin fit the ither said, the hale hoose gone quiet and grim wi it.
