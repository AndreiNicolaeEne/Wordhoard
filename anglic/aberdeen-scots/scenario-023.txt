Richt, sit yersel doon fer this ane, cause I'm nae letting ye aff wi jist "it wis affa bonnie", ye missed it in the flesh so ye're getting it aa noo, stert tae feenish, every bit.

I wis up afore sax, nae that I'd slept muckle onywye. Lay there in the dark jist thinkin, is this really happenin the day, and my stomach wis that tied up in knots I couldnae hae ett a thing even if somebody'd pit a fry doon in front o me. The mither cam ben wi a cup o tea I nivver drank and a bit toast I nivver touched either, jist crumbled it up on the plate tae look like I'd tried, so she widnae fash.

Then aboot hauf eight, wi the cars due at nine, we realised naebody hid the rings. The best man wis suppose tae hae them fae the nicht afore and he wisnae answerin his phone, and I'm staunin there in ma slip wi ma hair hauf done screamin doon the stair, "far's the rings, has onybody seen the rings," and the hale hoose in an uproar, ma mither on the phone tae his mither, his mither on the phone tae him, and turns oot he'd left them at his ain hoose in his ither jaiket pooch. His brither hid tae fly ower and back for them while we aa stood there wi the photographer due ony minute jist prayin he'd mak it.

Onywye, we got tae the place and, my friend, I hidnae seen baith sides thegither in the ae room afore that meenit, nae aince in my hale life, and it wis affa strange seein them aa mixed up, his fowk and mine, staunin in the ae hall wi naebody kennin fit tae say tae wha. But it settled quick eneuch, fowk aye find their fit, and afore lang aabody wis blethern awa like they'd kent ilk ither for years.

His mither wis first tae greet, richt afore I even got doon the aisle, jist seen me comin in the door and awa she went, and that set aff my ain mither and then hauf the front row, so I wis walkin doon tae a rake o sniffin and hankies afore I'd even reached him. He wis grinnin like a gype tryin nae tae greet himsel, the big chiel staunin there aa done up and shakin a bit.

I lost the line in the vows, richt in the middle, jist blanked, stood there wi ma mou open and naething comin oot, and the meenister jist waited, nae bother tae him, and it cam back tae me aa at aince and I got through it, a bit shaky at the end. And when it cam tae "I will", that wis it, that wis the bit that sealed it, jist the twa words and a squeeze o the hawn, and I mind thinkin, richt, that's us noo, nae gaun back on that.

We signed the papers efter, him and me, and a witness stood for each side, his best man for him, and my aulder sister for me, the baith o them signin their names doon aside oors, and it felt mair real than the vows in a wye, seein it aa doon in black and white like that.

Then the photos. Lord, the photos. We were staunin there for near on an oor while the photographer tried tae get every cluster o kin jist richt, "richt, aa the aunties noo, naw nae you, jist the aunties," and somebody's bairn greetin, and my faither's brither wanderin aff every five meenits for a smoke, and the photographer gettin mair and mair fashed tryin tae herd fowk that jist widnae bide put. The wee quinie fae his side that wis suppose tae be throwin petals wis mair interested in chasin the photographer's bag, and naebody could get her tae staun still either.

At the meal there wis a bit o a stramash wi the seatin, turned oot they'd pit ma mither's cousin richt next tae his auntie, the twa o them that hidnae spoken in years ower some auld faimly fitba fauling-oot, and ye could feel the chill aff that end o the table fae richt across the room. Naebody said naething, jist smiled thin smiles at ilk ither aa nicht, though there wis plenty claik aboot it fae the ithers efter.

The starters were awa in nae time, fowk fair fell on them, but there wis a fish course that naebody touched at aa, sat there gaun cauld on ilka plate, and the caterers must hae thocht we were affa rude, but naebody hid the heart tae say fit wis wrang wi it.

The best man's speech, he couldnae get through it, near enough greetin by the end, hid tae stop twice and tak a drink o watter, and the hale room jist sat there rootin for him tae mak it tae the end. My faither's wisnae much better either, tae be honest, his voice gaun aa ower the place and him haein tae stop and clear his throat mair than aince.

Then the dancin, my friend, ye ken fine wha I mean, there's aye ane, and this time it wis the youngest o his cousins, a wee loonie that widnae sit doon aa nicht, up on that flair fae the first sang tae the last, birlin aboot on his ain when naebody else wid dance wi him, and by the end he wis that trauchled he could hardly staun, but still widnae gie in and sit doon.

Somebody dragged oot that auld story again an aa, ye ken the ane, aboot the grandfaither and the coo that got intae the kirk hall, telt it tae the hale table like it wis the first time onybody'd heard it, though we've aa heard it a hunner times afore, and it got a richt lauch onywye, jist as gweed the twentieth time roond as the first.

And then, this is the bit I wisnae expectin, efter aa that, efter the hale day o it, the last guests awa hame near midnicht, and it wis jist him and me staunin there in a room fu o dirty glesses and hauf-ett cake and chairs aa ower the place, naebody left tae talk tae, and it wis that quiet efter aa the noise, ye could hear yer ain lug ringin. We jist stood there a meenit, nae sayin naething, gied ilk ither a bosie richt in the middle o aa the mess, and then he says tae me...

Him: fit like, wife.

Me: fit like yersel, husband.

, and we baith jist stood lauchin, tired oot the twa o us, ower tired tae even start clearin up, so we left it aa till the morn and gaed up tae oor bed, and that wis the day done. I'll tell ye, my friend, I wis that feart the mornin it wid aa gang vrang somewye, and it did an aa, a wee bittie here and a wee bittie there, but naebody minds it noo, it aa jist becam pairt o the story. It wis oor day, aa the mad rush and the greetin and the claik and aathing, and it wis affa, affa gweed.
