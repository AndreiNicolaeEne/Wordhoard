The washin wis oot on the line since the mornin, aa the guid sheets and the bairn's claes, and somebody had gaen and knockit the bleach ower richt anent it, splashed up the front o the best towels. The mother came oot first, and the ithers efter her, and it wis the quinie sittin quiet on the step that got the finger pointed at her afore onythin else wis said.

The mother: Wis it you did this? Ye were richt there.

The quinie: Wisna me.

The mother: Ye were sittin there the hale time, dinna tell me ye saw nithin. The bleach disna walk itsel.

The quinie: Wisna me.

The eldest and her workmate stood ahin, the pair o them that had been chasin ane anither roon the yard nae ten meenit afore, and neither o them opened their moo, jist looked at their feet like the grun wis affa interestin aa o a sudden.

The mother: Ye'll no even say sorry?

The quinie: I sweir I'd nithin tae dae wi it.

She said it aince, flat, and didna say it again, jist stood there wi her face gie still, gie even, gien the mother nane o the greetin or the flustered look that wid hae made it aa worth the shoutin. The mother glowered at her a meenit langer, waitin on mair, but there wisna mair comin.

Naebody said sorry efter, nae the mother, nae the eldest, nae her workmate wi their een still doon at the grun. The quinie turned and walked back intae the hoose, and the denial sat there ahin her, jist as flat as when she'd said it, wi naebody fashed tae shift it.
