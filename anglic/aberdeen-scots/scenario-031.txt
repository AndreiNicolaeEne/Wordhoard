Fit kind o meal wid I pick gin I got jist the one, ye ask? It's no a special-day meal, it's the meal for efter ye've been oot in the cauld aa day, wind comin aff the water and yer hands affa nippit raw. That's fan ye need it maist, that's the mood that caas for it.

The dish that stands oot is the skink, fish, tatties, ingans, aa intil the ae pot wi milk poured ower. It has tae be gweed an het, near burn-yer-moo het, and the fish has tae flake awa fae the bone at the first touch o the spoon, no a meenit langer on the heat or it's spylt athegither.

Ye'll hae had soup fae a tin that warmed ye through on a coorse day, this beats that a hale country mile. That isna warmth ye're borrowin, this is warmth that's yours athegither.

The smell o it reaches ye afore ye're even ower the doorstep, up the road and in aneath the door, that smoky fish smell that tells ye far ye are athoot lookin up.

Ye eat it wi a spoon and a hunk o breid for dippin, naebody rushin, naebody feart tae tak a second bowlie.

It's shared amang five or sax roond the table, nae mair, ony mair and it disna gyang roond richt.
