Fit like. Sit yersel doon a meenit, I'll tell ye fit gaed on at wir table the day, for it wis a richt stushie afore it wis feenished, though it startit fae naethin at aa.

We'd been roon that table a gweed while afore onything happened, mind, the hale efternoon intae it near eneuch. The soup wis lang by, the mince and tatties and neeps aa cleared awa, and we were ontae the puddin and a fly cup, aabody fu and easy in their chair, jaikets aff, belts loosed a notch, the wye ye get fan a denner's gaen on that lang. The wee loon and the quinie were doon the ither end, quiet eneuch, kennin fine tae bide oot o the wye o the grown fowk's spik. Aabody else lauchin at this and that, the aul stories comin roon again like they aye dae, and naebody thinkin twice aboot onything.

Noo there's jist the een thing at that table that naebody raises. Naebody ever said it oot loud that it wisna tae be spoken o, there's nae rule pinned up onywye for onybody tae read, bit aabody's felt fit happens gin it comes up, and ower the years we've aa jist learnt tae gyang roon it, quiet like, the wye ye step roon a hole in the road ye ken fine's there athoot needin tae look doon at it. There's claik aboot it awa fae the table, aye, plenty, but nivver at it.

Weel, it startit the wye it aye starts. Somebody said something in passin that wisna even meant tae stir onything, jist a wee comment, sideyways, hardly a full sentence, the kind o thing ye'd come oot wi athoot thinkin twice. Bit the meenit it wis oot, ye could feel the hale table gyan quiet for a second, aabody kennin exactly fit wis comin afore a word o argument had actually been said. The forks stoppin. Somebody's een gyan doon tae their plate.

The aunt couldna let it pass. Mild eneuch at first, jist testin the grund, like.

The aunt: Weel, I'm no sure that's richt, mind.

The uncle wisna haein that ava.

The uncle: Aye it is. Fit are ye on aboot.

And fae there the voices startit risin richt eneuch, ower the tap o each ither, the wye it aye dis.

The uncle: Ye dinna even ken fit yer spikkin aboot, tae be honest wi ye. Ye nivver hiv.

The aunt: I ken fine fit I'm spikkin aboot. Dinna you tell me I dinna ken.

Naebody backin doon an inch, the twa o them, and the rest o the table sittin there like steen, sayin naethin, waitin tae see fit wid come next.

The granny tried tae turn it, reachin for onything else tae spik on.

The granny: Dinna fash yersels, the pair o ye. Fit aboot the hairst this year, eh? They were sayin on the wireless it's been an affa dry ane. Mind fan we were aa at the skweel thegither, we'd nivver hae guessed...

Bit it didna work. The argument jist pulled richt back ower it, like a burn fowin back intae its ain channel, naebody even answerin the granny, the twa o them still at it, hetter than afore.

Syne the aul grievance cam up, the ane fae the last time this same thing got raised, years back noo, bit naebody's forgotten it, naebody's ever gonna forget it.

The uncle: Aye, and ye said the same vrang thing that time an aa, mind, at yer ain table, and look fit happened there.

The aunt: Dinna you bring that up. That's got naethin tae dee wi this.

Proof tae the baith o them, ye could see it richt across their faces, that this has aye happened afore and'll aye happen again, same twa fowk, same words near eneuch.

The father tried the ither wye, invokin the rule athoot needin tae say it wis a rule.

The father: We said we widna spik aboot this at the table. We aa agreed on that, did we no.

Bit that didna work aither. The argument gaed richt ower the tap o him, like he hidna said onything at aa, like he wisna even there.

The eldest pushed back fae the table then, chair scrapin lang and loud on the flair, no leavin the room, mind, jist puttin distance atween hersel and the rest o them, staunin ower by the windae wi her airms fauldit, lookin oot at naethin in particular, the meen jist comin up ower the roofs ootside.

And syne it gaed quiet. No the same quiet as afore, this wis a different kind athegither, an uncomfortable ane, and ye could hear the knives and forks ower loud in it, clatterin aff the plates, naebody sayin onything, aabody jist eatin their puddin like it wis a chore they hidna asked for. The aul mannie at the tap o the table jist shook his heid, slow, and said naethin either.

It wis a fair meenit or twa afore onybody tried tae smooth it ower. The mother said something aboot the puddin, that it wis a bittie ower sweet the day, or aboot some ither thing athegither, a bit o news fae doon the road, somebody's loon startin a new job, naethin tae dee wi onything. It worked, kind o, half wye. Fowk startit spikkin again, aboot ither things, bit the table nivver quite got back tae fit it hid been afore, that easy, guard-doon feelin fae the start o the meal. Ye could feel it hingin aboot the rest o the nicht, jist ablow the surface, like a stane in yer sheen ye canna get oot.

And that's the wye it feenished, mair or less. Naebody said sorry, no oot loud onywye. The eldest cam back tae the table efter a bit, and somebody gied her a bosie on the wye past her chair, and that wis that. We aa jist quietly agreed, athoot a single word said aboot it, the wye we aye dee, nivver tae bring it up again at that table.

Bit ye ken, and I ken, and aabody roon that table kens it fine weel forbye, that it'll aa happen ower again some ither day, aiblins the morn, aiblins no for months, bit some ither Sunday, some ither denner, somebody'll say something sideyways athoot thinkin, and the hale thing'll stairt up jist the same as it aye dis.
