A'll tell ye this ane the wye it happened, nae mair and nae less, for it's nae a story that deserves ony bigger a tellin, and ye sittin there nae sayin a word, jist takkin it in the wye ye aye dae, so A'll gie it tae ye straicht.

It wis a lang road A wis on, doon past the neep fields, near hairst time gin ye go by the state o them, on ma wye tae see the folk. There'd been a fash on for a puckle o days afore that, somethin tae dae wi trouble doon by the harbour, fowk sayin there'd been thievery and warse, and the polis wintin tae ken fa wis comin and fa wis gyan on that stretch. So they'd pit a checkpoint on, richt at the tap o the brae far the road narras doon tae the ae lane, twa o them in high-viz wi a caur pu'd in ahint them, and aabody hid tae stop and be seen, nae exceptions made for onybody.

Bi the time A got tae it there wis a queue already stretchin back roond the bend, cars nose tae tail, engines aff and on again, and A sat there a gweed half oor afore A wis onywye near the front o't. Plenty time tae think, and plenty time for the stamack tae gyang ticht, and A'll nae deny A wis feart, a wee bit, though A hidna deen a thing wrang in ma life, or nane that mattered onywye. Ye sit there gyan ower fit ye're gyan tae say fan yer turn comes, even though it's aa true, jist in case yer tongue lets ye doon at the wrang meenit, or ye stammer ower somethin simple and it looks like mair than it is.

Fan A got up tae the front there wis a mannie there, nae unfriendly, jist doin his joab the wye onybody wid. A hannit ower ma papers throwe the windae afore he'd even asked for them.

The one at the desk: Far ye headed the day, then?

Me: Doon tae see the folk.

That ane cam oot easy, plain and quick, for it wis the truth and there wis naethin tae dredge up for it. Syne he asked the ither een.

The one at the desk: And fit wis yer business up this wye afore the day?

That een took mair care. A hid tae haud a meenit, pit it richt in ma heid afore it cam oot ma moo, for it wisna a straightforward answer the wye the first een wis, and A didna wint tae soond like A wis hidin onything, even though A wisna, nae a scrap.

Me: A wis up seein ma brither, he's nae been weel. A wis up a day or twa.

He nodded, didna say muckle tae that, and took the papers back intae the hut wi him. A sat there wi the beat o silence gyan on ower me, the windae doon, the engine tickin ower, ma hands sittin on the wheel doin naethin, and it wis in that silence A minded, aa in a rush, that A'd forgotten tae pit doon far A wis bidin the nicht afore. A hid tae lean oot and gie it tae him richt there at the windae, feelin like a loon that hidna deen his hamework and got caught oot at it.

Afore me in the queue there'd been a chiel, aulder, in a caur that hid seen better days, and they'd held him back langer than the lave o's, twa o them staunin roond his windae, leafin throwe his papers ower and ower again, ane o them awa tae the hut and back twice. Naebody said a word aboot it oot loud, nae claik passed up and doon the queue the wye ye micht expect, but ye couldna help but glance, and syne A kept ma een straicht on ma ain steerin wheel efter that, for it wisna ma business and A didna wint tae draw ony attention masel bi lookin ower lang.

A hid tae work at it, keepin ma face still and ma voice even, for the wye yer voice gings fan ye're nervous is a gie-awa quicker than onything else, and A didna wint him thinkin there wis onything tae fin that wisna there. A jist answered fit A wis asked and nae mair, and smiled fan it seemed richt tae smile, and held masel the best A could.

The mannie wis decent aboot it, mind. Fan A telt him aboot ma brither he softened a bit, jist for a meenit, and said he hoped he wis daein better, and that wis mair kindness than A'd looked for, a wee thing in amang aa the officialdom o it, but it steadied me a bit tae hear it.

Efter fit felt a gweed while, but wisna likely mair than a couple o meenits aa telt, he cam back roond and hannit ma papers throwe the windae, and jist said, "Aye, on ye go then," and gied a wee tap on the roof o the caur wi the flat o his haun, and that wis it, A wis throwe, nae mair asked o me.

A drove on slow, keepin masel richt, nae wintin tae look like A wis in ony hurry tae be awa, though every pairt o me wintit tae pit the fit doon and nae stop till A wis miles clear. It wisna till A wis a gweed twenty meenits doon the road, past the last o the checkpoint signs and intae the ordinary fields again, that A felt the ticht knot in ma chist let go, aa at ance, like a braith A'd been haudin the hale time athoot even kennin it. Ma hands wis shakin a bit on the wheel efter that, and A hid tae pull in for a meenit at a lay-by jist tae settle masel afore A wis fit tae drive on proper.

That's fit it cost me, A suppose, though naebody wid ken tae look at me, and it's fit A dinna spik aboot tae aabody fa asks aboot the journey. A tell it quiet, and A tell it plain, for it wisna a big thing that happened tae me, jist a haaf oor at a checkpoint and a puckle o questions answered richt, and a mannie fa wis decent aboot it. But it wisna naethin either, for A felt it aa the wye doon in ma bones efter, and A widna wint tae mak it intae mair than it wis, nor less, for baith wid be a kind o lie, and this ane A'd raither jist tell straicht, the wye it wis.
