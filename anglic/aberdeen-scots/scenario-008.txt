The loon: Ma, can I nae get the bike? The big een fae the shop, wi the gears an aa?

The mother: Nae the noo, ma loon. We canna afford it, an that's the plain truth o't - we're affa short the noo, wi the bills an aa.

The loon: I'll dae the messages ilka day for ye, tak the bins oot an aa, gie ye ma pocket money forbye.

The mother: It's nae aboot chores, loonie. It's the price o the thing, it's affa dear. Even wi aa yer messages we widna fun that up ony quicker.

The loon: Fit aboot come ma birthday then, could I get it come then?

The mother: Gin yer faither gets on aifter the hairst, aye, we'll see come yer birthday. Bit dinna coont on't, mind, I'm promisin nithing the noo.

The loon: Bit yon chiel that bides doon the road's got een already! An his mam's nae even workin!

The mother: Fit he's got in his hoose has nae a thing tae dae wi fit we've got in oors, ma loon. It's no vrang o him tae hae een, bit ilka hoose has its ain purse tae mind, an oors is thin the noo.

The loon: Aabody's got een bit me, seems like.

The mother: Dinna fash yersel aboot aabody, an dinna be feart we'll sort ye oot. Gweed kens I'd gie it ye the morn if I hid the money, bit I dinna. Tell ye fit though - I'll get the auld bike doon fae the shed, get the mannie roon the corner tae look the chain ower an pit some air in the tyres. Ye can hae it tae play wi aifter skweel. It's nae the same, I ken that fine, bit it'll dae ye the noo.

The loon: ...Aye, awright then. Better nor nithing, I suppose. Can I get a bosie onywye?

The mother: Aye, come here, ye daft loon.
