Me: Fit like, the lot o ye - wifies an mannies thegither - sit in aboot, an I'll tell ye't aa, plain, the wey it wis, so ye hae it the same as I got it.

I wis sittin aside her, jist the twa o us, nae claik, nae fuss, an the furst thing she said wisnae aboot deein ava. She wis lookin at the windae.

The mother: Fit wye's it aa sae quiet oot? Will it be fine the morn, div ye think?

Me: Naethin mair than that, jist easin hersel intae the room. Syne her voice gaed douce an clear, nae swither in it ava.

The mother: The steen on yer father's grave's gyaan squint. Get that seen tae afore the hairst's oot, will ye, mind noo.

Me: Clear as onything, nae mistakin her meanin - she'd aye sorted aathing hersel, near eneuch, an it wisna like her tae leave a thing adae. An efter that she said the word she'd aye caaed me since I wis a bairn liftin neeps in the park wi her.

The mother: Ma quinie.

Me: Jist the once mair, the same as aye. Syne she askit me somethin I nivver looked for fae her, no her, no then.

The mother: Are ye feart?

Me: An I telt her the truth, as honest as I could get it oot. Aye, I said, affa feart, ye ken, bit I'm here, an I'm no gyaan awa. She gaed a wee smile at that, an the last hale sentence she got oot wis,

The mother: Dinna fash yersel, quinie, there's naethin vrang in greetin.

Me: I hae keepit that hale an haill iver since, ivry word o't. Efter that there wis nae mair spik in her ava, jist the quiet comin doon like a claith ower the room, the meen risin ahin the curtains, her haun in mine, an me haudin on tae it, haudin on richt till her een closed an she drifted awa fae me. It wis a gweed wey tae gyang, I think, quiet like that. That's it aa noo, the same as I got it. Come here, the lot o ye - you an aa, chiel - an gie's a bosie.
