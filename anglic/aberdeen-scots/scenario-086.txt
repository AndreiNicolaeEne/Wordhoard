Richt, ye wisna there sae A better tell ye the hale sorry business, or fit A can mind o it onywye, cause a gweed bit o the nicht's awa intae the dark noo.

It wis ma sister's engagement pairty, doon at her hoose, and she'd been on at me for a fortnicht, "ye're comin, nae excuses," sae A gaed. And ye ken fit she's like, she'll no let ye sit wi an empty plate for two meenits thegither.

The sister: fit like, are ye no eatin? Get anither helpin intae ye, there's plenty.

Me: A'm fine, A've had eneuch noo.

The sister: awa, ye've had naethin. Here.

And that wis me awa, aye, ae mair helpin, syne anither ane efter that, and A widna say no cause it wis aa affa gweed and she'd been at it aa day, the hale table happit wi food, and it wid hae been vrang tae leave half o it. Somebody had a bottle gaun roond an aa, and A wisna gaun tae be the ane sittin there wi a glass o juice while aabody else wis haein a rerr time. Sae A had a dram, syne anither, and A wisna coontin efter a fylie, tae be honest wi ye.

There wis music on, and the eldest got up dancin first, and then it wis jist aabody, the hale front room reeked wi it, and A mind laughin that sair ma face wis sair wi it. A telt masel A wid bide anither hauf oor and then awa hame, and A mind sayin that mair than aince, and ilka time A said it A wis still there an oor later, still on the flair.

The last thing A can properly mind is standin in the kitchen wi a plate in ma haund, spikkin tae somebody's man aboot naethin in particular, and lauchin affa hard at somethin that wisna even that funny. Efter that it's aa jist bits, flashes, nae the hale picter. A mind bein on the flair dancin wi ma airms up. A mind somebody sayin they'd cry a taxi. A mind singin, God help me, A mind singin. But fit order it aa gaed in, A couldna tell ye.

And syne it wis the morn, and A wis waukened by ma ain heid poundin like somebody wis chappin at it fae the inside, and ma stamack turnin ower jist thinkin on fit A'd eaten, never mind onythin mair. The licht through the curtains wis like a knife, honest tae God, and ilka soond in the hoose, the kettle, a door, somebody's fitsteps on the stair, wis three times ower loud. Jist sittin up in the bed took me a gweed five meenits o wark, A'm no jokin.

Somebody else in the hoose wis up afore me, gaun aboot like it wis a normal mornin, which A couldna understaun for the life o me, and o coorse they widna leave me be aboot it.

The brither: weel weel, look fa's surfaced. Ye're lookin affa peely-wally this mornin.

Me: dinna.

The brither: fit time did ye get tae yer bed, ken? Ye were still on the flair when A gaed up.

Me: A dinna mind, and A dinna want tae mind, sae jist leave it.

The brither: (laughin) ye were tellin the same story three times ower tae the same chiel. He wis affa polite aboot it an aa.

Me: fash aff.

He wisna bein cruel wi it, jist the usual teasin, but honest tae God A could hae greeted at him for it, ma heid wis that sair. A lay there efter he'd gaen and A made masel a promise, richt there, flat on ma back, starin at the ceilin, that A wid never let a nicht rin awa fae me like that again. And even as A wis sayin it tae masel A kent fine it wisna the first time A'd sworn the same thing, and it likely widna be the last either, but ye still say it, dae ye no, lyin there feelin like death.

A didna manage a walk till weel intae the efternoon. A jist bein up and washed felt like a hale day's wark. Ma mither had said she wanted a hand wi some messages, jist a wee thing, doon tae the shop and back, and A telt her A wid dae it and then couldna face it for oors, jist lay on the settee wi a blanket ower me, no even wantin tae watch onythin, jist wantin the world tae be quiet.

Aboot midday A managed a bit o dry toast, naethin mair than that, ma stamack widna hae taen onythin richer, and A sat there nibblin at it like it wis a chore in itself. Nae tea even, jist watter, sippin awa at it aa mornin. And A jist sat, didna spik much, didna want tae, jist waited on it passin, which it dis, gey slow, but it dis.

And here's the ither thing, the ane that's been sittin wi me since. Somewhere in aa the bits A can mind, there's a flash o me sayin somethin tae the sister's intended, aboot his job or his family, A canna even mind the exact words, but A mind the look on his face efter A'd said it, and A mind the sister giein me a look an aa. A've turned it ower and ower in ma heid since, tryin tae mind fit exactly A said, and A still canna piece it thegither richt, but A ken fine A owe the chiel an apology for it, whitever it wis. A'll hae tae say somethin tae him, "sorry for whitever A said, A wisna masel," and hope he taks it the richt wye, cause it's been sittin on me like a steen aa week.

By the evenin A wis mysel again, or near eneuch, could face a proper denner and think straucht for the first time aa day, and A sat wi the family and lauched aboot it a bit, the state o me, cause by then it's easier tae lauch than greet ower it.

But A'll tell ye this an aa, cause A'm no gaun tae lee tae ye aboot it: A ken fine A'll dae the same thing again some day. No on purpose, no plannin it, but there'll be anither pairty, anither nicht wi aabody in gweed spirits and the bottle gaun roond, and A'll say aye tae ae mair, and syne anither, and A'll wauken the morn efter wi the same sair heid and the same daft promise tae masel that A ken fine A've broken afore. That's jist the wye o it, A think. Ye ken yersel.

Onywye, that's the hale sorry tale o it. A'm affa glad ye werena there tae see the state o me, though A suppose ye'd hae had a gweed lauch at it aa.
