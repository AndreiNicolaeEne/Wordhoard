The neighbour: Ye ken fit happened tae the mannie's loon doon the yard? Affa carry-on, so it wis. He wis fechtin wi anither chiel ower the shift rota, gied him a richt doin, and they sacked him on the spot, nae warnin, nae naethin, jist oot the door wi his bag.

Me: Haud on, haud on. Gweed kens fit ye heard, but that's no fit happened. No even close.

The neighbour: Fit dae ye mean, no fit happened? That's fit I heard, richt enough.

Me: Weel ye heard wrang. I ken cause I wis there mysel, sittin haein ma piece. There wis nae fechtin at aa. Twa chiels got intae a bit o an argy-bargy ower the rota, richt enough, raised voices and aa that, but naebody laid a haund on onybody, no even a shove. And he didna get the sack. He haundit in his notice himsel, the Friday afore, cause he'd a better job lined up ower at the ither yard. Twa separate things athegither, and somebody's gaen and stitched them thegither intae the ae story, and made it a hantle worse in the tellin.

The neighbour: Is that richt, though? Cause that's no fit I wis telt at aa.

Me: Naw, it's no. By the time it got tae you it wisna even the same tale ony mair. That's jist claik gettin bigger as it gaes.

The neighbour: Fit wye, though? Wha telt ye that, I mean wha telt me. I got it fae the wifie at the coonter in the shop.

Me: And far did she get it fae?

The neighbour: She said her man heard it fae een o the chiels that works there.

Me: Aye, weel, there's yer answer. Somebody along that road's embellished it gey nicely, and it's gaen fae an argument tae a sackin by the time it reached you. Fae noo on, if aabody asks ye, ye tell them the true wye o it. He left o his ain accord, nae fechtin, nae sack, naething mair tae it.
