Me: Fit like?

The friend: No bad, no bad, foo's yersel? Affa cauld the day, eh.

Me: Aye, richt Baltic. I hid tae pit the heid doon aa the wye up fae the shop.

The friend: Ken that fine. Here, did ye hear the claik aboot the wifie doon the stair? Her loon's been suspendit fae the skweel again.

Me: Na, fit's he deen this time?

The friend: Same as aye, cheekin the teacher. She's affa fed up wi him, says he'll be the daith o her.

Me: Some loonie that een. Onywye, fit are ye up tae the morn, ony plans?

The friend: No really, jist the hoose and the messages. Fit like wi you?

Me: Ach, nae much. Listen, eh... this is a bittie awkward tae say, but... could ye see yer wye tae len us a hunner pound?

The friend: A hunner pound. Fit's it for, if ye dinna mind me askin?

Me: Ach, jist tichter than I'd like this month, that's aa it is. The rent's due and I'm a bittie short, naethin tae fash yersel aboot, it'll sort itsel.

The friend: Richt. Aye, I can dae that for ye, but ye pey us back by the end o the month, mind, nae like the last time.

Me: Aye aye, I promise, ye'll hae it back in yer hand by the Friday, soon as I'm peyed, on ma life.

The friend: Mind ye dae an aa, or I'll be roond yer hoose wi a neep tae yer heid.

Me: (laughin) Thank ye, honestly, ye've nae idea fit this means, ye're a richt gweed pal, I widna ken fit tae dae withoot ye, ye're the best chiel I ken.

The friend: Aye aye, dinna greet aboot it, jist mind and gie's it back afore I come skelp ye masel.
