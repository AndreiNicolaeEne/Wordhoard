The relative: Fit like? I heard ye were nae weel, fit's gaun on?

The sick one: Ach, nae great tae be honest wi ye. Started three days back noo, jist an ache in aa the joints and a scratchy throat wi it, and syne yesterday a fever cam on tap o that an aa.

The relative: A fever an aa? That's nae bonnie ava. Foo are ye the day, ony better?

The sick one: A bittie easier than yesterday, I'll gie it that, but still far fae richt. Heid's foggy and I'm affa sweemy on ma feet yet.

The relative: Fit hiv ye been deein aboot it?

The sick one: Weel, first aff, warm saut watter, gargled for the throat, that ay taks the edge aff it. The fever widna brakk on its ain sae I laid a cool cloot ower ma forehead, changed it through the nicht every time it got warm again. Kept tae broth and weak tea, naething mair, and sleepit whenever the body wid let me.

The relative: Weel ye maun rest properly noo, dinna push on through it like ye aye dae.

The sick one: Aye, I ken fine, dinna fash yersel. I'm gaun naewhere the day. But I'll tell ye this - if the fever climbs ony further, or ma braith gets ticht on me, I'll awa doon and get seen tae. Nae afore that though.

The relative: Fair eneuch. Div ye want me tae bring ower some fae for ye, or come sit wi ye for an oor?

The sick one: Ach, ye needna dae that tae yersel... but aye, mibbe for a wee while, if ye're nae aathegither thrang yer ainsel.

The relative: Nae bather ava. I'll come ower efter and we'll spik then.

The sick one: Ta, that's affa kind o ye. I'll be aaright, jist needin the time o it, that's aa.
