My friend: Fit like? Ah'm nae here tae claik, bit ah heard ye through the wall Setterday nicht, the pair o ye, thocht the hale close wis comin doon on tap o us.

Me: Aye, ah ken. Ah'm affa shamed aboot that noo, tae be honest wi ye. Ye probably heard mair than ye ivver wanted.

My friend: Weel come on then, oot wi it. Fit wis aa that aboot?

Me: Ye'll nae believe foo saft it startit. It wis the bins. Jist the bins, that's aa it wis at the beginnin. Ah'd asked him twice tae pit them oot, the Tuesday and then again the Wednesday mornin afore he gaed tae his work, and baith times he said aye aye, ah'll dae it the nicht, and baith times he forgot, and by the Thursday the hale kitchen wis mockit and stinkin and naebody had touched them. So ah lost the heid a bit.

My friend: That's aa? Ower the bins?

Me: Weel it wisna jist the bins by the time ah wis feenished. Ah'll be honest wi ye, ah raised ma vice first. Ah come in fae ma work and seen the bags still sittin there and ah jist stertit, "Fit is it wi you, div ye nae hear a wird ah say tae ye?" Nae quiet aboot it either, ah wis fair roarin.

My friend: And him?

Me: Oh he gied it back, nae bother. Same vice, same volume. "Ah'm nae yer skivvy," he says, "ye ken ah'm run aff ma feet at the work aa week, gie's a meenit tae draw breath afore ye're on at me." And that wis it, aff we went, the pair o us, back and fore, the vices gettin louder every time.

My friend: Fit got said that ye didna mean?

Me: Ah said, in the heat o it, ah said ah should nivir hae muived in wi him ava. Jist like that, straight oot, "ah should nivir hae come hame tae this." That wisna even true, ah didna mean a wird o it, bit it come oot afore ah could stap it, and ah seen his face when ah said it, like ah'd skelped him.

My friend: That's a affa thing tae say.

Me: It is, and ah kent it the meenit it left ma mooth. Bit he wisna feart tae answer, he threw something back an aa. He brocht up the money, the auld sore point, ye ken the yin, aboot the time his brither borrowed the twa hunner pound aff us and it's nivir been seen since. Ah'd said tae leave that alane months syne, that's a subject we dinna touch, and there he wis flingin it back at me, "aye weel at least ah dinna throw ma brither's business up in yer face every ither week," which wisna even fair since it wis me that brocht it up in the first place, nae him.

My friend: Sae fit happened then?

Me: He gaed through tae the bedroom and shut the door that hard the press on the waa shoogled, ah heard the cups rattlin in it fae the ither room. Nae broken naething, thank the Lord, bit it gied the hale flat a fricht, ah'm sure. And efter that, naething. He didna answer me, ah didna answer him, we jist sat, him ben the bedroom, me in the livin room, the hale rest o that nicht and the maist o the next day forbye, nae a wird atween us. Ah wis sleepin on the settee, he wis through the waa, and it wis affa quiet in that flat, quiet like ye widna believe.

My friend: A hale day?

Me: The better pairt o een, aye. Ah wis stubborn and sae wis he, the pair o us jist bidin in oor ain rooms like twa strangers. Bit it wis him that muived first, ah'll gie him that, he's aye been the yin tae crack first oot the twa o us. It wis the Friday nicht, he come through tae the livin room and stood there a meenit, and then he says, quiet like, "Div ye want a cup o tea?" That wis it, that wis aa he said, bit ah kent fine fit he meant by it.

My friend: And fit did you say?

Me: Ah said aye, ah'd tak a cup o tea. And he made it and sat doon aside me, and efter a meenit he says, "Ah'm affa sorry aboot yer brither, aboot the money, that wisna fair o me tae throw that back at ye when ye're jist tryin tae help yer ain fowk, ah should nivir hae said it." And that wis a real apology, ye ken, nae jist a "sorry" flung oot tae get it ower wi, he meant it.

My friend: And you?

Me: Ah telt him ah wis sorry an aa, sorry for sayin ah should nivir hae muived in wi him, that wis the wrangest thing ah could hae said and it wisna true, nae even a bit o it, ah dinna ken fit made me say it. And he jist nodded and said he kent it wisna true, bit it still hurt tae hear.

My friend: So foo did ye ken it wis richt atween ye again?

Me: He cried me quinie. He hidna cried me that since we first got thegither, years back, and it jist come oot o him, quiet, "c'mere quinie," and ah kent then, richt awa, that we were mendin. It wis the first thing that made me think aye, this is aa richt again.

My friend: Fit sealed it then?

Me: He gied me a muckle bosie richt there on the settee, held on tae me a lang while, nae sayin naething mair, jist the twa o us sittin like that. That wis it, that wis the makkin up richt there, better than ony amount o talkin.

My friend: Weel ah'm gled tae hear it, bit ah'm tellin ye, ah'm gettin ma hearin tested efter Setterday.

Me: (laughin) Aye, ye can chap the waa next time gin we get ower loud, we'll ken tae haud oor vices doon. Bit we're gweed noo, honestly, better than we've been in a while, tae tell ye the truth. Jist dinna be repeatin it tae aabody roond the toon, mind.

My friend: Ma lips are sealed. Weel, maistly.
