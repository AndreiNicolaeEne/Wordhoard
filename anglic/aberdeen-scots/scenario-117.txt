The rival: Nae bad. Sittin here wi the board up on the phone again, gaun ower yon gam fae Tuesday. Canna get it oot ma heid.

Me: Aye, me neither. Affa ane, that.

The rival: It wis the opening did it, mind. Ye played yon steady meevie, aa quiet like, an it gied me the hale board tae work wi. If ye'd came oot sharper we widna even be sittin here argyin, cause there widna hiv been a gam tae argy ower.

Me: Havers. I ken fine fit I wis daein. Ye canna tell me a gam's lost afore the third meeve. That's jist you back-seatin it noo cause ye ken hoo it went.

The rival: I'm no back-seatin naethin, I'm tellin ye fit I saw at the time an aa. I said it tae ye masel efter, mind, walkin oot.

Me: Ye said naethin o the sort, ye said "gweed gam" an that wis it.

The rival: Aye weel, there's mair than ae wye tae say a thing.

Me: Onywye. Early on we were baith fine, dinna fash yersel ower that bit. It wis level richt through, ye push here, I push there, naethin in it.

The rival: Level, aye. I'll gie ye that muckle.

Me: But then we get tae the middle o the thing, roon aboot meevie twinty-fower, twinty-five, an that's far it turns.

The rival: That's a plain mistake, that meevie. Naethin complicated aboot it, ye jist gied it awa. I dinna even ken fit ye were thinkin.

Me: It wisna a mistake, it wis richt for fit I kent at the time. I hidna seen yer answer, naebody wid hiv seen it, it wisna sittin there plain on the board. Ye canna cry a thing vrang jist cause o hoo it turned oot efter. That's the easiest thing in the warld tae dae, sittin here the noo wi aa the answers in front o ye.

The rival: It wis vrang the meenit ye played it, I jist didna say sae oot loud. I'm no cryin ye a chiel for it, I'm jist sayin fit it wis.

Me: Weel I'm sayin different, an I wis the ane sittin there.

The rival: Fine. Onywye, a bittie efter that we hid the swap, the big exchange, an that cost ye mair than the ither meevie ever did.

Me: That cost me cause o hoo you played it, no cause o onythin I did. Ye forced the thing, ye kent fine I'd hae tae tak.

The rival: I forced naethin, I gied ye a choice an ye took the vrang side o it. That's on you, no me.

Me: Aye, that's fit ye'd like tae think.

The rival: By that point the position wis lost. Naething mair tae it. Ye can coont it yersel, meevie thirty-echt, thirty-nine, it's deid.

Me: It wisna lost, it wis worse, aye, but no lost. There's a difference. I hid chances still.

The rival: Ye hid nae chances, ye hid a hope, an that's no the same thing.

Me: I hid a comeback in me an ye ken it, cause I nearly got there.

The rival: Aye, ye came at it, but ower late tae matter. By the time ye tried yon push it wis jist noise.

Me: It wisna too late, I wis a hair aff it. If I'd played the ither meevie there instead, the ane wi the rook comin roon, that saves it, or at least drags it oot langer than it went.

The rival: Ye didna play it though, did ye. Ye played fit ye played.

Me: I ken fit I played, I'm no arguin that bit. I'm sayin fit could hiv been.

The rival: Could hiv been disna win a gam.

Me: Naebody said it did.

The rival: An then the last meevie, that's the ane that decided it, an there's nae argyin ower fit it wis. Ye ken fit it wis.

Me: Aye, I ken fit it wis. I'm no disputin the meevie itsel, jist fit it means. You're actin like it proved somethin aboot the hale gam, an it didna, it jist ended it.

The rival: It ended it cause it wis the strongest thing on the board, that's fit it means.

Me: Sixty-ane meevies. That's fit it came tae, the hale thing, sixty-ane. That's ane thing we baith agree on onywye.

The rival: Aye, sixty-ane. That's aboot the only thing.

Me: At the time we jist shook hands. Ye said gweed gam, I said gweed gam, an that wis it, oot the door.

The rival: Aye, an noo we're sittin here an it's turned intae a hale different argument.

Me: Cause it wis a hale different gam than fit the ending made it look. I hid the stronger position the hale wye through, richt up tae near the end. You jist held on.

The rival: Held on? I wis in front fae the middle on, ye said it yersel, that meevie o yours "gied it awa". Ye canna hiv it baith wyes, first ye say ye made a mistake, then ye say ye hid the stronger gam aa alang.

Me: I'm sayin baith, cause baith's true. The mistake didna change fit wis really gaun on.

The rival: That's jist you no wantin tae say ye lost the fair wye.

The rival: I never said easy.

Me: Ye're actin like it noo.

The rival: Mind yon ither gam we hid, twa year back, the ane doon at the club? Same thing happened, you strong early, then it slips fae ye at the turn. That's a pattern, that, it's no jist the ane time.

Me: Dinna even start wi that. That gam's naethin tae dae wi this ane, twa completely different gams, twa different boards.

The rival: It's the same you daein the same thing.

Me: Away ye go. You're jist reachin noo cause ye ken fine this gam disna gie ye fit ye want on its ain.

The rival: I dinna need tae reach for onythin, I hiv the result sittin richt there.

Me: Aye, the result, fine, keep it. I'm no gien ye the rest o it though. I'm no sayin ye hid the better gam, an I'm no gaun tae.

The rival: An I'm no sayin yon meevie wisna a mistake, cause it wis.

Me: Weel we're baith stuck then.

The rival: Aye, we are that.

Me: Fit like a cuppie, seein as we're gettin naewye.

The rival: Aye, gie's a meenit. But I'm richt aboot the meevie.
