The first quinie landed in aboot fower, nae even a knock afore she wis in the door and doon on the chair, "Fit like?" oot her afore she'd her coat aff.

First friend: Dinna get me stertit. She's been affa short wi me aa week and I canna work oot fit I've deen tae her.

Me: Fit wye though? Fae the stert, fit happened?

First friend: We wis oot for wur messages Setterday and she jist stopped spikkin tae me in the shop. Stood there wi a face on her like thunner. I said naethin tae her, naethin ata.

Me: Naethin ata?

First friend: Naethin! She's jist affa touchy the noo, I dinna ken fit's got intae her.

Nae an oor efter the ither een's chappin at the door, face like a vratch that's been oot in the weet aa day.

Second friend: Ye'll no believe fit she's been sayin aboot me roon the toon.

Me: She wis in here hersel earlier, ye ken.

Second friend: Aye, I bet she wis. She started it, no me. Naggin on aboot ma chiel in front o aabody at the hairst dance, affa embarrassin that wis.

Me: She says it wis you stertit it, ower the messages on Setterday.

Second friend: That's a lie, she's the een aye stirrin the claik, never a wird o truth in her.

Me: Is this aboot the messages ata, or is it really aboot fit got said at the dance?

Second friend: ...Aye. Weel. Mebbe it wis mair the dance. I widna say I wis masel that nicht, richt enough, and she's nae far wrang aboot that.

Me: Richt. I think the twa o ye need tae spik yersels, nae me in the middle passin claik back and fore like a postie. I'll get ye baith thegither, the morn mebbe, and ye can say yer piece tae each ither, face tae face, and hae a bosie efter if it comes tae that.

Second friend: Aye... aye, awright. Better than aa this fashin on.
