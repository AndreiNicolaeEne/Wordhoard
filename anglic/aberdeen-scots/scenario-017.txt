The aunt: Fit like, quine. Noo Ah wis ower at the wifie doon the road the day, gettin the claik, and aabody's spikkin aboot the loon fae the fairm past the kirk, ye ken the een Ah mean. A richt gweed worker, up afore sax ilka mornin, nivver a day aff in his life. And temperament, patient as ye like, nae a crabbit bone in the chiel, ye widna hear a vrang word said aboot him. And the family ahint him, decent fowk, kirk-gaun, the mither a lovely wifie, the faither weel thocht o aa roon.

The niece: Aye, Auntie, Ah'm sure he's fine.

The aunt: Fine? Fit wye jist fine? Fit's vrang wi him, tell me that, a gweed worker, patient, decent stock ahint him, fit mair are ye wantin fae a chiel?

The niece: It's nae him, honest, Auntie. Ah'm jist affa thrang wi the work the noo, Ah dinna hae the time tae be thinkin on onythin else.

The aunt: Thrang, aye, we're aa thrang. Ye mind yer cousin, said the very same, thrang, nae ready, and her mither near grat wi the worry o it. Look at her noo, twa bairns and blissin the day she didna win that argument.

The niece: Ah ken, Auntie, and Ah'm nae sayin ye're vrang aboot him. It's jist nae the time for me the noo.

The aunt: Weel. Ah'll say nae mair the day. Ye dinna hae tae decide this meenit, bit dinna leave it aa the morn either. The offer bides, quine. Fan ye come tae yer senses ye ken far tae find me.
