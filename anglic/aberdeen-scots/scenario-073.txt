Me: Richt, sit yersel doon, cause ye're aboot tae get the hale story and ye're jist tae listen, aye? Ye can spik aa ye like efter, bit the noo it's me deein the tellin, so bide still and tak it in, cause this'll no come roon again.

It's near ten year noo since the hale thing stertit, believe it or no. It wis aboot hairst time, the year the fish hoose doon the harbour shut for the winter refit and half o's wis suddenly wi naethin tae dae o an evening. The eldest wis the een that pulled us aa thegither, mind ye, no me, fowk aye think it wis me cause I've been here langest, bit it wis her that phoned roon aabody she kent fae the work and said come oot for a pint, we're no jist sittin in the hoose greetin aboot bein idle. Half o us didna even ken the ither half afore that nicht. She's aye been like that, the eldest, canna bide tae see fowk sittin lonely, has tae have aabody roon the table. So that's far it aa cam fae, a wifie fae the fish hoose that widna let a guid crowd gang tae waste.

Noo there wis een amang us near didna stick. The quate een, she'd come alang that first nicht cause her cousin dragged her, and she sat in the corner the hale time barely sayin a word, and efter she telt the eldest straight oot she didna think this wis for her, she wisna the type tae fit in wi a crowd like that. Bit the eldest jist widna have it. Turned up at her door the next week wi a bottle and said we're gaun for chips, you're comin, nae argy-bargy. And that wis that. She's noo the een that never misses a single gatherin, rain or shine, and it's a fine thing tae see, cause she wid have missed the hale o this if somebody hadna gaen and fetched her.

There wis a falling-oot early on an aa, atween the peacekeeper and the een that's aye late noo, this wis afore either o them wis kent for fit they're kent for the day. It wis ower somethin daft, a borrowed jaicket that got ruint at a barbecue, the peacekeeper said it wis nae bather, the ither een said it wis vrang o her tae say that when she could see fine it wis bather, and it aa got affa heated for a fortnicht, neither een o them speakin tae the ither. It got mended the wye these things dae, somebody else jist forced them intae the same room and widna let them leave till they'd sortit it. Bit oot o that we got the joke that's still gaun the day, onytime onybody's late, somebody says "aye, weel, at least it's no yer jaicket," and it maks nae sense tae onybody new bit it aye gets a laugh oot o the rest o us, cause we ken far it cam fae.

We lost een fae the group efter a while an aa. He said at the time he wis needin tae step back, that the group had got tae be mair than he could keep up wi, work and aathing else, and naebody pushed him on it, cause fit else can ye dae. We still spik o him noo and again, wonder foo he's deein, bit he's no been at a single thing since, and that's jist foo it gings sometimes.

Efter that, the peacekeeper's cousin cam intae things, vouched for straight aff, cause fowk trust the peacekeeper's judgement, bit even so she wisna jist waved in the door. She wis tested a fair while, if I'm bein honest wi ye, naebody said as much bit ye could feel it, fowk watchin tae see if she'd stick, if she'd turn up when it mattered and no jist when it suited her. It took the best pairt o a year afore onybody stopped thinkin o her as "the cousin" and stertit thinkin o her as jist een o us. Noo naebody would ken the difference.

The thing that really bonded the rest o us thegither, though, wis the bothy trip. We'd gaen up intae the hills for a weekend, jist a daft idea somebody had, and the weather closed in on us and we wis stuck twa full days wi nae signal and a stove that widna licht richt and eneuch tinned soup tae feed hauf the toon. We wis feart for a bit, if I'm honest, bit we cam oot the ither side o it laughin, and there's nae quarrel that survives twa days stuck thegither in a bothy wi naethin bit each ither for company. That trip did mair for this group than onythin afore or since.

There's een we still feel the absence o, mind ye. Moved doon tae England for the work a few year back, and we spik on the phone noo and again, bit it's no the same as haein somebody sit across the table fae ye. Aabody still asks efter them at every gatherin, still leaves a space for them in the claik even though they're no there tae hear it. That's jist foo it is when somebody's awa.

These days aabody's got their fixed pairt tae play. The eldest still organizes aathing, aye has, aye will, if she didna, naethin wid ever get arranged, we'd aa jist sit waitin for somebody else tae dae it. The een that's aye late is still aye late, we've stopped even mentionin a time tae her, we jist tell her hauf an oor afore we actually mean and it works oot aboot richt. And the peacekeeper's still the peacekeeper, first een tae smooth things ower if voices stert risin, aye kens the richt thing tae say tae tak the heat oot o a room.

There wis a low point, mind, aboot five year back, when it near aa fell apart. It wis a row that stertit smaa and got oot o hand, fowk sayin things they didna mean, twa or three o us no spikkin for months. It felt like the hale thing wis deein, jist quietly, naebody wantin tae be the een tae say it oot loud. It wis the peacekeeper that sortit it in the end, got aabody in the same room ower a meal and made us aa say oor piece, and it wisna easy, bit it held. We're aa stronger for haein gaen through it, if I'm honest, even though nane o us wid want tae dae it again.

The een tradition that's held since the very stert, though, every year, on the day o that first nicht at the fish hoose, we aa meet at the same pub doon the harbour, same time, whitever else is gaun on in oor lives. Doesna matter if somebody's got a bairn noo or a new job or is livin through in England, they mak the effort, or they phone in for it if they canna come. It's the een thing that's never moved in ten year.

And you, ye've already deen yer bit, mair than ye ken. Ye turned up last time wi a dram for the toast withoot naebody haein tae ask ye, and ye laughed at the jaicket joke the first time it got said, didna need it explained tae ye twice. That's mair than some fowk manage in a year. So that's the hale story, as much o it as maitters, and noo ye ken it, ye're in it, nae different fae the rest o us. Ye're kin noo, plain and simple. Welcome tae it.
