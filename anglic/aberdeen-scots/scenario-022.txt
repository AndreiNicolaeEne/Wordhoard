The quine: Fit like! Losh, yon hairst festival fair wore me oot, I'm nae richt yet.

Her pal: Aye, me an aa, ma feet's still sair fae aa the stannin. Bit hing on a meenit, I've a bit o claik for ye fae last nicht.

The quine: Fit? Fa telt ye that?

Her pal: The wifie fae the baking stall, mind her. She wis richt there, saw it wi her ain een.

The quine: Weel come on then, oot wi it, dinna keep it fae me.

Her pal: Richt, she said yon chiel an the quinie fae doon the college wis stannin aff fae aabody, ower by the burn, laughin awa at somethin, an affa close thegither, closer than fowk stand fan they're jist pals.

The quine: Never! Naw, that's affa near fer naethin tae be gaun on, that.

Her pal: That's fit I thocht masel.

The quine: Bit dae ye think it's fit it looks like, or are we readin ower much intae it?

Her pal: Weel there's mair tae it. The same wifie said she saw them again efter, doon by the steen brig, an he'd his airm roon her, jist for a meenit, syne aff again like naethin happened.

The quine: Losh be here. Fa else kens by noo, dae ye think? Claik like that gings roon this toon like the vrang wind, aabody'll hae it by the morn.

Her pal: I widna doobt it, no wi that yin's mooth.

The quine: Aye weel, dinna fash yersel ower it the noo, it could aye be naethin ava.

Her pal: True eneuch. Only wye tae ken fer sure is tae speir the chiel himsel, or the quinie, straicht oot, an nae fae the wifie at the stall.

The quine: Aye. Ask them yersel. That's the only wye ye'll ken fit's fit.
