Eleven days it held, nae a single break in it, and fae the verra first day ye kent it wisna gaun tae be like ony ordinary cauld snap. Fit like, ye'd say tae onybody ye met on the road, and it wis aye the same, fair jeeled, near deid wi it. The sky went that hard bricht blue, nae a cloud in't, and yer breath hung in front o yer face like reek fae a lum. I mind sayin tae the mither, that first mornin, this is nae richt, this is caulder than they said on the wireless, and she jist gied a look and pit anither log on.

We keepit the stove gaun the hale nicht every nicht, took it in turns tae feed it, naebody sleepin richt for fear it wid gang oot afore mornin. The ootside tap, that's nivver froze in aa the years we've bidden here, went solid as a steen by the third day. Cairryin watter fae the byre in pails than, mornin and nicht, hands that raw ye couldna feel yer ain fingers.

The eldest: I mind the cauld snap fae years back, thocht naething wid ever beat that ane.

Me: And fit dae ye think o this ane, than?

The eldest: Awa, this ane's waur. Far waur. Nivver felt the like o it in aa my days.

The warst nicht wis the ninth, sky clear as gless, and the cauld came doon like a hammer. Lost near aa the hens by mornin, the puir craiturs stiff in the hoose, jist the ane or twa left, that feart they widna move. Affa sair loss, that.

Eleven days, and than the thaw came in quiet, drips aff the roof afore we'd hardly wakened tae it. Naebody said a word, we jist stood at the windae listenin, gey near greetin wi the relief o it.
