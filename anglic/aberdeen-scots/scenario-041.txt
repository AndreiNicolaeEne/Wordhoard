Richt, I'll tell ye the hale o't, fae the stert tae the feenish, because ye asked fit really happened and no jist the version I gied them.

I wis there twa year, near enough twa year and a hauf gin I think on it. Started aff fine, ken, the first sax month ye're jist gled tae hae a start and ye dinna see the vrang in onything. Then it creeps in slow, the wey it dis. A shift ye didna agree tae, a "can ye jist" that never got asked richt, a thenk-ye that never cam. Ye tell yersel it's nae that bad, it's jist a Tuesday, aabody his a Tuesday like that. But it biggs up, aa the same, like a hairst ye're nae even watchin growe till it's ower yer heid.

The last straw wisna even a big thing, that's the daft pairt o't. It wis a rota. They'd pit me on for the Setterday I'd asked aff months afore, said aa richt, said fine, and then jist swapped it wioot a word tae me, like I wis a chair they were shiftin roond the office. I fund oot fae anither quine, no even fae the manager. And I mind staunin there wi the bit o paper in ma hand, and it wisna even the Setterday I wis raised aboot, it wis aathing afore it. Twa year o "can ye jist" comin doon on ae bit o paper.

So I gaed hame that nicht and I rehearsed it. Actual rehearsed it, in front o the mirror like a right eejit, ower and ower, tryin tae get the tone o't richt. Nae greetin, nae rantin, jist calm, jist diplomatic, jist a chiel wi their heid screwed on richt gaun in tae say their piece and walk oot wi their dignity still on them. I practised the polite version, the ane that widna burn onything doon, the ane that soundit like a business decision and no like somebody wha wis hurtin and affa tired.

The morn I gaed in early, afore onybody else near enough, and I asked the ane at the desk gin the manager wis in, and could I hae a meenit wi them, in private like. They said aye, come through.

I sat doon and I said the line I'd practised, word for word, near enough:

Me: It's nae aboot ony ae thing in particular, I've jist been thinkin a lot, and I think it's time for me tae move on.

Which wis a lee, or hauf a lee onywey, because it wis aboot ae thing in particular, it wis aboot that rota and aa the rotas afore it, but ye canna say that, no if ye want tae leave wi yer heid held up and a reference ahint ye. And I'll tell ye noo, tae ye, the real reason I held it back in that meenit, sittin there: I didna want them tae see it hid got tae me. I didna want tae gie them the satisfaction o kennin they'd wore me doon. Ye keep that pairt tae yersel, ye bide dignified, even if it's costin ye somethin tae dae it.

The manager wis surprised, I'll gie them that, or they did a gweed job o lookin it onywey. They said they were sorry tae hear it, said I wis a gweed worker, said they'd nae wanted this. And then cam the counter, jist like ye'd expect. They offered me mair money, no a fortune but a fair bit mair, and said they could look at ma hours forbye, gie me mair say ower the rota gaun forrit. And I sat there and I thocht, aye, that's fine, that's fine, but it's nae the money that's the bother.

I turned it doon, and I gied them the polite reason instead o the true ane. I said I felt it wis time for a new challenge, time tae try somethin different, that it wisna aboot the job itsel, jist aboot me and far I wis at in ma life. Aa richt-soundin words. What I really meant wis: I dinna trust ye tae mean it this time, I've heard promises afore, and forbye, I'm affa tired, and nae amoont o money maks a body feel seen when they've spent twa year feelin like a name on a rota and nithin mair.

We agreed twa weeks. Fair enough, I thocht, gie them time tae sort somebody else, dinna leave them in the lurch even if they'd left me there mony a time.

But I'll tell ye, walkin oot that office door efter, I hid a richt doubt hit me, sudden like. A cauld feelin, richt in the pit o ma stomach. Hae I jist done somethin daft? A steady job, twa year in, folk I kent, a place I could dae in ma sleep near enough, and I've jist walked awa fae aa that ower a rota and a feelin. I wis nae sae shair aboot masel for a guid few days efter, I'll no lee tae ye aboot that.

The last day itsel wis a queer ane. Somebody hid brocht a cake, which wis kind, and there wis a card gaun roond that folk hid signed, "aa the best," "ye'll be missed," aathing ye'd expect. Mostly it wis hollow, if I'm honest wi ye, folk sayin the richt things because that's fit ye say, no because they meant it doon deep. The manager shook ma haun and said the door wis aye open, which I didna believe for a meenit, but ye say thank ye onywey.

There wis ae meenit though, near the end o the day, when ma workmate, the ane I'd sat aside for near the hale twa year, pit her airm roond me, jist a wee bosie, and said:

Workmate: Ye ken I dinna blame ye ae bit, dae ye. I'd hae gone lang syne if I'd the courage o ye.

And that near did it, that near hid me sayin the hale o't, the true reason, the rota, the years afore it, aathing I'd held back in that first meenit wi the manager. I could feel it richt there, in ma throat. But I jist said thanks, and gied her a bosie back, and left it at that. Ye dinna aye say the true thing even at the end, even when somebody's gien ye the openin for it.

And how's it been since, ye're askin. Weel. Mair relief than regret, I'll say that strecht oot, though the doubt niver fully leaves ye, it jist gets quieter. I sleep better. I dinna dread the Sunday nicht the wey I used tae, that sick feelin afore the week stertin. I'm nae rich, mind, and the new place his its ain vratches and its ain daft rotas, aabody's dis, but naebody there his wore me doon the same wey, no yet onywey.

If I hid that meenit tae dae again, sittin doon wi the manager, I widna change the wirds much, I think the polite version wis richt for the day it wis, ye dinna aye owe folk yer rawest truth. But I'd tell masel afore I gaed in: it's aa richt tae let it show a bittie. Ye're nae less dignified for lettin somebody see ye're hurtin. I spent that hale meenit hidin it, and I think, lookin back, I wis hidin it mair fae masel than fae them.
