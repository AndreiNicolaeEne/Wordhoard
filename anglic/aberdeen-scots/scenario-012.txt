Mother, I need tae tell ye something, an I've been pittin it aff for a lang, lang time, but I'm nae pittin it aff nae mair. It's aboot the scar on ma leg. Ye've asked me aboot it a hunner times doon the years, an every time I've gied ye the same auld answer, that I cam aff ma bike an opened it on the pedal. That wisna true. Nane o that wis true, an I'm affa sorry it's taen me this lang tae say it tae yer face.

Ye mind fit I wis like as a bairn, aye trailin efter the eldest o the pack we ran wi, feart tae be the one that wid'na dae fit they did. There wis a spot doon by the water, ye'll mind it, the big tree that hung ower the pool, an aabody kent ye could climb up an loup aff intae the deep bit if ye were brave eneuch. I'd backed oot o it afore, the simmer afore that, an they never let me forget it. Claik aboot it for weeks, so they were, every time we met up. "He wisna gaun up," the eldest wid say, laughin, an the ithers wid laugh alang. I telt masel I wisna gaun tae be that loonie again.

So the day it happened, we were aa doon at the tree efter the skweel, fower or five o us, an the eldest turned tae me an says:

The eldest: Fit like the day, are ye gaun up or are ye jist gaun tae stand there again?

Me: I'm gaun up.

The eldest: Richt tae the tap this time, nae half wye like afore.

I said aye, I wid, though I mind ma stomach turnin ower even as I said it. I climbed higher than I'd ever gone, higher than I'd meant tae gan, past the branch I'd used afore, richt up tae the one that bent under ma wecht when I stood on it. I mind lookin doon an the ithers lookin sae smaa fae up there, an ane o them shoutin up:

One of them: Dinna fash, the watter's deep there, ye'll be fine!

I wisna sure fit wye I believed him, but I loupit onywye. I misjudged it aa the wye doon, cam in at a coorse angle, an the watter hut me like a wall. It knocked the pech clean oot o me, an as I gaed under I felt something catch ma leg, something I never saw, hidden doon in the mud or amang the steens at the bottom, an it opened ma leg richt up. I didna even feel the pain o it richt awa, jist the cauld an the fear an nae air in ma lungs.

They got me oot atween them, aa panickin, wi the watter roon us gaun reid, an I mind the eldest greetin, actually greetin, sayin ower an ower:

The eldest: I didna mean for ye tae gan that high, I didna mean it.

We sat there on the bank, aa o us soaked through an shakin, an it wis me that said it first, I'll own that noo. I said tae them, richt there afore we'd even stemmed the bleedin richt:

Me: We canna tell them fit happened. We'll say I cam aff ma bike.

An they aa grabbed at it like a life-belt, because nane o them wanted tae be in trouble either, an so we sat an we worked it oot atween us, fit bike, fit road, hoo it wid hae happened, till we had it aa fittin thegither like a story that had aywis been true. I mind ane o them sayin:

One of them: Say ye cam aff at the corner by the shop, there's a curb there wi a lump o metal stickin oot, ye could hae catched yer leg on that fine.

An that's fit I telt ye. I cam hame that day, limpin an white, an ye were that feart for me, an I telt ye I'd cam aff ma bike at the corner, an ye believed me, because fit wye wid ye no? I wisna the kind o bairn that lee'd tae ye afore that, or so I aye thocht o masel. Ye cleaned it an took me tae get it stitched an the doctor asked hoo it happened an I telt him the same lee, an it went doon on some form somewye as a bike accident, an that wis that. Aathing aboot it settled intae bein true jist by everybody sayin it eneuch times.

An the warst o it, Mother, is hoo lang it's lasted. It wisna jist that day, or that year. It's been every simersa since, every time somebody's seen the scar an asked, at the pool, in the doctor's, wi a partner, wi ma ain bairns even, an every single time I've said the same false thing withoot even hae'in tae think aboot it nae mair, it cam that easy. Twinty year an mair o answerin a question wi a lee, an you, you that cleaned it an sat up wi me that nicht makin sure I wisna gettin a fever fae it, you've believed it the langest o onybody.

I've thocht a lot aboot fit wye I'm tellin ye this noo, efter aa this time. Pairt o it is I've a bairn o ma ain noo that's aboot the age I wis then, an I watch him wi his ain pack o loonies an quines, an I see hoo little it wid tak for him tae dae something daft jist tae no be the one that backed doon, an it fair frichts me. An I thocht, if he ever did something like that an cam tae me wi a lee ower it, I'd want him tae ken he could tell me the truth, an I couldna richtly want that fae him while I'm still haudin a lee fae ma ain mither aa these years. It didna seem fair, somehow, tae expect mair honesty oot o him than I've ever gien you.

An pairt o it, I'll be honest, is jist that I'm tired. I'm tired o the wee flinch every time somebody asks aboot the scar, tired o hoo easy the lee comes oot, tired o kennin there's this ane thing atween us that's nae true, that's been sittin there aa this time like a steen in ma shae. I dinna want tae cairry it nae mair, an I dinna want you cairryin a false story either, no when ye were the one that sat up wi me an fashed yersel sick ower a bairn that wisna even tellin ye fit had really happened.

So there it is. I didna faa aff a bike. I took a dare fae a pack o bairns because I wis feart o bein caaed a coward again, an I clam higher than I should hae, an I loupit intae watter I couldna see the bottom o, an something doon there cut me open, an we aa agreed, richt there soakin an shakin on the bank, tae tell you an aabody else something that wisna true. It wisna your fault ye believed it. Ye'd nae reason no tae. I wis your bairn an I telt ye a lee tae yer face an ye trusted me, an that trust is the thing I'm sorriest for, mair even than the loup itsel or the scar or the fricht.

I'm sorry, Mother. I'm sorry for the lee that day an for every day since that I let it stand. I'm sorry ye've spent twinty year believin something false aboot yer ain bairn's body, aboot a day I telt ye wrang fae the start. I should hae telt ye the truth that same nicht, soaked an feart as I wis, an I didna, an I've had tae live wi that mair than I've ever let on. I hope ye can forgie me for it, no jist the loup, but the lang lee efter, an I hope, noo it's oot, we can jist let the scar be fit it aywis wis: a mark fae a stupid, brave, feart thing I did as a bairn, nithing mair, nithing less, an nae mair a false story sittin atween us.
