Richt, my friend, seein as ye werena there tae watch ony o it, I'll tell ye the hale story fae the stert, for it's a fine ane tae tell noo even though it didna feel fine at the time, I'm tellin ye.

Ye ken foo aabody else roon aboot seems tae hae learnt this when they were nae mair than bairns, five or six year auld, wobblin aboot wi some faither haudin the back o them. Weel, I never did. Never had the chance, never had the fowk tae learn me, and it juist got pit aff and pit aff till I wis a grown mannie wi a grown mannie's boady, and ae day I thocht, richt, that's it, I'm nae deein this ony langer, I'm gyaan tae learn fit like it feels tae dae this thing aabody else taks for granted.

So I signed masel up, affa proud o masel and aa, walkit in the first nicht thinkin I'd hae it crackit by the end o the oor. I didna.

The first lesson wis hopeless, richt fae the stert. Ma mind kent fine fit it wis supposed tae be deein, but ma boady widna listen tae a word o it. I'd tell masel, richt, noo dae this, and ma legs wid dae the exact opposite, every single time. The instructor stood there watchin me, patient eneuch, but blunt wi it forbye.

The instructor: Ye ken this'll tak ye langer than the young eens, at your age.

Me: Aye, I'm nae expectin miracles.

The instructor: Guid, cause ye'll nae get ony.

That stung a wee bit even then, but naethin tae fit came efter. Within days, days mind, nae weeks, I wis bein outpaced by loonies and quinies half ma age, bairns that hadna been alive as lang as I'd been drivin a car, fleein past me like I wis stannin still. I telt masel it didna maitter, but it did, it stung mair than I let on tae onybody.

And the soreness, my friend, I canna tell ye. The hale first week ma boady ached in bits I'd forgotten I even hid. Muscles doon the side o ma legs I hidna used since I wis a loon masel, aa makkin themsels affa weel kent every time I moved a step. Gettin oot o bed wis a hale operation. Sittin doon wis waur.

But it wis the fall that I mind maist, oot o aathing. I went doon hard, nae a wee stumble, a richt doon, knocked the braith clean oot o me, and I lay there for a meenit, mebbe langer, juist starin up, sure, absolutely sure, that I wis feenished wi the hale idea. Naebody needs this at their age, I mind thinkin. Naebody needs tae be lyin on the grund gaspin like a landit fish in front o a puckle bairns that can dae it wi their een shut.

Me: I'm nae comin back. That's it. Deen wi it.

Ma workmate, that hid come alang tae watch, wisna haein ony o that.

Ma workmate: Ye'll come back the morn, or the day efter, whan yer legs hiv stopped shakkin. Ye're nae gien up ower ae fall.

Me: I meant it.

Ma workmate: Aye, ye aye mean it. Come back onywye.

And I did. Dinna ask me fit changed ma mind, I couldna tell ye exactly, but I went back ane mair time, and that ane mair time turned intae anither, and syne it juist kept gyaan.

Progress efter that wis affa slow, and humiliatin wi it, I'll nae mak oot it wisna. Weeks o gettin the same wee thing wrang, ower and ower, then finally gettin it richt for a saicont, juist a saicont, afore losin it again. And aa the time there wis a wee crood o onlookers, some o them encouragin, cheerin ye on like ye'd deen something graund for gettin three feet withoot fallin, and some o them clearly affa amused, laughin ahint their hauns, no cruel exactly, but ye kent fine fit they were thinkin.

Me: Fit are ye lauchin at?

The one at the side: Naethin, naethin. Ye're daein fine.

Me: Aye, richt.

The only comparison that ever did me ony guid wis masel a month back. Nae the fastest eens, never them, that road lay only fashin and greetin ower it. But masel a month afore, that I could look at fair, and see I wis better than I hid been, even if I wis naewhere near fit I wanted tae be.

Syne, roon aboot the fowerth month, something clickit. I canna pit it intae words richt, even noo, it wisna something I thocht, it wis something ma boady juist kent aa at ance, a feelin mair than an idea. And the thing that hid been impossible for months wis, aa o a sudden, juist difficult. Nae easy, dinna get me wrang, but difficult is a hale different country fae impossible.

I never did catch up tae the fastest learners, the eens that hid it crackit in an efternoon while I wis still greetin ower fa'in on ma erse. But by the end I'd made ma peace wi that fine. I wisna prood o bein the best, I wis prood o the things I could noo dae withoot even thinkin aboot them, things that at the stert I couldna dae ata, nae maitter foo hard I tried.

And lookin back on it aa noo, my friend, the hale humiliatin season sits fondly in ma mind raither than sourly. The falls, the soreness that hid me walkin like an auld mannie for a week, bein lauched at by a puckle onlookers, the instructor's blunt tongue, aa o it fauldit intae something I'm richt gled I did. I widna gie back a meenit o it, even the fall that knocked the braith clean oot o me. Especially nae that ane, I think, if I'm honest.

Sae that's the hale claik o it, fae stert tae feenish. I ken it maks nae sense tae start something sae late whan aabody else did it as a bairn, but I'm gled I did, and I'd tell onybody else thinkin aboot it tae dinna fash yersel ower fit the young eens can dae. Compare yersel tae yersel, that's aa that maitters, and gie it the year, or mair, it'll come.
