The one who wisna there: Fit like, the pair o ye. Foo did it go?

Me: Affa good, affa good. Bit ye widna believe fa wis there. The mannie fae the back row, mind, the ane that sat aside the windae aa through. I didna ken him at aa. Had tae look at his name badge twice.

My mate: Aye, I walked richt past him! Great muckle beard on him noo, and he's lost aa his hair forbye. Back then he wis a skinny wee loonie, aye lauchin, couldna sit still five meenits. The chiel that stood up noo, quiet, spoke affa slow. I says tae masel, that canna be him.

Me: And then there wis the quinie fae the front, the ane that did aa the talkin in the class. Exact same. Same lauch, same wye o barrelin intae a room like she owns it. Ye'd think nae time had passed at aa. She wis stannin there giein it laldy tae some poor mannie, jist like the auld days.

My mate: Funny that, foo some fowk jist dinna shift.

The one who wisna there: Fa didna come, then?

Me: The wifie that used tae bide next the shop, she wisna there, her man's nae weel, so she stayed hame tae see tae him. And ye ken fit I heard? The quine that aabody thocht wis gaan tae be a teacher, she's awa tae the toon and she's runnin her ain business noo, daein affa weel for hersel. Naebody saw that comin.

My mate: Naebody at aa.

Me: Fit aboot us, though. Div you think we've changed muckle?

My mate: Ach, a bittie. Mair grey, less patience. Bit I widna say we're that different.

Me: Naw, nae really. Same spik, same claik.

My mate: We shouldna leave it sae lang neist time.

Me: Nae, we shouldna. Afore we're aa auld and grey, eh, let's get thegither again suner.
