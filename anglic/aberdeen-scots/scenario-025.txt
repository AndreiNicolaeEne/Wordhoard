Her: Fit like, ay? That wis an affa lang goodbye at the door.

Him: She jist likes tae see us aff richt, that's aa.

Her: Forty-five meenits, min. Forty-five meenits o "mind and phone fan ye're hame" and "dinna forget yer bosie afore ye go," her een niver aff the clock the hale time, and in atween aa the claik aboot the neighbors forbye.

Him: Aye weel, she's nae getting ony younger, is she. She likes the crack afore we go.

Her: And yer feyther wi the same story again, the ane aboot the hairst and the auld tractor. Third time I've heard it, min. Third time, and he could spik for oors aboot it an aa, feart tae stop afore the end like it wid vanish if he didna finish it.

Him: He tells a gweed story.

Her: He tells the SAME story.

Him: Aye, weel, mibbe.

Her: And the cooking. Did ye nae hear fit she said? "Ye'll nae get neeps like that in the toon, quine." Like I canna mak a proper denner.

Him: She didna mean naething by it.

Her: There's nothing vrang wi ma neeps, and she said it twice, min, no ance, twice.

Him: Aa richt, aa richt. It wis aathing the day, richt enough, the story, the comment, the door, aa at ance.

Her: Thank ye.

Him: And aye, she did say it twice aboot the neeps.

Her: See! Ye heard it an aa.

Him: Fit wye dis she aye dae that. Aabody kens she does it and yet naebody says naething.

Her: Ken fit I mean noo, chiel?

Him: Aye. She'll be on the phone the morn tae see gin we got hame aa richt, and it'll aa stert again.

Her: Bide easy. We're near hame.
