The eldest: Richt, quinie, come here afore the rest o them turn up, I'll learn ye the game proper. It's tig, but there's rules tae it, we're nae jist rinnin aboot like glaikit stirks.

The young one: Fit like dae ye even stert it?

The eldest: Ye need fower at the least tae get gaun, ony less and it's nae worth it. Boundary's fae the wall there richt doon tae yon lamppost, and ower tae the hedge on the ither side. That's aa the grund we've got.

The young one: And fa's it first?

The eldest: Aabody pits a fit in, we coont doon, and fa's fit gets landed on last is chaser. Nae argy-bargyin aboot it after, that's jist hou it works.

The young one: Fit coonts as a tig though?

The eldest: See, jist a touch, open hand, on the back or the airm. Nane o yer pushin or grabbin, that's vrang and ye ken it.

The young one: Fit happens gin somebody rins ower the boundary tae get awa fae the chaser?

The eldest: Same as gettin tigged, they're oot, freeze richt there. Ye canna rin ower the line tae dodge it, that's cheatin, plain and simple.

The young one: And gin I'm tigged?

The eldest: Ye freeze, feet stuck, canna move a fit till somebody that's still rinnin comes ower and taps ye loose. Then aff ye go again.

The young one: Sae fan dis the round feenish?

The eldest: Fan aabody's frozen and there's nae mair loose tae tap onybody free. Whaever got tigged last is chaser fer the next een.

The young one: I'm affa feart I'll get it aa vrang.

The eldest: Dinna fash yersel, this first round disna coont fer naething. Get it vrang aa ye like, we'll jist stert again.
