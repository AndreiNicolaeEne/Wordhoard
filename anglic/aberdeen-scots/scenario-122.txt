Me:
Fit like. Aye, I ken fit ye're efter, sittin there wi yer knees up tae yer chin on a coorse nicht like this ane, waitin on me tae stert. It's aye the same wye it comes up, some young ane speirs fit wye naebody walks yon stretch efter dark, or fit wye their mither aye tells them tae be hame afore the licht gaes, and I ken then it's time. I dinna tell it tae onybody that hisna asked first. That wis the rule I wis gien, and it's the rule I gie on tae you the nicht.

Ye dinna see the thing, mind, no a richt shape tae it, no een nor a face nor hauns that onybody's ever tellt me o. Fit comes first, aabody that's heard it agrees on this, is a voice. Afore there's onything tae look at, there's a soond, and it's the soond that tells ye fit's happenin, no yer een. And gin ye're fool eneuch tae bide and listen langer, some say there's a thickenin tae the air efter, a wecht at yer back like somebody's stannin affa close ahin ye athoot a fitstep tae warn ye o it. Bit it's the voice that comes first, and it's the voice that does the work. Some jist caa it yon vratch, and I'll no argue wi that.

And the work it does is this. It cries oot in the voice o somebody ye loe. No a stranger's voice, that widna draa naebody aff a path, ye'd jist rin the ither wye and think nae mair on it. It's yer ain mither's voice, or yer bairn's, or the wifie ye mairrit, cryin yer name fae somewye aff intae the dark, soondin feart, soondin like they need ye richt this meenit. And fit it's efter, so they say, is tae draa ye aff the road and awa fae onybody that could help ye, intae the moss and the watter and the trees till ye dinna ken which wye's hame ony mair.

It only comes efter midnicht, mind, and only on a nicht wi nae meen ava. Gie it a clear sky wi the meen up and it disna come, canna come, some say. It needs the dark tae be complete afore it'll try onything on ye.

The spot it gets tied tae here is the bend afore the auld brig, doon by the burn, whaur the road narraes in atween the watter and the trees and ye canna see far in either direction. I'll no tell ye it's tied tae onywhere bigger than that. I've heard fowk fae ither places claim their ain bends and their ain burns hae the same thing cryin at them, and I dinna doobt them each their ain, bit this yin's oors, and it bides at that yin bend and naewhere else that I ken o.

I first heard it fae ma grunnie, when I wisna much mair than a loonie masel, afore I'd even startit the skweel, sittin jist whaur you're sittin the noo. She telt it plain, no a laugh in it ava, and that wis the thing that stuck wi me mair than the story itself did, that she wisna jokin. Ither fowk in the faimly wid tell it wi a bit o a laugh tae soften it for the bairns, "dinna fash yersel, it's jist a story," they'd say, and gie ye a bosie and send ye tae yer bed. Bit the laugh niver quite got up tae their een, gin ye looked close, and I aye looked close, even as a loonie.

The first proof they gie ye, if ye ask for proof, is the grund at that bend. Naething's ever grown there, no in leevin memory. Fowk hae plantit neeps there, grass seed, aathing, and it aye comes up bare, jist the steen and the black earth and naething else, while aathing roond aboot it's growin fine, gweed and green.

The second proof's the kind ye aye get wi these things, secondhand, somebody that heard it fae somebody else. A chiel fae twa faimlies ower wis said tae hae been oot late and heard a cryin fae the burn side, and telt his mither, and his mither telt ma mither, and that's hoo it comes doon tae us, no fae the chiel's ain mooth bit fae mooth tae mooth, and ye tak it for fit it's worth.

Bit the yin that gets telt in full every single time, the yin I'll gie ye noo aa the wye through, is aboot ma ain uncle, years back, a young chiel then, walkin hame late fae the toon on a nicht wi nae meen. He heard his ain name cried, clear as onything, fae aff the path doon by the burn, and it wis his mither's voice, sure as onything, cryin him tae come. And he turned aff the road and took three, fower steps toward it afore something stopped him. He couldna say fit it wis that stopped him, no even tae the end o his days. Bit he stopped, and he turned, and he walked hame fest, and he niver went near that bend efter dark again.

Fit he swore tae, word for word, every time onybody speired him aboot it, wis this: "I ken it wis her voice. I ken it wisna her. And I ken I widna hae turned back gin I hidna stopped when I did." That's fit he said, and that's fit gets repeated in this faimly, the same words every time, near eneuch a prayer by noo.

The detail that maks even the doobters gang quiet a wee second is that his mither, when he got hame and telt her, wis fest asleep in her bed the hale nicht, and had been for oors, and there wis nae wye she'd cried onything tae onybody. That's the pairt that stops the claik for a meenit, even fae they that dinna haud wi ony o it.

The detail that gies the doobters their openin, though, is that he'd been at a chiel's waddin that nicht and hidna exactly come hame sober, and there's aye somebody ready tae say a dram or twa'll mak onybody hear their ain mither cryin fae a burn side. Gin ye think that's vrang o them tae say, I'll no argue it awa masel. I jist say he niver went near the bend again efter dark, sober or no, and that's worth something tae.

The story changes a bit dependin on fa's tellin it, mind. Fae some mooths it comes oot softer, mair a warnin than a fear, jist a wye o spikkin that says bide aff the roads at nicht. Fae ithers it comes oot harder, and they'll add bits I niver heard fae ma grunnie ava. I've niver minded the difference. A story that disna shift a bit fae mooth tae mooth is a story naebody's really leevin in, tae ma mind.

The wee thing fowk here still dae because o it, tae this day, is this: they dinna answer if their name gets cried fae somewye dark, no a word, until they've seen wi their ain een fa it is that's cryin. Ye can cry a hunner times fae the dark and get nae answer fae onybody roond here, and it's no bad manners, it's jist the rule.

And I'll tell ye honest, since ye've sat and listened the hale wye through athoot a word oot o ye, that I dinna fully believe it masel. I dinna. Bit I hivna broken that rule yet, no aince, in aa ma years, and I dinna plan on startin the nicht.
