The gaffer: Richt, afore aabody scatters aff hame - the morn. We sort it noo or it's forgotten afore ye finish that pint.

The eldest: Aye awa then, fit time we startin?

The gaffer: Seven.

The eldest: Seven? Ma back's vrang eneuch withoot startin at seven, mak it aicht.

The young one: Ye're aye the same, feart o an early start. Seven's fine, chiel.

The eldest: Wha ye caain chiel, ye're a loonie yersel.

The gaffer: Seven it is, nae mair claik aboot it. It's the roof ower at the auld site, the chimney stack's needin teen doon afore the slates gang on.

The driver: I'll bring the van and the ladders. Fa's got the mixer?

The young one: I hae it, but we're affa short on boards for the scaffold, I couldna get mair the day.

The gaffer: We'll manage wi fit we hae, jist mind if the wind gets up we're nae gaun up there.

The eldest: And fa's daein the chimney? That's a filthy job, aa that stour doon yer neck.

The gaffer: You, seein as ye're that fu o complaints aaready.

The eldest: Aye, that's richt, gie the aulder mannie the vratched job. Some thanks that is.

The driver: Ye owe me fae last time onywye, so it's aa square.

The gaffer: Right, fower o us the morn - you, you, you, and the young one's brither. Is he comin?

The young one: Na, he canna, he's at the skweel run wi the wife, he'll no mak it.

The gaffer: Weel, we'll dae wioot him then, jist the three o us and I'll get anither hand doon.

The eldest: Aye fine, but somebody owes me a pint efter aa this.

The gaffer: Seven sharp, nae excuses, and dinna fash yersel ower the chimney, it'll no kill ye.

The eldest: It near enough will.
