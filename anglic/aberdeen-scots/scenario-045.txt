I'd gaen in for the jaicket, the een I'd ordered a fortnicht syne, and nae suner did the mannie lift it aff the rail than I kent aathing wisna richt. Twa buttons on the front, and the hem awa aff at an angle.

Me: Naw, naw, that's nae fit I askit for. Three buttons, and a straucht hem, nae this angled cairry-on ava.

The tailor: Weel noo, that's exactly fit we agreed, quine. I mind ye staunin richt there yersel.

Me: Ye dinna mind richt then, for I ken fine fit I said. Three buttons, straucht hem, and I'm nae haein onything else.

The tailor: Awa and bide a meenit, I'll awa throu and hae a look at the note.

He gaed throu tae the back, affa lang aboot it forbye, and cam throu again wi a wee scrap o paper aa foldit ower.

The tailor: Richt, here's yer measurements... three buttons, aye, that's fit's written doon richt eneuch. But it says angled hem here.

Me: Weel that's vrang then, for I never askit for nae angled hem in aa ma days. Somebody's written it doon wrang, and it wisna me that did it.

The tailor: Aye, ye're richt eneuch. I've gaen and mixed ye up wi anither chiel that cam in efter ye. I'm affa sorry aboot that, dinna fash yersel, I'll hae the hem pit straucht for ye, nae bather ava.

Me: When will it be ready than?

The tailor: Gie's twa days, and I'll hae it aa richt for ye by then, straucht as a die.

Me: Richt, I'll be back the morn's morn then. And nae mair angled hems, mind.

The tailor: Nae fear, quine, I'll dae it richt this time.
