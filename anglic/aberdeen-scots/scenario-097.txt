Come here aside me, min, sit yersel doon. I ken ye dinna want claik the noo, ye jist want somebody sittin wi ye that's been through it, so I'll spik and you jist listen, aye, that's aa I'm askin.

The first days, honest tae God, I mind near naething o them. Ye'd think it wid be the sairest bit but it wisna, it wis jist a blur, fowk comin and gaan, the door gaun the hale day, cousins I hidna seen in years staunin in ma kitchen wi a casserole dish and nae kennin fit tae say tae me, and me makin them tea onywye because that's fit ye dae, that's fit yer hands ken tae dae even when yer heid's away somewye else. Aa the arrangements, the phone calls, the paperwork, some mannie fae the undertakers speirin questions aboot coffins and hymns and I answered them aa affa calm, and folk said tae me after, ye're bein that strong, and I wisna strong at aa, I jist hidna hid a meenit tae feel onything yet. There wis nae room for it. Aabody wis there filling up the space so the reality couldna get in the door.

It wis efter, that's fan it got ye. I mind the last o them gaed hame, the last car awa doon the road, and I steed in the hoose and it wis that quiet, min, I've nivver heard a quiet like it afore or since. Nae the telly on, nae onybody movin aboot upstair, jist the clock. And it wis like the hoose kent afore I did, it hut me like a steen. I steed in the middle o the flair and I couldna move for a fair while.

The weeks efter that ma body did some affa strange things. There wid be days I couldna eat a thing, naething, ye'd pit a plate in front o me and I'd look at it like it wis fae anither planet. Then ither days I'd eat like I hidna seen food in a week. Sleep wis the same, nivver a hale night, jist an oor here, twa oors there, wide awake at three in the mornin staring at the ceilin, then droppin aff again jist as the alarm wis due. Ye dinna get tae choose it, it jist comes and goes as it likes, like it's got its ain mind.

The first month wis the strangest o the lot, because the warld jist kept on gaan. Fowk back tae their work, the shops fu, traffic on the roads, aabody caught up in their ain aathing, and I couldna understaun hou they could aa be gettin on wi it fan naething wis the same for me. Ye feel affa left ahin, like the rest o them are on a train that's awa and you're still staunin at the platform.

And then there wis a day, jist an ordinary day, naething marked on it, nae onniversary, nae birthday, naething I'd been dreidin, and it undid me completely, oot the blue. I couldna even tell ye fit wis different aboot it. Ye think ye'll ken fan the sair days are comin so ye can brace yersel, but ye canna, some o the worst anes gie ye nae warnin at aa.

Months on fae that, it wis smells, wee things. A smell o a certain soap, or seein some object in a drawer, and it wid come ower me like a wave oot o naewhere. Nae pattern tae it, ye couldna predict it, it jist took ye.

The middle stretch, I'll be honest wi ye, wis a lang fog. It didna feel like grief exactly, mair like naething at aa, a dullness that sat on ivry day, even the days that should hae been fine anes. Ye'd be somewye nice, wi fowk ye liked, and it wid aa jist feel far awa, like ye were watchin it through glaiss.

There wis a chiel said tae me, meanin nae herm I'm sure, "ye'll be ower it in nae time," and it stung far mair than it should hae, I wis raging inside for days ower that ane line. But then there wis a wifie fae ma work, hardly kent her afore, left a wee note through ma door, jist said she wis thinkin on me, naething else, nae fuss, and that daft wee note did mair for me than aa the official condolences pit thegither.

The onniversary, I dreidit it for weeks afore it cam, couldna sleep richt for the thocht o it hingin ower me. And fan the day itsel cam, I got through it, clumsy like, nae graceful aboot it at aa, greetin ower ma tea, then laughin at somethin daft on the telly twa oor later, and that wis it, that wis the day done. And it wis eneuch jist tae hae got through it, that wis the hale point, ye're nae supposed tae dae it bonny, ye're jist supposed tae come oot the ither side.

The appetite and the sleep, they cam back, but nivver aa at eence, jist a bittie here and a bittie there, twa steps forrit and ane back, some weeks I'd think I wis richt as rain and then it wid slip awa again.

There wis a day, I'll nivver forget it, I laughed, a real laugh, fae the belly, at somethin some daft loonie said, and the meenit it wis oot o me the guilt cam richt in ahin it, like fit right hid I tae be laughin fan they were gone. Naebody tellt it tae come, it jist did.

By the end o the year I wis cairryin it different, lichter in some bits, I could get through a day withoot it flatten me, but it wisna gone, min, it's nivver gone, it jist sits different in ye efter a while, ye learn tae cairry it so it disna show sae much.

So that's aa I've got tae tell ye, and I'm nae tellin ye it tae frichten ye, I'm tellin ye because it disna bide this sair forever. There's nae clock on it, naebody can tell ye the morn or the month it'll ease, so dinna fash yersel measurin yer ain year against mine, or onybody else's. Yours'll be yours, and that's richt eneuch.
