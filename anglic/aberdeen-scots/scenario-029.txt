Fit like, you're wantin tae ken fit oor mornings are actually like in this hoose? Right, sit yersel doon wi that tea and I'll walk ye through it, cause it's the same performance near enough every single day, and I've seen it that mony times noo I hardly hear it masel onymair. You jist listen, I'll dae the tellin.

It's the mither that's up first, aye, afore onybody else and affa aften afore the alarm's even had the chance tae go. Naebody kens foo she does it, she jist wakens, like her body's got its ain clock that disna care fit day o the week it is. First thing she does, every single time, is she pits the kettle on and stands at the windae wi her first cup, jist lookin oot at naething, the hale hoose still and daurk aroon her. Five meenits o quiet, that's aa she wants afore the rest o us are fair game, and naebody, but naebody, is alood tae spik tae her afore that cup's teem.

Syne it's the man's turn, and he wakens tae the same grumble every single mornin, aa aboot the wash-room, cause there's jist the ane in this hoose and he swears blind I take langer in there than onybody has a richt tae. We've been haein the same argument aboot it for years noo and it's nivver gettin settled, nivver will, and I dinna think either o us wants it settled tae be honest, it's mair a habit than a fecht at this pynt.

The man: You've been in there hauf an oor again, I can hear ye singin tae yersel in there.
Me: I hivna been in there five meenits, ye're jist feart ye'll be late again like the morn and the day afore that.

That's hoo it gings, gie or take a word, near enough every single mornin.

The bairns get roosed next, and they aye get roosed in the same order, nivver changes, nivver has since the youngest wis oot her cot. The eldest first, cause she's the lightest sleeper and'll be up wi jist a knock on the door, syne the middle ane, wha needs the licht pit on and a shak o the shoothers afore onything registers, and last the youngest, wha'd sleep through the hale hoose fawin doon aroon her, so it's the covers pulled aff and the cauld air that does it, naething else works.

The mither, up the stair, same three calls every mornin, same order, like she's readin fae a script naebody else has seen:

The mother: Quine, up ye get, the day's no waitin! Loon, come on noo, I'm no sayin it again! Quinie, oot o that bed this meenit, ye'll catch yer death lyin there!

Same words, same order, every single mornin, and the middle ane never once answers first time, ye could set a clock by it.

And then there's the argument that happens maist mornings, near enough the same words every time, aboot fa's wearin fit tae the skweel.

The eldest: That's my jumper, she's aye takin my things withoot askin first.
The middle one: It wisna even yours tae start wi, it wis Granny's, ye're jist claimin it.

The mother jist sighs, same sigh every time, and says the same thing back: Wear yer ain claes or I'll gie the baith o ye something tae greet aboot.

The dog's got her ain fixed pairt in aa this forbye. She sleeps at the fit o the eldest's bed every single nicht and the meenit that door opens she's aff doon the stair like the hoose is on fire, and she sits at the fit o the table through the hale o breakfast wi thon look on her face like the vratch's nivver been fed in her puff, though she's fed twice a day like clockwork and kens it fine.

Breakfast's the same every mornin an aa. Parritch for the bairns, tea for the rest o us, and the eldest nivver, nivver varies her order, has tae be toast, cut corner tae corner, nae butter near the edges, and if it's cut wrang she'll no touch it, disna maitter foo hungry she is, she'll sit there wi a face on her like thunder afore she'll eat it cut wrang.

There's aye something gone missin an aa, maist mornings. Aftenest it's the middle ane's ither shae, and it's aye hunted for in the same three places, unner the couch, ahin the door, and in the dog's basket, and nine times oot o ten that's exactly far it turns up, though he swears every time he's looked there already.

Ye ken the hale hoose is properly waukened when the toaster gings, that click and that pop, cause that's the sound that has aabody comin ben the kitchen aa at aince, like it's a bell caain them tae their places. Afore that it's aa grumbles and half-shut een, efter it aabody's spikkin at aince.

It's aye the man that's runnin a few meenits ahin, every single mornin, nivver fails, and he's the ane fashin aboot the time while the rest o us are stannin at the door waitin on him. And then it's the rush, coats grabbit aff the hooks, bags haundit ower tae the richt bairn, and naething's ever forgotten, only cause it nivver is, we've done it that mony times the hale hoose kens the drill better than we ken oorsels, even the dog kens tae get oot the road at that pynt.

Sunday's the ae day the hale rhythm changes. Naebody's roosed, naebody's caaed doon the stair in ony order, and the hoose jist gings quiet insteid, like it's haein a rest fae itsel efter a week o the same show. Even the mither bides in her bed a while langer, and that tells ye aa ye need tae ken aboot foo different it is.

But every ither mornin, the same handfae o words get said at the door afore onybody leaves, a wee bosie for the wee ane and the same line every time: Mind and behave yersels, the lot o ye, and dinna fash yer teachers. And that's it, oot the door, gone, aabody scattered tae their ain day.

Div ye no think it's affa, the hale thing, when ye hear it said oot loud like that? A performance, near enough, played oot the same wye every single mornin, doon tae the same words in the same order, and here's me tellin ye aa aboot it like it's the maist ordinary thing in the world. Tae you it's probably the daftest thing ye've ever heard, a hale hoose runnin itsel like clockwork on grumbles and toast and a wash-room fecht that nivver ends and a dog that thinks she's starvin. But that's hame for ye, that's exactly fit it is, every single mornin, near enough word for word, and I widna hae it ony ither wye.
