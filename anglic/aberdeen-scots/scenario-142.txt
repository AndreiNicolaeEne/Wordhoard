Wauk up an the first thing that comes tae mind is nae the dream I wis in, it's the promise. Months back I stood in the kitchen an swore plain that I wid gie ower a hale day tae redd oot the auld steadin, aa the rubbish the faither niver cleared awa, an here's the day, sittin on me like a steen.

Nae a bit o me wants up. The bed's affa warm an the sky ootside's the colour o auld watter. But a sworn thing's a sworn thing, so up ye get, ye eejit, ye did this tae yersel, naebody else tae blame.

Doon the road I'm girnin the hale wye, naebody tae hear it but masel an a couple o gulls. Fit wye did I ever open ma mooth an say aye tae this. Should niver hae. The length o the job alone wid gie onybody the rue - a hale steadin fu o boxes an broken cairts an neeps gaun tae rot in the corner. An the timin o it, a Setterday, the ae day I could hae bidden in ma bed.

Every hoor there's somethin new tae claik aboot tae masel: ma back, the stoor, the wecht o the auld dresser, ma ain bad jeedgement iver swearin sic a thing in the first place.

Aboot three o'clock, airms fair deid, I think, jist leave it, come back the morn. Then: na. Ye swore. Ye ken fine ye did. That's the end o that thought afore it gets ony further.

The workmate come by wi tea.

The workmate: Fit like? Ye look done in athegither.

Me: Aye, I am that. Nae stoppin though, dinna fash yersel on my account.

Grit ma teeth through the worst o it, the byre floor near black wi years o muck an cauld. But by the gloamin it's aa redd, swept, dune, an naethin gaed vrang. Every bit o it, jist as I swore. Complained the hale day, never stopped complainin, but never stopped workin either.
