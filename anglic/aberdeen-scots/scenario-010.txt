Mother: Richt, the hale lot o ye, doon here nou. Somebody's broken the steen jar aff the windae sill and I'm no movin till I ken fa did it.

The eldest: No me. I wisna even in the room, I wis up the stair aa mornin.

Mother: Ye were doon here no half an oor syne, I heard ye masel.

The eldest: Aye, weel, I wis doon, but I didna touch naethin, I wis jist sittin.

Mother: Fit aboot you then, quinie?

The middle one: No me eether. It'll hae been the cat, it wis up on that sill aa mornin chasin its ain tail like a daftie. Or it wis the wind, there wis an affa draught through the hoose the day, it could easy hae knocked it aff itsel.

Mother: The windae's been shut since I shut it afore denner, sae there wis nae draught tae speak o. And the cat's been ootside since brekfast.

The youngest: It wis her, she wis in there wi it, I seen her wi ma ain een.

The middle one: I wisna near it! You're the een aye claikin on folk, you'd say onythin tae get somebody in bother.

The eldest: Baith o ye wheesht, it wisna me and naebody's proved it wis you.

Mother: A meenit syne you said the windae wis open, nou you're sayin the wind did it wi the windae shut. Which is it, quine?

The middle one: I- I canna mind richt eneuch. Mibbe it wisna open at aa, mibbe I'm thinkin o yesterday.

Mother: Naebody's gonnae own up tae it, are they.

The youngest: Nae me, Mother, I sweir.

Mother: Fine. Nae pooch money for ony o ye the morn, the three o ye the same, since naebody'll spik the truth o it. And dinna fash yersels lookin sae feart, the jar's still needin mended and somebody in this hoose kens fine fa did it.
