Up afore the licht again, the sky still black as a steen, nae a sign o the meen even, jist the caul comin in aneath the door and the fire nae lit yet. The mither wis first up as aye, rattlin the pot and chappin on the stair.

Mother: Come on noo, aabody up, we've a hale hairst tae get in and the day's nae gettin ony langer for yer lyin.

Naebody needit tellt twice wi the hairst on. First job o the day gaed tae the youngest een able tae dae onything at aa, sent doon tae the shed athoot his boots near on tae fetch the flasks and the piece-bags afore the rest o us wis even oot o oor beds richt.

Father: You, awa doon and get the flasks filled, and dinna fash aboot the milk, jist get gaan.

The eldest took the heaviest bit athoot a mutter, doon at the far side o the park far the grund wis roch and the drills gey lang, heid doon and intil it fae the first, strong as a horse that een aye wis, and naebody thocht tae argue it should be onybody else.

We warked the first section throu in gweed time, the licht comin up slow ower the hill, cauld and grey and then a bit o gowd creepin intil it, and eence that bit wis clear we aa shiftit roond, some tae the cairtin, some tae the liftin, naebody stannin aboot fashin ower fit tae dae. Ye jist kent yer job and got on.

Her workmate wis fair trailin ahin by mid-mornin, her back nae richt since the spring, but naebody made a soun aboot it. The loon jist slipped in aside her athoot a word and evened the rows up atween them, quiet like, so she widna be shawn up in front o the lave.

Workmate: I'm sorry, I'm jist nae keepin up the day.

The loon: Away, ye're daein fine, jist gie's a haun here and we'll hae it sortit in nae time.

The meal cam oot aboot eleven, the mither and the quinie cairryin it doon atween them ower the rigs, breid and cheese and a pot o tea fit wid near strip the varnish aff a door, and we sat doon on the steens at the dyke tae tak it, legs oot afore us and the sweat gaan cauld a meenit. There wis a fair bit o claik gaed roon ower the tea, fa'd been seen at the mart, fa's quine wis coortin fa, and a mannie fae the ferm ower the road that cam by tae gie a haun for an oor, greeted the same as ever.

The neighbour chiel: Fit like the day?

Father: Nae bad, nae bad. Affa a lot o grund tae cover yet, mind.

The eldest and the chiel fell tae arguin ower the richt wye tae haud a scythe, the eldest sayin the chiel wis aa vrang aboot it and wid never keep his pace up gaan that road.

The eldest: Ye're haudin it aa vrang, that's fit's slowin ye doon, look.

The chiel: Away and bile yer heid, I wis at this afore you wis oot yer cot, I'll manage fine.

That got a lauch aa roon, even the mither hidin a smile ahin her cup, and on we gaed, better humoured for it.

Aboot twa the sky ower the hill turned an affa ill colour, black as a steen and rowin in fest ower the tap, and Father wis up on his feet in a meenit.

Father: Richt, leave the neeps for the noo, we're intae the far park, aa hands, this meenit, or we'll lose the lot tae the weet.

We shiftit the hale squad ower and warked like daft, and jist as the first drops cam on the wind it seemed tae haud aff again, the clouds rowin by athoot brakkin, and we got a look o relief atween us and pushed on throu the efterneen while it held aff.

Aboot fower the auld binder jammed solid, chowed itsel up wi strae, and we lost near hauf an oor ower it, Father doon on his knees wi oil tae his elbows, sweirin at it saft-like.

Father: The vratch o a thing, it aye picks its meenit.

The youngest fund the spanner it needit fae the shed athoot bein askit twice, and it wis freed again, and on we gaed athoot mair said aboot it.

By the time the licht startit tae turn, Father cam roon aa the parks himsel, nae waitin on onybody tae come tae him.

Father: Come on noo, pit yer back intilt, we've nae lang afore the dark's on us, get it in afore we lose the licht athegither.

Naebody needit tellt twice at that either, aabody kent fit it meant, nae mair chat, jist the heids doon and the wark gaan hard.

The hinmaist bit o the hale hairst wis a wee corner ahin the steading, and by the time we got roon tae it there wis nae need for onybody tae be tellt fit tae dae, we aa converged on it thegither, the hale squad o us, and got it doon in nae time, laughin near the end o it wi the sheer relief o bein throu, the licht nearly gaen and the first stars oot ower the hill.

Fan it wis aa coontit up we'd gotten in near aa the hairst, jist a puckle rigs left ower for the morn if the weather held, and Father said it wis a gweed day's wark, ane o the best we'd hid in years, and naebody argued wi him ower that.

We aa sat doon on the steens ootside the steading efter, nae a body able tae spik muckle, legs deid aneath us and the sweat gaan cauld noo the sun wis doon ahin the hill, and the mither brocht oot the last o the tea and gied the workmate a bosie fan she sat doon, seein she wis near greetin wi tire, and naebody said a word for a fair while, jist glad tae be aff their feet, aabody's een half shut athegither.

But afore we wis lat awa tae oor beds the mither turned tae the youngest, fa wis near sleepin sittin up.

Mother: You, awa and shut in the hens afore they're aa ta'en by the fox, then ye can get tae yer bed.

And aff the youngest gaed, ower tired even tae fash aboot it, feart o naething the noo but his ain bed, and that wis the hairst dune for anither day, aathing in, and hame tae oor beds at last.
