Friend: Fit like the pair o ye? Foo's the wee een daein?

The mother: Ach, no bad, ye ken. Takkin it a day at a time.

Friend: Aye, that's the wye tae dae it.

The mother: Weel... tae tell ye the truth, since ye're askin, it's affa. Up fower, five times a nicht wi her, canna get her settled ava. Three oors sleep in a stretch if that, and that's a good nicht.

The father: And that's nae a lee either. My back's been at me aa week fae liftin her in and oot the cot, canna straichten up richt in the mornin. And look at ma hands, they winna stop shakkin, jist fae bein sae forfochen.

Friend: Ach, ye poor craiturs, that's nae wye tae live. I mind fine masel, ye're runnin on naethin.

The mother: I pit the milk in the press yesterday insteed o the fridge, didna find it till it wis fair off. And I couldna mind whaur I'd pit ma ain phone, it wis in the freezer, if ye can believe that.

Friend: (laughin) Och that's affa funny, but I'm nae surprised, honestly. Richt, I'm nae takkin nae for an answer noo. I'll come by the morn's efternoon and tak her aff yer hands a whilie, gie ye baith a sleep. And I'll bring ye ower a pot o soup and some o that stovies, ye're nae tae worry aboot cookin.

The father: Och, ye dinna hae tae dae that...

Friend: Dinna fash yersel, I'm daein it. Fit time suits ye?

The mother: (sighin, gied in) Aye... aye, gaun on then. We'd be affa gratefu, tae be honest. We're jist deen in.

Friend: That's settled syne. Get some rest, the baith o ye.
