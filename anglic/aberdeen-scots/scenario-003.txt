The father: Fit aboot Elsie, eh? Affa bonnie name, that.

The mother: Elsie? Na, canna hae that. Mind the wifie doon the stair, her dog wis cried Elsie. I'm no haein a bairn named eftir a mannie's dog that barked the hale nicht through.

The father: It wisna even his real name for it, jist fit he cried it...

The mother: Disna maitter fit he cried it, ilka time I hear the name noo I'll think on that dog. Nae.

The father: Fine, fine. Fit aboot Morag, then?

The mother: Morag's aa richt on its ain but yer sister's quine is Morag awready. We'd hae twa in the fam'ly an aabody mixin them up at every hairst supper an Christmas forivver.

The father: They bide the ither side o the toon, it's no like they'd be...

The mother: Aye, an we'd still be explainin it tae aabody till the day we dee. I'm no haein that.

The father: Weel fit aboot eftir yer ain mither, then. Cry her Jean, like yer granny wis afore her.

The mother: Cannae dae that either, ma sister's lassie's cried Jean an so's oor auntie doon the road. That's twa awready, we dinna need a third Jean in the fam'ly.

The father: Christ, ilka name I say ye've a reason it's vrang.

The mother: I'm no fashin masel ower a name that'll dae her nae favours her hale life, bein mixed up wi some ither quine or some feart wee dog.

The father: Weel fa's tae say fit it's gonna be, then? It's no me that gets it, it's no you that gets it, fa decides?

The mother: ...

The father: Aye, that's fit I thocht.

Naething settled, the pair o them sat there forfochen, the bairn asleep in atween them nae kennin she'd nae name yet, an baith ower tired the nicht tae say anither word on it.
