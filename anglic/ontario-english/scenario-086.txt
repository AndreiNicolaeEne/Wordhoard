Okay so I have to tell you about the weekend, eh, because I still owe like three different people an apology and I'm not even sure I've got the full picture straight in my head.

So it was my sister's thing, right, the big one, and she went all out, had the whole backyard done up, string lights, the works, and my mom had been cooking since like Thursday. You know how it goes. I told myself going in, I'm gonna have like two and call it, I got work Monday, I'm being a responsible adult about this. Famous last words honestly.

First it's just a beer with my brother-in-law standing by the grill, chirping him about the burgers being raw in the middle, which they were not, they were fine, but you gotta give the guy a hard time or what's the point of being family. Then my aunt shows up with a two-four like it's May two-four already, and somebody's cousin's got a mickey he's been keeping in his jacket the whole time like it's a big secret, and honestly by that point I'd lost count of what I was even drinking. There was wine at some point too, somebody's fancy bottle from a trip, and I remember thinking that was a mistake even as I said sure, pour it, no worries.

The dancing part I actually remember pretty good, that's the thing, we had the speaker going in the yard and my sister put on all the stuff from when we were kids and everyone's up dancing including my dad which never happens, and I kept telling myself okay, one more song, one more song, and then somehow it's midnight and my legs are just gone from being on them so long. Last really clear thing I've got is my sister grabbing me for a picture and saying something about how she can't believe I stayed the whole time because usually I bail early, and me telling her, no way, not tonight, I'm giving'r tonight. That's the last frame that's actually in focus. After that it's just, pieces. Somebody laughing really hard at something I apparently said. Being outside for a bit. Somebody's arm around my shoulder walking me somewhere. It's all just flashes after that, like someone's shuffling a deck and only showing me every fifth card.

Woke up the next morning and, buddy, I have never in my life wanted to just not exist for a few hours the way I did right then. Head absolutely pounding, like something with a heartbeat of its own living behind my eyes. Somebody had left the blind up a crack and that one little strip of light across the wall felt like it was personally attacking me. Every sound in the house, the fridge humming, somebody's footsteps upstairs, felt about four times louder than it had any right to be. Just the idea of sitting up took like a full minute of negotiating with myself. And food, forget it, my stomach did this slow roll just from picturing eggs, I actually had to close my eyes and breathe through it.

My mom was up already, obviously, moving around normal like a completely different species of human, and she was not about to let me suffer in peace, not a chance.

The mother: Well well well, look who's up. You want some coffee, or are we not doing solids today.

Me: Please don't talk so loud.

The mother: I'm whispering.

Me: You are not whispering.

The mother: You danced for four hours straight last night, you were giving'r out there, and now you can't handle a normal voice, that's rich.

Me: I'm begging you.

She wasn't being mean about it, she was actually laughing the whole time, but every laugh went straight through my skull like a drill. I just lay there on the couch not moving, staring at the ceiling, making this promise to myself, dead serious, lying there like a corpse, that I was never doing this again, that from now on it was two drinks and done, home by midnight, no exceptions. And even while I was making that promise I kind of knew, somewhere underneath it, that I have made that exact promise before, word for word, more than once, and here we are again, so.

The whole morning just evaporated on nothing. I tried to walk to the corner store for a Gatorade, which is like a four minute walk normally, and it took everything I had, I had to stop halfway and just stand there for a second like I'd run a marathon. Came home and even something small like loading the dishwasher felt like a chore assigned by the government. Mostly I just sat wrapped in a blanket on the couch, sipping water, eating some plain toast, no butter even, just dry, because anything richer than that and I knew where it was headed. Kept the TV on low with something I wasn't really watching and just let time pass over me.

And here's the part I keep turning over, the flashes coming back through the day, one at a time, and one of them is not great. Apparently at some point I told my sister's workmate, the one who came with her, some very blunt opinion about her ex that nobody asked for and that was absolutely not mine to give, especially not that loud, especially not in front of half the yard. I don't even remember the full thing, just the shape of it coming back to me around like two in the afternoon and going oh no. So that's an apology I still owe, and I'm dreading it a bit, because how do you even open with that, sorry for the thing I said that I don't fully remember saying but I believe you that I said it.

By evening though I finally started feeling like a person again. Ate an actual real dinner, my mom's leftovers, and it stayed down fine, and my head cleared up enough that I could hold a real conversation without wanting to lie back down. Watched the rest of the game with my dad, actual attention this time, not just noise in the background, and felt more or less steady by the time I went to bed.

So that's the whole saga. The promise still stands, for what it's worth, two drinks and out the door, and I mean it this time, same as I meant it the last four times. I know myself though, eh, next wedding or stag and doe or whatever comes along, somebody's gonna hand me something and say come on, one more, and I'll say sure, no worries, and away we'll go again. Anyway. Wish you'd been there for the good part at least, the dancing part, before it all went sideways. Next one, for sure.
