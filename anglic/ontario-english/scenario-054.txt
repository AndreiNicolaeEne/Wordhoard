Okay so you weren't around for any of this, eh, so I gotta start from the actual start. We were five of us stuffed into a three-bedroom for like two years past when we shoulda moved, the youngest still in with us cause there was nowhere else to put her, the eldest doing homework on the kitchen table cause his room was basically a closet with a bed jammed in, and my mother-in-law down in the basement half the year when she came in from out east, sleeping on a pull-out next to the furnace. Everybody tripping over everybody. So finally one night in the spring the wife and I sat down at this table, same table the homework was getting done on, and said okay, we're doing the addition, we're putting a proper second storey up the back and giving these kids their own rooms before somebody loses their mind.

We figured it out that night, actual pen and paper, who we'd need and how long. My brother-in-law does electrical for a living so he was in no question. I got a buddy who frames houses for money, he said he'd give us weekends. Figured with him and me and maybe one more set of hands we'd have it up in like four months, give'r hard on the weekends, maybe finish before the snow. Eleven months later I'm laughing thinking about that number, but hey, you gotta start somewhere with a guess.

First real mess happened before we'd even got a wall up. Buddy and I set the footings ourselves off a string line we were sure was square, and it was off. Not by a little either. Found out two weeks in when the inspector come by and just stood there looking at it, didn't even say much, just kind of tilted his head. So we're sorry, we're sorry, we'll fix it, and we tore the forms out and did it all again from scratch. Two weeks gone just like that before we even had anything to show for it. That one stung.

Then there was this one Saturday, maybe six weeks in, we're out there with the framing barely started and I look up and there's the neighbour from three doors down, this buddy I barely knew past waving at him getting the mail, standing in the yard in work boots with his own drill. Didn't call him, didn't ask him, he just seen the lumber pile and figured we could use another back. His kid come too, seventeen, wanted to learn, kept saying sorry every time he handed you the wrong screw like it was gonna kill somebody. That was a good day. No worries, buddy, get in here.

June we lost pretty much the whole week to rain. Not a shower, just day after day of it, the tarps we had over the sub-floor filling up like a pool and us bailing them out every morning before work even started. Couldn't pour, couldn't frame wet lumber and expect it to sit right, so we just sat there drinking coffee under the carport watching it come down, everybody in a mood.

Round about August we ran short on the two-by-sixes, had the order in but the yard was backed up and wasn't coming for another ten days, and we had a wall we needed up before the weather turned again. So we made do, used what was kicking around, some deck boards from the old deck we'd torn off, some stuff my buddy had left over from a job of his own, sistered it all together so the inspector wouldn't cry about it later. Wasn't pretty for that one section but it held, and honestly you can't tell now unless you know exactly where to look.

Somewhere in there I come off the scaffold. Wasn't high, maybe ten feet, but it's enough. Went to reach for a piece my brother-in-law was passing up and my foot just wasn't where I thought it was, and down I went into the dirt, landed mostly on my shoulder and a pile of insulation bags, which probably saved me honestly. Whole yard went dead quiet for a second. The wife come running out of the house white as anything. I'm laying there going I'm fine, I'm fine, just knocked the wind out, and she's not buying it for a second, made me sit down for an hour with an ice pack even though there was nothing on me but a bruise the size of a dinner plate. Scared the life out of everybody, me included, but nothing worse come of it. Toque stayed on the whole time somehow, never even fell off.

Had a real go-around with my buddy over the eavestrough, of all things. He wanted it run one way off the back roof, I wanted the other, and it turned into this whole chirping match out there with the two of us up on ladders yelling past each other about pitch and where the downspout should land, him deking around every point I made like we were still in beer league. Went on for a good hour before the wife come out and just said, boys, it's a gutter, and somehow that ended it. We did it his way. He was right, for the record, not that I told him that for a while.

Worst part of the whole thing was the load-bearing wall. Middle of September, my brother-in-law's up there running conduit and he notices the top plate isn't sitting flush, and we get the level on it and the whole wall's out, framed off that same bad string line from way back at the start probably, nobody caught it till then. Had to strip the whole thing back to studs and do it over completely, all of it, in a weekend we didn't have spare. That one took the wind out of me more than the fall did honestly.

But then the day the frame finally stood, whole thing, roof trusses up and braced, that was a beauty. Stood in the yard with everybody, my brother-in-law, my buddy, the neighbour, his kid, the wife's cousin who'd come by a few times to help hang drywall later, a guy from my work who'd shown up twice just cause he liked doing it, six of us total who'd put hands on this thing over the months, and somebody cracked a two-four right there in the yard and we just stood looking up at it. My mother-in-law brought out a plate of butter tarts she'd made that morning like she knew. Kids ate ketchup chips off the tailgate. Felt like the whole thing turned a corner that day.

Last stretch, last four nights before we needed it done, we were out there past dark every single one of them, work lights strung off the porch, everybody in toques even though it wasn't that cold, just cause it felt like the thing to wear. Give'r till eleven, midnight one of those nights, trim going up, washroom fixtures getting set, the wife running back and forth with coffee and eventually just handing out mickeys straight from somebody's glovebox cause nobody was driving anywhere anyway.

And then it just was done. Walked through it that morning before anybody else was up, just me, hand on the new wall, and it didn't feel real yet. Eleven months, six people who'd given up weekends and evenings and one whole scared afternoon, from a spring night at that kitchen table to standing here in a room that didn't exist a year before.

Now the eldest's got his own door to slam, the youngest's got a room with her name taped on it, we play euchre up there some nights now instead of downstairs, and every so often I'll just stand in the hallway up top for no reason at all, just to feel it holding. It's ours, every crooked first try and every fix of it. Sorry, gonna sneak past ya, somebody'll say, squeezing down that hall with a laundry basket, and it still gets me a little, how normal that sounds now, like the space was always there waiting on us.
