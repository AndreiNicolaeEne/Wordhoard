Okay so you missed it, eh, and honestly I'm still recovering. So this year I put my hand up to host, which in hindsight, hoo boy. Every other year it's been my mother's place or my aunt's, and I always just show up with a bag of chips and my good coat and that's my whole contribution. But my apartment's finally big enough, the table's big enough if you squint, and I thought, you know what, give'r, I'll do it this year. Soon as I said it out loud to the family group chat I regretted it a little. Like immediately my stomach dropped, sorry, but it did.

So the nerves start basically that same night. I couldn't sleep, I'm lying there doing math on how many people can actually sit at my table versus how many people are going to show up regardless of what the table can hold. I drew up this menu that was, in retrospect, unhinged. Turkey, ham as a backup in case the turkey went sideways, mashed and scalloped both because somebody's always in a mood about one or the other, a whole separate vegetarian situation for my cousin's partner, stuffing, gravy from scratch because I'm an idiot, butter tarts for after because obviously, and I figured I'd do a pie too on top of that. My workmate looked at the list at lunch one day and just went:

Workmate: Buddy. That's not a menu, that's a shift.

And she wasn't wrong. But I didn't scale it back, because that would've required admitting I overreached, and I wasn't there yet.

Then the guest count. It started as me, my parents, my brother and his girlfriend, so six with me. Fine, manageable. Then my aunt asks if she can bring my uncle since they're not doing their own thing this year, sure, no worries, eight. Then my cousin texts, hey can she bring her partner, of course, ten. Then my brother's girlfriend goes, oh also her sister's in town, is that okay, and by this point I'm just saying yes to everybody because saying no felt rude, and we land at fourteen people in an apartment that comfortably seats eight. Fourteen, eh. For a table meant for eight.

Shopping trip was its own event. I did the big one two days before, whole cart, took me forever, and everything went fine except for one thing, I could not for the life of me find a turkey the size I needed. Every grocery store I hit either had these massive ones meant for twenty or these little ones like it was a turkey for one guy eating alone on a Tuesday. Nothing in between. I drove around to like four different places, the one closer to the highway, the one by the DVP, this whole loop, and finally found a decent-sized one at the very last place I checked, and I near cried in the parking lot, not even joking.

Night before, I'm up prepping till like one in the morning. Chopping, pre-measuring, writing myself notes, doing the stuffing mix, peeling a mountain of potatoes. I even wrote out an actual schedule, like an hour-by-hour thing, turkey in at this time, potatoes on at this time, table set by this time, very organized, very proud of myself, taped it right to the fridge.

That schedule did not survive contact with the actual morning. Not even a little bit. Turkey needed longer than I planned because of course it did, the oven runs cooler than it says it does apparently, my mother calls at eight in the morning just to check in which throws off my whole rhythm, and by nine thirty I'm already forty minutes behind my own paper plan and just staring at the fridge like it personally betrayed me.

Then the actual disaster hits. I had the stuffing in one pan and I put the dinner rolls in for what I thought was a quick reheat, got distracted dealing with the gravy going lumpy on me, and by the time I remembered the rolls they were basically charcoal. Just done. Black on the bottom, smoke coming out when I opened the oven, the smoke detector goes off, the whole thing. I'm standing there going, oh no, oh no no no, and my brother's girlfriend's sister, who'd shown up a solid hour early because she wanted to help set up, just walks straight into the kitchen without even taking her coat off and goes:

The one who came early: Okay, don't panic, do you have a second bag of flour, I'll just make a fresh batch, go deal with your gravy.

And honest to god, bless her, she just took over the rolls right then and there like she lived in my kitchen, apron and everything, didn't even ask twice, just gonna sneak past ya she said at one point squeezing by me at the stove, and within like forty minutes there's fresh rolls rising like nothing happened. Absolute lifesaver, that.

Meanwhile my one oven is trying to hold a turkey, scalloped potatoes, and now a second round of rolls all at the same time, and my stovetop's got the gravy, the mashed potatoes, and a pot of green beans all going at once, and I've got exactly four burners and about six things that need one. It was chaos. Pure chaos. Pots getting shuffled around, someone holding a lid over something just to keep it warm on the counter because there's nowhere else for it to go.

But then, the one bright spot, the vegetarian dish I threw together kind of last minute, this squash and chickpea thing I wasn't even sure about, came out an absolute beauty. Like genuinely better than anything else on that table. I wasn't expecting much from it and it stole the show a bit, which, fine by me.

So dinner was supposed to start at one, we actually sat down closer to two thirty, everybody standing around with drinks in hand the whole time, somebody's uncle cracking a two-four in the kitchen, somebody else with a mickey they'd brought along just in case, real relaxed vibe honestly even with the delay. Once we finally sat and people started digging in, the reactions came fast. My father goes, first bite of turkey:

Father: Oh, that's not bad, that's actually not bad at all.

Which from him is basically a standing ovation. My aunt tries the gravy and goes:

Aunt: This is way better than mine, don't tell your mother I said that.

But then my uncle, bless him, takes one bite of the scalloped potatoes and just puts his fork down and goes:

Uncle: Yeah these are a bit sad, eh. Bit dry. Sorry, just being honest.

No sugar coating from that man, ever, and everyone kind of laughed because it was true, they'd dried out a bit sitting under foil too long waiting for everything else to catch up.

But that squash and chickpea dish, gone. Completely gone within like ten minutes, people going back for seconds on a side dish, my cousin's partner practically fighting her own girlfriend for the last spoonful, everyone going on about it, beauty, this is amazing, who made this. Loudest praise of the whole meal, hands down, for the dish I almost didn't even bother making.

By the time the butter tarts came out everyone was leaning back, undoing top buttons, that whole post-turkey stupor setting in, and my mother turns to me and goes, only half serious:

Mother: So. Same time next year?

And I looked around at my kitchen, which looked like a hydro line had exploded in it, dishes stacked to the ceiling, flour on the floor, and I just went, ask me again in like a week, and everyone laughed, and honestly, yeah. Probably. Give'r again next year. Just maybe with a smaller turkey and somebody else on rolls duty.
