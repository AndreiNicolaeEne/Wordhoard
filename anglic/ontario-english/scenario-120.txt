Okay so you weren't around for any of this, eh, so I gotta start from the actual beginning or none of it makes sense. So I says to everybody, like at my cousin's stag and doe even, I go, I'm doing the marathon this year. Full one, not the half. And I hadn't run one single step yet. Not one. I just opened my mouth at a table full of people with a two-four half gone already and told the whole room, and after that there was no quiet way outta it, right, because my aunt's already going "oh that's so great" and posting it and everything, so now I'm locked in, I gotta actually do the thing.

First couple weeks, oh my god. I did not expect that. You think, I walk everywhere, I'm not in bad shape, how bad can it be. Real bad, is the answer. Every single session left me more wrecked than the one before, like there was no bottom to it. I'd get home and just lay on the kitchen floor for a bit, hydro bill be damned, I had the fan going full blast on me right there on the tile. My legs the next morning, sorry, I couldn't even do the stairs proper, I was doing this weird sideways shuffle down them like a crab or something.

But then it kinda settled, you know, after those first few weeks of dying. I got myself a routine and I just stuck to it most days, whatever was going on outside. Snow, didn't matter. Rain off the lake so it's basically sideways, didn't matter. I'd just give'r regardless, out the door, toque on, gloves on, go. Some mornings I did not want to, not even a little, but you just go anyway, that's kind of the whole job at that point.

Then partway in I got hit with this thing, my hip or something, some overuse thing, and honest to god it near ended it before it even properly started. I had to stop cold, no running at all, for like three, four weeks, and for a stretch there I genuinely did not think I was getting back to it. Just laying around thinking, well, guess I told the whole family I'm doing a marathon and now I'm doing zero training, beauty, real smooth move. But it came around eventually and once I did get back into it I cut the plan right back, way more careful, none of that "make up for lost time" nonsense, just slow and sensible.

Around then a buddy from work started training for the same one, so for a good chunk of the middle of it we were doing our long runs together, and honest to god that made all the difference on the days it needed making. Like some mornings you're both just miserable and cold and neither of us is saying much, just kind of running along not talking, but having somebody there beside you not talking is a whole different thing than being out there alone not talking, if that makes sense.

One day though. One day just about finished the both of us. Long run, way out past where we usually go, and everything went wrong at once, like the body just quit and the head quit right along with it same time. I remember stopping dead on the sidewalk somewhere and my buddy's like

The training partner: you good?

Me: no.

The training partner: yeah, me neither.

And we just kind of stood there a second not sure what happens next, whether the whole thing's off. That one stuck with me way more than anything after it, honestly.

Middle of the whole season is where the doubt really landed hardest on me, like the actual race day still felt so far off it didn't even feel real, it felt like something happening to some other person eventually, not me. You're putting in all this work for a thing you can't picture yet. That's a rough stretch mentally, way rougher than any one training day, if I'm being honest with you.

Then not that long after, nothing huge, just one regular Tuesday run that went better than any run before it, felt smooth, felt like my legs remembered how to do the thing without me fighting them the whole way, and that one session kind of put the wind back in it for me. Small thing. Renewed the whole outlook though.

Toward the end I tightened everything right up, watching what I ate way more, actually sleeping on purpose instead of just falling asleep watching something, and then that last week I backed right off instead of cramming more in, which felt so wrong the whole time, like I'm not doing enough, but you gotta trust the months already in the bank at that point, there's no more fitness showing up in seven days.

Night before I barely slept a wink, just laying there in the dark running the whole route in my head over and over for absolutely no reason, like reviewing it wasn't gonna change one single thing about how the legs were gonna feel at kilometre thirty.

And then the day of it, honestly the whole first half just kind of happens, you're carried along with everybody, feels almost easy, and you're thinking oh this is fine, this is beauty, no worries. And then it flips. Last stretch, the last six or seven k, that's where it actually got me, everything in me wanting to just stop right there on the road and walk it in, and I didn't, I don't fully know how, I just didn't. Legs going, head going, some guy chirping his buddy up ahead trying to get him to keep pace and it's helping me too somehow, deking around slower folks near the end just trying to hold whatever was left together.

And then it's just done. And I gotta tell you, nothing like what I pictured. I thought there'd be this big feeling, arms up, whatever. There wasn't, really. I just kind of stopped and stood there and felt sort of hollowed right out, no big emotion at all at first, just done, just quiet, like my body finally got the memo it's allowed to stop now and it didn't know what to do with that information. Took a good while after, sitting on a curb somewhere with a space blanket on, before it even felt like something happened.

Days after, oh, my body was not mine again for a good while. Stairs were a joke for like four straight days, I was doing that same sideways shuffle again, and it took the better part of a week before I felt normal walking around like a regular person instead of somebody's grandpa.

But I'll tell you, it changed what I think I can do, straight up. Before all that I'd have said no way, not a chance, that's not me. Now I know different. Doesn't matter that the middle of it near broke me, doesn't matter I basically had to start twice. I got through it. So next time somebody says something's impossible I just think, yeah, we'll see about that, eh.
