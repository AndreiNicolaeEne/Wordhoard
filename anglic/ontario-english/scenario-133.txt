Okay so, the plot. I been on that wait list at the community garden since like three years back, and I finally get the email in February going congrats, you're in, and I'm like finally, eh, thought they forgot about me completely.

Spent most of March just thinking about what to put in, going back and forth with the guy who's got the plot next to mine, he's been doing this like eight years so I figured I'd pick his brain a bit.

Him: You gotta do tomatoes, everybody does tomatoes, but do the cherry ones too, they don't get the blight as bad.
Me: Yeah for sure, and I want to do some beans, my mom always did beans up the fence.
Him: Beans are a beauty crop, honestly, they just take care of themselves.

So that's basically the plan, tomatoes, beans, some peppers, a row of carrots I definitely regretted later because carrots are so much work for like nothing, and zucchini because everybody tells you one zucchini plant is plenty and I did not listen.

Put everything in the third week of May, after the long weekend, my dad always said don't plant before the two-four, the frost'll getcha, and he was not wrong once in his life about it so I waited. Then we got this run of just gorgeous weather right through into June, sunny every day, warm but not brutal yet, and everything just took off. The tomatoes were already up past my knee by Canada Day and I was feeling pretty good about myself, ordering my Timmies on the way to the plot every morning like I was some kind of farmer, double-double in one hand, watering can in the other.

First thing that went sideways was aphids on the beans, little green guys all over the undersides of the leaves, just swarming, sorry, it was disgusting honestly. I tried the dish soap and water spray everybody online says works, and it did work, kind of, knocked the population down but they just kept coming back every week or so, like they were commuting in off the 401 for a shift. Never fully got rid of them the whole season, just kept it manageable, spraying every few days, chirping them back every time they'd try and make a comeback.

Then July hits and it just does not rain. Like three and a half weeks, nothing, and hydro bills aside I'm now hauling water in from the tap at the far end of the lot every single day after work, two big jugs at a time, back and forth, back and forth, give'r basically every evening just to keep the tomatoes from wilting right over. My arms were done. That's when the whole thing with the water turn started, cause there's only the two taps for like forty plots and everyone's supposed to go by the sign-up sheet, and the fella two rows down, buddy just starts filling up whenever he feels like it, doesn't matter whose slot it is.

Me: Hey, so, I think that's actually my time right now, eh, I signed up for six to six-thirty.
Him: Oh, sorry, I didn't see the sheet.
Me: No worries, just, it's brutal dry right now so everybody's kinda counting on their slot.
Him: Yeah no for sure, my bad.

That worked for like a day and then he was back at it, filling his can right through my time again, and I'll be honest it was getting to me, I was chirping about it to my workmate at lunch every day that week. Finally the woman who kinda runs the sign-up list, unofficial garden captain type, she calls like an actual little meeting by the shed, maybe six of us standing around with our watering cans like it's a hostage negotiation, and we just redid the whole sheet with tighter time blocks and put it in a plastic sleeve so it stops blowing away and getting "lost." After that it was fine, honestly, the guy turned out to be alright, we ended up trading tomatoes for his extra basil later in the summer so no hard feelings in the end.

Best producer by a mile, no contest, was the zucchini, obviously, the one I planted too many of. That thing gave'r all season, I was leaving bags of zucchini on my neighbours' porches like it was some kind of problem I was trying to offload, which honestly it was. Beauty crop if you don't need more than like three of them ever in your life.

Meanwhile the carrots got hit with rot, the tops looked fine but I pulled a bunch up in August and they were just mush underneath, soft and gross, I think I overwatered trying to fight the dry spell and then it swung back the other way. Lost probably half the row to it. Live and learn I guess, sorry carrots, you did not deserve that treatment but also you were a pain the whole way through so I wasn't too broken up.

Then mid-August we get that wind storm come through, real fast, real hard, one of those ones where the sky goes green and you're like okay something's happening here. Flattened like four rows across the whole garden, mine included, the tomato cages just tipped right over, stakes snapped, beans hanging off the fence in this sad little tangle. Spent the whole next Saturday out there with the guy next door actually, the water tap guy, both of us just staking everything back up, tying it off with old pantyhose cause that's apparently what you use, who knew, propping the tomato cages back with rebar so it wouldn't happen twice. Took like four hours and we were both a mess after, dirt everywhere, but it was actually kind of a good afternoon, we had the radio going, talked hockey the whole time, deking around the actual work half of it if I'm honest.

End of the season I finally sat down and did the tally, just for my own curiosity, and it worked out to way more than I figured going in. Tomatoes did great once they recovered, beans kept going right into September even with the aphid situation, zucchini obviously never stopped, and I traded a giant grocery bag of it plus some of the tomatoes to a lady two plots over for a whole bunch of her butter tarts and some of the raspberries off her canes, which honestly might've been the better end of that deal, her tarts are unreal.

Closed the plot down first weekend of October, pulled the dead stuff, dug the compost in, threw a tarp over the bed so it's not just bare dirt sitting there all winter getting compacted. Stood there for a minute after with my toque on, first cold morning of the year, and honestly, for a first year, I'd call it a win. Wasn't perfect, lost the carrots, fought the bugs the whole way through, hauled more water than I ever want to haul again, but the wins outweighed it easy. Already thinking about what I'm doing different next spring, less carrots for sure, maybe one zucchini plant instead of three, but yeah. Good year. Beauty of a year, actually.
