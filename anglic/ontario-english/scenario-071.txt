Okay so. You know that big silver maple, the one that hung right over the fence line between the two houses on the corner, the one that dumped leaves in everybody's eavestrough every single October so bad you could hear people complaining about it at the mailbox. Windstorm come through Tuesday night, like four in the morning, this huge crack, and by the time anybody was up for work the whole top half of it was down. Laying clean across three driveways, tangled right into the hydro line, one big limb resting half on the corner house's garage roof. Nobody was getting to work that morning without moving a tree first, and I mean that's not a one-household job, not even close. Not with a hand saw and good intentions.

So it's the guy two doors down that gets it started, the one with the pickup and the trailer hitch, out there at like seven still in his work boots just standing there looking up at it with his coffee going cold. And he doesn't even finish his coffee before he's knocking on like six doors. Word went up and down that street faster than the group chat, I swear to god, faster than anything, cause by eight-thirty there's people coming out of houses I didn't even know had people living in them. We figured maybe eight guys, ten tops, with a couple chainsaws between them. Ended up more like thirty people out there by nine. Way, way over what anybody was expecting, and half of them showed up with their own gloves already on like they'd been waiting for an excuse.

Work kind of sorted itself out without anybody really deciding it, eh. Couple guys on saws doing the cuts, a whole rope-and-hauling crew dragging the cut pieces off to the side, somebody directing traffic so nobody backed a car into the mess, and then just this endless line of people stacking brush at the curb. But it was the guy with the pickup doing the real heavy end of it all day, the winch, the biggest rounds, up on the garage roof at one point checking the shingles didn't get punched through, and by two he'd taken his jacket off and you could see steam coming off him even though it wasn't that warm out.

Had one bad moment around eleven, actually. Big limb they thought was clear swung wrong when they cut it and took out somebody's whole flower bed and put a good dent in a garbage can that went flying clean across the yard. Nobody hurt, thank god, but that got everybody's heart going for a second there, everybody just froze. And that's when the argument kicked off, cause one guy's saying you gotta drop it in sections, take your time, and buddy with the truck's saying just hook the winch on and give'r, we'll be at this till dark otherwise. Bit of chirping back and forth for a few minutes there, pretty heated actually, but the guy whose flower bed it was just sort of laughed it off, said no worries, wasn't much of a flower bed anyway, and that took the wind out of it. Ten minutes later they were working together like nothing happened.

Households kept the whole thing fed all day, that's the other thing. Somebody's mother came out with a whole tray of butter tarts before ten in the morning, no joke. Another house did a run to Timmies for like fifteen double-doubles and a couple boxes of Timbits, and by the afternoon somebody'd cracked a two-four and there was talk of a mickey somebody had sitting in the glovebox for after, once the saws stopped. Bag of ketchup chips and a bag of all-dressed both got passed around and were empty inside of ten minutes. The kids got put on kindling duty, hauling the small stuff, the twigs and the little branches, over to the curb pile in a wagon, and they took that job dead serious, like it was the most important part of the whole operation.

The oldest guy on the street, must be pushing eighty, wasn't having anybody tell him to sit this one out either. Kept saying he could still hold a rope as good as anybody, and honestly he wasn't wrong, he ended up on rope duty most of the day and wouldn't let anybody spell him off it even when his hands were clearly done.

And then there was the one bit everybody still laughing about tonight, which is the guy's toque, the pickup guy, got knocked clean off his head by a branch coming down and landed right in the middle of the brush pile, and he didn't notice for like twenty minutes, kept working bare-headed in the wind, and when somebody finally pointed it out he had to dig through the whole pile to find it again while everybody just stood there howling. Nothing even that funny about it looking back, but in the moment the whole street was in tears.

Figured the whole thing would be wrapped by one, maybe two at the latest. Ended up going till almost five. Three hours over what anybody guessed, easy. But by the end that tree was gone, I mean completely gone, cut down to rounds and split, whole boulevard cleared, three driveways you could actually pull a car into again, garage roof checked out fine, hydro come by and confirmed the line was okay. Whole street cheered when that last big round finally came off the winch, actual cheering, people clapping.

And the street just felt different after, even just for that one evening. Somebody dragged a card table out onto the boulevard and got a euchre game going right there on the grass, mickey came out properly, kids still running around even though it was getting dark, and everybody just sort of hung around out front of their own houses instead of going in. Felt like a whole different street than the one from that morning, even if it's probably back to normal by tomorrow.
