Me: I don't even know where to start, eh, so I'm just gonna start.

The first few days, honestly, I don't remember a lot of it clear. It's not like the movies where you're bawling on the floor the whole time. It's more like you go numb, right, and everybody's in and out of the house, bringing casseroles, dropping off flowers nobody knows what to do with, your aunt's on the phone in the kitchen sorting out the funeral home, somebody's kid is running around because nobody could get a sitter, and you're just sort of standing there being handed things to sign. There's so many arrangements, so many people, so much coming and going, that there's almost no room in the day to actually feel it. You're too busy saying thanks for coming, sorry, can I get you a coffee, no it's no trouble, there's a whole pot on. It's like grief gets scheduled around.

And then everybody goes home, eh. That's the part nobody warns you about. The funeral's done, the last of the family's pulled out of the driveway, somebody's left half a butter tart on a plate on the counter, and the house goes quiet in a way that just about knocks you over. I remember standing in the front hall with my coat still on and just, nothing. No sound. No hydro humming, no fridge, nothing, or maybe there was and I just couldn't hear past my own ears ringing. That silence hit way harder than the funeral did. The funeral had people in it. The house after didn't have anybody in it but me.

My body did its own thing for weeks after, too. I wasn't eating, like, at all some days, and then I'd eat a whole box of KD standing over the stove at eleven at night for no reason. Sleep was the same, all broken up, an hour here, forty minutes there, wide awake at four in the morning staring at the ceiling. Never one solid stretch of anything. You'd think exhausted enough you'd just go down, but no, your body doesn't care what it's supposed to do.

The first month was the strangest part for me, honest to God. Everybody else's world just kept going, right, like the 401's still backed up at five, Timmies still has a line at the drive-thru, people are still complaining about the weather, and you're standing there thinking, how is any of this still happening. You feel like you got left behind somewhere and the whole city just kept driving.

And then there was this one random Tuesday, nothing marked on the calendar, no anniversary, no nothing, just an ordinary day, and it wrecked me completely. No warning at all. I couldn't even tell you after why that day and not some other one. Grief doesn't check the calendar first, apparently.

Months in, it'd still get me sideways sometimes. A smell, right out of nowhere, somebody's laundry, or a certain kind of coffee, or just some dumb little object, a toque in the front closet that wasn't mine to keep but nobody moved it, and boom, you're right back at square one for an hour. No pattern to it. Could be a Tuesday, could be at work, could be standing in line at the grocery store.

Then there's the long stretch in the middle, eh, and that's the part I don't think people talk about enough. It's not the sharp stuff, it's the fog. Months of just being kind of dulled out. Good days would come along, actual good days, and you'd still feel like you were watching them through glass. Nothing was terrible, nothing was great, you were just sort of getting through it, going through the motions, doing your job, doing the dishes, not really landing anywhere.

Somebody said something to me during that stretch that still bugs me, some coworker, meant well probably, said something like "well at least you had time to prepare", like that helps, like that means anything. Stung way worse than it should've, and I still think about it. But then there was this other thing, small, nobody planned it, a buddy from work just showed up one night with a two-four and didn't say much of anything, just sat with me on the porch for a couple hours. Didn't try to fix it, didn't say the right words, there aren't any right words. That did more for me than any of the official stuff, the cards, the flowers, any of it.

The anniversary I dreaded for weeks beforehand, for sure. Weeks of just this low hum of dread building up. And then the day came and I got through it. Clumsy, not pretty, I cried in the washroom at one point for no good reason, but I got through it, and that was the whole point, right. Getting through was enough. Nobody said it had to be graceful.

Appetite and sleep, they came back slow, and not even, like, in order. Some weeks I'd sleep fine and still couldn't eat proper. Other weeks the opposite. It wasn't a straight line, not even close.

There was a day, though, months and months in, where I laughed. Like really laughed, not the polite kind. And right behind it came this guilt that just about flattened me, like how dare you laugh, how dare that feel good. Nobody invited that guilt in, it just showed up.

By the time the year was out I was carrying it different. Lighter in spots, for sure, some days you'd almost forget for a minute. But it's not gone. It's never actually gone, it just changes shape on you.

I guess what I want you to know, sitting here, is it doesn't stay this hard forever. There's no set timeline on it, no worries about getting it "right," there isn't a right. And whatever your year looks like, don't go measuring it against mine or anybody else's. Yours is just gonna be yours.
