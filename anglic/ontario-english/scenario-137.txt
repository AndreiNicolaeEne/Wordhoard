We got back from the Tim's run and the whole corner of the porch was standing in water, right down along the fresh eavestrough, the one that went in the day before.

Foreman: That's yours, eh. You hung that corner Tuesday, I watched you do it.

Me: Wasn't me.

Foreman: Don't be doing that with me, I stood right there and watched you do it.

Me: Wasn't me.

The labourer just kept stirring his double-double, looking off at the fence like it was the most interesting fence in the whole city. The other apprentice, the one who'd actually been up that ladder half the afternoon while I was hauling shingles off the truck, didn't say a word either.

Foreman: You were up there all afternoon, buddy, don't tell me it wasn't you.

Me: I swear to God I never touched that corner.

He kept at it a bit, chirping me in front of the two of them like saying it twice was gonna make it true, and I just stood there, didn't give him a thing, no face on it, nothing to grab onto. Sorry, said the labourer, stepping around me for his thermos, not sorry for anything really, just sorry the way it comes out here for existing near a person.

Nobody said it wasn't me. Nobody said a word, either of them. Foreman just shook his head, muttered something about how he give'r all day for this kind of thing back, and I picked up my bag and walked off the porch, out to the truck, left it exactly like that, eh. Wasn't gonna stand there and beg a man to believe me twice.
