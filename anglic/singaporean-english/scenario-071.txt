Wah, today damn tired, my legs like not mine one already. But let me tell you what happen, because I keep thinking about it whole evening also.

Last night the storm very heavy, whole sky thunder like that non-stop, you hear or not. That kind of storm, every time also like that, branch drop here drop there. This morning I go down to throw rubbish, see the whole car park corner blocked, the big rain tree, the one that shade half the motorbike lots and lean over the covered walkway to the coffeeshop, one whole branch snap and come crashing down. Not small branch ah. Damn thick one, two grown men also cannot hug, is it. Half fell right across the walkway roof, zinc all dent, and the other half just lying across both exit, no car can go out, no motorbike can squeeze past. That kind of job, one family how to clear alone. Need chainsaw, need manpower, need lobang for a lorry to cart the big rounds away. Confirm plus chop too big for one household.

So the committee uncle, you know the one, always first to complain in the group chat if the corridor light flicker, he go down early, see the damage, and straight away call. Not fancy call ah, just walk along the corridor shouting up, "Eh! Big tree fall, come down help lah!" Same time he throw the message into the block chat. I tell you that message spread so fast, within maybe twenty minutes half the block already scrolling, some still in pyjama but already coming down the stairs.

Early on got maybe seven, eight people standing round scratching head, that's the usual regulars you always see at the void deck. But by half past eight, wah, more and more people keep coming down what. Some our block, some from the next block also say they heard got tree fall, want to come help. In the end must be near thirty people standing there, way more than anybody expect, well over a dozen extra easy. The committee uncle himself keep saying, "Where got I expect this many come, sian, now must find enough gloves for everybody leh." One auntie from the next block only found out after, come running down complaining, "Bojio! Why nobody call me earlier," even though she stay two blocks away.

Work divided out quite natural after that. Some people sweep sweep, rake rake, pile up the small branches and leaf at the side. Some carry tools, fetch water, hold the ladder steady. Auntie already chope one bench near the void deck with a pack of tissue, for whoever need to sit and catch breath. But the heaviest part, the main trunk, the thick branches, that fell on the guy who do renovation work, my neighbour's husband. Everybody just call him for this kind of thing because he got the chainsaw and he very strong one. Whole morning he the one sawing, dragging, lifting the heaviest piece almost by himself, shirt wet through, but never once say sian.

Then small disaster happen. Halfway through, they trying pull down one stubborn branch still hanging half broken above the walkway, and when it finally give way, it swing the wrong side and, bang, knock straight into somebody's motorbike parked below, scratch the whole side panel and knock the mirror clean off. Alamak, some auntie scream, wah lau somebody shout. Heng ah nobody standing there at that moment, just suay for that one motorbike. Owner come running down after hearing, face already very black, but what to do, already happen liao.

That one lead to the argument. The renovation guy want just saw off the whole remaining branch from the base first, fast, get it over with. Committee uncle insist must cut from the top down bit by bit, say safer, don't want another motorbike or worse a person kena hit. Two of them getting quite loud, one saying the other blur like sotong, don't understand physics; the other saying don't so kiasu lah, we not doing rocket science. Somebody at the side mutter, buay tahan the arguing already, can or not just start. Everybody around also giving opinion, quite kancheong for a while. In the end the old auntie who stay at the corner unit just walk over with a whole tray of kopi-o and teh-c peng and say loud, "Aiyo, drink first, argue later lah, tree also won't run away." Everybody laugh a bit, tension broke, they settle on the slower way after that.

Food and drink, wah, non-stop whole morning into afternoon. That auntie brought out the kopi and teh for whoever want, another household brought a whole tin of biscuit and some curry puff still warm, somebody wife tapao economy rice for like fifteen people near lunchtime because by then nobody want stop and go up cook. Chin chai lah, just makan what got, nobody fussy that day, agak agak enough for everyone can already.

The children must give something to do also, otherwise just run around underfoot. So the small ones got the job of picking up the little twig and dead leaf into the rubbish bag, and stacking the smaller branch piece neat by the roadside for town council to collect later. They take it damn seriously, some even competing who can fill bag fastest.

And the oldest one there, must be near ninety, the grandfather from the flat below mine, his son want him just sit at the bench and watch, but he not having it. "Die die must help what," he say, and go take over holding the rubbish bag open for people to throw branch in, and every now and then shout instruction nobody ask for. "Cut there, cut there!" Nobody dare tell him sit down after two round of that, so he just carry on, happy like that.

The moment everybody laugh together same time, wah, that one I still remember. Halfway through clearing the thick part of the trunk, they found a whole nest of kitten curled up inside a hollow part of the branch, mother cat nowhere to be seen, four little one just blinking at everybody like nothing happen. The renovation guy, big strong man, chainsaw and all, suddenly very soft, carry them out one by one like carrying egg, and everybody just look look at him laughing at how gentle he suddenly become after being so fierce about the sawing just now. Somebody even joke, "Eh where your fierce gone, catch no ball how you become like that," and he just paiseh, laughing along with everybody.

Job supposed finish by lunchtime, everybody thought maybe two three hour max. In the end, by the time they gostan the lorry out with the last big round loaded, and the walkway swept clean, already close to six in the evening, a good three hour later than anybody expect. Tally what actually got done also not bad leh: whole car park exit clear, both motorbike lot usable again, walkway swept and the broken zinc panel tie down temporary until town council come fix properly, even the scratched motorbike got somebody who know a bit about panel look at it, so not so jialat in the end. When they drag the very last big piece to the roadside, everybody just cheer, some clap, the grandfather cheer loudest of all, fist up like he personally chop down the whole tree himself. Shiok that feeling, I tell you.

That evening the whole street felt different leh. People who never really greet each other before, that evening nodding, chatting a bit at the void deck, some even carrying leftover food round to share again. Maybe by tomorrow everybody go back to their own business like usual. But that one evening, our whole street felt like one family already, even if only for a while.
