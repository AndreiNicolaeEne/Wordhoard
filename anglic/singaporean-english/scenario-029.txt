Wah, you really want to know ah, what happen every morning in this house? Okay lah, sit down, let me tell you, but don't laugh halfway through.

Must start with my father. That one always first up, always, before the alarm even ring, before the sky even properly light one. Nobody teach him, nobody wake him, he just open eye like that, every single day, rain or shine, weekday or not. First thing he do, very quiet one, he go to the corner where we keep the little altar, light three joss stick, put his hands together for a while, don't say much, just stand there. The whole flat still dark, still got that just-woke smell, and he's the only one moving, shuffling around in his singlet and shorts, slippers going pak pak pak on the floor. I never see him skip that, not once.

Then my mother, she's the second one up, and every morning she wake to the exact same grumble, I tell you, like clockwork. The water heater.

Mother: Wah lau, why the water not hot again, your father use up all the hot water is it? Sian ah, everyday like that.

Same complaint, same tone, every morning also, like she recording it and playing back. And right after that, the real war starts, the wash-room. My mother and my eldest, they have this standing contest, never settle, never will. My eldest go in first, lock the door, and inside the shower can run so long you think she migrating there. My mother banging on the door already.

Mother: Oi, you doing what inside, bathing or diving, hurry up lah, other people also need to use what!

The eldest: Coming, coming, almost done liao!

The eldest: Can I get five more minutes?

Mother: Cannot!

That "almost done" can stretch to fifteen minutes, I promise you. Every morning, same fight, more or less the same words, and nobody ever win.

Then come the children, and my mother rouse them in the exact same order every day, never change, eldest first because she take the longest, then the youngest after, so the youngest don't have too long to sit around and get into mischief. The commands, also same order every time, like a drill:

Mother: Wake up! ... Brush your teeth! ... Wear your uniform, don't play play!

You can set your watch by it, I swear.

And almost every morning also got the same argument between those two, close to word for word. Usually about the Milo tin.

The youngest: You finish the Milo powder again!

The eldest: Where got, I never touch that one, you always blame me for everything.

Every time also this one. Where got, where got, that's my eldest's answer for everything, whether she guilty or not.

We also got a cat, and even the cat has its own fixed part to play. The moment my father step toward the front gate, that cat already sitting there waiting, tail curl round its own feet, like it clocked in for duty. It wants to slip out to the corridor to walk walk, go say hello to the auntie two doors down, then come back before anyone even notice it gone.

Breakfast, my mother make it the exact same way every day, soft-boil egg, bread with kaya and butter, and kopi for whoever want.

Mother: Makan already, come sit down, egg going cold.

My father, though, he's the one who never vary, not once. Kopi-o kosong, black, no sugar, no milk, he's like that one, don't even need to ask. My youngest, half the time still blur like sotong at the table, staring at the egg like he don't know what to do with it that early.

Something also always goes missing, most mornings, same thing, the EZ-Link card. Nobody know how, it just disappear, and the hunt always happen in the same few spots: under the sofa cushion, inside the school bag pocket where it "definitely is not," and on top of the shoe cabinet where somebody swear they put it "just now." Whoever lost it sure kena a look from my mother, every single time, then heng also usually turn up in the end, two minutes before we all need to leave.

The eldest: Aiyo, sorry, found it already, it was in my pocket the whole time.

And there's a sound, every single morning, that means the whole house is finally, properly awake, the rubbish truck. It come round about the same time, reversing with that beep beep beep, and I don't know why but that sound wake people up better than any alarm clock ever can.

The youngest: That truck so noisy, cannot sleep also lah.

But secretly I think everybody grateful for it, because after that beep beep, the whole flat just switch on, like someone flip a switch.

My eldest, she's the one always running late, same few minutes every day, no more no less. Always still fixing her hair when everyone else already at the door, one shoe on, one shoe still in hand.

The eldest: Wait, wait, jialat, I'm going to be late again!

And then the rush, everybody a bit kancheong, bags grabbed, umbrella shoved into whoever's hand because the sky look like it want to cry, water bottle pushed at somebody, my mother, kiasu as usual, checking the bags two three times even though nothing ever actually missing by then, only because it's never once been left behind before, so why take chances.

Sunday, though. Sunday everything different. Nobody rush. The whole house just go quiet, the wash-room war on ceasefire, and even my father, who still light his joss stick same as every other day, after that he just sit, drink his kopi slow slow, read the papers proper, not rushing through headlines. Everything chin chai on Sunday, whatever also can.

But every other morning, before anyone step out that door, my mother always say more or less the same handful of words, like a chant:

Mother: Got eat your breakfast or not? Wear properly ah, don't play play on the road, go careful, go!

More or less the same words, every single day, and everybody just nod and go, like it's a password to leave the house.

I don't know lah, when you say it out loud like this, sound quite mad right? Same grumble, same fight, same missing card, same everything, over and over, every morning, and none of us even notice anymore, it just happen. First time somebody see it up close, confirm catch no ball, because so many things going on the same time. But I find it quite shiok actually, in a way, the whole silly repeating performance of it. You never see one single morning of it yourself, and honestly I don't know how to explain it properly until you live through it, but that's the house, lah, every morning, more or less exactly like that.
