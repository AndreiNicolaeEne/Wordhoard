Wah, you never come help that day, you know how heng you are. Let me tell you the whole thing from the start, then you dun feel so paiseh for bojio-ing yourself out of it, confirm you will feel shiok you missed it after you hear.

Supposed to start eight o'clock sharp one, lorry uncle already confirm with us the night before. But the mother, she pack until 2am still not finish, so when the alarm ring nobody get up. I open eye already nine plus, sun very high already, and I shout in the hall like that, "Wake up wake up, lorry coming already!" Everybody blur like sotong, walking walk walking walk in the corridor not knowing what to grab first.

Then before we even step out the door, first thing confirm kena. The main door key, cannot turn. Try left, try right, try shake shake the whole door, still cannot. The father come and try also cannot, he say "this lock jialat already lah, must be the humidity" but actually I think somebody pack the WD-40 into a box already sealed up, so how. End up call the locksmith down at the void deck, wait forty minutes for him, and the lorry uncle downstairs already honking, very kancheong, keep calling "you all ready or not, I got next job at twelve you know."

While we still stuck at the door, guess who appear. Never jio one ah, just appear at the lift lobby with his own trolley, the neighbour from three doors down. He say, "eh I see got lorry downstairs, need help anot," just like that, chin chai one, didn't even wait for us to say yes, straight away start carrying the small boxes down. Very heng we got him, because by then it was already us minus one pair of hands, the brother went to shift the car and never come back so fast.

Neighbour: "Where you moving to, still same town or?"
Me: "Different town lah, further one, atas area a bit, but smaller flat."
Neighbour: "Never mind lah, small also can, main thing got roof over head."

So we start carrying down, box by box, and that's when the shout came. The mother's Guanyin statue, the porcelain one from her own mother's time, sitting on top of a stack because nobody dare put it inside a box properly cushioned, everybody thought "later I come back and wrap it." Somebody's elbow catch it, it slide, and. CRASH. Whole corridor hear the shout, the mother scream "AIYO!!" so loud the whole block also hear, two units open their door to see what happen. She sit down on the floor right there, just staring at the pieces, don't even want to cry, just very quiet, and everybody freeze like that for maybe one full minute. Then she say, real soft:

The mother: "Nevermind lah, statue also cannot bring luck if I so careless with it. Just sweep up, don't step on the glass part."

Nobody talk much after that one. We just pack faster, quieter.

Somewhere in all that rushing, one box disappear. Small one, taped up, marked "MISC. KITCHEN" in the father's handwriting. We know it went down to the lorry because someone remember carrying it, but by the time we reach the new place, gone. Not on the lorry, not in the old flat, not fallen along the corridor, nowhere. Until today still don't know where it go. The mother keep saying "that one got my good scissors and the spare house keys from your grandmother's time" and every time also she bring it up until now, still saying "where got possible, a whole box just vanish like that."

The worst one was the wardrobe. Solid wood, the old rosewood one from before I was even born, damn heavy, two men also buay tahan carry alone. Whole family gather round arguing how to angle it.

The father: "Must go on the side, tilt like this, cannot flat, flat cannot fit the corridor."
The eldest: "No lah, tilt like that you will scratch the wall, must go straight up, carry vertical."
Neighbour: "Aiyo you all arguing here, statue confirm break number two, just let me and him carry, you all direct only."
The father: "I direct what, I also carrying what, where got I just stand there."

In the end nobody agree, so everybody just lift together and hope for the best, which of course was the worst plan. Reached the stairwell door at the new block, this new place got no proper lift for big furniture, must use the stair door at the side, and the wardrobe simply would not go through. Turn it this way, corner catch. Turn it that way, top catch the ceiling beam. Tilt up, bottom catch the step. Must be twenty minutes we all stand there sweating, agak agak-ing the angle, someone say "gostan, gostan, bring it back out first," reverse the whole thing back to the lorry, try again from a completely different angle. Finally the neighbour say take the legs off first, why nobody think of that from the start, and true enough, legs off, slide through no problem. Everybody just look at each other, no need to say anything, all also thinking same thing, so blur, wasted twenty minutes for nothing.

By early afternoon the sky also decided to not help us. Was hot hot the whole morning, then all of a sudden dark clouds roll in, and the rain just pour, real sudden one, the kind where you got maybe one minute warning. Half the boxes still sitting exposed at the void deck of the old block. Everybody dashing back and forth like mad, throwing tarpaulin over things, some boxes still got wet corners, cannot help it.

That's when the meal saved us. Somebody. I think it was the neighbour, bless him, went down and tapao-ed a whole stack from the kopitiam nearby, economy rice for everyone, plus a round of kopi-o for the men and teh-c peng for the mother because she say she "buay tahan the heat already, must have something cold." We ate standing up, plates balanced on top of sealed boxes, rain still coming down outside the void deck shelter, nobody talking much, just chewing, and honestly that ten minutes felt like the best meal I ever had, real shiok, even though it was just plain economy rice with one veg one meat.

After the rain stopped, we pushed on, but by then everybody running on empty already. The last two, three loads, I tell you, that was the worst part, worse than the wardrobe even. Legs like jelly, everybody moving in slow motion, nobody talking, just carry, walk, put down, go back, carry, walk, put down. My workmate who came by in the late afternoon to help for an hour, even she say, "wah you all so sian already ah, look like zombie," and true, we looked terrible, all of us.

Before we locked the old flat for the last time, I walked through every room alone. Empty already, no more furniture, just the marks on the floor where things used to stand, patches on the wall where the calendar and the photo frames used to hang. Very strange feeling, echo when you talk, your own voice bounce back at you funny. Stood in the kitchen a while, remembered every meal, every quarrel, every Chinese New Year reunion dinner squeezed round that small table. Didn't cry, but throat a bit tight lah, I not going to lie.

We reached the new place well after nine at night, dark already outside, void deck lights the only thing lighting the whole moving process. Half the boxes still on the lorry because the driver say cannot wait anymore, must go pick up next job, so a good number of things got left behind for the next day, including, we found out much later, the missing box, which never did turn up at all, not that night, not the next day, not ever. Started the morning thinking we'd sleep in a fully set up flat with the statue still whole and the good scissors still with us. Ended the night with cracked porcelain in a rubbish bag, a scissors-shaped hole in the mother's story, and half our life still sitting downstairs in a lorry that already drove off. Die die must try to remember, next time, hire proper movers who wrap the fragile stuff themselves, one lah.
