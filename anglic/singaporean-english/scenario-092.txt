By the time it come down to just us five, the whole void deck gone quiet already, only the fan going tk tk tk overhead and the joss sticks half burnt down in the urn. Everybody else signed the book and went home liao, the neighbour upstairs also gone back up, only the eldest, the second brother, the brother-in-law, the nephew and me still sitting round the plastic table, styrofoam cups of kopi gone cold in front of us.

The eldest was the one who started it, quite soft, like she never plan to say it out loud.

The eldest: You know she always tapao the extra char siew rice from the corner stall, right, even when nobody ask her. Every Sunday like that.

The second brother: Wah lau, it's not Sunday leh, it's Wednesday she go. Sunday the stall closed what.

The eldest: Is it? I thought Sunday.

The second brother: Wednesday lah, Thursday the market got no parking, she always complain about that.

The eldest: Aiyo, true also. I always get that one wrong one. Every time also I say Sunday, everybody correct me.

The nephew let out a laugh he clearly didn't mean to, more like a snort into his own collar, and covered his mouth after, but nobody minded, the brother-in-law even patted his back like it's fine.

The brother-in-law: Ei, no need paiseh, laugh what, she also very funny person mah.

The eldest: She never go atas restaurant one you know, always the ulu stall at the back, she say the atas ones just charge you for the aircon only. Like to walk walk first around the whole market, see see what's fresh, never terus grab the first thing she see. And chope the table with a tissue packet before she even go queue, that kind of person.

Then the second brother said the thing that made all of us go quiet for a second, because we all heard it the exact same way, in the exact same voice.

The second brother: You know what she always say when we tell her don't eat so much. She say, "aiyo, die die must try lah, don't waste." Every single time, no matter what food, new stall, old stall, she also die die must try one.

Me: Die die must try lah!

I said it back exactly the way she used to, the same little rise at the end, and everybody laughed a bit, soft laugh, not loud, and for a moment it felt like she was still sitting there saying it.

The nephew was the one who came out with the memory that surprised the rest of us.

The nephew: Actually you all know she used to save up and send money back for her own auntie's grandchildren in Johor, every month, never tell anybody one. I only find out because I saw the bank book when I was helping her with the phone bill last time.

The eldest: Wah, really? She never say anything leh.

The second brother: Alamak, I don't even know this one.

For a moment nobody said anything, just the fan going, and the smoke off the joss stick curling up slow into the dark.

Then the brother-in-law got up and started passing the tau sar piah tin around again, an excuse more than anything.

The brother-in-law: Eat, eat, still got some, don't waste, later throw away also sayang. Shiok one when still a bit warm, she always insist we eat it warm.

Somewhere in the passing of the tin the eldest and the second brother's old thing came up again, quick, the way old things do.

The eldest: You still never pay me back for that renovation deposit thing ah.

The second brother: Where got, I already transfer you half already, don't anyhow say.

The eldest: I never say anything what, just say only.

The second brother: Aiyo, don't start lah, not tonight.

Nobody pressed it further, the tin kept moving hand to hand, and it passed.

Then it was the brother-in-law's turn, and this one had real fondness in it, no edge left at all.

The brother-in-law: Eh, you all remember the rice cooker thing? The one already spoil, the lid also cannot close properly, we buy her new one three times already, she throw all three away, still go and use that old one, tie the lid down with a rubber band. Kiasu about throwing away things one, cannot let go.

The second brother: Ya lor, we scold her already how many times, she just say "still can use what, why waste."

The eldest: That's her lah. Stubborn like that until the end. Buay tahan one sometimes, but that's how she is.

And somehow after that, without anybody saying it plain, it felt settled between us, that that's the one thing everybody would remember her for, that she never give up on anything, small thing also she stubborn about, and that's not a bad thing to be remembered for at all.

Midway through all this the nephew started crying, quiet, not making any sound, just the tears coming down his face, and nobody stopped to make a fuss over it, the talk just kept going gentle around him, the second brother even reached over and patted his shoulder without breaking his own sentence.

The brother-in-law: She very kancheong spider about time also, always want to reach early, cannot stand being late for anything. Remember she still remember how to gostan the car at seventy plus, better than some of us somemore.

The eldest: And her lobang for the cheap stuff, wah, always got some discount coupon or some auntie friend selling something. Sometimes she talk so fast about it we all catch no ball, but she very happy already, so we just nod along.

The nephew: The whole insurance claim thing very chim, but she handle all by herself one, never ask anybody for help. Now everything also I blur like sotong, don't know where she even keep the documents.

The eldest: Heng we still manage to see her before, if not really suay.

Then it turned practical, the way it always does eventually.

The eldest: Tomorrow I go settle the certificate thing at the office downstairs, second brother you help me call the temple people ah.

The second brother: Can, can. Brother-in-law you handle the flowers and the wreath people since you got the contact.

The brother-in-law: Can lah, no problem, I call first thing.

Me: I do the calling round to the relatives overseas, time zone a bit of a problem only, but can manage.

The eldest: Cannot lah, too early to call now, wait till morning first.

The second brother: Sian all this admin thing, but got to do what.

The eldest: Ok lah, settled like that first, tomorrow see how.

By then it was really the small hours already, the whole estate outside gone completely silent, only the odd taxi passing far off. The eldest was the last one properly awake, the rest of us half dozing in the plastic chairs, and she lifted her cold kopi cup, just like that, no warning at all.

The eldest: Ok lah, before we all go home. To her lah. Wherever she is now, must be still telling somebody up there, die die must try. Cheers.

We all raised whatever we had in hand, cold kopi, half a cup of teh-c, and drank, and that was that, the night closing out quiet around us, the fan still going tk tk tk over our heads.
