Okay lah, let me tell you the whole saga of my plot this year, from the signing up until the clearing at the end, because honestly ah, got ups got downs, but overall very shiok one.

So how it started, the community garden downstairs got a plot free because one auntie moved out to her son's place, and the committee auntie jio me one day when I was walking walk near the notice board. She say, eh you want or not, one plot empty leh, chope fast before somebody else grab. I say okay lor, why not, chin chai also can, at worst waste some time only what.

First thing was deciding what to plant. I go kopitiam with the uncle from three plots down, order my usual kopi-o, he teh-c peng, and we sit there agak agak what will grow easy for a first-timer like me. He say don't be kiasu, don't plant twenty different things and end up catch no ball managing all, just plant a few hardy ones. So we settle on chilli padi, kai lan, kangkong, some okra, and two tomato plants because my wife like tomato. Very standard heartland combo, nothing atas, nothing ulu also, just the reliable stuff.

I put everything in the ground a few weeks after the year-end monsoon die down, when the nights not so cold and windy already, because plant early during that spell also useless, the seedlings just kena beaten down by the wind and rain. Once the weather turn nice and steady, wah, everything shoot up like nobody business. Every time I go down also see new leaves, very heng that stretch, sun also not too fierce, rain also not too much, just naik naik nicely. I was quite blur like sotong about gardening before this, but that first month everything seem to prove I got green fingers, very shiok feeling.

Then the first pest come. Small caterpillar, green green ones, munching my kai lan leaves until full of holes, jialat. I ask around, one auntie say spray dishwashing liquid mix with water, cheap and won't harm the vegetable. I try, and it worked half half only, the caterpillar population go down but never fully clear, still got some diehard ones hiding under the leaves, so I end up hand-picking them out one by one every few days, very sian process but no choice.

After that come the dry stretch. Weeks and weeks with hardly any rain, the soil turn hard and cracking, and I had to carry water from the standpipe by hand, bucket by bucket, every single day just to keep the plants from wilting. Buay tahan lor, that period, especially after work, tired already still must go down carry water, sweat until shirt basah. But die die must try what, cannot let the whole plot die just because lazy one day.

That was also when the whole watering business caused a bit of friction with the plot next to mine. There is a shared tap area near our row, and during the dry spell everybody kancheong to fill up before their turn, so naturally got dispute. The neighbouring plot-holder felt I was hogging the tap.

The neighbouring plot-holder: Eh, you use the tap already ah, everyday also you first one, other people also need what.

Me: Where got everyday first, I come down after work only, sometimes seven eight plus already.

The neighbouring plot-holder: Is it? I feel like whenever I come, you always there lor.

Me: Aiyo, not on purpose lah, my plants also very thirsty during this dry spell, cannot help it.

It dragged on a few days like that, small small remarks here and there, until the committee auntie step in and paste up a proper rota list on the noticeboard, morning slot and evening slot, name against timing, very fair. After that no more issue, we even started greeting each other properly again, and one time she even help me top up my can when I was running late. So that one settled nicely in the end, no more bad blood, just misunderstanding due to the heat and everybody kancheong for water.

Now, the best producer of the whole year, hands down, was the chilli padi. That thing grew like a monster, non-stop flowering and fruiting, I was harvesting small handfuls every week from July onwards. My wife say don't need to buy chilli from the market anymore, we got enough to makan and still extra to give away to relatives. Very heng, very shiok, that one alone made the whole plot worth it already.

But then the tomato give me a different kind of problem, the birds. Mynahs and sparrows, they peck at the tomato the moment it start turning red, half-eaten fruit lying around, very wasteful. I tried netting over the plants, that help a bit, but some cheeky ones still find a way through the gaps, catch no ball how they even squeeze in. So the tomato yield was disappointing compared to what I expected, but I chin chai already, cannot control the birds one.

Partway through the year also got one storm, real heavy squall, thunder rolling and rain coming down like nobody business for maybe forty minutes. Went down the next morning and wah lau, whole row of kangkong and the okra stems all flattened, lying flat on the mud like someone step on them. Quite heartbreaking that morning honestly. Spent the whole Saturday with the uncle and the auntie next door propping the stems back up, tying them to little bamboo stakes we borrow from the shed, patting the soil back around the roots. Half of them recovered after a week, some never quite bounce back, but overall the salvage effort paid off, better than just leaving them to rot on the ground.

By the end of the year when I finally tally everything, the chilli padi was clearly the star, followed by the kangkong which also did solidly despite the storm setback, the kai lan came back fine after the caterpillar episode, the okra so-so, and the tomato the weakest performer because of the birds. I traded a good bag of chilli padi with another grower two rows down for a bunch of her turmeric and some pandan leaves, since I don't grow those myself and my wife always need them for cooking, and I tapao the rest of the harvest home in stages over a few weekends, sharing out to neighbours who don't have a plot themselves.

Toward the end of the year I clear everything out, pull up the old stems, turn over the soil, put in some compost, and cover the bed with the black cloth to rest before next planting season, basically putting the whole plot to sleep until it's time to start again.

Looking back on the whole year lah, I would say worth it, more good than bad. Got the pest, got the dry spell, got the small quarrel over water, got the storm damage, but also got the chilli padi glory, the friendship with the other growers, and the shiok feeling of eating something you grow yourself. If they ask me next year whether I want to chope the same plot again, I will say can, definitely can, no need to think twice one.
