Wah, where do I even start ah. You know I decided last year, very the atas thing to do at my age, to learn how to cycle. Like real bicycle, not the exercise bike at the gym one. Everybody around me, when they were small already learned, only I never learn, because my father always say don't play play near the road, so my bicycle time never come. So last year I think, enough is enough, die die must try, I go sign up for the adult class at the CC, every Saturday morning at the open field near the void deck.

First lesson, jialat one lah. The instructor, one uncle, very patient but also very straight to the point kind, he look at me wobbling there holding the handlebar like it going to run away from me, and he just say straight up.

Instructor: At your age ah, will take longer one. Don't rush, but also don't expect too fast.

I catch no ball what he explaining half the time, all this about balance and looking far ahead not looking down, mouth explain until dry, I still blur. And paiseh until don't know where to put my face somemore, because next to me got two kids, maybe primary two primary three only, and they already pedalling pedalling round the field like nobody's business, while I still cannot even balance for two seconds without falling to one side. Within days sia, these small ones overtake me already, and I tell you, that one really sting more than I expect. I keep telling myself don't compare, don't compare, but how to not compare when the six-year-old beside you already can let go both hands and shout look no hands.

Then the whole first week my body pain like siao. Muscle ache in places I don't even know got muscle there. Sit down also pain, stand up also pain, climb the HDB stairs also pain, everywhere also pain, I tell you, third day already buay tahan, whole body like been through some ordeal. My husband laugh at me, say I walk like just came back from reservist. Every time also I sit down I go "aiyo" first before I even settle properly.

Then got one fall, wah, that one I still remember until now, very clear. I was trying to go a bit faster because instructor say don't so slow lah, and then front wheel wobble, I panic, next thing I know I already flat on the ground, whole body down, and the wind all knock out of me until I cannot even shout. Just lay there on the field looking up at the sky, thinking, this one jialat already, I done, I quit, this hobby not for me one. Heng also never break any bone, just knee scrape until bleeding a bit, elbow raw. Small crowd see see only, some go "wah, auntie ok or not," some also laughing, cannot help it, honestly if it wasn't me lying there I also would laugh.

That evening I go home, I tell my neighbour, the one who always jio me for makan downstairs, I say I done, not going back, too paiseh already, too old for this nonsense, better I just chin chai forget about it.

The neighbour: You really want to give up like that, is it? One time fall only what, everybody also fall one when they learning. Come with me one more time, if still cannot then we stop, ok or not?

So kena talked into it basically, but I go. Alamak, half of me still shivering thinking about the ground, but I go. And true enough, after that day I don't fall so bad anymore, though still wobble like blur sotong most of the time. Progress after that very slow, I tell you, very the humiliating kind of slow. One week I finally can balance three seconds without instructor holding the seat behind me. Next week I finally can pedal ten metres before falling sideways into the grass patch. Small small thing only, but each one feel like some achievement, while the parents and grandparents sitting at the void deck bench watch, some encourage, some just there to see show, more kancheong for me watching than I was kancheong for myself.

I learn quite early on, must compare against myself last month only, cannot compare against the ones who pick up in one afternoon, otherwise confirm sian until want to give up whole thing. Instructor also always remind, everybody own pace, don't so kiasu, chin chai first, don't rush the process.

Around month four, something click. Cannot really explain in word what happen, but one Saturday morning I just, balance already, like the body itself remember somehow, more than my head figuring anything out. The wobble gone just like that. I pedal round the whole field, then round again, and instructor just nod, don't say much, but I know that nod mean something big.

Instructor: Ok, now can already. See, I say what, just need time only, no need rush one.

I never catch up to those two small kids lah, they already doing no-hands, standing on the pedal, all sort of show-off stunt by the time I just learn how to signal turn properly. But by the end I make peace with that. I proud instead of what I can do now that I couldn't do at all when I start, cycle to the market on Sunday morning, go round the park connector, even brave enough to gostan a little when I misjudge the kerb, don't need to jump down and push the bicycle like some jialat scene anymore.

Now when I think back on the whole season, honestly, it sit quite fondly in my memory, not sourly at all. The falls, the muscle ache, the being laughed at by small crowd at the void deck, the paiseh moments, all folded into one story I actually glad I went through. Some weekend now I cycle down to the kopitiam, order my kopi-o, sit there watching the new batch of adult learners wobbling on the same field where I used to wobble, and I feel a bit shiok inside, like, wah, I also went through all that leh.

So that's the whole story lah, the one you never see any of it, because you always busy those months, bojio also never bojio you, I know you got your own things going on too. But now you know already, how I start so late, the worst paiseh moment when I fall flat in front of everybody, the slow climb after that, the day it finally click, and where I land by the end: not the fastest one, never will be, don't even try to compare with those kids, but got can already, and that's enough for me one.
