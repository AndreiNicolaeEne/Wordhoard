Still dark when the alarm go, five thirty, and outside got that damp smell already, like the rain come and go while everybody sleeping. I quickly wash up, wear the old shirt, the one full of bleach spots from last time, don't care how look one, today going to be dirty work anyway. Downstairs the void deck already got people standing around the trolleys, torchlight moving here and there, somebody's radio playing Chinese oldies very softly like don't want to wake the whole block.

The auntie in charge, she the one running the community garden committee, she already dishing out the jobs before the sun even up.

The auntie in charge: Okay okay, don't stand around like blur sotong, come come, take your gloves, today we clear the whole plot one time, cannot drag until next week already.

She very kiasu like that, always want finish early finish fast, say if wait until it too hot everybody heng ah kena sunstroke somemore.

First job she give out is the easy one, and of course go to the youngest, her grandson, maybe primary five like that, still rubbing his eyes.

The auntie in charge: You, small boy, you go collect the snails from the leaves first, put in the pail, don't squash, just pick pick. Easy job for you, can or not?

The youngest one: Can lah.

He go off swinging his pail, whistling like never mind the hour, young got that don't-care-hour energy one.

Then the heaviest part, everybody know who going to get it before even she open mouth, the big uncle from the next block, arms like that, always the one carry the compost drums and dig up whatever need digging.

The auntie in charge: Uncle, you take the sweet potato section lah, must dig, the roots go deep, need strength one, you the strongest here what.

The uncle: Aiyo, every time also me. Okay lah okay lah, no choice, who ask me got this body.

He laugh when he say, not really complaining, just his style, and off he go with the fork and the changkul, start turning the soil, sweat already coming out before six thirty.

We start on the kang kong patch first, that one fastest to clear, everybody squat down in a row pulling, snapping the stems, throwing in the basket. My mother beside me, she quite fast one, hands like machine, don't even need look.

My mother: You cut too high leh, waste the stem, cut lower a bit.

Me: Aiyo, cut cut lah, later also grow back one.

My mother: Where got, you cut like that next time nothing to harvest already.

We bicker a bit like that but nobody really angry, just the usual back and forth while working, keep the hands moving and the mouth moving too.

Once the kang kong section clear, the auntie in charge call out to shift everybody over to the chilli and the bittergourd trellis, that one need more patience, must look carefully which one ripe which one not yet.

The auntie in charge: Move over here already, this section more chim, don't just pluck anything, must look properly, the small green one leave it, come back next week.

Somewhere in the middle, the auntie with the bad knee, she always come every harvest day, cannot squat long already, knee problem, but she never say cannot, just slower and slower until she quiet, sitting on the little stool doing the sorting instead of the picking. Nobody make a big thing out of it, my mother just quietly pass her the basket of already-picked stuff.

My mother: Auntie, you sit here sort the good from the spoilt one can already, don't need squat, save your knee.

The auntie with the bad knee: Aiyo, paiseh leh, everybody working so hard, me sit here like that.

My mother: Where got paiseh, everybody got their part, this also help what.

Nobody make her feel bad, she just get folded into the lighter job, and work continue like that, natural, no fuss.

Around nine plus somebody's husband come down with the tapao, big packet of bee hoon, some fried egg, and a whole carrier bag of kopi-o and teh-c peng, ice all melting a bit already from the walk over.

The husband: Eh, makan break, everybody stop first, come drink something, so hot already sweat until shirt basah.

Everybody gather round the little table, hands still got soil on them, wipe on the trousers, don't care, eat standing up, some sit on the overturned pails. The bee hoon shiok like that when you tired and hungry, taste extra good somehow.

The youngest one: Wah lau, my pail already got like twenty snails leh.

The uncle: Twenty snails also want to boast, small case lah, wait until you dig sweet potato like me, then you know what is real work.

Everybody laugh, the boy paiseh a bit but also grinning.

Halfway through the meal the sky start to change, wind pick up suddenly, the auntie in charge look up and her face change one kind.

The auntie in charge: Eh, look like going to rain leh, everybody fast fast, cover the baskets first, bring in whatever already cut, don't let it get wet and rot.

Everybody kancheong now, drop the food, run around covering baskets with the tarpaulin, moving the crates under the void deck shelter. The wind blow quite strong for a while, some drizzle come down, but heng ah, it don't really pour, just threaten threaten only, pass after twenty minutes like that.

The uncle: Wah, scare me for nothing, the rain also blur like sotong, don't know want to come or not.

Once it clear up we push on, the afternoon sun come out hot and sticky, everybody back to the trellis, back to the sweet potato patch, working through it, nobody complain even though tired already, just quiet steady work, occasional joke thrown across the rows.

Then near two plus, the trolley wheel, the one we use to move the full baskets, it just give way, wheel come clean off, basket almost topple, some of the bittergourd roll out onto the dirt, a few damage, jialat like that.

The auntie in charge: Aiyo, cannot use already this one, somebody go gostan the trolley to the shed, get the other small one instead.

The youngest one run to fetch the spare trolley, and we salvage what still good, the damaged ones set aside for eating first at home rather than sharing out, chin chai like that, no point wasting time crying over it.

By four plus the light start going already, that late afternoon haze coming down, and the auntie in charge start calling out louder.

The auntie in charge: Okay everybody, faster a bit, light going down soon, cannot see properly later, must finish this last row before we stop.

Everybody converge on the last section together then, no more separate jobs, whoever nearest just pitch in, pulling, cutting, carrying, the big uncle even come over to help pick leafy stuff even though not his usual job, don't care already, just want finish.

Last basket come in maybe five forty, sky already turning orange-grey. The auntie in charge do a rough tally out loud, counting the crates.

The auntie in charge: Okay, today got like twelve baskets kang kong, six bittergourd, plenty chilli, and the sweet potato drum, quite good harvest today ah, not bad not bad.

Everybody just collapse where they standing, some sit right down on the pails, backs aching, hands stiff, nobody want to move for a good few minutes, just breathing, somebody pass around the last of the teh from the carrier bag, gone cold already but still drink one.

The uncle: Wah, today confirm plus chop tomorrow whole body pain lah.

My mother: Die die must try what, cannot skip harvest day, the vegetable also die die must come out.

Just when everybody thinking can go up and rest already, the auntie in charge remember one more thing.

The auntie in charge: Eh, small boy, before you go up, help me wash the pails and stack back in the shed first ah, don't leave for tomorrow, later rot the smell.

The youngest one: Aiyo, always me one.

But he go anyway, dragging the hose over, rinsing out the pails one by one in the near dark, while the rest of us finally sit back, too tired to even talk much, just watching the sky go properly black, the whole day's work sitting there in the crates, done already.
