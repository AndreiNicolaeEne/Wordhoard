Down at the void deck was the part that really got me. That was the first time in my whole life I see both sides of the family standing together in one place, my father and his father shaking hands very stiff like two uncles who just met at the kopitiam, my mother and his mother already hugging and crying, the rest of the aunties and uncles all standing around a bit blur, not sure where to stand, some smiling, some pretending to check their phone. Then the vows part. I had it all written nicely, practised so many times at home in front of the mirror, but standing there in front of everybody my mind just went blank halfway, completely blank, like the page inside my head just wiped clean. I stood there maybe five six seconds not saying anything, everybody quiet, my hand shaking holding the paper, and then somehow the line just came back to me, like it was waiting there all along, and I continued. After that the celebrant asked the question, and when he said yes, I do, and I said the same, that was it, sealed, just like that, so simple, but the whole hall got very quiet for that one moment, even the small children stopped fidgeting.

After the vows we went to sign the papers, the both of us and one witness standing for each side, his uncle stood for his side and my colleague stood for mine, everybody very serious signing signing, checking checking, the officer double confirming this line here, that line there, and then done, papers all in order, we officially husband and wife already, liao, just like that with a pen.

Then came the photo session, wah, that one took forever. The photographer very particular one, kept arranging cluster after cluster of relatives, this side stand here, that side stand there, taller ones at the back, must smile naturally but also must not blink, so many groups, with my parents, with his parents, with all the aunties together, then all the uncles, then the cousins, then the whole extended lot squeezed into one shot where somebody's elbow always in somebody else's face. Everybody standing there sweating in their nice clothes under the sun, some uncle keep asking where got so many photos meh, we just take a few can already, but no choice, must follow the list.

At the dinner there was a bit of a seating mix-up, two people who really cannot be near each other end up on the same table practically shoulder to shoulder, very awkward, both of them just smiling stiffly and talking to everyone else except each other the whole night, poor things, somebody must have swapped the name cards without checking properly.

Food-wise, the whole table finished the chilli crab within minutes, gone, not a shell left, everybody fighting for the last piece of bread to mop up the sauce. Then the toast. His brother stood up to give the speech and could not get through it at all, kept stopping halfway, voice breaking, had to take a sip of water, start again, break down again, the whole hall going "awww" and clapping to help him along, in the end he just gave up on the prepared speech and said whatever came to his heart, and that one hit harder than anything written down.

One of his cousins would not sit down the whole night, dancing dancing non-stop even when the DJ played the slow songs, dragging aunties and uncles onto the floor one by one, everybody laughing at her, some complaining buay tahan already so tired, but she just keep going, shiok for her lah, at least someone enjoying to the max.

Somewhere near the end my aunt dragged out that old story again, the one about my father losing the ring at his own wedding all those years back and how everybody had to search the whole restaurant floor on their knees, she told it to the whole table like nobody has heard it a hundred times before, and everybody laughed like it was brand new, my father just shaking his head, face a bit red, saying wah you all still remember this one ah.

By the time the last guest left it was already so late, the hall half in darkness, chairs all over the place, some flowers on the floor, leftover food packed into containers on the table. We just stood there for a moment, the two of us, not saying much, too tired to even talk properly, just looking at the mess and at each other, and honestly that quiet bit right there, just us and the rubbish and the empty chairs, was maybe my favourite part of the whole day.
