I bring the mother down to the kopitiam under our block on a Sunday, before I even open mouth I go chope the corner table with a tissue packet first, then queue up and order, kopi-o kosong for her, the way she always drink, teh-c peng for me because my throat already dry dry before I say one word. I bring the tray back, put down, sit. She look at me one kind.

Mother: You ah, sit down also like that. Face like you kena scolded by your boss. What happened.

Me: Nothing lah. I mean, nothing happen today. I want to tell you something. Old thing.

Mother: Old thing also need this kind face? Wah lau, say lah, you make me kancheong here.

Me: Remember my leg. This scar, the long one on my shin. I tell you I fell off my bike one, that time.

Mother: Ya I remember what. That day you come home all blood, crying non stop, I scold you cannot watch the road ah, then bring you Mount E clinic, they stitch you up. Why, it pain again is it? So many years already also can still pain meh.

Me: No, it don't pain. It's, that story, the bike story. That one not true one.

Mother: ...what you mean not true.

Me: I never fall off any bike that day. I don't even bring my bike out that day.

Mother: Then how you kena that cut. Aiyo, you tell me now, after twenty over years?

Me: I know. I know it's very late already. But I carry this one alone for too long, and I sian of it, every time somebody ask about the scar I just say the same lie so smooth, like reflex, and each time I say it I feel more jialat inside. So I want to say the real one to you first, before I say it to anybody else. Can I finish first, then you scold me, okay?

Mother: ...okay. You talk.

Me: That time we still small, primary school. There's this gang of us, three four of us always hang out together after school, go the ulu part behind the old quarry, near where they never fence up properly yet. There's this one big tree there, everybody call it the jumping tree, grow right at the edge of the water. All the older boys and some of the girls too, they climb up, jump down into the pond, very shiok, everybody talk about it like it's a whole thing you must try once.

Mother: You never tell me you all go there. That place so dangerous, no lifeguard, nothing, aiyo.

Me: I know, that's why I never tell you all these years also. Anyway, one time before, we already dare each other to climb, and I climb up a bit only, look down, my legs shaking like mad, and I chicken out, climb back down. The rest of them never let me forget, everywhere also bring up, "eh remember you scared until like that", laugh at me every time also. So the next time we go back, the eldest one among us say, today die die must try, no more backing out. I say okay lor, kiasu also don't want to lose face already, so I climb.

Mother: Then?

Me: I climb higher than I ever plan. Higher than even what they dare me. I don't know why, maybe I want to prove extra, don't want anybody say I blur like sotong again. I get to the highest branch I dare reach, I look down, water looks calm, I think can one, so I jump.

Mother: Alamak.

Me: I misjudge the water. From up there you cannot tell how the water moving, where got shallow where got deep, and I land wrong, all the wind knock out of me first, then under the water my leg hit something, some rubbish or metal or whatever sunk down there, I never see it coming, one sharp pain, then I feel the water go warm around my leg and I know that's not good.

Mother: Wah lau eh. You could have die there, you know or not?

Me: I know. The rest of them pull me up, everybody panicking, one of them crying already, we all shivering by the pond side, blood coming out non stop, and right there, still wet, we all agree together, cannot tell the real story, our parents will never let us go near that place again, and also we all scared got trouble. So we make up the bike story on the spot. I go home like that, tell you, you believe, bring me to the clinic, they clean it, stitch it, and that's that. The story just stick since then. Every time somebody ask, I say the same thing, so many times already I almost believe it myself.

Mother: Twenty over years you carry this.

Me: Ya. And every time you or anybody ask about this scar, I feel a bit paiseh inside, but I just chin chai answer, don't think too much, agak agak say the same line, move on. But it never really move on for me leh. I keep thinking, if that day heng a bit less, if the cut a bit deeper, or if none of them manage to pull me up in time, this whole story different already. I don't even want to imagine.

Mother: So why you tell me now. Why not just keep carrying, since already so long.

Me: Because I sian of lying to you, of all people. You're the one who sat by my bed that whole week, changed the dressing every day, worried the wound infect or not, and the whole time you worried over a story I make up. That not fair to you. And also. I don't know, maybe I getting older, I catch myself thinking about that pond more and more, and I don't want this to be one more thing I bring all the way to the end without saying. So I tell you now. I'm sorry, really sorry, that I lie to you for so long, and that I let you believe something not true about your own child, and that I make you worry about the wrong thing instead of the real danger I put myself in. I'm sorry I never had the guts to say it earlier when you could still march down there yourself and check the place, tell me never go back.

Mother: ...

Me: You very quiet.

Mother: I don't know whether to scold you or to hug you first. You this rascal. All these years I think I know my own child's story and turns out, aiyo. But, okay. Thank god you still here to tell me lah, that's the main thing. If that time really jialat, worse than jialat, I don't even want to think.

Me: I know. That's why I keep saying sorry, for the lie, and also for the stupid thing I did in the first place, letting people's teasing push me to climb that high. I should have just say no again that day, chicken out again, let them laugh, whatever lah. Face is not worth this.

Mother: Aiyo, you all kids, always kiasu about stupid things, scared lose face in front of friends, don't think about the real risk at all. Next time your own children dare each other like that, you better tell them this story, the real one, not the bike one.

Me: I will. I promise.

Mother: Come, drink your tea, already so long already sitting here talking, your peng also melt liao.

Me: Okay. And, thank you for not being angrier.

Mother: I still angry what. Just angry quietly first, drink my kopi first, then angry properly after.

We both sit there a while, not saying much more, the fan turning slow above us, the uncle at the next table shouting his order for two more kopi, life going on around us like normal, but between the two of us something finally settled after all these years, the real story out at last, sitting there plain and true between the two cups going cold.
