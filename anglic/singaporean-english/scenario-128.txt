Wah, that one ah, until now I still tell people, they also catch no ball how bad it was, must really be there to know one. Let me tell from the start lah.

That afternoon the sky went very strange already. Not the usual heavy grey before rain, this one different, sort of yellow-green and very still, like the whole estate holding breath like that. No wind at all leh, so humid until shirt also stick on back, but somehow you just know something coming, the body knows one. Even the mynahs at the void deck all quiet, not making noise, usually so noisy one but that day never. The mother came out to the corridor, looked at the sky, and straight away say:

The mother: Eh, this one not right hor. Better bring everything in now, don't wait.

So we kancheong start moving. The laundry pole with all the clothes, quick quick bring in. The shoe rack, the plants along the corridor, the birdcage, even the pot of curry leaves the mother been growing for years, all drag inside. My hands were shaking a bit already, not from cold, from panic, but never say out loud, just chin chai act calm calm for the kids' sake. Within one hour only, the wind started picking up, and fast one, not slow slow build up like normal storms. One moment still nothing, next moment the whole corridor railing rattling.

By then everybody gathered in the living room already, all of us close together on the sofa, the mother, the husband, the two kids, even brought the neighbour's cat in for a while because that poor thing was outside meowing non-stop, buay tahan the noise I think. Power went off not long after, just like that, whole block dark, no warning, no announcement, gone. Everyone quiet for a second then the younger one started to cry.

The younger one: Why the lights all closed one?

The eldest: Not closed lah, blackout. Don't be scared.

But honestly the eldest also scared, I could see, just trying to be atas about it, act brave in front of the sibling.

Then the wind really came. Aiyo, that part I don't even know how to describe properly. So loud until we stop talking to each other, like literally cannot hear, mouth moving but sound just eaten up by the wind. The whole flat shaking a bit, windows humming like that, and something outside kept banging, banging, we didn't know what. Then we heard it, a tree coming down, not far, maybe two blocks away but sounded like right at our doorstep, one long crack then a heavy thud that we felt through the floor almost. My heart nearly stop. The mother grabbed my hand very tight, didn't say anything, just held.

Somewhere along the way part of our roof at the service yard got stripped off. We didn't see it happen, just heard this metal screeching sound, very jialat sound, like someone tearing zinc sheet with bare hands, and then a bang somewhere below, must be it landed in the corridor or the carpark. Rain started coming straight into the kitchen through the gap, we quick quick threw every towel and basin we had, but where got enough basin for that kind of rain, water still came in everywhere.

Then the window in the kids' room cracked. Not shattered, thank god, but a long crack right across, spidering out from one corner. The grille was the only thing holding it in place I think, otherwise that glass sure fly in already. We pulled the kids out of that room immediately, no need to think twice, just grab and go.

That was the worst stretch, maybe twenty, thirty minutes but felt like forever sia. I was so scared, really very scared, though I never said it then, just kept saying to the kids don't worry don't worry, we ok one, but inside I was thinking, wah lau, if the window really go, if the roof come off more, then what. Only after, days after, I admitted to the husband I thought we might really kena something bad that night.

Then, just like that, the wind dropped. Sudden one, not slow fading out, more like someone switch off a machine. One second everything roaring, next second so quiet you can hear your own heartbeat, hear the rain just dripping normal already, no more howling. Nobody moved for a while, all of us just sitting there in the dark, catching our breath, not sure if it's really over or is it just the eye passing, so we waited more before anyone dared to go check.

When we finally stepped out, aiyo, the whole place transformed. The tree that came down was blocking the whole side road out of our block, so thick and long nobody could even walk past, must wait for town council to come cut and clear before any car or even people could go that way. Our service yard roof, half gone, just open sky where the zinc used to be, water everywhere inside, the whole place needed proper repair, not just a patch job, real contractor work.

But then we walked a bit further and saw the neighbour's fence, the one downstairs with the little garden, completely flattened, whole thing gone, flat on the ground. The neighbour came out shaking head.

The neighbour: My fence confirm need to redo already lah. Whole thing gone.

Compare to what we lost though, roof and window crack and all, I told the neighbour honestly, heng ah, at least it's just a fence for you, can fix fast one. The neighbour looked at our roof and just went quiet, understood what I meant.

The days after, jialat also, but different kind of jialat, the slow tiring kind. No power for almost two days, so whatever in the fridge all spoil, throw throw throw, waste so much food, sian only. Boiled water on the gas stove, ate simple, whatever can tapao from the coffeeshop when it reopened, kopi-o and some economy rice, nothing fancy, just to survive lah. Spent the whole weekend mopping, drying, moving furniture out to air, calling the town council, calling insurance, taking photo photo of everything for the claim. The mother kept saying die die must fix the roof before next rain come, don't procrastinate, and she was right, we chased it hard.

Now when I tell people the story, I notice I still cannot help it, every time I reach the tree part or the window part my voice just goes up, alamak this, wah lau that, hands moving around like I'm still there. The husband always tease me, say I tell it more dramatic every time also, but I say where got, I'm just telling it how it really was that night. Some things you go through, the fear doesn't fully leave you, it just waits there quiet, and comes back out the moment you start telling the story again.
