Right, we'm all here now, so I'll tell yer how it went, plain as I can, so's yow have it same as me.

It was just me sat with mom at the end, and I'll say it straight though you'll think I'm saft going on. First thing her said wor nothing about dying, not a word of it. Her just looked over and said, remember when our kid used to gambol about the lawn all summer, donnies filthy before tea, never wanted washing. That's all, just easing us both into the room like.

Then her changed, went proper serious, and her said mind I got our kid's ring off the top drawer before anyone else got their hands on it, the gold one, not the tin looking thing. Her day want that sold, nor forgot, no chance of it. I told her it wo be a bother, I'd sort it, and I must have looked yampy stood there working out which drawer her meant, but I said I would, and I meant it.

Her called me bab then, just the once more, same as her'd called me my whole life, and summat in the way her said it told me her knew what wor coming.

Her asked me summat then I day expect at all. Have I bin a good mom to yer, bab, her said. I told her straight, no lie in it, that her'd been bostin, the best there is, and I wor only saying it cause it wor true.

Last whole sentence her managed, not long after, wor, don't you go blartin over me now, bab, go and put the kettle on. That wor her all over, right to the end.

After that it wor just the quiet. I held her hand, her held mine back tight as her could manage, and I sat there till her went, and didn't let go for a good while after neither.

That's it. Not going all round the Wrekin about it, that's the whole of it, same as I've kept it, so yow've got it now too.
