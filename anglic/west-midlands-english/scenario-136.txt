I keep it in the tin behind the flour, low hundreds, meant to see us through to the end of the month, and this morning it was lighter than it ought to be. I counted it twice, right there at the table, cor believe what I was seeing, counted it again just to be dead sure. Then I went and checked the other place, under the stairs where it goes if the tin's full, just in case it'd got moved and nobody'd said owt. Nothing there neither.

So I started asking round, mild like, not making a thing of it yet.

Me: Anybody bin in the tin? I ay accusing nobody, just asking has it bin moved.

The mom: Not me, bab. Her ay touched it, her says.

The dad: Nor me. I day even know where the tin was kept these days.

Our kid: I ay seen it. Swear down, I ay.

Everybody the same flat no, quick as yer like, no bother in it at all.

But it was our kid who'd bin in the house on their tod that day, everybody else out at the shops or at work, and when I put it to them straight, no mildness left in it now, the answer come back just as flat as before.

Me: Yow was the only one in this house all day. It wo add up any other way. Where's the money gone?

Our kid: I day take it! How many times am I gotta say it. I ay a thief and yo cor make me one just cause I was in.

Me: Don't gimme that, it's the only thing that makes sense.

Our kid: Well it ay true, whatever sense it makes to yow. That's yampy, that is, going on at me like that.

Voices was up proper by then, and our kid stood there blartin, saying it over and over, and me still saying it had to be them, cause who else. Neither on us give an inch. It just stopped, hanging there, nobody believing the other one, nobody backing down neither.
