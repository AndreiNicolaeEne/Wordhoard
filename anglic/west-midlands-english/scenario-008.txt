The child: Mom, can we get one though. A trampoline. Everyone's got one.

The mother: We ay getting one, bab, not this side of Christmas and not the side after that neither.

The child: Why though.

The mother: Cause there's no money for it, that's why. I ay dodging it, I'm telling yer straight. Yer dad's hours got cut at the yard and the leccy's gone up an all, so it's not happening, not this year.

The child: I'll do the bins every week if we get one. And hoover through an all.

The mother: Yer'll do the bins cause yer live here, love, not to buy one off me. That ay how it works.

The child: What about me birthday then. Could it be for me birthday.

The mother: If it's still what yer want come yer birthday, and if there's a bit spare by then, we'll have a proper look, the two of us. I ay promising nothing. But I'll look, that's fair ay it.

The child: The one up the entry's got one already, and her mom's not even working.

The mother: Good for her mom. What goes on in her house is her business, this is ours, and I'm telling yer how it stands in it. Somebody else having one don't put money in my pocket, does it. I never got one for our kid neither when he was your age, and he lived.

The child: (goes quiet, kicking the skirting)

The mother: Tell yer what, though. I'll gerr yer a proper football off the outdoor on the way back, and Saturday, if yer donnies are washed and yer behave, we'll have yer down the park where the big one is, free, long as yer like.

The child: S'pose that'll do. For now.

The mother: Tara-a-bit of a sulk then. Go and get yer coat.
