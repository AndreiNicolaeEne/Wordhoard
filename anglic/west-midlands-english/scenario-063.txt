You remember that night the wind was up something wicked, banging the bins about and the whole street rattling like it wanted to come down an all. Well I got up the next morning, half seven, kettle not even on yet, looked out the back and thought, that ay right, that is not right at all. The fence between us and next door, the whole panel of it, flat on the ground. Crushed right down under this great big old tree, branches everywhere, right across where the runner beans was.

First thing I done was go and have a proper look, cause you want to know whose tree it is before you say owt to anybody, don't yer. And when I got right up to it I could see clear as day the roots of it was sat bang on the line, maybe a whisker more on their side if anything, but it'd been leaning over towards ours for years and years. Everybody up the street knew that tree leaned. It'd been leaning since before we even moved in near enough.

So I went round the front and knocked on, and the neighbor come to the door in her dressing gown looking about as rough as I felt.

Me: You seen the state of the fence?

The neighbor: I have. Ay it terrible. That wind last night, I never heard nothing like it in me life.

Me: It's your tree brought it down though.

The neighbor: Well that's the wind for yer, ay it. Nothing anybody could've done about that.

Me: That tree's been leaning for years though. That ay just the wind, that's the wind finishing off what was already going.

The neighbor: It's a tree, they lean. What am I supposed to do about that.

Me: Yo cor just say it's nothing to do with yer, come on.

And when I told me mom about it that dinnertime her says straight off, oh I remember saying summat to her about that years back, when we had the man round doing the guttering, I said to her then it wants looking at before it comes down on somebody, and did anybody listen, did they heck as like.

So then we was stuck, cause I wanted half the cost of putting the fence back off her, and she wo have it, said it was an act of God and nothing to do with negligence or nothing like that, proper word her used an all, negligence, like we was stood in a court somewhere. Her grandkids doing gambols on the lawn like nothing was the matter while the two of us stood there being proper yampy about it, if I'm honest, both digging our heels in when we could've sorted the whole thing in an afternoon if either of us had just said fair enough, let's split it and be done.

It was the old fella two doors up sorted it in the end, and nobody even asked him to. He'd been watching the whole palaver over his hedge for weeks I reckon, and one Saturday morning he just come round, no fuss, stood there with his hands in his pockets and says.

The older neighbor: Now then. I've been thinking about your tree business.

Me: Oh ar.

The older neighbor: I reckon the fairest thing, and I ay taking sides mind, is you split the wood and the panels straight down the middle, but whoever's tree it was pays for the disposal of it, cause that's a job in itself getting a tree that size took away, and that's got nothing to do with whose fence it damaged, that's just whose tree it was in the first place.

And do you know, that worked a treat, cause it give her a way of saying yes without saying I'd been right all along, and it give me a way of saying yes without saying I'd backed down over nothing. Her paid the tree surgeon to come and take the trunk and the big limbs off, and we split the panels and the posts and the gravel boards between us, straight down the middle like he said, and that was it settled, no more said about it.

First Saturday, me and our kid out there with the wheelbarrow and the bow saw, and her and her husband out the other side with theirs, and honestly bab it was that stiff you could've cut it with the saw and all. Nobody hardly talking, just morning to yer, morning, and getting on with it. Took us the best part of three weekends all told, what with one thing and another, her husband's back playing up one week so we lost a Saturday there, and then we had to wait on the gravel boards coming from the timber yard out past the islands, felt like we went all round the Wrekin just to get one fence finished if I'm honest, but we got there in the end.

Better in a way if anything, cause now we know we can get through a proper falling out and come out the other side of it alright, not blartin about it for months after.
