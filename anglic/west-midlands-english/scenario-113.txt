Alright bab, sit down a minute, I gotta tell yer about last night else I'll burst with it.

So it's me, our kid, the neighbour from over the entry, and a mate from work, round at me mom's for cards. Kids had been having a gambol on the lawn out front all afternoon so it was donnies washed before tea and then straight into the front room once the plates was cleared. Agreed the stake before a card went out, tuppence a hand, nothing that'd hurt nobody, just enough to make it mean summat when yer lost it.

First few hands went by normal like. Nobody up, nobody down, just passing the deal round and having a laugh, our kid moaning about her cards same as always, on about how her never gets owt worth holding. The workmate had come the long way round, all round the two islands by the lights cause of the roadworks, so he was still going on about that an all, moaning he'd gone all round the Wrekin just to get there.

Then about hand five or six, the neighbour's luck turned. Her won that one, then the next, then the one after, four hands running her had, and yer know how it goes, first couple you think aye, her's on a run, good for her. By the third you're looking a bit harder at the cards going down. By the fourth the workmate says summat, dead quiet like, more to himself than to anybody.

The workmate: That's a clean shuffle and all, ay it.

Nobody made much of it at the time. Our kid laughed it off, said leave her be, some folk just get the luck of it, saft to make owt of a good run. So we carried on, and it come round to the neighbour dealing again, and this time I'm watching the cards come off the bottom of that deck proper close, and summat about it just wo right. Too neat, too quick, like her'd already sorted where they was going before her'd even started dealing them out.

I day leave it alone this time.

Me: Yer dealing off the bottom there, I seen it plain.

The table went dead quiet for a second. The neighbour looked up at us, face like stone, not a flicker on her.

The neighbour: I never. I day do nothing of the sort.

Me: I'm telling yer, I saw it, plain as owt, right there.

The neighbour: Well yow saw wrong then, cause it ay happened. It never has and it wo happening now neither.

And that was that, flat as a shut door, no give in it at all. Our kid sided with us straight off, said her'd noticed it too the last couple of hands, summat off about how quick the cards was moving off the bottom, her'd just been too polite to say. But the workmate, who'd made the crack about the shuffle in the first place, went and turned round and backed the neighbour instead, said he was only having a laugh before and now everybody's making a mountain of nothing, that the neighbour's always been straight with cards long as he's known her, and calling her a cheat over a card game's a yampy thing to be doing over tuppence a hand.

So there we are, going back over hand after hand, who held what, who played what and when, trying to work out if any of it added up wrong. And honest bab, it settled nothing. Yer can argue a hand backwards all night and never prove a thing, cause once the cards is in the discard yo cor unpick it, can yer, they'm just a pile on the table by then. We went round and round it like going all round the Wrekin, the long way about, and ended up back where we started, nowhere.

Still, nobody got up from the table. That's the daft thing about it, we just kept on playing, dealt the next hand and the one after that. Only the whole feel of the night had gone a bit off after that, a bit careful, everyone watching everyone else's hands a shade too close now, no more laughing over who was blartin about their cards going bad, just heads down and quiet, somebody nipping out to the outdoor at half time for a couple more cans just to have summat to do with theirselves.

Eleven hands we played in all by the time we called it, and when we tallied up at the end the neighbour was up by two hands over the rest of us put together, which, depending who yer ask, either means her'd just had a bostin night on the cards or it means summat else entirely, and nobody at that table's agreed which, not to this day.

Last hand of the night, as it goes, it wo the neighbour what took it. It was our kid, best hand her'd had all night and about time an all after moaning the whole way through about never getting owt. Her sat there dead pleased with herself, said that's one in the eye for whoever thinks the luck round here's crooked.

We packed the cards away, had a last cuppa, and the neighbour was straight up saying tara-a-bit like nothing had gone on, breezy as owt, out the door and off up the entry home towards the cut same as always. The workmate went not long after, said he was keeping out the oss road before it got proper dark with them roadworks still on. Me and our kid sat there a bit longer just going over it again between us, same as before, getting nowhere same as before.

And that's the thing, bab, it's never gone away since. Every time the four of us sit down again, somebody brings it up, even if it's only a joke to start with, someone'll say go on then, shuffle it proper this time, and the neighbour'll go all quiet and stony again, and half the table's laughing and half of it ay laughing at all, and we go right round it again same as that night, and it settles nothing, same as it never has.

I don't reckon it ever will and all, if I'm being straight with yer. Some things just sit there at the table with yer forever, don't they.
