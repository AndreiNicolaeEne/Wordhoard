The mother: I’m tellin’ yer now, I want to put me mom’s name on her. It’s only right, bab.

The father: Yower mom’s name? Cor, no. Every other babby down this road’s got that one already, it ay original, ay it.

The mother: It ay about bein’ original, it’s about respect for the family.

The father: Well what about that one off the telly then, the one all the wenches am callin’ their kids now?

The mother: That’s a yampy idea, that is. Saft as anything. Her’ll get ripped to bits at school for havin’ everybody else’s name an’ all.

The father: Better than yower idea though, that wo.

The mother: It ay, so don’t you start.

The father: Well what about me dad’s name then, for the middle bit? That’d be bostin, that would.

The mother: Our kid’s little wench has already got that off yower side, and so has yower sister’s babby. That’s three of ’em runnin’ round at Christmas wi’ the same name, nobody’d know who yam shoutin’ at.

The father: So whose gerrin the final say on this then, eh? Cause it ay gonna be yow, actin’ like it’s just yower call to make.

The babby starts blartin’ through on the monitor.

The mother: Now look what yer’ve gone and done, her’s off again. I day sleep proper last night neither, an’ I ay startin’ tonight.

The father: I never said it was just me, bab. I’m sayin’ we cor keep goin’ all round the Wrekin wi’ this every night and gerrin nowhere.

The mother: We’m gerrin nowhere either road. I’m wowld out.

The father: Get yer donnies washed then and get up them stairs. We ay sortin’ this tonight.

The mother: Tara-a-bit then. Keep out the oss road.

The father: Night night, bab.
