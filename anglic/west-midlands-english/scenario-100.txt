Right, bab, sit yerself down a minute, cos I know where yow'm at right now, and I ay just saying that to say it. I've bin there. There's one year out of all the years I've had where if yo asked me straight, which one broke yer hardest, I wouldn't have to think twice. I don't need to tell yer the year, it dae matter what year it was, it's just the year, the one I mean when I say "that year" and everybody who knows me knows exactly which one I'm on about.

It started with the job going. Not sacked, like, nothing I'd done, they just come in one Friday and said the whole line was shutting, no more shifts after the month, and that was that. One minute I've got a wage coming in reg'lar as clockwork, next minute I ay got nothing coming in at all. And it ay like factory work grows on trees round here no more, not like it used to when the mom's generation could walk out one gate and in another the same day. I put in for everything. Everything. And half the places dae even write back to tell yer no, yo just sit there waiting on a phone that dae ring.

First thing to go was the daft stuff, the stuff yo dae even notice yo're spending till it ay there no more. Sky went. The Friday chippy went, and that hurt more than it should've, cos that was our little thing, us and the kids, chips and a bit of telly of a Friday, nothing fancy, but it was ours. Days out went. New donnies for the kids' trainers went, and I remember stood there in the shop looking at the price of school shoes and just putting em back on the shelf and saying we'd manage another few weeks with the old ones taped up.

Then it got past the small stuff and onto the stuff that really costs yer summat to give up. The car went first, cos yo cor run a car on nothing, and that meant walking everywhere, which meant walking the kids to school in the rain and back, which meant I couldn't take on nothing that was any distance away even if it come up. Then it was the heating, we had it on of an evening only, everybody in one room under blankets like we was camping in us own front room. I remember our kid saying "it ay even cold in here mom" just to make his sister feel better about it, and her knowing full well it was blimmin freezing.

And then come the bit I found hardest of the lot, which was having to ask. I'd never had to ask nobody for nothing in me life before that, not properly, and I mean that as a fact not a boast, it's just how I was brought up, yo manage yer own. But there come a point where the rent was due and there wor nothing there for it, and I had to go round to the mom's and say the words out loud, ask her to lend us enough to get through a couple of month. Stood on her step saying it and feeling about two foot tall. Her never made me feel small about it, not once, her just said "well why day yo say summat sooner, yampy article" and went and got her purse. But it dae matter what her said, I still felt it, that shame of it, cos I'd always bin the one helping other folk out, not the other way round.

That went on for close on six month. Six month of barely a shift here or there, a day's work if I was lucky, off the books cash in hand jobs that dae even cover the electric some weeks. And I'll tell yer the day that felt like the very bottom of it. I'd gone down the job centre and got told there wor nothing, again, and I walked home the long way, all round the Wrekin near enough, cos I couldn't face going straight back in and telling the family there wor nothing again, and I ended up sat on the wall by the cut, just sat there, and I'm not ashamed to say it now, I sat there blartin like a kid, proper blartin, on me own, where nobody could see us. That was the bottom. I remember thinking I don't know how we get out the other side of this one.

But summat always comes, and it come from where I dae expect it. A workmate from years back, one I hadn't spoke to in donkey's, heard off somebody I was struggling and turned up at the door one evening with a boot full of shopping and wouldn't take a penny for it, said "yo'd have done the same for me, now shut yer mouth and take it in before it goes off." And a week or two after that a bit of work come up through him and all, agency work, only a few shifts a week to start, nothing to write home about, but it was summat, it was money coming in again, and that changes everything, even a little bit of it does.

From there it was slow. It took the best part of eight month to get anywhere near back to how things was, bit by bit, shift by shift, the odd bit of overtime when it come up, before I'd got summat steady again yo could rely on. And in among all that I learned who yo can actually count on when it comes down to it, and it ay always who yo'd think. Some as I'd have put money on standing by us just went quiet, dae answer the phone, and some as I'd hardly given a second thought to was the ones turned up with the shopping, or the lift, or the "how'm yo getting on" that meant it. Yo find out quick who's just talk.

And I learned summat about meself and all, which is that I could stand a lot more than I ever thought I could, and still get the kids' donnies washed before tea and still sit down and play a bit of a gambol on the lawn with em of an evening even when inside I was frit half to death about the letters coming through the door. Yo don't know that about yerself till it's tested, and I wouldn't wish the test on nobody, but I'm glad I know it now.

It started to feel survivable, if I'm honest, round about the time I could go the outdoor of a Friday again without doing the sums three times in me head first. Small thing. But it meant summat had shifted, that we'd come up out the worst of it even if we wor all the way back yet.

So that's why I'm telling yer this now, bab, cos I know this is your hard year, I can see it on yer, and I ay gonna sit here and tell yer it'll sort itself, cos it dae just sort itself, it takes yer getting through it, day by day. But what I am telling yer is yo ay doing it on yer own, not while I'm about. Any shift yo need summat carrying, any night yo need somebody to just sit with yer so it ay so quiet in the house, any time yo need to borrow enough to get yer through, yo come to me first, yo hear, and yo dae have to feel two foot tall about it neither, cos that's what I'm here for. I'll be about. Keep out the oss road, and I'll see yer soon, alright, tara-a-bit for now.
