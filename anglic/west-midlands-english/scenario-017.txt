The aunt: Now bab, sit yerself down a minute, I ay come round just for a cuppa.

The younger one: Go on then, what yo saying.

The aunt: I was talking to yer mom's friend from up the club, and her says this one's ever so well thought of, all round. Hard grafter, out the house by six every morning, never a day sick off work in his life. Temperament with it an' all, gentle as owt, none of that gobby carry-on some of 'em have, and he ay saft neither, got his head screwed on proper. And the family, well, yow know the family, decent through and through, never a spot of bother off any of 'em. We'm not all wrong about this one, bab.

The younger one: I know, I know, I ay saying nothing against him.

The aunt: So what's wrong with him then, go on, tell me. What exactly is it that ay right about a fella like that.

The younger one: It ay nothing wrong with him, honest. I just ay got the time for it at minute, what with work and everything, I'm rushed off me feet as it is.

The aunt: Rushed off yer feet. Yo sound just like yer cousin, day yer, on the same how's-yer-father, blartin her eyes out saying her wannit ready, her wannit ready, and look at her now, married five year and glad as anything her never won that one. Her'd have gone all round the Wrekin looking for summat better and found nothing, and her knows it.

The younger one: I hear what yer saying, and I ay being funny with yer, I ay. But it's my business when I'm ready, and I'm just not, not yet.

The aunt: Aer, awright bab, I wo push yer no more. But the offer's there, yo know where I am, and it wo be going nowhere while yo make yer mind up. Just don't leave it too long, that's all I'm saying.

The younger one: I hear yer. Ta for coming round.

The aunt: Tara-a-bit then, love. Keep out the oss road till next time.
