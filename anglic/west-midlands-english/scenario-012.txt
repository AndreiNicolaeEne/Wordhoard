Me: Mom, sit down a minute, will yer. I need to tell yer summat and I need yer not to say nothing till I've finished, or I'll never get it out.

The mother: Yer frightening me now. What's up, bab?

Me: It ay nothing bad like that, it's just been sat on me that long it's gone heavy. It's about me leg. The scar.

The mother: The bike one?

Me: That's just it, mom. It wo the bike. I day fall off no bike, not that day nor no other day. I've told yer that for over twenty year and it's a lie every single time, and I'm sick of hearing meself say it.

The mother: What are yer on about?

Me: Yer remember I used to go down the cut with our kid and them two off the end of the road, the whole summer we was about ten. There was that big tree hanging over the water past the bridge, everybody's tree, and we all used to climb it and jump off the low branch into the cut. Everyone said it was bostin down there of a summer, best spot going, and it wo even deep really, but it felt like summat when yer was that age.

The mother: I remember yer coming home wet through every day of that summer, aye.

Me: Well there was a dare going round. One of the others had gone up to the next branch and jumped, and I'd backed out the time before, stood there like a wammel while they all called me saft and yampy and went on home laughing about it. They day let me forget it neither, weeks of it, "oh here he's come, scared of a tree." So this one day I said right, I'll show yer, and I went up past where I meant to go, right up to the top one, higher than any of us had ever gone, cause I wanted it done proper so nobody could say nothing again.

The mother: Yer never.

Me: I did, mom. And I stood up there and me legs was shaking summat rotten but I wo coming down without jumping, not this time, so I went. And soon as I hit the water I knew summat wo right, it knocked all the wind clean out me and I couldn't get me breath, and then I felt me leg go, like it had opened right up on summat under there, a bit of iron or a trolley or God knows what, yer never see what's down the bottom of the cut. It stung like nothing I've ever known since.

The mother: Oh bab.

Me: The others pulled me out onto the towpath and they was all blartin, proper crying, cause there was blood everywhere and none of us knew what to do, and I was blartin an' all, from the shock of it more than the pain by then. And we sat there soaked through, all of us, and one of them said, right, if yer mom finds out we was jumping off the top we'll all get killed, all of us, not just yer. And that wo a lie neither, cause I'd have got proper leathered and so would they, cause their moms and dads all knew each other and it'd have gone round the whole street by tea time.

The mother: So yer made summat up between yer.

Me: We agreed it there and then, still dripping on the towpath. We'd say I fell off me bike coming down the entry by the garages, caught me leg on the pedal or the wall, summat like that. And that's what I told yer, straight out, soon as I got in, donnies still filthy and me leg wrapped in somebody's jumper, and yer believed it, and yer took me straight up the doctor's with it, and it got seen to and stitched under that story, and I've told the same story ever since. Every time anybody's asked about it, every girlfriend, every doctor, our kid's kids when they were little and nosy about it, I've said bike, every time, twenty odd year of saying bike.

The mother: I never once thought different. Yer told it that quick and that straight I never had cause to.

Me: I know, mom, that's half of what's been sat on me. Yer believed me straight off, no messing, and I've let yer carry that belief round for over twenty year without knowing it wo even true. It ay a big thing in the grand scheme, I know that, it's an old scar off an old jump, but it's the lying to yer that's got heavier the older I've got, not lighter. I've got me own now, and the thought of them ever lying to me like that, straight-faced, for years and years, and me never knowing, it turned summat over in me. I couldn't have that sat between us no more, not for another twenty year, not going all round the Wrekin every time somebody asks and me giving the long way round the truth instead of just saying it.

The mother: Yer had me proper going there for a minute, I thought yer was gonna tell me summat terrible.

Me: It is terrible in its own little way though, ay it. I let yer treat a lie for twenty year.

The mother: Yer was a kid, bab. A kid up a tree yer had no business being up, taking a dare off kids who should've known better an' all. What was yer, ten?

Me: About that, aye. Our kid was there an' all, jumping off the lower branch same as ever, and he never said nothing about it neither all these years, so it wo just me carrying it.

The mother: I could've told yer meself it wo the bike, if I'm honest. I always thought that cut on the pedal story was a funny shape for a bike wound, but yer was that upset and that sure of yerself I let it go. Mothers know more than they let on half the time.

Me: That doe make it right though, me letting yer think what yer thought.

The mother: No, it doe. But it doe make yer a wammel neither. Yer was a kid who took a stupid dare, hurt yerself summat rotten, and panicked with yer mates, and yer've carried the guilt of a daft lie for twenty odd year longer than most grown adults would even bother to remember it. That's not nothing, that's yer being too hard on yerself if anything.

Me: I just wanted to say it proper, finally, and say sorry for it an' all. Sorry for the lie, and sorry for how long I let it sit there believed.

The mother: Well yer've said it now, and I'm not going to sit here and cry over a bike that never existed. Come here.

Me: Mom, don't start blartin over it now, that's the last thing I need.

The mother: I'm not, I'm not. I'm just glad yer told me, that's all. It's a load off yer, I can see it on yer face already.

Me: It is, honest to God, it is.

The mother: Right well. Next time yer decide to jump off the top of a tree into a cut yer cor see the bottom of, don't. That's all I'll say on it.

Me: I think me jumping days are well behind me now, mom.

The mother: Good. Now put the kettle on, and we'll say no more about it. Go on, I'll have one an' all.

Me: Tara-a-bit then, I'll pop the kettle on and come straight back through, I ay going nowhere.

The mother: Yer better not, after all that.
