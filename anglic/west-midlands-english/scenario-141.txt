Yer all know summat's coming already, I can tell by yer faces, everybody gone quiet and watching me like I'm about to tell yer somebody's died. Nobody's died. But I ay going all round the Wrekin about it neither, I'll say it straight. It's about the fire. The night the back kitchen went up, when I wor still a kid meself, and our kid got the blame for it and never shifted it off since.

Yer all remember how it was. Dad had gone up the outdoor for a bottle, mom was next door, and the pan got left on and the chip fat caught, and by the time the engines had got up the entry half the back of the house was black and gone. Mom stood in the yard in her nightie blartin her eyes out, and everybody looking round straight off for somebody to point at. And it landed on our kid, cause our kid was the last one seen going in that kitchen, and it wor never questioned again after, not once. The row that come after it. I ay never heard shouting like it before nor since, mom and dad going at each other in the doorway with the black bones of the kitchen still smoking behind em, and our kid stood there taking it and not saying one word back, cause what was there to say. And it never wore off neither. Every do since, every Christmas, every wedding, somebody's managed to get a dig in under their breath about pans and kitchens, and our kid's had that look on people's faces for the best part of thirty year now. I remember our kid doing gambols on the lawn that summer before it happened, not a care in the world, and after it, our kid never wor that easy in a room again, not properly, not once.

It wor never our kid.

I've known that the whole time. I saw it meself, that same night, before any of the shouting started. I saw the uncle come stumbling out that kitchen half cut, leaving the door on the latch behind him, and I day understand what I wor looking at, not for years after, cause I wor no age at all. And then, later, I got telled it straight, in confidence, by somebody who ay here to speak for herself no more, and I promised her I'd say nothing, and I kept that promise a good deal longer than any promise ought to be kept.

I nearly said it once before tonight. At the funeral, if yer remember, out the back having a smoke, and it wor right there on the tip of me tongue, and I looked round at the state of everybody that day and I thought, not on top of this an all, and I swallowed it back down, and it's sat in me ever since like summat I ay never properly digested.

I kept it cause I wor frit of what it'd start up again, all that anger that had finally gone quiet after all them years, yo cor put a fire like that out twice, and I didn't fancy being the one lit the match on it a second time. I kept it, if I'm honest, to protect the uncle an all, cause he wor in a state them days, living down by the cut on his own with next to nothing, and I couldn't see the sense in throwing him under it on top of all that. And mostly I kept it cause I didn't want to be the one that caused more damage, stood there with a truth in me hand while everybody else stood on ground that felt settled. It wor never settled though. It just went quiet. There's a difference, and I know it, cause I've felt that difference every day for thirty year.

It's took summat out of me, carrying it. Yer don't know what it does, watching somebody carry a name that ay theirs to carry, year in year out, past all the islands and every family do going, and knowing the whole time yow could put it down for em with one sentence, and yow don't. I've felt saft for it more times than I can say, sat on a truth like that. That's bin mine to carry an all, that has, right alongside our kid's.

So there it is. Said now, finally.
