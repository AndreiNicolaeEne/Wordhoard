Six o'clock and I'm up for the bathroom and there's water under the back door, only ankle deep, creeping in under the step like it ay bothered about being seen. Thought, right, that's the drain gone again, kettle on, don't fret about it yet. Rang the mom.

Me: It's nowt, just a drop, I'll sort it before work.

The mom: Leave the towels down and get yer wellies on, don't be saft about it. Tara-a-bit, ring us when it's sorted.

Went back through about seven and it'd come up past me ankle proper, right along the hall carpet and up the entry between us and next door an' all, and I thought this ay no drain, this is summat else. Started shifting what I could, telly up on the sideboard, the lad's school bag up the stairs, our kid's boxes of records up on the landing, photos off the low shelf first because they don't come back once they're gone.

By half nine it were up to me knee, cold as owt, and I'm stood in the kitchen doorway wondering where else it can go. Chairs on the table, the microwave on the chairs, everything what could climb, climbing. You'd have to be yampy to go out the front by then, even the island down the bottom of the road had gone under, and word was the cut had burst its bank again. Our kid come round in his waders.

Our kid: This ay stopping, bab, get them boxes up higher now, not in a minute.

Me: I know, we'm doing it, giz a hand with the settee, before her legs go under an' all.

It kept coming all morning, past the skirting, past where we'd put stuff safe before, till gone two it were up past the top of the settee cushions stood on end, higher than owt else had gone. Then it just sat there for hours, slapping the walls, that dull sound coming through the brick, and it wo going down, not for hours yet, and nobody blartin, just watching it, waiting.

About seven it started dropping, dead slow, an inch you'd only clock if you'd been staring at the same brick for an hour, which I had.

Went back the next day and there's the line right round the front room wall, dark as a tide mark, well past the settee, past the telly stand. Stood there looking at it a good while, saying nowt, then keep out the oss road, our kid said, and off he went.
