The mother sat the pair of them down on the settee first, close in beside her, before she said one word about it. Her waited till they was both looking right at her.

The mother: Now listen to me a minute, the both of yer. I got summat to tell yer. Yer grandad's died, bab. Last night, in his sleep. He's gone.

The eldest: But what does that mean though, mom, like, forever? Is he just nothing now?

The mother: I ay gonna tell yer nothing but the truth of it, love. It means we cor see him no more, we cor talk to him, he ay coming round of a Sunday with his bag of humbugs ever again. His body just stopped working and there ay no fixing that, not this time. It's alright to feel however yer feeling about it, whatever that is.

The youngest: Was he cold, mom?

The mother: He day feel cold, bab, and he day feel no pain either. He was warm in his bed and he wo have known a thing about it. I promise yer that.

Her pulled the pair of 'em in close then, one under each arm, holding on proper tight.

The mother: Come here, bab, come on then.

The youngest: Will we ever see him again though?

The mother: Not like we used to, no, not in the flesh, and I ay gonna tell yer summat daft just so it feels easier. But he's in the stories we tell, and our kid's got his laugh, yer know. That's summat, even if it ay everything, and there's nothing saft about crying over it neither.

Her held 'em a minute longer before letting 'em go.

The mother: Right. Over the next few days there's gonna be people coming round the house, and we'll go and see him one more time to say tara properly, and then there's the funeral after. It'll be a sad old few days but yow ay on yer own in it, either of yer. Go and wash yer donnies now and have yer gambol on the lawn a bit, I'll give yer a shout for tea. Tara-a-bit.
