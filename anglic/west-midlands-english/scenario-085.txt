The eldest: Sit yerself down, bab, I ain't stopping long, me knees are giving me gyp again.

The second: They always are.

The eldest: Fourteen year now if it's a day. Cor get down the entry steps of a morning without they crack like knuckles. Doctor reckons the cartilage's gone, wore right through to the bone.

The second: Cartilage, hark at her. I'll tell yer what's wore through, it's me shoulder. Three, four nights a month I'm up half the night with it, cor get settled on either side, end up sat in the chair blartin' with it till it's near enough time to get up anyway.

The third: You two dow know yer born. I've had this back since before either on yer knew me, since I were a girl gooin all round the Wrekin on me bike sooner than sit still of an evening. It ay never once let up, not a week's peace off it in, what, sixty year now?

The eldest: Sixty year.

The third: Sixty year, bab. Yow try telling me a bad knee's owt against that.

The second: Well go on then, since we'm all sat here comparing notes, who's got it worst? Come on, out with it, nobody's saying.

The eldest: Her has, I spect. Her's had longest to build the case.

The third: Cheeky mare, that ay how it works.

The second: I ay backing down neither, mind, me shoulder's proper yampy some nights, worse than either on yer's.

The third: And me back's saft an' all if that's owt to go by. We're all in the same state, bab, just in different bits of us.

The eldest: Aye. Saft the lot of us.

They all laughed then, same as they always did, none on 'em give an inch, and the eldest put the kettle on and said no more about it, only, keep out the oss road going home, the pair on yer, tara-a-bit.
