Right bab, listen here a minute, cause this is summat yer wanna know before yer settle in proper.

There's a fella goes round here, always smilin, always got a deal on the go. Yow'll clock him by the island near the shops, hangin about outside the outdoor of an evenin, or collarin folk down the entry an' all, chattin like he's known yer donkeys years. Friendly as owt, calls everybody bab and love, asks after yer mom and yer kids like he's family already. We'm all new once, bab, and that's exactly what he's after. That's the sort to watch, the ones that friendly that quick, it ay natural.

He'll tell yer he's got summat goin, a flat goin cheap, a job lined up, a deal on a phone or a car, sounds a right bostin deal, too good to say no to. That's the whole trick of it, bab, it sounds too good to go askin questions about. Yo cor trust a deal that good, yo cor. He'll want a bit up front, just to hold it for yer, nothin much. Yer give him that and it's the last yer ever see of him or yer money. He's gone, off round the Wrekin and back, never seen on this road again.

Our kid fell for it, few years back, when he first got a little flat down by the cut. Bloke had two hundred quid off him for a room what never existed. Our kid ended up blartin down the phone to me mom, lost his rent money and had to go cap in hand to the council. He ay yampy, our kid, just too trustin, and even the sharp ones can look saft when someone's that smooth.

Signs of it: he never gives yer a proper address, changes his story if yer ask twice, wants cash only, and it's always today or never. I day believe it meself til it happened that close.

If he comes at yer, just say no and keep walkin, yow don't stand about bein polite about it. And before yow hand a penny to anyone new askin for money up front, ask the one at the corner shop first, her's been here donkeys years and her knows every face round here.

Right, I'll leave yer to it, keep out the oss road now. Tara-a-bit, bab.
