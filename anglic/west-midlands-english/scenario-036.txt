Bab, sit down a minute, I've got to tell yer about this Christmas because it wo half a day.

So it started back in November when I said I'd have everyone round this year, right, cause me mom's had it every year since I don't know when and her's getting on a bit now, and I just come out with it, said go on then, I'll do it, how hard can it be. Soon as it were out me mouth me stomach went. I day sleep proper for a week after, just laying there of a night thinking about gravy and whether the oven was even big enough.

I drew the menu up dead ambitious, I did. Not just a turkey and the trimmings neither, oh no. I'd got it in me head I'd do a beef an' all for them as don't really go on turkey, and a veggie thing for the eldest's girlfriend, and three different potatoes cause our kid's kids won't touch a roastie if it's got any bits on it, and a trifle made from scratch, and pigs in blankets done proper, not shop ones. Looking back at it now it wo a menu, it were a list of ways to have a breakdown.

And the numbers kept creeping an' all. First it was just us lot, then me mom says could her bring the neighbour cause the neighbour's on her own this year, then our kid goes can he bring his girlfriend's mother, and by the time I'd finished counting on me fingers it'd gone from six to twelve and I still hadn't got a bigger table sorted.

Went to get the big shop done, trolley the size of a small car, and do you know I could get everything on that list, everything, bar one thing, parsnips. Gone. I went all round the Wrekin looking for parsnips, bab, honest, the big one out by the islands, the little corner shop, even tried the outdoor thinking they might have a few veg knocking about, they don't obviously, I wo thinking straight by that point. In the end me mom turns up on the day with a bag of em out her own garden like it were nothing at all, and I could have cried on the spot.

Night before I was up till gone one doing prep, peeling and chopping and getting stuff in bowls with cling film stretched over the top, kitchen looked like a bomb had gone off in a Tupperware factory. I'd even wrote meself a schedule, proper timed out on a bit of paper, turkey in at this time, potatoes on at that time, everything ticking along like clockwork of a morning. Yo cor plan a morning like that though, no matter how yampy you are with a biro the night before, it just day happen how you'd drawn it.

Cause come the morning the turkey wo playing ball at all, oven running hotter than it should, and I'd got meself that far behind I put the pigs in blankets in and went off to sort veg and forgot all about em, and next thing there's smoke pouring out the oven door and I've got a tray of what used to be pigs in blankets and is now just a row of little black logs. I stood there looking at em thinking, right, that's it, first one I've ever done and it's already ruined.

Had to bin the lot and start a new batch, legging it round the kitchen trying to find more bacon in the fridge, swearing under me breath the whole time and getting saft with meself over summat so daft.

That's when the neighbour turns up early, bless her, hour before anyone else was meant to be there, come round the entry rather than knocking the front like she normally would, and I just looked at her standing there and went...

Me: "Yow couldn't just wash yer donnies and get stuck in, could yer."

And to be fair to her her did, no messing about, straight in wrapping bacon round sausages like her'd been doing it her whole life.

By this point the oven's rammed, top shelf's got the turkey still going, bottom shelf's got the beef trying to squeeze its way in, hob's got four pans going and not a scrap of room for a fifth, and I'm stood there juggling potatoes and gravy at the same time thinking summat has got to give. Ended up doing the trifle on the side, balanced on top the washing machine, which I know sounds manky when I say it out loud but needs must on a day like that.

One thing though, one thing that come out better than I had any right to expect after the morning I'd had, the veggie thing for the eldest's girlfriend, this nut roast sort of affair I'd knocked together not really knowing what I was doing with it, and it come out gorgeous. Bostin, honest to God, better than owt else on that table if I'm saying it meself.

Meal was meant to be on the table for one, ended up gone half two before anybody sat down, everyone starving by then and a bit merry on the sherry, but nobody minded too much once the plates started going round.

First bites went down alright. Me mom went, "Ooh that's lovely that is," about the beef, and the neighbour reckoned the roast potatoes were the best her'd had in years, which set our kid off looking dead pleased with himself for no reason at all cause he day even peel em, I did.

Only real casualty were the sprouts, which I'd rushed at the end and they'd gone all soft and sorry for theirselves, and the aunt, who never minces owt, just looked at one stuck on her fork and went...

The aunt: "These am a bit sad, bab, if I'm honest with yer."

Nobody argued the point.

But it was the nut roast that went first, gone before the turkey practically, everyone going back for seconds of it, even them as had been funny about "having veggie stuff at Christmas" a month before when I first mentioned it. The eldest's girlfriend looked made up with herself. The mother reckoned it were the best thing on the whole table and said so twice, just to make sure I'd heard her the first time.

By the end of it we were all sat round proper stuffed, the little ones gone off for a gambol on the lawn even though it was perishing out there, everyone else just sat where they were, not moving an inch, cups of tea going cold in front of em. Me mom turned to me and went...

The mother: "Well, her's only gone and pulled it off then."

And I said, dead serious, "Never again, I swear down, never again," and everyone had a right laugh at that cause they all know full well I'll be the first one offering next year an' all.

Spent the rest of the evening walking people out to the door, one lot going tara-a-bit, another lot going keep out the oss road, half of em blartin a bit cause it had all come together that nice in the end despite everything, and I stood there in the doorway watching em go off up past the cut, thinking, God help me, bab, I probably will do it again.
