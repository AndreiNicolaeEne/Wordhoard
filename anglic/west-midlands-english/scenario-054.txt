Right, sit yerself down, cause this in't a two minute tale, this took the best part of a year out me life and I'm gonna tell it proper.

So it started cause we'd flat run out of room. Three of us in two bedrooms, our eldest sleeping on a put-you-up in what was meant to be the front room, and the mom coming round every week going "wherever am yer gonna put another one when it comes." Kitchen you couldn't swing a cat in either, me and the wife bumping donnies every time we both wanted the kettle. So we sat round the table one Sunday, tea going cold, and we reckoned it up proper. Me, the wife's brother, and a mate from work who'd done a bit of brickying, three of us, and we said four month, tops. Bostin plan. Dig out the back, put a proper extension up, do the loft an all while we're at it.

Four month. I'll say that again for how it makes me laugh now. Four month turned into eleven, and by the end there'd been six different pairs of hands through that garden, not three.

First fortnight was the wrong turn, and I mean that literal. We'd took the footings out to what we thought was the depth off the plan, got the concrete in, felt dead pleased with ourselves, and then the fella come to check it, turned out we'd read the wrong line off the drawing and it wanted going down another two courses for the clay round here. Had to break the lot back out. Two solid weeks, gone, just to get back to where we thought we already was. Me mate stood there going,

The mate: I told yer it looked shallow, I told yer.

Me: Yo day say nothing of the sort, yo just stood there drinking me tea.

Anyroad. Back to square one, proper depth this time, and off we went again.

Then there's the day the extra hands turned up and nobody'd asked em. I come out the front to get summat off the van and there's the wife's dad and his neighbour, both in old work trousers, wheelbarrow already loaded, not a word said to nobody.

The father-in-law: Well yo cor do it on yer own, can yer.

Weren't a question. He just got stuck in. That was the best day for weeks, cause with two extra pairs of donnies we shifted more spoil in one Saturday than we had the whole fortnight before.

Weather turned on us proper nasty round about month four. Rained the best part of a week solid, ground gone to soup, the trench full of water every morning like a moat round a castle. Couldn't lay a block, couldn't mix a bag of sand and cement, nothing. Just stood in the kitchen looking out the window at it, going yampy with the waiting. Our kid come round one day, took one look and went,

Our kid: Yo'm gonna need a boat, not a barrow, out there.

Ran short of block about halfway through an all. Order was meant to come Thursday, didn't turn up till the following week, and we'd near enough finished what we had. So we made do, used the leftover reclaim off the old wall we'd took down at the start for the bit round the back where it wouldn't show, mixed in what was left of a different batch for the inside skin. Looks a bit patchwork if you know where to look, but nobody looking at it from the lawn's any the wiser.

Had a proper scare an all, up on the scaffold doing the ridge line. Foot went from under me, must've had grease off me boot on the board, and down I went, caught meself on the lower lift, more luck than sense, landed on me back in the flower bed the wife had only just done. Knocked all the wind out me, lay there a minute not sure which way was up. Her come running out the back door blartin her eyes out thinking I'd broke me neck.

The wife: Don't yer dare move, don't yer dare move, I'm ringing for someone.

Me: I'm alright, love, I'm alright, just knocked meself silly.

Nothing broke, mind, just bruised right down one side and shook up proper for a couple of days. Could've been a lot worse off that height and everybody knew it, even if nobody said it out loud after.

Had a right ding-dong of an argument over the stairs going up to the loft an all, me wanting em turned to save the landing space, me mate from work saying straight run was stronger and quicker and I day know what I was on about. Went back and forth over that for the best part of a fortnight, drawing it out on the back of a fag packet more times than I care to say, before we settled on his way in the end. He wor wrong neither, if I'm honest, though I never told him that to his face.

Worst of it, though, was the side wall. Got most the way up it, stood back to have a look, and it just day look right, out of true by a good bit, you could see it with the naked eye never mind a spirit level. Turned out the first course had gone down crooked right at the start and every course after just followed it out further. Nothing for it but to take the whole thing down, every block, and start again from the footing up. Sickening, that was. Proper sickening, seeing a fortnight's graft coming back down in an afternoon. But it wo going up crooked, not with my name on it, so down it come.

Best day of the whole job, though, was the day the frame finally stood. Roof timbers up, you could stand inside it and see the shape of the rooms for the first time, see where the window'd be, where the kids'd have their beds. Stood there of an evening with a can in me hand looking up at it and thought, right, that's it now, that's the worst behind us. Everyone who'd had a hand in it come round that weekend just to have a look, standing in the mud going bostin, well bostin that, going all quiet and proud like.

Last push was four nights on the trot working past dark, floodlight rigged up off an extension lead, finishing the plastering and the fitting so we could get the family in before winter properly set in. Fingers gone numb, eyes gone square, in bed by half ten and up again for work at six, but you keep going when you can see the end of it.

And then the day it stood finished, really finished, not a tool left lying about, not a bag of sand in the garden, that was a funny sort of day, quiet after all that noise. Stood in the new kitchen with the wife and just looked at it a good while, neither of us saying much.

Tallied it up after, sat with a pen and the back of an envelope like we had at the start. Eleven month start to finish, near enough three times what we reckoned, and six pairs of hands through it besides me own, the wife's brother, the mate from work, the father-in-law and his neighbour, our kid, and a lad from two doors down who turned up for a fortnight near the end and never really left.

Stand in it now of an evening, kettle on, kids thundering about upstairs where the loft used to be nothing but boxes and cobwebs, and you'd never know the half of what it took to get here. Every wall's got a story behind it if you know where to look, mind. I know where every patch is, every bit that went up twice. Nobody else'd ever see it. But I know.
