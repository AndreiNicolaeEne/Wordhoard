The customer: Ey up. This ay mine, this. I come for the grey one, three button, straight hem.

The tailor: It is yours, bab, I only finished it this mornin, I've had it hangin right there.

The customer: Well it ay what I ordered then, is it. Look at it, two button, and the hem's gone all angled at the front. I never asked for that in a month of Sundays.

The tailor: I'm tellin yer, that's exactly what we agreed on when yer come in. Two button, angled hem, I remember writin it down an all.

The customer: Yo cor remember it right then, cause I stood right there and said three button, straight hem, plain as owt. I dai ask for no angle.

The tailor: Aight, aight, don't get yer knickers in a twist, I'll go and check me book, hang on a tick.

The tailor: Right, I've got it here... yer was right about the buttons, it does say three on the ticket, I must've read it wrong off the pad. But the hem, look, it says angled here plain as day, that bit's down to yer.

The customer: Does it now. Well fair enough then, I'll gi yer that one, must've said it wrong meself that day, my head was all ovver the place.

The tailor: No bother, bab, honest mistake on both sides then. Tell yer what, leave it with me, I'll put the extra button on and straighten that hem out proper, have it done for yer in two days, no charge for the mix up.

The customer: Bostin, ta very much, I ay in no rush, our kid's weddin ay till next month anyroad.

The tailor: Sound. Come back Thursday then and it'll be spot on, I promise yer.

The customer: Right y'are then. Tara-a-bit.

The tailor: Tara, bab, mind how yer go.
