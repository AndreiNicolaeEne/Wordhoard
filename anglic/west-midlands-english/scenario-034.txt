The stallholder: Now then, bab. Chips and gravy, bit o' cheese on top, is it?

The regular: Go on then, but hold the cheese today, our kid says I'm gerrin a belly on me.

The stallholder: Yow? Never. It's all muscle that is.

The regular: Yampy, you are.

The stallholder: I ay yampy, I'm honest. Anyroad, where yer bin all week, ay seen yer since Tuesday.

The regular: Working doubles, ay I. Then Sunday the little un had us up the park doin cartwheels and gambols on the lawn till her wore herself out. Donnies black as anything before her'd come in for her tea, mother went spare.

The stallholder: Bless her. Yow still comin down here what, three times a week now?

The regular: More like four if I'm honest. Cheaper than the outdoor and better for yer than what's in there anyroad.

The stallholder: Cor say fairer than that. Trade's been a bit iffy mind, this weather's kept folk in. Tuesday it wo worth lightin the fryer, hardly a soul about 'cept the bloke from over the cut wanting his usual.

The regular: It'll pick up. Always does.

The stallholder: Yeah, well. Here y'are then, bab, mind it's hot.

The regular: Ta. How much I owe yer?

The stallholder: Call it three fifty, seein as yer a regular an all.

The regular: Yow's too soft, yer know that.

The stallholder: Saft in the head, my nan used to say. G'won, get off before it's cold. And tell our kid I said her should come down an all, none of this going all round the Wrekin to avoid us.

The regular: Will do. Keep out the oss road, then.

The stallholder: Tara-a-bit, bab. See yer Thursday.
