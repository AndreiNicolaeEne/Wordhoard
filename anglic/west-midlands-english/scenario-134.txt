Yow ay never seen anything like it, not in all the years we'm had that plot down by the cut. Clear early an' all it was, soon as the marrows come up we knew this one was gonna be a stonker, must've bin getting on for twice what we normally get off it, an' that's no lie, I day exaggerate one bit.

It was the runner beans what done it though, went barmy they did, never seen a count like it off one row, miles past what they normally give yer. Last time anyone could remember owt like it was donkey's years back, before our kid was even born hardly, and even that wo half the size of this lot.

Had to get extra hands in an' all, cousins, neighbours, anyone what'd stand still long enough, cos it was coming in that fast it'd have turned on us else, and yo cor let a good crop sit about. Once we'd sorted what the families needed, rest went in jars an' the freezer, and what wouldn't keep we traded on down the club, swapped for taters an' that off the other plots.

The oldest grower on the site, he's had his patch longer than anyone can remember, he says to me:

The oldest grower: In all my years I ay seen a harvest to touch this one, bab. Best I've known, and I've known a fair few, don't yow worry about that.

Me: What's done it then?

The oldest grower: Rain come at just the right time, see, and we had a mild stretch right through, none of yer sudden cold snaps to knock it back. That's yer answer, plain as owt.

Her from the next plot reckoned it was bostin an' all, said the outdoor had never done such trade off homemade wine neither, everyone wanting a drop to celebrate.

Still talk about it now, we do. Any year after, if it's a poor showing, someone'll only have to say it's nowhere near what we had that year, and everyone knows exactly which one they mean, no need to say no more.
