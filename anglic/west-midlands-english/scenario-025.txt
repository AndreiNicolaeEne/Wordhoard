Her: Am I going mad, or did yer mom just tell me my dumplings were "interesting"?

Me: Her never meant nothing by it, bab.

Her: Her stood there stirring her own pan, going "oh I allus put extra suet in, keeps 'em heavy, that." Heavy! That's a dig and yo know it is.

Me: It ay a dig, it's just how yer mom talks.

Her: And then yer dad started on about the wagon works again. Third time I've had that story, word for word, down to the same bit where he does the foreman's voice.

Me: He tells a good tale though.

Her: He tells the same tale. I could say it along with him now.

Me: Ar, awight, he does go on a bit. But it's only cause he's chuffed yer there.

Her: Chuffed enough to keep us on the doorstep three quarters of an hour. Coats on, keys in me hand, and yer mom's still going "and tell yer sister," and yer dad's still "keep out the oss road now, mind that lorry," and not one of 'em moving to shut the door.

Me: ...Ar, that bit wo great, I'll give yer that. I thought we'd be there till it went dark.

Her: See, yo do know what I'm on about.

Me: I know, bab. It's bostin seeing 'em, honest it is, but I'm proper yampy by the end of it an' all. All that "tara-a-bit, tara-a-bit" and nobody going anywhere.

Her: And we still gotta go round by the island cause of the roadworks on the cut.

Me: Going all round the Wrekin to get five mile home. Fittin, that.

Her: Don't set us off blartin laughing, I've had a day of it.

Me: Tara to yer mom's cooking, anyroad. My belly's still recovering.

Her: Now yo'm just being saft.
