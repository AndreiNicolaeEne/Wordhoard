The mother: Right, get yowselves in ere now, all three of yer. Look at the state of that. Me nan's vase, in bits all over the carpet. Who's done this then?

The eldest: Wasn't me. I ay even bin in ere since dinner.

The mother: Yo sure about that, bab?

The eldest: I'm sure. I day come near it, honest.

The middle one: It must've just fell, mom. There was ever such a draught coming through when the back door was open, and the cat was up on the sill an all, chasing summat.

The mother: A draught blew it clean off the sideboard onto the middle of the carpet, did it? It wo even near the winder, love. That vase sits by the telly.

The middle one: Well... the cat might've got up there then, mightn't it.

The eldest: First yo says it blew off, now yo's saying the cat done it. Make yer mind up, yo'm going all round the Wrekin instead of just saying what happened.

The middle one: Shurrup, you. Wasn't me anyroad. It was probably our kid, avin one of er gambols round the front room again like her always does.

The youngest: I never! I was out the back the whole time, avin a gambol on the lawn, ask anybody yo like.

The middle one: Yo was in ere first though, I saw yer stood right by it when I come in.

The youngest: I never touched it, I day, I was outside, I was...

The mother: Stop yer blartin, bab, it ay gonna help yer now. One of yer's proper yampy if yer think I'm gonna believe any of that, and yo cor all just stand there saying nothing and expect me to guess. Nobody's owning up, so nobody's having the telly tonight, none of yer, till one of yer tells me the truth. Now get yer donnies washed, tea's nearly ready.
