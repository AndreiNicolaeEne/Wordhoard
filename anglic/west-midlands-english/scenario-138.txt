Me: So I'm saying to her at work, I said her old man from down our entry, he's been nicked, aye, proper nicked, took off in the van and everything, cos of that bust up outside the...

The mom: Now hang on a minute, that ay what happened. That is not right at all, bab, not even close.

Me: Ay it?

The mom: It wo him what got took anywhere. He day even go down the pub Friday, he was in with his tea same as always. It was her sister's lad, the young one, and he day get nicked either, he went over on the cut path in the dark and cracked his head, so they took him up the General to get checked over. That's it. No police, no van, nothing.

Me: Blimey. So who's saying he's been arrested then?

The mom: Nobody with any sense, that's who. By the time it got to yow it'd gone all round the Wrekin, from a lad falling over to a full-on arrest. That's some journey for a story to make between the entry and here.

Me: I day mean owt by it, I was only saying what I'd heard.

The mom: I know yo day, love, but yo want to be careful, saying things like that about folk when yo don't know the half of it.

Me: Where'd yo even hear it wor an arrest, if her sister's lad only fell?

The mom: I never said it wor an arrest, yow said that. I only heard about the fall off her at the outdoor Saturday dinnertime, plain as owt, nobody blartin, nobody carrying on.

Me: Ar, that's where I got it an' all, off her, or off my workmate who'd had it off her at the counter. One of 'em's gone yampy with it, stuck the police in for good measure.

The mom: Well, whoever's saft enough to keep telling it that way wants stopping before it gets round the whole street. He fell, he's on the mend, and that's the whole of it, nothing else to add. Yo tell it that way from now on, whoever asks yer, awright bab?

Me: Awright. Tara-a-bit.
