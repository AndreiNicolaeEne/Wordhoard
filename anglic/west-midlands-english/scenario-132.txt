Eleven days it went on for, no let up at all, and I day reckon from the first morning it'd bite as hard as it did, windows iced up on the inside before we'd even had our breakfast. Kept the fire going day and night the whole stretch, coal in be four of a morning, last log chucked on gone midnight, and still yo cor keep yer hands warm in that kitchen without gloves on.

Then the tap out the back went. Never froze in all the years we'd had that house, not even when our kid was a babby, but this time it just stopped dead. Carried buckets in off next door's tap till the mom got the immersion sorted and the pipes lagged proper.

The eldest sat by the fire the whole eleven days, two cardigans on, saying her'd seen worse but her wor sure when. We'm frozen solid in here, her kept on, it wo let up, yow mark me. Ninth night in, her went quiet a minute then come out with it.

The eldest: This ay nothing like the one back in the seventies, our kid. That one held longer, but this one's got more bite in it.

Me: Yow reckon it's worse then, this one?

The eldest: Bostin question that, bab. Aye. This wins it.

That ninth night was the coldest of the lot, proper Baltic, the cut froze thick enough somebody yampy enough to walk it, which somebody wor, and it held. Lost the old hen out the back that night, first we'd ever lost to weather in all them years keeping her.

Traffic sat stuck at the islands, folk going all round the Wrekin sooner than chance the main road iced. Kids still out for a gambol on the lawn of a morning, donnies washed before tea, blartin something rotten for how cold their fingers'd gone. Only bostin thing all week was the tea, thank God for that.

Eleven days it held and then it broke, sudden as owt, dripping off every roof down the entry. Stood in the yard just listening to it. Keep out the oss road to that cold, we said, tara-a-bit, and good riddance.
