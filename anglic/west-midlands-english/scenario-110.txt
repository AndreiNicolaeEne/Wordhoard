Mom: Well go on then, sit down, tell us. What's it actually like, London.

Me: It's big, mom. Bigger than yo'd think even after I've told yer it's big.

Our kid: Bigger than Brum though? Cor believe that meself.

Me: Way bigger. Yo could put about six Brums in it and still have room left over. Honest to God it ay like here where yo can nip from one side to the other in twenty minutes if the lights are with yer. Some days it takes the best part of an hour just to get across, and that's not even leaving the borough, that's just gerrin from one end of it to the other on the tube.

The auntie: An hour? To get anywhere? That'd send me yampy that would, stuck under the ground for an hour.

Me: Yer get used to it. But I'll tell yer what does me head in still, it's the noise. It never stops, bab. Here of a night it goes quiet, dun it, yo get the odd car and that's yer lot. There it's like summat's always going on somewhere, sirens and that, people everywhere even at two in the morning. Took me ages to sleep proper when I first went.

Mom: Sounds bostin, that does, not.

Me: It's not all bad, mom, honest.

Our kid: Wheer do yer actually live then? Cause our lot keep saying yo've got some massive place and I keep telling 'em it ay like that at all.

Me: Our kid's right, it ay like that at all. It's one room really, bit of a kitchen bodged on the side of it, and that's yer lot. Smaller than my old room here even, and I'm paying more for it in a month than I used to pay in three back home. Rent up theer'll make yer eyes water. A round of drinks that'd cost yer a fiver here is more like nine or ten quid theer, and don't get me started on what they charge for a cob and a bit of cheese in one of them coffee shops.

The auntie: Daylight robbery that is.

Me: It is a bit.

Mom: And are the people alright? Friendly and that?

Me: That's not a straight yes or no one, mom, if I'm honest. Some of 'em are lovely, some of me workmates I'd say are as good as anyone round here. But the general run of it, people keep theirselves to theirselves more. Yo can stand next to somebody on the tube every day for a year and never say two words to 'em. Nobody says ta-ra to the bus driver like they do here. It ay unfriendly exactly, it's just, I dunno, everybody's in a rush and keeping their head down. Took me a while not to take it personal.

Our kid: That'd get on my wick that would.

Me: Yer just have to find yer own little pocket of it, and once yo do, it's alright.

The auntie: And what about getting stuff, shopping and that, is there a market or summat?

Me: There's markets, yeah, few of 'em, not like ours mind, nothing like going down ours of a Saturday. There's one near me does all sorts, fruit and veg dead cheap if yer go near closing, bit of everything else besides. No outdoor like we've got round the corner though, none of that, if yer want a bottle of summat of an evening it's a proper shop and a queue half way down the street.

Our kid: What about work then, how'd yer get sorted with that?

Me: It's mostly online, applying and that, sending yer stuff off into the void and waiting to hear summat back, which half the time yer dow. Once yer in somewhere though it's not that different to here really, same sort of graft, just further to get to it of a morning and a lot more of it about if yer looking.

Mom: And did yer get lost much when yer first went? I bet yer did.

Me: Lost? Mom, I was lost about three or four times in the first fortnight alone. Proper lost an' all, not just a bit turned round, I mean standing on a corner not having a clue which way was which, phone dead, feeling a right wammel about it. Took me ages to get the layout of the place in me head, it ay like here wheer yer know one entry from the next and which cut runs where without even thinking. Over theer everything looks the same to yer at first, same shops, same sort of streets, and half the time the quickest way from A to B looks like it's sending yer all round the Wrekin when it's actually the straight road.

Our kid: Sounds like a nightmare.

Me: It wo much fun at the time, I'll say that.

The auntie: What about the food then, is it any good?

Me: Better in some ways, worse in others, that's the honest answer. Yer can get anything yer want, any time, any sort of food from anywhere in the world practically on yer doorstep, and some of it's smashing, proper lovely stuff I'd never even heard of before I went. But for yer ordinary stuff, yer chippy, yer Sunday dinner sort of thing, it ay a patch on here. I've not had a bap round there worth the name. And donnies washed before tea don't mean the same thing when half the time yer eating off yer knee out of a box.

Mom: So is it better than here then, or worse?

Me: Ah, mom, it ay that simple.

Mom: Go on, yer can say.

Me: Honest, it's different, that's all I'll say on it. Some days I think it's the best thing I ever did, and other days I'm sat theer thinking what am I even doing this for. Depends which day yer ask me.

The auntie: Yer being saft now, dodging the question.

Me: I ay dodging nothing, I'm telling yer the truth, it changes.

Our kid: So will yer ever come back then? For good I mean, not just visiting.

Me: I dunno, our kid, honest to God I dunno. Some days I think aye, definitely, I'll be back living round the corner having a gambol on the lawn with our kid's little 'uns before yer know it. Other days I think I've got too settled now to ever properly come back. I ay saying never. I ay saying yeah neither. We'll see how it goes, wo we.

Mom: Well yo know theer's always a bed here if yer want it.

Me: I know, mom. Cheers for that.

Mom: Right, who wants another cup of tea then?

Our kid: Go on then.

Me: Aye go on, I'd better get off in a bit anyroad, said I'd see me mate before I head back.

Mom: Alright, love. Tara-a-bit then, mind how yer go.
