Me: Go on then, tell us how you do the dumplings for the stew. The proper ones, like Nan's.

The mother: Oh here we go again. Right bab, you want your self-raising flour first, good big handful of it, well I say handful, more like a mugful if I'm honest, her never weighed nothing so I only know it by how it wants to look.

Me: And then?

The mother: Then, oh, I nearly forgot the suet an' all, you have to have your suet in there, else they ay dumplings, they'm just balls of flour and water and no good to anybody. So flour, suet, about half the size of the flour again, pinch of salt, mix it dry first before owt else goes in.

Me: What about the wet bit?

The mother: Just water, bit by bit, till it comes together and it ay sticking to your donnies no more. I couldn't tell you how much though, I've never measured that in me life, you just know when it looks right, when it feels right.

Me: Then what, straight in the pot?

The mother: Roll 'em in your palms, not too big, drop 'em on top the stew while it's bubbling away. And this is the one, yow dow lift that lid. Her'd go mad if you lifted it, said you let the steam out and they'd sink like bricks. Leave it be twenty minutes, don't you dare rush it.

Me: How do you know when they'm done though?

The mother: They come up. Sit there big and fat on the top, all puffed out, that's your answer, not off any clock.

Me: Could you do it with veg suet 'stead of beef?

The mother: Yeah, go on, you can do, it ay quite the same, bit lighter, but it'll do you bab.
