Me: Ey up, bab, what's gooin on wi' yo? Yo look like yo bin cryin since Tuesday.

My mate: I ave. Me and him's finished. Last night he stood there in the kitchen and just says he ay happy, ay bin happy in ages, and that wo it, packed a bag and off down the entry to his mom's.

Me: Ar.

My mate: I asked him straight, was there summat I'd done, and he kept sayin it wo me, it wo nothing like that, but if it wo me then what wo it?

Me: Mm. Go on.

My mate: And her, his mom, her's been on at him for months an all about us not gettin a place sorted, so I keep thinkin, was it cause of that, or cause I'm always tired off work, or cause I never -

Me: Yo stop that right now, this minute. It wo yo. It never wo yo. He's gone cause he day have the bottle to say what he wanted till it wore the both of yer down him lyin about it week after week. That's his doin, bab, not yorn.

My mate: I just keep goin over it though, what I could've done different -

Me: No. Listen here to me. There ay nothing wrong with yo. Yo'm the same soft, bostin wench yo've always bin, and any fella that cor see that's the yampy one, not yo.

My mate: (a small laugh) Yo'm daft, yo.

Me: I am an all, but I ay wrong wi' it. Now come on, yo ay stoppin in that house on yer own blartin all night on yer tod. Get yer coat, we'm goin down the outdoor first for a bottle, and yo'm stoppin at ours the night, I'll get the bed made up.

My mate: I ay got nothing packed, bab -

Me: I ay askin yer twice. We'll go the long way, all round the Wrekin if we ave to, just to get some air in yer afore we sit down.

My mate: (laughs properly now) Go on then. Ta, bab.

Me: That's it. Keep out the oss road, that lot. Come on, tara-a-bit to feelin like this, anyroad.
