Y'all, I still can't hardly talk about that year without gettin' a little worked up. It was clear early on this one was fixin' to whup every year we'd had on record, and it did, come in at damn near double what we usually pull off that ground. The corn was the one that just showed out something fierce, count so far past normal it was cattywampus to even look at the numbers side by side. Best we'd had before that was years back, and this one plumb buried it.

We had to bring in extra hands just to get it all in 'fore it turned on us, mama an' them's whole side of the family out there, kinfolk we hadn't seen since who knows when, everybody mashin' buttons on equipment that ain't run right in years. Once we covered what the house and the stock needed, the rest we put up in the bins, and the surplus past that we traded off directly, whatever the market would bear.

The oldest grower out there, done seen more harvests than the rest of us put together, he set it against his whole memory and didn't hem and haw about it one bit.

The oldest grower: Best I ever seen. Might could go my whole life and not see another one like it.

Me: What you reckon done it?

The oldest grower: Rain come right when it was s'posed to, and a mild stretch clean through the season. God willing and the creek don't rise, might see it again, but I ain't holdin' my breath.

Folks still talk on it now, directly somebody brings up a bad year and here comes "well it ain't like that year," and everybody knows just which one they mean.
