My friend: Girl, don't think I didn't hear all that hollering coming through that wall Tuesday night. I like to dropped my fork. What in the world was going on over there?

Me: Lord have mercy, don't even get me started. But since you asking, I reckon I'll tell it, the whole mess of it, both the fighting and the making up after, 'cause it all worked out fine in the end.

My friend: Well come on then, I got sweet tea and I ain't got nowhere to be.

Me: Okay. So it wasn't even about nothing big to start with, that's the part that gets me. He was fixin' to come home for supper, I had a mess of greens going and some cornbread, and he text me saying he was gonna be a little late, fine, no big deal. Then a little late turned into an hour and a half and he ain't answer his phone one time. I called him three times. Nothing. By the time he walked in that door my greens was cold and I was hot.

My friend: Mm-hmm.

Me: He come in smelling like he'd been at the bar with them boys from the shop, and I swear I didn't even let him get his boots off good before I lit into him.

Me: Where in the world you been? I done called you three times and texted twice, you couldn't mash a button and tell me something?

My friend: Ooh.

Me: And he got that look, you know, and he hollered right back.

Him: I was working, then I stopped for one beer, one, and I don't gotta report my whole comings and goings to you like I'm one of your young'uns.

Me: And that's when I said the thing I ain't mean, the exaggerated part, I said...

Me: You never think about nobody but yourself, not once in your whole life, you don't care two cents about this family and you never have.

My friend: You know that ain't true.

Me: I know it ain't true! He works his tail off, he's out there in that heat all day. But when you're mad you'll say a thing plumb bigger than the truth just to make it hurt, and that one hurt him, I could see it land. So he went and dug up something too, something I don't hardly ever bring up between us and he don't neither, usually we leave it alone.

Him: At least my mama don't call here every other day trying to run our business. Least I don't need mine and them hanging over every decision we make like you do yours.

My friend: He didn't.

Me: He did. And that's an old sore spot, my mama an' them, we had words about that two years ago and it's supposed to be buried. So now we're really into it, both of us hollering, and he's so mad he's showing out, and he goes to the kitchen and slams that cabinet door so hard the glasses in there rattled and one of my good jelly jars fell out and busted right there on the floor. I don't even think he meant to break it, he was just needing somewhere for all that anger to go and the cabinet took it.

My friend: Bless his heart.

Me: Bless his heart is right, 'cause after that neither one of us said one more word. I mean nothing. I went and sat on the porch, he went out to the garage, and that was that. We didn't speak the rest of that night, and come next morning he left for work without saying two words to me and I ain't say nothing to him neither. Better part of a whole day we spent like that, him in the garage when he got home, me in the back room, just quiet as death in that house.

My friend: Lord, that's how it gets though, ain't it, when it's the silent kind.

Me: It surely is. I was sitting back there thinking about it, getting madder than a wet hen all over again just replaying it, and then getting sad, and it was coming up a cloud outside too, thunder rolling, and I thought, well, that fits.

My friend: So who broke first?

Me: He did, bless him. Come knocking soft on the door round about supper time the second night, and he didn't come in hollering or nothing, he just stood there and said...

Him: I fixed you a plate. You don't have to eat it with me if you ain't ready, but I made it.

Me: And I like to cried right there, 'cause that was his way, you know, feeding somebody's his way of saying sorry before he can even get the words out proper. So we sat down at that table together and he finally said it plain.

Him: I'm sorry for slamming that door like that, that wasn't called for, and I'm sorry I let it get so late without calling you, that's on me, you had every right to be put out with me over that.

Me: And I told him mine right back.

Me: I'm sorry I said you don't care about this family, I know good and well that ain't true, and I had no business dragging your mama into it neither, that wasn't fair of me and I know it.

My friend: That's a real sorry, not just a "sorry you're mad" kind.

Me: That's what I'm saying, we both actually said what it was for, not just throwing the word around. And then he looked at me, first time all day with any softness in it, and he said...

Him: Come here, shug.

Me: And that's the word, that's how I knew we was coming back around, 'cause he ain't called me that since we was first courting, that's his old word for when things is mending between us. So I went on over there and he pulled me down in his lap right there at that kitchen table.

My friend: And?

Me: And I told him, come on now, gimme some sugar, and he did, right there, and that was it, that was what sealed it, plain as that. Wasn't nothing fancy. We ain't even finish the plate he fixed, it went cold same as them greens the night before, but we didn't care one bit.

My friend: I'm just glad it worked out. I was laying in there thinking I might need to come knock on y'all's door and ask was everybody alright.

Me: Well now you know, you ain't have to worry, we're right as rain. Though I told him, I said, our friend heard every bit of that hollering through that wall, and he just laughed and said tell her she's welcome to come over next time and referee if she wants to be all up in our business that bad.

My friend: Don't be ugly now.

Me: I'm playing, I'm playing. But that's the joke between us now, whenever one of us starts getting a little loud about something silly he'll say "better hush, the wall's got ears," and we'll both bust out laughing and that's usually the end of it before it even gets going good. Things is better than fine between us now, if anything I reckon we needed to get it all out and see we could come back around from it. Ain't a thing cattywampus about us no more.

My friend: Well good. Now pass me that pitcher, this tea's about gone.

Me: Mash that button on the fan first, it's hot as blazes in here. And hush about the hollering, ma'am, or next time I won't tell you nothing.
