Come here, baby, sit on down next to me. You don't have to say nothing. I know you ain't got the words yet and that's alright, ain't nobody expects you to. I just want to tell you how mine went, first year of it, 'cause somebody oughta tell you the truth of it instead of all that Hallmark card mess.

Them first days, I couldn't tell you one true thing about how I felt, 'cause I didn't feel nothing. Numb plumb through. Mama an' them was in the house before I even got my coat off, and it was one thing after another after that, the funeral home, the preacher, kinfolk pourin' in the front door with casseroles and a mess of greens and I don't know how many gallons of sweet tea, folks I ain't seen since I was a young'un huggin' my neck and askin' was I alright, and me just sayin' yes ma'am, no sir, doin' fine, over and over till them words didn't mean a thing no more. There was so many people comin' and goin' there wasn't hardly a minute to feel it direct. You're too busy answerin' the door and mashin' the button on the coffee pot for the tenth time that day to sit still with it.

And then everybody went home. That's the part don't nobody warn you about. Every last one of 'em hugged me and told me call if I need anything and pulled out the driveway, and I stood in that house and it was quiet like I ain't never heard quiet before. I remember I couldn't even cut the lights on for a good minute, just stood there in the dark of the kitchen. That silence landed on me like something with weight to it. Like it had been waitin' behind the door the whole time for everybody else to clear out so it could get to me proper.

My body did some strange things after that, I'll tell you. Couldn't eat for days at a stretch, food just tasted like nothing, like chewin' on paper. Then I'd get to where I'd eat too much of the wrong thing at two in the morning 'cause I sure wasn't sleepin' whole nights no more. Used to could sleep like the dead, pardon the expression, and after that I'd get an hour here, forty minutes yonder on the couch, wake up not knowin' what time it was or if it was even still the same day.

That whole first month was the strangest thing, 'cause the world just kept on movin' like nothin' happened. Folks back at work talkin' about their weekend, the mail still comin', the trash still needin' took out to the curb, and I'm thinkin', don't y'all know? Don't none of this seem cattywampus to anybody but me? I wasn't near ready for the world to keep goin' at its regular speed. I was fixin' to ask it to slow down and it wouldn't.

And then it was just a plain old Tuesday, nothin' marked on the calendar, nothin' I was bracin' for. I was pushin' a buggy through the Piggly Wiggly, of all places, reachin' for a coke off the shelf, and it hit me so hard I had to leave the buggy right there in the aisle and get to my car. No warnin' at all. That's the one that got me worse than the funeral did, if you can believe that.

Months on, it'd still catch me. Some smell, or somebody's old shirt foldin' out the dryer, or a song comin' on the radio I wasn't even thinkin' about, and I'd liketa fell out right there wherever I was standin'. Never on a schedule. Never when you're braced for it. That's the part that'll make you feel a little crazy, 'cause you can't guard against it, it just comes up a cloud outta nowhere and there you are, soaked through before you knew it was even comin'.

The middle of it was the longest part, and honestly the hardest to explain, 'cause it wasn't sharp like them first days, it was just a fog. Dulled everything down, even the days that shoulda been good ones, birthdays, a Sunday with good weather, didn't matter, everything come through that fog the same gray color.

Somebody said something to me in there that I still think about, and it wasn't even meant ugly, I don't reckon, but it stung worse than anything anybody meant hateful. Told me I oughta be "getting back to myself" by then. Made me madder than a wet hen, if I'm honest, 'cause I wanted to say, back to what self, exactly. But then right alongside that, somebody else, didn't even know me all that well, just showed up on my porch one evening with a plate of supper and didn't ask me one single question, just set it down and gave me a hug and said "gimme some sugar" and went on home. That did more for me than every organized, official kindness put together.

Then the anniversary come up on me. I dreaded that day for weeks beforehand, plumb sick about it, countin' down like it was fixin' to be some kind of test I could fail. And I got through it. Clumsy as anything, cried in the mornin', laughed at something dumb by the afternoon, cried again at night. But I got through it, and I'll tell you, gettin' through was the whole entire point. Wasn't supposed to be pretty.

Appetite came back slow, in fits and starts, same with the sleep, a good night here, three bad ones right after it, no rhyme or reason to it, just slow like molasses in January.

And then one day I caught myself laughin' for real, not the polite kind you do for other folks' sake, but a real one from down deep, and right behind it come the guilt, quick as anything, like I wasn't allowed. Don't you let that guilt win when it comes for you, 'cause it will. Don't be ugly to yourself over feelin' a little bit of joy. You're allowed it.

By the time that year was out I was carryin' it different. Lighter in some spots, I won't lie to you. But it never left, not all the way, and I don't reckon it's meant to.

So here's what I want you to hold onto, and then I'll hush: it does not stay this hard forever. It eases up, directly, in its own time, and ain't nobody can tell you when that'll be, so don't you go measurin' your year against mine or anybody else's. Yours is gonna look like yours. God willing and the creek don't rise, you'll come out the other side of it same as I did. Right now you don't have to do nothing but sit here. I got you.
