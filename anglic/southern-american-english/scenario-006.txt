The older one: Awright, c'mere right quick 'fore the rest of them young'uns get here, I'm fixin' to run it down for you so you ain't standin' out here lost.

The younger one: Okay.

The older one: We need four to start, five is better so it don't drag out waitin' your turn. Boundary run from that big oak yonder clean down to the fence line, and from the porch steps over to where the driveway go cattywampus round the corner. You go past that, don't matter how hard somebody chasin' you, you out.

The younger one: What if I'm runnin' and I step outside without meanin' to?

The older one: Don't matter meanin' to or not, love. You cross that line while you runnin', you froze right where you at, same as if you got tagged proper. Ain't no arguing it, and don't be ugly about it either.

The younger one: How we pick who's it first?

The older one: Odds and evens, whoever mismatch first round is the chaser. Watch, like this, see, two fingers, just a tap, ain't gotta be no shove. (demonstrates) That's all a tag is, touch clothes or skin, don't matter which.

The younger one: And if I get froze?

The older one: You stand plumb still, can't move a muscle, till one a the still-runnin' ones reach over and tap you loose. Then you back in it, quick as anything.

The younger one: How it end?

The older one: Round over when everybody froze or we done run outta light, whichever first. Whoever got caught last, they it next round.

The younger one: What if I mess up?

The older one: First round ain't gon' count against you, I promise. Just watch me, you'll get it directly. We playin' long as it's daylight, God willing and the creek don't rise, less it come up a cloud on us. Reckon you'll do fine.
