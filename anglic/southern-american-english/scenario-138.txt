Her workmate: So I heard the preacher's boy got throwed out the house Sunday night, him and his daddy done got into it so bad the daddy put his clothes out on the porch and changed the locks and everything...

The mother: Hold up now. That ain't what happened. Not even close.

Her workmate: Ma'am?

The mother: I was over there myself Sunday evening. Wadn't nobody throwed out of nowhere. His grandmama took a fall Saturday, and he packed his own bag and moved on over yonder to sit with her, cause can't nobody watch her round the clock but him right now. His daddy is the one drove him over there and helped him tote his things in.

Her workmate: So he wadn't put out.

The mother: Not one bit. Him and his daddy is thick as thieves, always has been. Whoever told you that got it plumb backwards. The one part they got right is he ain't sleeping in that house this week, and that's the onliest part.

Her workmate: Well where in the world did that come from. I heard it from the one at the counter down at the Piggly Wiggly, and she said her sister heard it after choir practice.

The mother: Mm hm. And I reckon by the time it come on round from choir practice to the counter to you, somebody done went and added the fighting and the locks and all, to make it a better tale to tell.

Her workmate: Lord, I'm sorry. I didn't mean no harm by it.

The mother: I know you didn't. But that boy's mama don't need folks saying her husband put him out in the street, when the truth is he's over there being good to his own grandmama. So next time somebody asks you, you tell it the way it really was, you hear, whoever asks. Not what got handed to you.

Her workmate: Yes ma'am. I will.
