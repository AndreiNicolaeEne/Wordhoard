Well I reckon it is past time I sat down and wrote you a proper letter instead of them two-line texts I been firing off. It has took me the better part of three months to get to this, and I ain't going to pretend otherwise, so there it is, laid out plain. Every evening I'd tell myself directly, directly, and directly never came. But here I am now with the sweet tea made and the fan going and nothing but time, so I'm fixin' to tell you everything at once.

Start with the house, since I know that's half of what you're wondering about. The porch steps finally got done. Daddy had that wood sitting under the carport near on two years, swearing he was fixin' to get to it, and this spring he finally did, with the neighbor's boy holding the other end of the level and mama an' them standing over both of them hollering advice nobody asked for. They look good, I ain't going to lie. Solid enough you could bring a truck up 'em. Course now the screen door off the back has gone and give up the ghost, hanging cattywampus on the one hinge, and every time it slams the whole frame shakes like it's fixin' to come down with it. Daddy says he'll get to it directly. I told him I done heard that before about them steps and he just looked at me like I ain't have no business remembering things that long.

We got the garden going good this year, planted tomatoes along the back fence and a fig tree out past the clothesline, little bitty thing right now but mama says give it three years and we'll have more figs than we know what to do with, and a mess of greens besides, which I already regret because I am the one that ends up washing 'em.

Now the big news, and I been sitting on this one a while trying to figure how to tell it right. Mother finally moved out the house. I know, I know, you're sitting there right now saying it ain't possible, that woman was going to be carried out of there in a box before she left on her own two feet, and for the longest that's what I believed too. But her knees finally had the final say in it. She was going up and down them stairs a dozen times a day for years and this past winter she just couldn't do it no more, plumb wore out by supper time most days. The preacher's wife is the one found her the little place, closer in, everything on one floor, and I want you to know she fought it right up until she seen the kitchen, and then she got quiet in that way she gets when she's already decided something and don't want to admit it yet.

I went with her the day she signed on it and she wouldn't say much, just stood in the middle of that empty front room a good while. Then she said,
Mother: "Well. Ain't got but the one level to sweep now."
Me: "That's a blessing, ain't it."
Mother: "Don't you smart-mouth me, I raised you better."

And that was the whole conversation, but I could tell she was pleased even if she wasn't fixin' to say it outright.

I ain't going to say who ate the last piece of the buttermilk pie off the counter that night and let everybody go on thinking it was the dog, but I will say the dog does not eat pie and never has, and I have had that particular sin sitting on my conscience for the better part of a year now, so consider this letter my confession, since I sure ain't saying it out loud to nobody's face.

The little joke still holds, by the way. Tell your workmate you know somebody who liketa drowned in a grocery buggy at the Piggly Wiggly, and see if they don't look at you funny. Some things don't need explaining twice, do they.

Neighbors have shuffled some since you been gone. The family that was across the street packed up and went up to Nashville chasing a job, and a young couple moved in behind 'em with two little young'uns already and a third one on the way, so that end of the street has got loud again in the good way, bicycles and hollering and somebody's ball ending up in our yard about every other day. The one next door, her daughter had her baby back in the spring, a big healthy one by all accounts, and now that whole family has doubled its kinfolk practically overnight. Makes the porch sitting a whole lot more interesting of an evening, I'll tell you that.

Two people in particular keep asking after you near every time I see 'em. Your old workmate from the shop stops me at the counter about once a month wanting to know are you doing all right up there, and I never rightly know what to tell her besides what you tell me, which ain't much, so gimme something to work with next time. And the one at the church, the older lady who always sat behind us, she asks after you like clockwork, calls you by that old nickname of yours and wants to know if you're eating right, bless her heart, she means it kindly even if it does make me laugh every time.

Last season was something else. Summer come in madder than a wet hen and stayed that way clean through, hot as anything I can remember, the kind of heat that has you cutting the lights on soon as you're in a room just so it don't feel so much like an oven. Then along about the tail end of it we had two evenings running where it come up a cloud something fierce, sky going that green-black color, and everybody out here was watching the news and half expecting to be sent to the hall closet, but the creek didn't rise and we come through it fine, God willing. Garden took a beating from the hail one of them nights though, lost about half the tomatoes before they ever come ripe.

So now you tell me some things, since I have gone on and on about my end of it. What's it like there day to day, the actual living of it, not just the headline version you send? Have you found you a place yet that feels like it's really yours, somewhere you could point to and say that's mine, or does it still feel borrowed? And I want to know if you've made you any real friends up there yet, the kind you could call at midnight, or if it's still all just folks you're being polite to. Don't you dare tell me you're too busy to answer all three, I know how you are, you'll pick the easy one and skip the rest, and I will notice.

I ain't got much more room on this page though I could go on all night if you let me. Your mother says to tell you she loves you and to quit being ugly about calling more. I say the same, minus the ugly part, since that's more her department than mine.

I'm fixin' to get that recipe in the mail to you finally, the one you been asking after since before you even left, I know, I know, I've said it a dozen times and never done it, but I mean it this time, directly, I promise. Gimme some sugar next time you're anywhere near a phone and call your people. We miss you plumb to pieces here.
