Me: Lord have mercy, is that you? I liketa didn't recognize you standing there behind that buggy.

The old classmate: I know it, I know it, it has been a coon's age. Look at you though, you ain't changed a bit.

Me: Bless your heart for saying so, I have changed plenty, I just carry it well.

The old classmate: Ha, you always did have a mouth on you. What you doing over this way, y'all still living out east?

Me: Naw, we moved on off from over there a good while back. I'm just fixin' to get a few things for supper, kinfolk's coming over directly and I ain't got a thing in the house.

The old classmate: Well ain't that nice. I ought not to keep you standing here then, but Lord it is good to see a face I know. Feels like it has been a hundred years since we were sitting up in that school together.

Me: Don't even get me started. Where else in this life you got to sit still six hours a day with somebody hollering over you.

The old classmate: You talking about homeroom, or you talking about her.

Me: Oh I'm talking about her. I ain't thought about that woman in years and you just said "hollering" and put her right back in my head, clear as day.

The old classmate: Lord. Every single child in that whole school was scared of her, first period to last. You remember the rule she had?

Me: The talking rule. If she was in the middle of a sentence and you so much as whispered to your neighbor, that was it, you were done for.

The old classmate: That's the one. Didn't matter if you were asking to borrow a pencil, didn't matter if the building was on fire, she caught your mouth moving while hers was moving, you were in it now. First thing out your mouth better be "yes ma'am" or you were really in for it.

Me: And you know what "in it" meant. The line, or worse, that whole sheet of paper.

The old classmate: The line. Lord have mercy, the line. "I will not talk while the teacher is talking." A hundred times, every time, didn't matter was it your first offense or your fifth.

Me: I done wrote that sentence so many times I could about write it in my sleep this very minute. My hand would just about seize up on me.

The old classmate: Mine too, and she wasn't taking no shortcuts either. You couldn't write it once and draw a ditto mark down the page, she'd check every last one.

Me: I remember her standing up there saying it, plain as if it was yesterday. "Ladies and gentlemen, I only have one mouth and I intend to use it. When it is moving, yours had better not be." Said it the same way every single time, like her own little sermon.

The old classmate: That is word for word. That is exactly how she said it. I have not heard that in twenty-some years and you just handed it right back to me like she was standing here in this parking lot.

Me: You remember when she got aholt of that boy that used to sit behind me, for passing a note? We all just sat there and watched it happen.

The old classmate: I do remember that. She didn't even raise her voice, that was the scary part of it, she just went real quiet and made him stand up and read the note out loud to the whole class. Poor thing was red as a beet, you'd have thought she'd have got madder than a wet hen, but naw, quiet was worse.

Me: And after that wasn't nobody passing notes in that room for a solid month.

The old classmate: Naw sir. I'll tell you what though, to this day I remember what it felt like getting called on and not knowing the answer. My whole body would just freeze up, like I forgot how to talk English at all.

Me: Girl, same. Heart just about jump clean out my chest. And she'd stand there and wait on you, wouldn't help you out none, wouldn't move on to the next person, just wait, and that waiting was worse than anything she could have said.

The old classmate: You remember the one time somebody talked back to her though?

Me: Lord, yes. I think on that to this very day and I still can't hardly believe it happened right in front of us.

The old classmate: Me either. Tell me you remember it the same way I do.

Me: I remember she was fussing at that boy in the back over something, and he just looked at her, cool as you please, and said something right back to her, and she stood there a second like she couldn't believe her own ears.

The old classmate: And didn't one thing happen to him. Not one lick of trouble. He was just showing out, but to this day I don't know if she respected it or if she plain didn't know what to do with it.

Me: I reckon we'll never know. But every child in that school had heard about it by the time the last bell rang. He was a legend around there for a good while.

The old classmate: You remember that thing she used to do with her ring though, tapping it on the desk whenever she was thinking?

Me: Lord, yes, tap tap tap, and we all used to do it behind her back, tapping our own rings or our pencils, making a mockery of her.

The old classmate: We were ugly about it, if she'd have ever caught us at it we'd have been writing that sentence a solid week.

Me: But you know, she wasn't all bad. You remember when that one girl's grandmother passed, right there in the middle of the year?

The old classmate: I do remember. I remember her keeping that girl in at lunch, not to fuss at her or nothing, just sitting there with her the whole time.

Me: That is the one time I ever saw her soft like that. Just a whole different person for that little while.

The old classmate: You ever hear what become of her? I heard she retired a long time back.

Me: That's what I heard too, but somebody told me she moved off yonder with her kin somewhere, and somebody else swore she's still around here close by. I don't know that anybody rightly knows for sure.

The old classmate: I reckon she could be anywhere by now. God willing she's doing alright wherever she's at.

Me: I hope so, I truly do. Looking back on it though, I don't know that it was ever really fear, if that makes sense. Feels more like respect now, looking back.

The old classmate: It does make sense. I was plumb terrified of that woman two straight years, and now I look back and think she might have been the best teacher I ever had in my life.

Me: (laughing) Same here. Lord, we would not have told you that back then though.

The old classmate: (laughing) No we sure would not have. I'd have told you she was the devil herself, and meant it.

Me: I'll tell you the truth, even now, if I pictured her walking up behind me right this minute, I'd about freeze up same as I did back then.

The old classmate: (laughing) Me too, and we grown women with our own young'uns.

Me: Grown women scared of a woman who used to could shut a whole room up without hardly raising her voice, and who's probably pushing eighty by now, if she's even still with us.

The old classmate: Ain't that something. Well listen, I might should let you get on to your supper, but how's the rest of your kin doing? Your young'uns must be about grown by now.

Me: They are, my oldest is fixing to graduate, if you can believe that. Time just got away from me.

The old classmate: It surely does. Mine are the same, one's off working already, one's still under my roof pitching a fit over every little thing, driving me plumb crazy.

Me: Lord, I remember that age. Don't be ugly to your mama, tell them I said so.

The old classmate: (laughing) I'll tell them. And how's your husband, he still over at that same job?

Me: He is, working hard as ever, might could retire in a few years if the good Lord's willing. How's your mama doing, she still out that way?

The old classmate: She's doing alright for her age. Mama an' them had a big to-do last month, whole house was cattywampus for two days straight, kinfolk in and out the door, all y'all know how that goes.

Me: I bet that was something.

The old classmate: It was a mess, a good mess though. Had a mess of greens and near about everything else you could think to name, sweet tea by the gallon, and the young'uns wouldn't touch the tea, all of them just wanted a coke, didn't matter to them what kind, just so it was cold.

Me: Now that sounds like my kind of mess. Well listen, we ought to swap numbers before we go on, I don't want to lose you again for another twenty years.

The old classmate: You're right about that. Here, mash that button right there, and I'll put mine in yours too.

Me: There you go. Look at that, it's coming up a cloud out yonder, I best get on before it opens up on me and I got to cut my lights on halfway to the house.

The old classmate: Go on then, get on home before it starts. It was so good seeing you, come here and gimme some sugar before you go.

Me: You too, love. Call me now, don't be a stranger.

The old classmate: I won't, I promise you that. We'll get together proper, God willing and the creek don't rise.

Me: Amen to that. Bye now, you be sweet.

The old classmate: Bye now.
