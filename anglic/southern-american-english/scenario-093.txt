Y'all come on and sit down, I'm fixin' to tell it to you just the once, plain as I can, the way I'd want somebody to tell it to me.

It was just me and Daddy in there at the end, monitor beepin' soft, and the first thing out his mouth wasn't nothin' about dyin' at all. He looked over at the window and said, "It's comin' up a cloud out yonder, ain't it." Just like that, like we was sittin' on the porch of a Sunday. I said yessir, looked like it, and he nodded like that settled him some.

Then he got to studyin' the ceiling and said, "Now that back fence, it's leaned cattywampus for two summers runnin' and I never did get it painted. Y'all might could see to it directly, and don't let it go a third." Clear as anything. No mistakin' what he meant by it.

Then he said, "Come on here and gimme some sugar," and when I leaned down he patted my cheek and said, "That's my sugar," same as he'd called me since I was knee-high to a June bug.

He asked me somethin' then I wasn't fixin' to answer, not right there. Asked was I gonna be alright without him. I liketa broke down, but I held it, and told him the truth best I could, said I reckoned I would, though I wasn't rightly sure of it, and yessir, I'd try.

Last whole thing he ever said to me, and I done held it whole ever since, he said, "Tell your mama an' them I love 'em, and mind them young'uns behave, and don't let nobody be ugly about what's whose after I'm gone."

After that it was just quiet. I held his hand right tight till he drifted on off, plumb peaceful, no fussin' to it.

That's all of it, kinfolk. Bless all y'all's hearts for sittin' still and lettin' me get it said. Now that's every bit you get, and it's yours same as it was mine.
