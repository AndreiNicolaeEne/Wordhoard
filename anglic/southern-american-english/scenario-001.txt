It started that evening after supper, just a little ache low down, and I told my husband I reckoned it wasn't nothing, probably just how I was sitting, or a mess of greens not settling right. My mother had already come and got the oldest that morning, taken her on home with her, so it was fixin' to be just us two whenever the time did come. I didn't think it'd be that night.

By about nine it wasn't letting up and it wasn't spacing out neither, it was just coming and coming, and I looked at my husband and said,

Me: I believe this might be it for real this time.

Husband: You sure? You ain't sure it's the same as before?

Me: I'm sure. Go on and cut the lights on in the other room, I'm fixin' to need to walk.

So we walked. Lord, we walked that house half the night, up and down the hall, me leaning on the counter mashing my hand flat on it every time one hit, him rubbing on my back not saying much of nothing, just there. Somewhere past midnight it turned hard, turned frightening, harder than I'd let myself think it could get, and I recall telling him I didn't know if I could do this, and he just kept on saying I could, I could, hold on.

That's when my sister come, must've been one, two in the morning, still in her nightgown near about, and she just took right over, held my hand, told the nurse what I needed before I could even get it out.

Sister: She's about done in, get somebody in here.

By the last hour I wasn't fixin' to do nothing but push, everything else gone plumb out of my mind. And then it went quiet, just for a few seconds, five, six, felt like a year, nothing, nobody breathing right, and then that first cry come up out of her, and inside of a minute they laid her right up on me, still hollering, little and mad as a wet hen about the whole business, and I just held onto her and cried myself, and my sister was crying, and I said, come here, gimme some sugar, look at her, look at her.
