The neighbor: Girl, how'd last night go with that dog?

Me: Lord, don't even ask. She started actin' off her feed round suppertime, wouldn't touch it, just layin' there cattywampus in the corner not movin' right, and I said to myself I ain't leavin' her by herself all night like that, not a chance. So I made me a pallet right there on the kitchen floor and stayed with her from the jump.

For the longest while wasn't nothin' changed. I'd try to get her to take a little water, coaxin' her, holdin' the bowl right up to her mouth, and she'd barely touch it. Round about two, three in the mornin' she took a real bad turn, breathin' all funny, and I'm not gon' lie, I liketa died. I sat there thinkin' had I done wrong keepin' her home 'stead of runnin' her to somebody, and I like to worked myself into a state over it. But I stayed put, kept her wrapped up warm, just talkin' to her low and soft the whole time, the way you'd talk to any young'un that's scared.

Then right along toward first light I caught it, just a little somethin', her breathin' evened out some and her ear give a twitch at a noise the way it always does. I about could've cried right there. By full mornin' she was steadier, not right yet, mind you, but steadier, and it wasn't till I was good and sure of that, that my own tired hit me all at once, like it had been waitin' on permission.

The neighbor: Bless her heart. And bless yours too, settin' up all night like that.

Me: Well, somebody had to. She's kinfolk far as I'm concerned. I reckon she might could use another day of watchin', but I ain't fixin' to leave her neither. God willing, she'll be right as rain directly.
