The eldest: That clock is comin' to me. Mama told me that her own self, plain as day, back before she got so bad.

The younger: She never told me no such thing, and I was over here fixin' her supper near 'bout every night of the week.

The eldest: I ain't lyin' on Mama. Don't be ugly about it.

The younger: I'm not bein' ugly, I'm sayin' what I know. You always did want that clock more'n you wanted her, seem like.

The eldest: That is plumb hateful and you know it.

The younger: Fine. Let's talk the money, then, since we're doin' this. 'Cause I know good and well you done took more'n your half outta that account 'fore it ever got near the lawyer.

The eldest: I ain't took one dime that wasn't mine. I paid for her medicine outta that account, every bit, while you was off yonder in Nashville not answerin' your phone.

The younger: That is a lie.

The eldest: Call me a liar again, see what happens.

The younger: I ain't fixin' to fight you over a clock and three thousand dollars. I just. I wasn't here enough. I know I wasn't. And she's gone now, and me gettin' mad or you gettin' that clock ain't gonna fix that, none of it.

The eldest: I know it. I ain't there enough either, not the way she needed, not really.

The aunt: Y'all hush, both of y'all, hollerin' in this woman's front room 'fore she's even cold in the ground. She'd be madder than a wet hen hearin' this. Y'all are kinfolk. Act like it.

The eldest: ...I'm sorry.

The younger: Me too. Just take it. I don't even want it, comin' to it like this.

The eldest: We'll get to the money directly. Ain't nothin' says it's gotta be tonight.

The younger: Listen to us. Fightin' over a clock and some cash, and Mama not but three days gone.

The aunt: Mm-hm. Y'all come on and eat. There's a mess of greens on the stove goin' cold, and Lord willing and the creek don't rise, we'll get through this without nobody sayin' somethin' they can't take back.
