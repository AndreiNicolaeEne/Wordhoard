The groom: Okay, we fixin' to have to cut about thirty off here, cause the hall flat out told us they cain't seat but a hunnerd and eighty, and we settin' at two-ten.

The bride: Lord. Awright, well, let's start with the easy ones. Your mama an' them's third cousins we ain't laid eyes on since the funeral, and them two boys you work with that you don't hardly talk to no more.

The groom: That'd get us near halfway there, I reckon. Fifteen, sixteen maybe.

The bride: Good. Now off my side, there's...

The groom: You ain't touchin' my aunt. She has to be there, don't matter what. She practically raised me, she ain't goin' on no cuttin' list.

The bride: I ain't said cut her! I'm just sayin' if she stays, somebody off your side's gotta go, cause right now y'all got near double what my side's got, and don't act like that ain't the same fight we had at the shower too.

The groom: Here we go again with that.

The bride: It's true and you know it's true! Every time it's "well she has to be there," and my kinfolk keep gettin' pushed down the list.

The groom: What if we done a little somethin' after, at the house, for whoever don't fit? Just cake and coke and all.

The bride: No. Absolutely not, we ain't doin' a whole second to-do, that's fixin' to make more mess than it fixes, and people'll be madder than a wet hen they wasn't in the first one.

The groom: Awright, awright, don't be ugly about it. Let's just recount.

The bride: One-ninety-two.

The groom: Still twelve over.

The bride: Lord, God willing and the creek don't rise, we'll get there. Fine. I'll drop them two coworkers off mine too.

The groom: That's somethin'. Ain't everything, but it's somethin'.
