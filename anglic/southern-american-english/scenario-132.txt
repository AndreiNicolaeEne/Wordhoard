Lord, I still get a chill just thinking on it. My whole life I ain't seen a cold like the one that set in on us and held eleven days straight, not one lick of a break in it. It come in meaner than anybody looked for too, first morning it dropped, and I said to myself well this'll ease up by Thursday, and Thursday come and it hadn't eased a bit. The sky had been coming up a cloud somethin' fierce the evening before, and even then it caught us plumb off guard.

We kept that stove going round the clock the whole stretch, somebody up every couple hours mashing more wood into it, and it still wadn't hardly enough. I was fixin' to lay in more before the week was even out, but yonder in the shed the wood pile went down faster than we could cut it.

The pipe under the house froze plumb solid, first time in all our years there it ever done that, and we hauled water in from kinfolk down the road for near on a week. Directly the whole yard had gone cattywampus, fence down, everything iced over crooked.

The eldest set it right up against that cold spell from back when she was a girl and said,

The eldest: This one's beat it. My daddy might could handle that one, but this one here, Lord, I ain't never seen the like.

Me: Yes ma'am, I reckon you're right, ain't nothing like it in my time neither.

The worst night of it, the middle Saturday, the wind liketa tore the porch clean off, and that's the night we lost the hog, plumb froze where it stood, and mama took on over it something awful, said she was madder than a wet hen at the weather itself, like that'd do a thing.

Come the eleventh day the sun broke out proper, and the ice let go off the eaves all at once, dripping and dripping, and y'all, we stood out in that yard just soaking it in, huggin' on each other and gimme some sugar all around, glad as young'uns, God willing and the creek don't rise on us again anytime soon.
