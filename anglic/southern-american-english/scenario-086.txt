Girl, I have been meaning to write you since Saturday and I am just now getting to where I can hold a pen without my whole head throbbing behind my eyes, so bless my heart, let me tell you about the mess I made of myself.

It was my sister's birthday, the big one she been talking about since Christmas, and mama an' them decided we wasn't doing no little cake-and-candles deal, no ma'am, we was doing it up right in the backyard with the string lights and two folding tables pushed together yonder by the fence. Mama had been pushing a buggy up and down the Piggly Wiggly two whole days before, getting paper plates and pop and every kind of chip they had, because Lord knows this family cannot celebrate nothing small. I told myself before I even left the house, I'm fixin' to have one plate and one drink and be home by ten because I got church in the morning same as always. The Lord and everybody else knows how that turned out.

My cousin had done made a whole spread, a mess of greens with the ham hock still swimming around in the pot liquor, dressing, mac and cheese, and about four different cakes because nobody could agree on whose recipe was the one, and every auntie in that yard was fixin' a plate for me before I could even set my purse down. I said I'm just having a little bit, and my auntie said mm-hmm, uh-huh, yes ma'am, and put enough on that plate to feed a young'un for a week. Then somebody's husband cut the lights on out back and pulled the tables to the side so people could get to dancing, and my sister grabbed my hand and said y'all better get up here, she wasn't having nobody sit down at her own birthday, and she would've been madder than a wet hen if I hadn't got up. That right there is about the last completely clear thing I remember, her pulling me out into all that noise because it was her song playing.

After that, girl, it is pieces. I remember somebody handing me a coke that wasn't a coke at all, if you know what I mean, and I remember saying I reckon one more won't hurt nothing, and I remember it not being one more, it being about four more, and I remember dancing so long my feet had gone plumb cattywampus in my own shoes. I remember my mama saying it was coming up a cloud out there and we might ought to bring the good chairs in before it rained, and I remember thinking that was about the funniest thing anybody ever said in the history of the world, which tells you right where my judgment had got off to by then. After that it just goes soft at the edges, like a picture somebody smeared before it dried.

I woke up the next morning and, friend, I want you to know suffering. My head was pounding like somebody had it set to a beat, my stomach turned just from the smell of the coffee my brother had going downstairs, and the light coming through that one thin curtain felt like it had personally been sent to finish me off. Every sound in that house, the dog's tags jingling, my niece's cartoons, my brother mashing the button on the remote over and over hunting something to watch, it was all about ten times louder than it had any right to be. I laid there a good while before I could even manage to sit up, and when I did, the whole room went a little sideways on me.

My brother was up before me same as always, moving around normal as you please, frying his eggs like a man who hadn't touched a drop the night before, and soon as he heard me creaking down them stairs he could not leave well enough alone.

My brother: Well look at what the Lord dragged up out that bed. You look like you been rode hard and put away wet.

Me: Don't be ugly this morning, I am begging you.

My brother: I ain't being ugly, I'm being honest. You was out there showing out something terrible last night, might could hear you hollering clean from the kitchen.

Me: Bless my heart is right. Just gimme some grace and a glass of water.

My brother: Gimme some sugar first, then I'll think about the water.

He wasn't trying to be mean about it, that's just how he is, and I let him have his little moment because Lord knows I earned it fair and square. But I laid myself right back down on that couch after and I made myself a promise, plain and quiet, right there in the dim living room with my arm over my eyes: I am never letting a night get away from me like that again. I know good and well I have told myself that exact same thing more than once before, and it did not stick them times either, but I meant it Saturday morning same as I meant it before. God willing and the creek don't rise, this is the last time.

Girl, the smallest things felt like a mountain that whole morning. My niece asked would I walk with her down to the corner store and I liketa cried at the thought of putting shoes on my feet, let alone walking anywhere in that sun. Mama asked would I just carry the trash out to the can and I sat there and looked at that bag like it had personally wronged me. I couldn't manage nothing but a dish at a time, set one down, catch my breath, pick up the next one directly. Come midmorning I got myself up enough to make some plain toast and a little dry cereal, nothing with a lick of grease or sugar on it, because my stomach was letting me know in no uncertain terms it wasn't ready for nothing rich. I sipped on some sweet tea my auntie had left in the icebox, watered down some because even that felt like too much, and I mostly just sat still on that couch with a wet rag on my neck and let the day happen around me without me being much a part of it.

Round about midday, pieces of the night kept flashing back on me, and one of them liketa made me sick all over again for a whole different reason. I remembered getting up in somebody's face at some point, saying something ugly to my sister's friend, something about her dress or her man, I honestly cannot even swear to which, but I remember the look on her face right after, and I remember it wasn't a good one. I owe that woman an apology and I have been putting it off since Sunday, plumb embarrassed about it, turning it over in my head every single time I go to pick up the phone and call her. I reckon I am going to have to just do it, call her up and tell her I was not myself and I am sorry, and hope she is gracious about it, because she has always been a sweet soul and I hate to think I pitched a fit at her on my own sister's birthday of all nights.

By the time evening rolled around I finally started feeling like my own self again, steady enough to eat a real supper, some of that leftover ham and greens mama sent home with me, and steady enough to think a whole thought all the way through without it running off from me halfway. I sat out on the porch and watched it get dark and thought on the whole thing, how good it felt in the middle of it, dancing and hollering and not caring one bit about tomorrow, and how bad it felt the morning after, and how I meant every word of that promise I made myself lying on that couch.

But I am not going to sit here and lie to you, friend, and tell you I believe it is going to hold. My kinfolk throw too good a party, and I love them too much, and there is always going to be another birthday, another baby coming, another one of mama an' them turning some big number, and when that music starts up again I am going to be right back out there saying yes to one more helping same as I always do. I reckon that is just who I am. So I am sorry in advance to whoever it is I end up owing an apology to next time, and I mean that the same as I meant it Saturday morning, even knowing full well what all that is worth.
