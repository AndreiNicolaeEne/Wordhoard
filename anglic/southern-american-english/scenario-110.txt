The screen door was still slapping behind the visitor when the questions started, before the sweet tea was even poured out good.

Mother: Well come on in here and set down, let me look at you. All right, tell it. What's it like up there, for real for real.

The visitor: It's a lot, I'll tell you that. Big. Everything about it's big.

Auntie: Big how. Bigger'n downtown here, or bigger like the whole county.

The visitor: Bigger like three or four Memphises stacked up and then some. You get on the road and you just keep goin and it don't never turn into nothin but more of itself.

The eldest: Lord. How long it take you to get clear across it, then. Like from your place to the other side.

The visitor: Some days? Liketa an hour, easy, and that's if wasn't nobody done had a wreck on the highway. I ain't lyin, I've sat still long enough to eat a whole sandwich and not moved but a mile.

Mother: An hour just to cross it. See now that right there would put me in the bed. I can get from here to mama an' them's house and back with time to spare for the store.

The visitor: I know it. And it's loud the whole way too, horns goin, folks ridin your bumper, all of it. First few months I couldn't hardly sleep for how loud it stayed even at night.

Auntie: Mercy. Bless your heart, that's a lot to get used to.

The eldest: Where you stayin at now, anyway. Is it one of them tall buildings like on the TV.

The visitor: Naw, naw, it ain't nothin like that. It's a little one-bedroom, smaller than my old room back here if you can believe it, and I pay more for it a month than mama pays on this whole house.

Mother: Get on. More than the house note.

The visitor: More than the house note, ma'am, I ain't foolin you. Everything up there cost you double what it costs down here. Gas, groceries, all of it. A buggy full at the store'll run you near double what it does at the Piggly Wiggly.

Auntie: And here I am frettin over a nickel goin up on coke.

The visitor: You'd faint dead away up there then.

The eldest: Well are the people nice at least. Friendly, like.

The visitor: That's the hard one to answer straight. Some of em is, real nice, help you carry somethin or hold a door, no different than here. But then plenty of em walk right past you like you ain't even standin there, won't look at you, won't say good mornin, nothin. Took me a while not to take it personal.

Mother: Don't be ugly, that's just how city folk get raised I reckon, everybody in a hurry.

The visitor: That's about the size of it. Ain't nobody bein ugly on purpose, they just movin too fast to be kind about it, if that makes sense.

Auntie: Where do y'all even buy your groceries and your greens and all, they got a market like ours?

The visitor: They got these big ol markets, bigger'n anything here, but I found me a little farmer's market on the weekends where I can get a mess of greens and some good tomatoes, cheaper than the big store and better tastin too. That's where I do most my shoppin now if I'm honest.

The eldest: And how'd you find you some work up there. That still goin all right?

The visitor: It went all right, took some doin though. Wasn't like here where you might know somebody who knows somebody. I had to just apply and apply, and it was a good little while fore anything come through. Now I'm at a place, decent people, I'm fixin to maybe get moved up here directly if it keeps goin the way it's goin.

Mother: Well good, I'm proud of you for that.

Auntie: Did you get lost much, startin out? I know I liketa died the one time I got turned around downtown here, and I been here my whole life.

The visitor: Lost? I got lost three, four times good just my first month, turned all the way around, endin up somewhere plumb across from where I meant to be. One time I was tryin to get home and wound up forty minutes the wrong direction entirely. It's laid out cattywampus if you don't already know it, nothin runs straight like it does here. But you learn it. Took a couple months and now I could about drive it with my eyes shut.

The eldest: And how's the food up there, since you brought up the market. Better or worse'n here?

The visitor: Both, honestly. They got food from just about anywhere in the world you could think to want, and some of it is real good, stuff I never had before comin here. But ain't nobody up there know how to fix a plate of greens right, and don't even get me started on what they call barbecue. It ain't barbecue. So it's better in some ways and it's worse in others, I ain't gonna lie to you.

Mother: So which is it then. Better or worse than home. Plain out.

The visitor: Mama, that ain't a plain-out kind of question.

Mother: I'm askin it plain out.

The visitor: They're just different is all. Some days I miss it so bad I could cry and other days I don't think about home a whole lot at all, I'm just livin my life. It ain't fair to hold em up side by side like that.

Auntie: Bless her heart, she ain't answerin it, y'all notice that.

The visitor: I heard that, Auntie.

The eldest: Well let me ask you this one then, and you got to answer this one. You ever comin back for good?

The visitor: I might. I might could, one day. Ain't sayin never and ain't sayin soon neither. Right now I got a life built up there, and it took me a good while to build it, but this'll always be home, and home don't let go of you that easy. So I reckon we'll just see.

Mother: Well. That'll have to do for now I suppose.

Auntie: Look at that sky yonder, comin up a cloud. Best get them peas shelled fore supper and fore it starts.

The eldest: Go on and cut them lights on in the kitchen then, it's fixin to get dark in here early with that storm rollin in.

Mother: And you, come gimme some sugar fore you get to talkin my ear off about that traffic again. I'm just glad to have you home a spell.
