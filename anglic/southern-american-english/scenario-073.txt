Me: Alright now, pull that chair on up and get you some sweet tea, 'cause I ain't telling this quick. You fixin' to be part of this whole mess, so you might as well know how it got built in the first place.

It started about ten years back now, over at that rib joint my cousin was working the counter at, the one used to be off Lamar before it went under. My cousin's the one that pulled the rest of us in, one at a time, like she was building something and didn't even know it. She'd just say "come on by after your shift, we fixin' to sit a while," and next thing you know there's five of us crammed in two booths pushed together, and none of us worked there but her. That's just how she does. She could make kinfolk out of a stop sign if it stood still long enough.

Now the one who almost didn't stick around, that was the quiet one, and I mean quiet like a church mouse them first few months. Wouldn't hardly say two words, just sat on the edge of things like she was fixin' to leave any minute. We liketa lost her for good right around Christmas that first year, 'cause her mama took sick sudden and she just went dark on us, didn't answer nobody. My cousin didn't let it lie though. Showed up at that hospital three nights running with a plate of food and didn't say nothing about it being strange, just set it down and sat with her. That's what changed it. The quiet one always says that was the night she quit being a guest and started being family, God's honest truth. And now, bless her heart, she's the one keeps the rest of us from killing each other, but I'll get to that.

Early on we had us a falling out, between the quiet one, well no, between two of the others, over of all things a buggy at the Piggly Wiggly. One of 'em left her cart cattywampus in the lot and it rolled right into the other one's car door, and Lord, you'd have thought somebody'd committed a crime the way it blew up. Didn't speak for near two months. My cousin finally sat 'em both down and made 'em hash it out over a mess of greens she'd cooked special, said she wasn't cutting the lights on in that kitchen till they shook hands. They did, eventually, and made up proper, but to this day if anybody so much as taps a car with a buggy handle, the whole group hollers "here we go again" and that one turns red as a beet. That's just the joke now, older than half the friendships in this group, and it ain't going nowhere.

We did have one leave us, a few years in. Said it was for work, moved off to Nashville for a job that paid better, and I reckon that part's true enough. Didn't none of us push on it too hard. She still texts mama an' them around the holidays, sends her love, but the group itself moved on without her, way these things go sometimes. Wasn't ugly about it, just quiet, the way most real leavings are.

The one who joined up later, that was my cousin's little sister, vouched in proper, my cousin said "she's alright, y'all just gotta let her prove it," and we did make her earn it some, I won't lie. Made her come to three suppers running before anybody'd trust her with the good gossip. But she cooked a pot of greens that made grown men cry a little, and she never once showed out or tried to make herself the center of things, and that's what did it. Took the better part of a year before she was really all the way in, but she is now, sure as anything.

The thing that really bound the rest of us together, though, was that trip down to the coast a few summers on, for a wedding one of the kinfolk was having. Car broke down halfway through Mississippi, middle of nowhere, and the sky started coming up a cloud right as we were standing there with the hood up, no phone signal to speak of. We ended up walking near two miles to a gas station, all of us, in dress clothes, laughing so hard we liketa fell out in the ditch more than once. Made it to that wedding an hour late, soaked through, and it's still the thing everybody brings up first when they're telling this history to somebody new. You had to be there, but you'll hear it plenty, I promise you that.

There's one who's gone from us now too, moved clean out to Texas for her husband's job, and we feel that absence yet. She was the one always had something funny to say right when things got heavy, and suppers just run a little flatter without her in the room. We talk on the phone directly, every few weeks, but it ain't the same as her being across the table.

These days everybody's got their spot, same as always. My cousin still runs the whole show, always has, calling everybody to see who's coming and who's bringing what. The one from the buggy mess, bless that heart, is always, always late, could be given a head start of an hour and still walk in last, and nobody even fusses about it no more, we just plan around it. And the quiet one, well, she's the one keeps the peace now, same as that first Christmas, anybody starts pitching a fit over something small, she's the one gets between 'em, calm as still water, and it works every time.

We did have a real low point, few years back, over money of all things, a trip everybody was supposed to chip in on, and it went sideways, folks feeling used, folks feeling accused, and for about two months this whole thing might could've just come apart at the seams. Madder than a wet hen don't half cover it. What mended it was the quiet one again, calling each one of us separate, not taking sides, just saying "we done built too much to let a little money wreck it." And she was right. We had us a supper, said what needed saying, and moved on. Ain't brought it up since, not to be ugly about it anyway.

One thing's held since that very first booth at that rib joint, and that's Sunday supper, first Sunday of every month, rain or shine or the creek rising or not. Don't matter who's got what going on, you show up if you're able, and if you can't, you call and say so, 'cause that's just the manners of it.

Now you, you already started proving yourself 'fore you even knew you was being tested, showed up at my cousin's without being asked when her mama was poorly, sat right there in that waiting room half the night, and you brought a mess of greens to the last supper that was seasoned near about right, which is saying something for a first try. That's more than plenty. That's kinfolk behavior, and don't nobody in this group do anything halfway, so don't you start now either.

So that's the whole history of it, near as I can tell it right. Ain't nothing left to do now but gimme some sugar and get on in here, 'cause far as I'm concerned, and far as the rest of 'em are concerned too whether they said it yet or not, you're already family. Might could take a minute for it to feel that way to you, but it's already true on our end. Welcome on in, sugar. Mash that button on the porch light before you go, would you, it's fixin' to get dark.
