Me: If I woke up tomorrow with a pile of money fell in my lap, I'm fixin' to buy me one of them big trucks, the kind with the extra cab and all.

My friend: Lord have mercy, you and that truck. Bless your heart, you'd have it sitting cattywampus in the yard showing out for the whole street before the week was up.

Me: I'd pay cash for it, I ain't stupid.

My friend: Mm-hm. Well I reckon I'd put some back first. Can't be spending it all like it's fixin' to melt in your hand.

Me: You always was the sensible one out the two of us.

My friend: Somebody's got to be. Mama an' them raised me right, and so did yours, don't be pitching a fit about it.

Me: Alright, alright, what would you do then, since you so wise.

My friend: I'd fix the roof, pay off what we owe, set some back for the young'uns, and the rest I might could put in one of them accounts that grows on its own.

Me: That's about as much fun as a mess of greens with no ham hock in it.

My friend: Well what you want, a boat? We ain't got nowhere yonder to even put a boat.

Me: I was fixin' to say we ought to buy us an island. Just me and you, no bills, no work, cut the lights on when we want and off when we don't, and let somebody else mash the buttons for a change.

My friend: An island. Lord, you done lost your mind.

Me: I liketa died just thinking about it, I ain't gon' lie.

My friend: Alright now, come back down here with the rest of us. How about this, we fix the roof, put half back, and the other half we go on and get us a real nice supper, sweet tea and all, and call it a day.

Me: Now that I could live with.

My friend: See, that's plumb reasonable, and a lot less cattywampus than an island.

Me: Lord, we sitting here planning out millions and I ain't even got gas money to get to the Piggly Wiggly directly.

My friend: Don't be ugly, now. A body can dream, madder than a wet hen or not.

Me: Yes ma'am, they sure can. Come here, gimme some sugar, we fixin' to figure this money out.
