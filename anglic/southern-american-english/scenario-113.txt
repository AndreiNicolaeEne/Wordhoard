Alright, my friend, let me tell you about Friday night, 'cause I am still plumb put out about it.

Four of us sat down at my kitchen table, me, the brother-in-law, the cousin, and the neighbor from two doors down. Young'uns were already down for the night, mama an' them had gone on home after supper, we'd had us a mess of greens and cornbread and some chicken the cousin brought over, said she'd run to the Piggly Wiggly for it earlier and the buggy had a squeaky wheel the whole way through the store. Once the dishes were done we cleared it all off the table and got the cards out. Quarter a hand, that's all, small enough nobody was fixin' to go hungry over it. Pitcher of sweet tea sittin' right there in the middle the whole night, wasn't a drop of nothing stronger touched, and somebody wanted a coke besides, root beer's what we had in the icebox but it's still a coke far as anybody around here's concerned.

First few hands went along ordinary as you please. Nobody up, nobody down much, just dealing and folding and a little talk about whatever mama an' them's show was coming through the wall. Had to get up and mash the button on the radio down some so we could hear ourselves think, and the cousin got up and cut the lights on proper 'cause it was coming up a cloud outside and got dim in there quick. Thunder grumbling off yonder somewhere. We joked we might get some weather later on and went right back to playing.

Then somewhere around hand five or six, the brother-in-law's luck turned. And I mean turned. He took that hand, then the next one, then the one after that, then a fourth one right on top of it, and by then it wasn't just a run no more, it was starting to look like something else altogether. Now I ain't saying nothing yet, I'm just telling you what it looked like sitting there watching it happen.

The cousin: "You're dealing awful clean tonight."

Said it easy, like it wasn't nothing, half a joke. The brother-in-law just laughed it off.

The brother-in-law: "Cards are cards. Don't be ugly just 'cause you're losing."

And that was that, first time through. Everybody let it go, kept on playing. I reckon nobody wanted to be the one making a thing of it over a quarter a hand.

But it come back around to him dealing again a couple hands later, and this time the neighbor was watching close, real close, and something about how them cards were coming off the bottom of that deck caught her eye. I don't know cards well enough myself to say what exactly she saw, but she'd done seen it plain, and this time she didn't let it go by quiet.

The neighbor: "You are pulling cards off the bottom of that deck. I watched you do it just now. All y'all saw it same as I did, don't tell me you didn't."

Said it right to him, named him outright, no beating around it. Table went dead quiet a second.

The brother-in-law: "No ma'am, I ain't doing no such thing. Never have, never would, and you know good and well I wouldn't."

Flat as a board, no give in it whatsoever. Didn't even blink.

The cousin: "Bless your heart, four winning hands in a row and you want us to believe that's just cards falling right."

Me: "Now hold on, we don't know that for sure."

Well, that's when it went cattywampus, the whole rest of the night. The cousin sided right in with the neighbor, said she'd thought something was off two hands back and just hadn't said it yet. I was somewhere in the middle, 'cause he's kinfolk to me, my own sister's husband, used to could shuffle one-handed when we were kids, always showing out for whoever was watching, and I didn't want to believe it of him, but I couldn't swear to what I'd seen either. We went back over two or three of them hands he'd won, tried to work out could he have dealt himself that many good cards fair and square, and Lord, we argued that up one side and down the other a good fifteen or twenty minutes and didn't settle a blessed thing. Couldn't prove it, couldn't disprove it, just went around and around.

The neighbor was madder than a wet hen about it, I liketa died watching her go at him, and the cousin about pitched a fit too, saying if he wanted to play like that he might could just go on and play by himself. The brother-in-law never did raise his voice, just kept saying he hadn't done nothing, said it plain and said it again, and after a while there wasn't nowhere left for the talking to go.

So we kept on playing. Wasn't much choice, we were already there and the cards were already out, but I'm telling you the ease of it was gone clean out of the room. Everybody dealt real slow and real obvious after that, laying their hands out over the table like we were fixin' to get frisked, watching that deck like a hawk every time it come around to him. Wasn't near as much talk and laughing the rest of the night. Right tense is what it was.

Last hand of the night, the eleventh one all told, the neighbor took it. Three of a kind, beat the cousin's two pair clean, and the brother-in-law didn't have a thing that hand, for once. Little bit of satisfaction in that, I reckon, for the ones still put out with him.

But when we added it all up, eleven hands played, every one of them counted, the brother-in-law had took five of them. The neighbor had three, the cousin had two, and I had myself one sorry little hand out of the whole night, which tells you about what kind of night I was having even before all the mess started. So he ended up two hands ahead of the next closest, which was the neighbor, over everybody else by the time it was through. Might not sound like much on a quarter a hand, but it sat wrong with everybody, considering.

We packed up directly after that, didn't linger like we usually do. The neighbor was still stiff about it walking out, and the cousin gave me a look on the way to the door like she wasn't done talking about it, not by a long shot. The brother-in-law gave me some sugar 'fore he left same as always, said he might should get on home before the rain caught him, acted like the whole thing never even happened.

And that's the thing, my friend, it ain't never settled. We've sat down and played I don't know how many times since, and it comes up every single time, somebody brings it back around, half joking and half not. The neighbor'll say something about watching his hands close this time, the cousin'll bring up how he done won four hands running that one night and ask you how that's supposed to be luck, and he'll say the same thing he always says, that he ain't never cheated a card game in his life and he ain't fixin' to start now. Nobody's ever proved it one way or the other, and I don't reckon anybody ever will at this point. God willing and the creek don't rise, we'll all sit down again this weekend and do it all over, and somebody'll bring it up again before the second hand's even dealt, same as they always do.
