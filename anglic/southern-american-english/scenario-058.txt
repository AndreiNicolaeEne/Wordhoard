Me: Girl, I come by 'cause I'm 'bout to lose my mind over this roof. It's leaking bad right over the corner of the back bedroom, where the closet sits, and every time it comes up a cloud you can hear it dripping 'fore you even see it. Been dripping on and off since spring, easy four months now.

The neighbor: Lord. Four months? What'd he say when you first told him?

Me: First time I told him, he said he was fixin' to get a man out there directly, soon as he could get somebody scheduled. I done told him twice more since then and it's the same tune every time, "I'm on it, I'm on it." Ain't nobody been out there. Ain't no scaffolding, ain't no tarp, ain't nothing.

The neighbor: And what exactly did he promise, the last time y'all talked?

Me: Said he'd have somebody by the end of the month. That was near six weeks ago.

The neighbor: Okay. Well you got a few ways you can go. You can keep on asking and hope he does right, but honey he's already showed you what he's fixin' to do, which is nothing. You could put it in writing, send him a letter he has to sign for, so it's on paper and not just talk. Or you go on and call the city, get somebody out to look at it official-like. That one might rile him up though, and some of these landlords get ugly when you call on 'em.

Me: I don't want him showing out and trying to put me out over it.

The neighbor: I hear you. Reminds me of the place we had off Lamar, landlord swore up and down he'd fix that ceiling, kept saying next week, next week, and me and mine liketa drowned in that front room every time it rained. We wrote him a letter finally, sent it certified, and wouldn't you know it, man was out there in four days.

Me: Bless his heart.

The neighbor: Mm hm. So that's what I'd try first with yours. Write it down, date it, keep you a copy, and see does that light a fire under him before you go calling anybody.

Me: Alright. I reckon that's worth a shot before it gets plumb out of hand.
