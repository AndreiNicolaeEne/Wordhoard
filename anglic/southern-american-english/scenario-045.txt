The customer come up to the counter and liketa dropped the bag right there, seeing it laid out on the hook.

The customer: Well hold on now. This ain't right at all.

The tailor: What ain't right, ma'am? That's your jacket, pressed and ready, same as we agreed on.

The customer: I asked for three buttons down the front and a straight hem across the bottom. What's hanging there's got two buttons on it, and that hem is cut cattywampus, angled clean across like it's fixin' to go somewhere fancy.

The tailor: Now sir, I wrote that ticket up my own self. Two buttons, angled hem, that's exactly what got agreed on that day, plain as anything.

The customer: I done told you twice when I dropped it off. Three buttons, straight hem. I ain't confused about my own coat, and I ain't about to be.

The tailor: Well let me go on and check the ticket then, 'cause I know what I wrote, and I ain't fixin' to stand here arguing over a coat.

The tailor goes on to the back and comes out a minute later with the ticket held up.

The tailor: Alright. I stand corrected on the one. Ticket says three button, right here in my own hand, so that's on me somehow, plumb got cut down to two. But it says angled hem clear as day, so that part we did have right.

The customer: Well bless your heart, at least the hem was mine to misremember. I reckon I might could live with the angle if it lays decent on me, but that button count has got to be fixed. I ain't walking out of here looking like some young'un buttoned his own self.

The tailor: No ma'am, I hear you, and I ain't about to send you off looking a mess. Give me directly two days and I'll have that third button set and pressed up right, no charge on it.

The customer: Alright then. I do appreciate you owning up to it 'stead of pitching a fit about it.

The tailor: Yes ma'am. Two days, God willing and the creek don't rise.
