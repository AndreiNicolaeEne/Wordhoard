Three of 'em was setting out on the porch after supper, sweet tea sweating rings on the rail and a cloud coming up yonder past the trees, and here it come again, like it does ever' single time.

The eldest: Y'all know my knees been actin' up something terrible. Going on fifteen year now, ever since I fell off that ladder cleaning gutters I had no business cleaning at my age. Some mornings I can't hardly get up off the couch without hollerin' first.

The second one: Fifteen year, bless your heart, that ain't nothin'. I got this hip that comes up wrong three, four nights a month, right regular as the mail, and it don't let me sleep a lick. I'm layin' there at two in the mornin' plumb cattywampus, can't get comfortable no way I turn.

The third one: Don't be ugly to one another over your little aches now. I done had this shoulder longer than either one of y'all has known me, and that's sayin' somethin', cause I knowed you two since we was young'uns. Liketa killed me the first time it locked up, and it ain't never once let go good since.

The eldest: Well who you reckon's got it worst, then. Go on and say it, if you're brave enough.

The second one: I ain't sayin' nothin' out my own mouth, but it sure ain't me that's carryin' on the loudest.

The third one: Naw, it's me, plain as day, and I ain't backin' down off it, neither one of y'all's gon' talk me out of it.

The eldest: Might could be all three of us just gettin' old together, is what it might could be. Ain't a one of us fixin' to win this argument.

And they all commenced to laughin' right there together, same as always, cause it wasn't never gonna get settled noway, and directly the second one hollered for somebody to cut the lights on before that cloud let loose on 'em.
