Girl, let me tell you about this Thanksgiving, cause I am still tired from it and it has been near on two weeks. You know it was my turn to host this year, first time ever, and I had been fixin' to do it for about three years runnin' but kept chickenin' out. Well this year mama said her knees wasn't gon' let her stand at that stove all day no more, so it fell to me, and I said fine, I'll do it, and then I laid up that whole night before just starin' at the ceiling like a fool, plumb nervous about it. I kept thinkin' about all the way it could go wrong and none of the ways it might go right.

So I drew up this menu, and I am tellin' you I got above my raisin' with it. I wasn't just doin' a turkey and dressing and callin' it done, no ma'am, I had it in my head I was gon' do a turkey AND a ham, a mess of greens, mac and cheese, dressing, giblet gravy, candied yams, green beans, rolls from scratch, two kinds of pie, a pound cake, and deviled eggs, like I was cookin' for the whole church and not just my kinfolk. My sister looked at that list and said,

The eldest: You lost your mind. Who all is comin' that you need two meats and two pies?

Me: I don't know yet, that's the problem.

Cause that's the other thing, the guest count kept creepin' up on me. First it was just gon' be mama an' them, my sister and her husband, and my two young'uns. Then my auntie called sayin' she heard I was hostin' and she wasn't about to miss it, and she was bringin' her son. Then my husband's people found out and said well if her side's comin' our side oughta come too, and by the time Wednesday rolled around I had gone from cookin' for seven to cookin' for like nineteen people, and half of 'em I hadn't even accounted for in the buggy at the store.

Speakin' of which, that shoppin' trip. Lord. I'm out at the Piggly Wiggly the Tuesday before with a buggy so full I couldn't hardly steer it, and every aisle is a madhouse, everybody and their mama out there fixin' to do the exact same thing I'm doin'. And I could not for the life of me find a decent size roastin' pan big enough for that turkey. I went to three different stores. The one at the first store, somebody done already got the last one, and the girl at the counter just looked at me and said,

The one at the counter: We been sold out of them since Sunday, ma'am, everybody's askin'.

I like to had a fit right there in the aisle. Ended up havin' to borrow one off my neighbor, thank the Lord she had one and wasn't usin' it, or I don't know what I'd have done, maybe cooked that bird in two pans and prayed.

Then that Wednesday night, I did not go to bed hardly at all. I was up past midnight peelin' potatoes and choppin' up greens and makin' my pie dough, cause I know if you don't do that mess ahead of time you're sunk the next mornin'. I had wrote myself out a whole schedule on a piece of paper, turkey in the oven at 6 AM, ham goes in at 9, rolls start risin' at 10, and I taped it right up on the cabinet like I was runnin' a restaurant.

Well. That schedule did not survive first contact with the actual mornin', not even a little bit. I woke up late cause I had only got about four hours of sleep, the turkey went in near an hour behind, and then right when I'm tryin' to catch up, my youngest comes runnin' in hollerin' that somethin's burnin', and I go flyin' in there and my rolls, my first batch, the ones I was so proud of, was black as tar on the bottom. I don't know if the oven runs hot or I just plumb forgot 'em with everything else goin' on, but they was ruint, done for, straight in the trash.

I liketa died. I'm standin' there at 8 in the mornin' with a burnt tray of rolls and a turkey behind schedule and I said out loud to nobody,

Me: Lord, why did I do this to myself.

But you got to fix it and keep movin', so I mixed up a second batch quick as I could, and thank God my husband's cousin showed up early, like a whole hour before she was supposed to, just to help me set up, and I about cried with relief when I seen her at that door. I didn't even let her take her coat off good before I had her in that kitchen washin' greens and settin' the table, and she didn't complain one bit, just rolled up her sleeves and got after it.

By midmornin' that stove was fixin' to give out on me, every eye was goin', the oven had the turkey and the ham fightin' for space and time, and I had greens on the stove and mac and cheese waitin' its turn, and I was doin' math in my head about what could come out when so nothin' sat cold. My cousin looked at all them pots and said,

Her workmate: This kitchen looks cattywampus, how you keepin' track of all this.

Me: Baby I am not, I am just prayin'.

But one thing did surprise me, my candied yams, which I almost didn't even make cause I figured wasn't nobody gon' touch 'em with all that other food around, they came out better than I ever made 'em before, browned up just right on top, and I don't even rightly know what I did different.

So dinner ended up startin' near two hours later than I had planned, we was supposed to eat at one and didn't sit down till near three, but everybody was just glad to finally be eatin' by then, wasn't nobody complainin' about the time once that food hit the table. First bites went 'round and people started makin' all kinds of noise, mm-mm-mm and oh this is good, and my auntie, she don't hold back, she took one bite of them rolls, the second batch, and said,

The eldest's sister: These rolls is a little dense, baby, I think you overworked that dough.

And she wasn't wrong, bless her heart, I had been rushin' and I musta handled it too much. But then everybody went for them yams and there wasn't a spoonful left inside of ten minutes, gone before the pie even got mentioned, and my uncle sat back and said,

The uncle: Now that right there is what I'm talkin' about, who made these.

Me: I did.

The uncle: Well you done outdid yourself on that one.

And that made the whole day worth it right there, I ain't gon' lie. By the time everybody had ate and the young'uns was runnin' around outside and the men was half asleep in front of the TV, I sat down for the first time all day, feet just throbbin', and my sister came and sat next to me and said,

The eldest: So you gon' host again next year?

Me: Girl, ask me that in about a month.

The eldest: Bless your heart, you know you gon' do it again.

And I probably will, cause even with the burnt rolls and the pan I couldn't find and bein' two hours behind all mornin', wasn't a bad day. It was a good day. Just about wore me clean out is all.
