My mama always said when it goes like that you're fixin' to see something, and Lord, she wasn't lying.

I was out back hanging wash and the air just quit. Wasn't a bird making a sound, wasn't a dog barking two streets over, nothing. I hollered for everybody to come help me before I even thought it through good.

Me: Y'all get out here, it's coming up a cloud and I mean right now!

The eldest come tearing out the back door barefoot, didn't even stop for shoes, and we like to run ourselves ragged getting the grill cover on, dragging the porch chairs up under the eave, hauling the young'un's bike into the carport. My husband was up on a stepladder trying to get the flag down off the pole because Lord knows that thing woulda took off like a kite, and I was screaming at him the whole time to get down off there.

Me: Get off that ladder, you are not more important than a dollar-store flag!

The husband: I'm fixin' to, I'm fixin' to, just lemme get this last...

And that's when the first real gust hit and about took the ladder out from under him, and after that we quit trying to save anything else. We just went in the house.

By the time we had the young'uns gathered up in the hallway with the dog and a flashlight and a stack of towels, the wind had gone from loud to something I ain't got a good word for. It wasn't like no wind I ever heard before, it was a roar, like a train coming through, and everybody always says that about a bad wind and I used to think that was just something people said, but I am here to tell you it is exactly what it sounds like. Wasn't but a few minutes in there wasn't nobody talking. You couldn't have talked if you tried, honestly, the wind was too loud, and even if it hadn't been, ain't nobody had the words for what we were hearing.

The lights went out first. Just cut off, no flicker, no warning, just gone, and the youngest one grabbed a hold of my arm so hard I had a bruise there for a week after. Then we heard it. A tree coming down, not far, close enough you could hear the crack of it and then the whole ground seemed to shudder when it hit. My mother-in-law, God rest her, used to say I liketa died anytime something scared her bad, and I finally understood in my bones what she meant by that, because I did. I liketa died right there in that hallway.

The eldest: What was that? What just fell?

Me: I don't know, baby, just stay down, stay down low.

We heard something tearing up on the roof right over us, this awful ripping, scraping sound, and I grabbed the young'uns' heads and pulled 'em into my chest like that was gonna do a bit of good against a house falling in, but a mother does what a mother does. I said a prayer right then I ain't ashamed to tell nobody about, and I ain't a woman that prays out loud regular either.

I will tell you the truth now, sitting here after the fact, because I wasn't about to tell it then and scare everybody worse. I was plumb terrified. Not scared, terrified, the kind where your mouth goes dry and you can't hardly swallow. I didn't let on. You don't let on, not with the young'uns looking right at you to see if it's safe to be scared or not. And then, near as sudden as it started, it just quit. The wind dropped off like somebody turned a knob, and it went so quiet it near about scared me worse than the noise had. That kind of quiet after a thing like that, it don't feel like peace, it feels like the world's holding its breath right along with you.

I looked over at the neighbor's yard and I want to tell you, whatever fear I still had in me kind of eased looking at that, because her whole fence was laid flat, every panel of it, flat as a pancake in the yard, and I thought to myself, well, bless her heart, that is nothing next to what we're looking at over here, and I ain't ashamed to say I felt a little relieved it wasn't the other way around.

The neighbor: Would you look at that mess. Y'all alright over there?

Me: We're alright, we got a tree down and part of the roof, but we're alright, that's what matters.

Took us the better part of a week to get that tree cut up and hauled off so we could even get a car out, and longer than that waiting on somebody to come look at the roof, because Lord knows every soul in three counties needed a roofer that same week. We had a tarp up over that hole for going on two weeks, mama an' them coming by with casseroles and extra hands, kinfolk you ain't heard from in a year showing up with a chainsaw like it wasn't nothing.

And I tell it now and I still can't help myself, I still get loud right at the part where that window cracked, I still say Lord have mercy and my hand goes right up like it did that night, because some things you can tell calm and some things your body just won't let you tell calm, and that storm is one of 'em, and I reckon it always will be.
