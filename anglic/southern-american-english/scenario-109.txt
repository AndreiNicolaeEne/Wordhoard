The screen door was already unlatched, and I only got one knuckle on it before it swung open on its own, like the house had been standing there waiting on me the whole eight years.

The mother: Lord have mercy.

She said it soft, like she wasn't even sure she oughta believe her own eyes, and then it was like the sound caught up to what she was seein' and she like to hollered the porch down.

The mother: LORD HAVE MERCY, IT'S HIM! Y'ALL GET IN HERE, IT'S HIM AT THE DOOR!

She had both hands up over her mouth and her eyes just floodin', and before I could even set my bag down she had ahold of me, arms locked around my neck like she was fixin' to never turn loose again. I mean she held on. She held on past the point where a hug's just a hug and it turns into somethin' else, into somebody makin' sure with their own two arms that you're real and not just a dream she'd had one too many times.

The mother: Let me look at you. Let me LOOK at you.

She pushed back just enough to hold my face in both her hands, and her chin was doin' that little wobble it does.

The mother: You have changed so much I liketa didn't know you standin' there. Look at you. Plumb near didn't recognize my own child.

The eldest: Move over, move over, I ain't waited eight years to stand behind you in line...

And then it was like the whole house came unstuck all at once, everybody pourin' out of the kitchen and the hall talkin' over top of each other so fast I couldn't hardly answer one question 'fore three more landed on it.

The eldest: When'd your flight get in? Did you drive from the airport or did somebody...

The aunt: Are you hungry, you look hungry, I got a mess of greens on the stove right now...

The father: Boy, cut the lights on in here, can't even see him good, it's dark as a pocket in this hallway...

The aunt:, and cornbread, and I can put on some more if...

The eldest:, 'cause I woulda come got you my own self, you know that, all you had to do was call...

The father: Look at the size of him. Lord.

The aunt was tryin' her best to hold it together, dabbin' up under her eyes with the back of her wrist like maybe nobody'd notice if she did it quick enough, and her voice kept catchin' halfway through her own sentences.

The aunt: I'm sorry, I ain't. I told myself I wasn't gon' do this, I told myself...

The mother: Don't you be ugly and tell her not to cry, everybody in here 'bout to cry.

Off toward the kitchen doorway there was a little one hangin' back against the frame with one arm wrapped around it, half hid, just watchin' all the commotion with big eyes. Wasn't more than knee-high last I'd seen anybody that size, and now here was a whole child, shy of the whole fuss, not sure yet what to make of the stranger everybody was carryin' on over.

Me: Well hey there. You 'member me at all?

The youngest: No, ma'am.

The youngest: Sir.

The youngest: I mean, no.

Everybody laughed at that, and it broke somethin' loose in the room, like the air let go a little.

The father: That's your own kin, now, don't be shy, get on over here.

The youngest didn't move much past the doorframe, just a step, still holdin' onto it like it might be needed for gettin' back.

Somewhere in all that hollerin' my nose caught up to what my ears'd been tellin' me the whole time, and I like to stopped in the middle of the hallway.

Me: Is that, y'all got buttermilk biscuits goin'? I can smell it clear from the door. Smells just exactly the same as it always has, I swear this house smells exactly like it did the day I left.

The mother: 'Course it does. Some things don't need to change.

My bag was still sittin' right there where I'd dropped it inside the door, forgot about entirely, everybody steppin' around it like it was furniture, and nobody made a single move toward it, 'cause who's got time to fool with luggage when there's eight years of news to get through in the first five minutes.

The eldest: Now you don't know all what's happened since you been gone. Your cousin got married, back in the spring, real nice little ceremony, out at the...

The aunt:, and mama an' them moved, you know, out of the old place, over to...

The father:, and the young'uns, Lord, they ain't young'uns no more, look at that one right there hidin' behind the door, that one's fixin' to start school here directly...

The eldest:, and your other cousin, the one used to could barely sit still in church, he's done gone and got hisself a whole house now, if you can believe that...

Me: Naw. Naw, I can't hardly believe that one.

The father: Believe it. Boy's growed up on us when we weren't lookin'.

I stood there turnin' in a little circle just takin' it all in, and it hit me how much was the same and how much wasn't, both at once, layered right up on top of each other.

Me: Y'all's porch light's still busted, I see. And that same crack's still runnin' up the wall yonder, right where it always was.

The mother: Your daddy keeps sayin' he's fixin' to get to it.

The father: I might could get to it this weekend.

The mother: You been might-could-ing that crack since before he left the first time.

Me: But this here, this whole front room's done got rearranged, and the fence out back's gone all cattywampus, leanin' near'bout sideways, and is that a new truck sittin' in the drive?

The father: That old truck give out on me plumb dead, wouldn't crank no matter what I done to it, so I had to go get another'n.

The aunt, still sniffin', reached over and just grabbed a hold of both my hands like she needed somethin' to do with hers.

The aunt: Gimme some sugar, come on, I ain't got near enough of that yet.

The father: How long you fixin' to stay this time? Tell me you ain't just passin' through again.

Me: I reckon I can stay a good while, if y'all'll have me.

The mother: Oh, we'll have you, don't you worry about that. I'm already studyin' on what to fix for supper, I'm thinkin' the whole family oughta come, all y'all, every last one, I'll call your aunt on the other side too, we'll make a whole occasion of it.

The eldest: She's been plannin' a spread since the day we got your call sayin' you was comin', don't let her fool you.

The mother: I have not. I'm just sayin' a person don't come home after eight years to a bowl of cereal.

The father glanced out toward the window, where the light had gone that strange greenish color it gets.

The father: Looks like it's comin' up a cloud out there. Might be we get some weather tonight, God willing and the creek don't rise, but I reckon we'll all be inside anyway, packed in here thick as thieves.

The eldest: Speakin' of thick as thieves, you 'member what mama used to call the two of us, when we'd get in some kinda mess together?

Me: Two peas hollerin' in a pod.

The eldest: Two peas hollerin' in a pod!

Nobody else in the room knew a thing about what that meant or where it come from, and didn't nobody ask. It just landed between the two of us like it'd never left, same as it always had, and we both about doubled over laughin' at somethin' that wasn't even that funny to begin with, it was just ours.

The mother: Lord, them two. Showin' out already, five minutes in the door.

For just a second, right there in the middle of all that noise, everybody kind of went quiet at the same time, like it happened by accident. The aunt still holdin' my hand, the father with his arms crossed leaned up against the wall, the mother just standin' there with her hand pressed flat against her chest, and the little one peekin' out from the doorframe a little braver now. Nobody sayin' a word, just lookin', like they were tryin' to fix the sight of me standin' there into memory so it couldn't slip off again.

Then somebody's timer went off in the kitchen, and the aunt jumped like she'd been shot.

The aunt: Oh, my cornbread!

And just like that the room eased on back down into somethin' calmer, everybody driftin' toward the kitchen table, pullin' out chairs, somebody fussin' at somebody else to go on and mash the button on the coffee pot, somebody askin' did I want a coke or sweet tea, like there was ever really a question on that one. My bag sat there by the door a good while longer yet, and wasn't a soul in a hurry to move it.
