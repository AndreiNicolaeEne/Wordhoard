Lord, my friend, I am plumb wore out. Let me tell you how this day went, start to finish, cause my body ain't gonna let me forget it noway.

We was fixin' to start at six but I wasn't even halfway woke when the truck pulled up, and they had us tearing off that old roof before I'd got my gloves broke in good. First thing they put me on was hauling shingles down off the ladder, bundle after bundle, and that set the pace for the whole rest of it, I'm telling you. By eight o'clock my hands had already commenced to complain, blisters coming up under the tape, and my back wasn't far behind it, tight right across the middle like somebody had cinched a strap on me.

We got maybe fifteen minutes round ten to sit in the shade and drink a coke, and that was over before it started good. Wasn't near enough. Then come the early afternoon and that is when it got ugly. Sun straight up, no breeze, and I done told myself twice I might just quit right there and go on home. I was thinking on it hard, my friend, real hard, but you don't leave a crew short like that, so I stayed.

One thing did go right though. The flashing round the chimney, the part I figured would eat up a whole hour, we had that done in twenty minutes flat, quick little win in the middle of all that grinding. Foreman handed me forty for it straight out, cash in hand, and said, "Good work today." I said, "Thank you kindly."

Tomorrow they might could have me back same as today, or they might let me set this body down a day and rest. Either way, I'm fixin' to eat my supper, drink me some sweet tea, and not move another inch tonight.
