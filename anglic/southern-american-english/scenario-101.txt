Wife: Lord, you shoulda seen it, we hadn't but got in the door good and I liketa didn't know her own self, standin' right there by the sweet tea like it wadn't nothin'.

Husband: Which one you talkin' about now?

Wife: The one that used to run around with mama an' them, back when we was all young'uns down at the church. She was plumb near two hundred pound the last time we seen her, and now she's done slimmed all the way down, hair different, everything different. Had to ask her name twice, I ain't lyin' to you.

Husband: Now her brother, he ain't changed one bit. Same ol' mess, showin' out same as he always did, still sayin' yes ma'am, no ma'am to every woman in the room and not meanin' a word of it. You'd a thought no time had passed at all.

Wife: That's the truth of it. And you recollect how quiet she used to be back in school? Wouldn't hardly say two words to nobody. Well now she's the one runnin' the whole reunion, bossin' folks where to set the buggy of cokes at, tellin' the men where to park. Plumb turned clean around from how she was.

Husband: Word got passed her sister wasn't comin', on account she's fixin' to have some kind of surgery next week.

Wife: And did you hear about the one everybody figured wouldn't amount to nothin', droppin' outta school like he did? Come to find out he's some kind of engineer up in Nashville now, doin' real good for hisself. Liketa knocked me clean over when I heard it.

Husband: Bless his heart, good for him.

Wife: What about us though, you reckon we've changed all that much?

Husband: Not a whole lot, I don't guess. Little grayer. Little slower gettin' outta the truck.

Wife: I reckon I'll take that. We oughta do this again 'fore ten more years get by us.

Husband: Might could do it come spring, God willing and the creek don't rise.
