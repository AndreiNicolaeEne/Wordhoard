The wife: I was fixin' to ask do you want me to warm that coffee up, and you ain't even lookin' at me. Where'd you go off to?

The husband: I'm sorry, hon. My head's off yonder someplace, I can't hardly help it.

The wife: What's the matter with you this mornin'?

The husband: I can't shake this dream I had. Been turnin' it over and over since I opened my eyes, and I done let this coffee go half cold just settin' here studyin' on it.

The wife: Well go on and tell it, then, 'fore it eats you up. What kind of dream's got you like this?

The husband: It started off plain as anything. I was just walkin' home from work, same way I always go, right on down past the church and on. Wasn't nothin' strange about it, not at first.

The wife: Alright.

The husband: Only the road kept on goin'. I'd walk and I'd walk and home never come up where it's supposed to be. I passed ever' marker I knew, the four-way, the ditch, all of it, and it just kept stretchin' further out, like somebody kept movin' the end of it on me.

The wife: That'll put a knot right in your stomach.

The husband: When I finally did get there, wore plumb out, the house had a room in it I ain't never seen in my life. Not in this house, not in no house.

The wife: What kind of room?

The husband: I'm gettin' to it, hold on. It was off the back of the kitchen, where there ain't a room at all in the real house, just the wall goes straight on to the yard. But there it was, door standin' open, light on inside.

The wife: And?

The husband: And my daddy was settin' right yonder in it.

The wife: Your daddy.

The husband: Settin' there calm as you please, like he never went nowhere, like ten years didn't even happen to him. Talkin' to me too, easy, like it was any old mornin'.

The wife: Lord. Talkin' about what?

The husband: That's the thing, I can't call it back. I know he said somethin' to me. I know I felt told, like it mattered, like it was somethin' I was s'posed to carry off with me. But the actual words are just gone.

The wife: Well, Lord have mercy.

The husband: I done tried three, four times this mornin' to pull it back up. Layin' there 'fore I even got outta bed, tryin' to catch it 'fore it slipped off all the way. Couldn't do it. It's like tryin' to hold water in your hand.

The wife: You might should keep a pad by the bed, write it down quick as you wake, 'fore it gets away from you like that.

The husband: I used to could remember my dreams clear as day, when I was a young'un. Now they're gone soon as my feet hit the floor.

The wife: I know it, mine too.

The husband: Anyway I give up on the words directly, wadn't no use fightin' it no more. But then the whole dream shifted right on me, and I was huntin' for somethin'. Lookin' everywhere, under things, through drawers that wadn't even drawers I own, and it wadn't turnin' up nowhere it should've been.

The wife: What was you lookin' for?

The husband: I could not tell you. Even inside the dream, huntin' as hard as I was huntin', I didn't rightly know what it was I was after. I just knew I had to find it, and the longer it went on the worse I got about it, throwin' stuff around, gettin' plumb frantic.

The wife: That'll do it too, huntin' for somethin' you can't even put a name to.

The husband: My heart was just a-goin' by the time it broke loose on me. Woke up plumb outta breath, right hand to God, layin' there in the dark and for a good minute I wadn't even sure which one was real. The room with my daddy settin' in it, or this bed right here.

The wife: Bless your heart. No wonder you're off in yonder someplace this mornin'.

The husband: I laid still a good while 'fore I trusted it. 'Fore I said to myself, naw, that's done and over, you're home, this is the real one, the ceilin' fan and all.

The wife: Well what do you reckon it means, him turnin' up like that so calm? Y'all's whole side of the family dreams on the dead, your mama does it too.

The husband: She does. I don't know what it means. Mama always did say when the dead come to you easy like that, not sad, not tryin' to warn you of nothin', it means they're at peace and just checkin' in on you a minute.

The wife: Mama an' them'll tell you anything means somethin'. My aunt always said if you dream on the dead it's fixin' to rain.

The husband: Might could be both, for all I know. He wadn't cattywampus about it, wadn't upset one bit, wadn't actin' ugly. Just settin' there like a Sunday dinner. If it'd been anybody actin' the fool in there I'da come up outta that bed madder than a wet hen, but it wadn't like that at all.

The wife: He always was steady like that, even when everything round him was fallin' apart.

The husband: He sure was. Loved a mess of greens of a Sunday and never said three words while he ate 'em.

The wife: Maybe he just wanted to see you and didn't have but a minute to do it in.

The husband: Maybe so. I liketa cried just now thinkin' on it, and I couldn't even tell you why, seein' as it wadn't a sad dream.

The wife: Don't go gettin' yourself worked up over a cup of coffee. Drink what's left of that 'fore it goes stone cold, you done let it sit there this whole time talkin'.

The husband: Yes ma'am.

The wife: You'll be alright. Ain't nothin' but a dream, even if it don't feel that way settin' here.

The husband: No, it sure don't. Feels more real than most things I done today already.

The wife: Well, God willing and the creek don't rise, you'll get your feet back under you here directly. We'll talk on it more come supper if it's still botherin' you.

The husband: Alright. Come here and gimme some sugar 'fore them young'uns get up hollerin' and this whole house goes cattywampus on us.

The wife: Lord, don't remind me. Here.
