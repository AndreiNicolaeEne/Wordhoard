Okay so lemme tell you about this, cause you know how much I love that fool and you gone need to hear it in order or it ain't gone make no sense.

By the end of the night my friend was gone. Not just tipsy, not just loud, I mean plumb gone, eyes half shut, head lolling like it wasn't even attached right. And the whole night before that my friend kept telling everybody "I'm fine, I'm fine, y'all worry too much," which if you know anything, that right there is the first sign, cause the ones who are fine don't never have to say it four times.

The real tell though, the one that let me know we had done gone too far, was when my friend went to stand up off that barstool to go on and show out on the little dance floor they got and them legs just did not want to hold. Just folded up like wet cardboard. I grabbed a arm quick or my friend woulda gone down right there in front of God and everybody, and when I asked "you good?" what came out my friend's mouth wadn't even words no more, just kindly mushed together sounds, like a tape played slow. That's when I said, out loud, to nobody, "well. here we go."

Getting my friend to actually leave, now that was a whole negotiation. My friend was fixin' to order one more, I promise you, hand already up trying to mash at the bartender to get her attention. "One more, I'm fine, one more and I'm done, I swear." I said no ma'am we are not doing that, we fixin' to go home right now, and my friend pitched a whole little fit about it, said I was being ugly, said didn't nobody ask me to be the mama of the group. I told my friend hush, we going, and after about ten more minutes of that back and forth I finally got a arm up under my friend's arm and we made our way to the door.

The walk home I kept my arm around my friend the whole time, had to, cause without it my friend woulda walked right off the curb into the street. We hadn't gone but two blocks when my friend stopped dead and said "hold up hold up" and then was sick right there in somebody's flower bed, poor azaleas didn't do nothing to deserve that. I just stood there rubbing my friend's back saying it's alright, get it out, we ain't in no hurry. After that we sat down a minute on somebody's front steps just to let my friend catch a breath, and my friend leaned that head right on my shoulder like we was fixin' to watch a sunset instead of sitting on a stranger's porch at one in the morning.

A man walking his dog came by right about then and looked at the two of us a good long minute, my friend all slumped over smelling like a bar rag, me looking wore slap out. He didn't say nothing ugly, just kindly shook his head and said, "Lord have mercy, y'all have a good night now," and kept on walking, dog looking back at us longer than he did.

Somewhere in there, I ain't even gone lie, we took a wrong turn. My friend was mumbling directions, or what my friend thought was directions, and I was half listening half just trying to keep us upright, and we ended up a whole street over from where we needed to be, standing in front of somebody's driveway that was decidedly not my friend's house. Had to double back, my friend fussing the whole time that I don't never listen, which, considering the source, I let slide.

Got my friend to set down on a porch step somewhere along in there so I could try and get some water in. My friend was not having it. "I don't want no water, I want you to gimme some sugar and leave me be," which, bless my friend's heart, was said with about as much conviction as a drunk person can say anything. I kept at it though, told my friend straight, you gone drink this water or we ain't moving another step, and after a minute of pouting my friend finally took the bottle and near bout drunk the whole thing, which I was glad to see, cause a body needs it after all that.

That's when my friend got real sentimental on me, out of nowhere, grabbed my hand and got all teary and said "you know you're the only one who really gets me, I don't know what I'd do without you, I mean that, I really do," voice cracking and everything, the kind of thing don't nobody say sober. I just said I know, I know, come on now, let's get you home, cause you cain't hardly take a drunk person serious when they start in on that, even when it's sweet.

Finally got back to my friend's place and the door, Lord, the door was its own whole ordeal. Keys wouldn't go in right, my friend kept trying to help and just making it worse, hands shaking, laughing about it even though it wasn't funny yet. I finally got it, wrestled that door open, and we more or less fell into the front room together.

Got my friend down onto the couch, laid out flat, took the shoes off, turned my friend on the side just in case. And right before my friend's eyes closed for good that night, my friend grabbed my wrist and said "I ain't never doing this again, I mean it, never again," dead serious sounding, like it was a vow before a preacher. I said okay, okay, sure you ain't, patting that shoulder, not believing one word of it, cause we done heard that promise before and the creek always do rise again eventually.

Next morning I went and checked on my friend, found my friend sitting up on that same couch looking plumb wore out, holding a glass of water like it was the most precious thing in the world. Asked what all my friend remembered and got back mostly a shrug, bits and pieces, the bar, something about flowers, not much past that. So I filled it in, told it slow, the bartender, the porch, the wrong turn, the man with the dog, and by the time I got to the part about the sugar and the promise never again, my friend put that face down in both hands and groaned so loud I thought the neighbors mighta heard it. "I did not say that." I said you sure did, honey, every word, and my friend just laughed, that kind of laugh where you're mortified and grateful all at the same time, and said, "well. bless my heart."
