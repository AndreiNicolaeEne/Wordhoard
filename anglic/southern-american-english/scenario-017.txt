The aunt: Mash them lights on for me, sugar, it's comin' up a cloud out yonder and I ain't fixin' to sit here in the dark.

Me: Yes ma'am.

The aunt: Now set down a minute, I want to talk to you about somebody, and don't be ugly before you even hear me out.

Me: Alright, alright.

The aunt: The choir director's boy. I was talkin' to your mama an' them after service Sunday and everybody sayin' he is somethin' else. He got a good job out at the plant, been there going on six years, and they done already made him a supervisor. He's even-keeled too, don't never raise his voice, don't never go showing out like some of these young'uns runnin' round here pitching a fit over nothing. And his people is good people. Kinfolk clean back to your granddaddy's church, and his mama used to sing right alongside your granny in that choir, God rest her.

Me: I hear you, Auntie.

The aunt: Well what could possibly be wrong with a man like that. Tell me. What.

Me: Ain't nothin' wrong with him, ma'am. I'm just plumb busy right now, between work and everything, I ain't got room for it. Might could think on it later, I just ain't ready this minute.

The aunt: Mm. Busy. Your cousin told me that exact same thing about her husband, said she wasn't ready, ain't got the time, all that mess, and I liketa fell out my chair listenin' to her go on same as you doin' right now. Look at her today. Three young'uns and happier than a hog in slop, and she'll tell you herself, plain as day, she's glad I didn't let that go.

Me: Yes ma'am, I know it, and I ain't sayin' you wrong about him. I just ain't there yet.

The aunt: Mm hmm. Alright then, I ain't gon' fuss on it no more today. But that offer stand, and it's gon' keep standing whenever you get your head right. Now gimme some sugar, I got to get on directly before this creek comes up.
