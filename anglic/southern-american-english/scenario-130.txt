Lord, I liketa died before it was even light good. I got up 'bout dawn to go mash the coffee pot on and there it was, water right up at the door, just ankle deep, not nothin' to worry a body yet. I stood there a minute like a fool, thinkin' well that ain't so bad, and went on and got the young'uns up anyway 'cause somethin' in me said don't wait.

Me: Y'all get up now, water's come up on the porch.

The eldest: It ain't but a little bit though, ain't it?

Me: It's fixin' to be more than a little bit, I reckon. Get them shoes up off that floor.

Checked again 'bout an hour on and it had done crept up past ankle deep, comin' in under the door good now, and that's when we all commenced movin' things, books, the good lamp, whatever wasn't nailed down, up onto the couch and the tables while there was still time to do it decent instead of scramblin'.

By midmorning it was up to the knee, cold and gritty-lookin', and we had ever'thing we owned stacked on whatever stood highest, countertops, the top of the china cabinet, the washer. Kept climbin' the whole while, slow but it never once let up, till it peaked out early afternoon higher than anything we had raised anything to. My mother's chair, the one nobody ever moves, had two foot of water standin' round it.

Then come the waitin', and that was the worst of it, honest, settin' there listenin' to that water push up against the walls, the house makin' sounds a house ain't got no business makin'. Didn't nobody say much. Ain't nothin' to say, really, 'cept please and hold on.

Several hours after it peaked it finally commenced goin' back down, slow as it come up, and we just set there watchin' it inch off the baseboard till we could see wood again.

Went back the next day and there it was, that line, dark and plain all the way round the room, higher than my mother's chair, higher than I ever want to see again.
