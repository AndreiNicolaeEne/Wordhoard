I keep the house money in the coffee can up on top the fridge, low hundreds, just enough to get us to the end of the month for the lights and whatnot. Went up there Thursday fixin' to pay the water bill and it come out lighter than it had any business being. I counted it twice right there at the counter, laid every bill flat so I wouldn't miscount, and it still come up short. Went and checked the tin in my dresser too, just in case I'd went and moved it my own self and forgot, but it wasn't there neither.

So I started in asking, easy at first, going round the house.

The mother: Y'all seen any of that money offa the fridge? Anybody borrow some and just ain't said?

The eldest: Wasn't me, ma'am. I ain't been in that can since Sunday.

The youngest: I ain't touched it. Swear.

The mother: Well somebody done got in it, cause it's near sixty dollars light and it don't just walk off.

Wasn't till I got to studying on it that I recollected only one of 'em had been home by theirself that whole day, the rest of us out at work and school. So I went at that one different, no more easing into it.

The mother: You were the only one here Tuesday. Ain't nobody else coulda got in that can. Where's my money at?

The middle one: I told you I don't know nothin' about it! I didn't take a dime!

The mother: Don't you lie to my face in my own house. I ain't playin' with you.

The middle one: I ain't lyin'! You always thinkin' the worst of me, and I'm sick of it!

The mother: You the only one coulda took it and you know it! Ain't nobody else here!

The middle one: I said I didn't take it and I meant it! Believe what you want then!

Neither one of us give an inch after that, and the money's still gone, plain as day.
