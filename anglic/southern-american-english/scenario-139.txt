Me:

I asked you to sit with me today cause I got something I been carrying too long, and I'm fixin' to say it plain so there ain't no way to mistake it for something else. This is me apologizing to you. For what happened between us. Lord, it's been so many years now the number don't even sound real when I say it out loud. I done tried to start this conversation in my head a hundred times and I always come up short of a place to begin, so I reckon I'll just start where it actually started.

You remember that fight. I know you do, you ain't forgot a thing in your whole life, not since we were young'uns riding in the buggy at the Piggly Wiggly splitting a coke between us so mama wouldn't have to buy two. It started over something so little I can't hardly say what set it off no more. Might could've been money. Might could've been who was gonna look after mama an' them. Might could've been nothing but two grown, tired people standing in the same kitchen at the wrong hour of the night. Whatever it was, it got away from both of us the way these things do when nobody backs off in time. It quit being about the one little thing directly and got to being about everything, every year, every slight, every last thing either one of us had swallowed down and never said out loud. By the end of it we weren't showing out over what started it no more. We were pitching a fit at each other over just being alive in the same family.

And there was a minute in there, I know exactly which one, where my own temper got a hold of me plumb all the way through, and something come out of my mouth I aimed straight at you. I ain't gonna repeat it, you don't need to hear it twice and I don't need to say it twice, but I went after the one thing you'd always been tender about, the thing you never did feel sure of even as a child, and I used it like a weapon cause I knew, I knew, exactly where it would land and how deep. And it worked. I watched it land on you. I watched your face change and I did not take it back. Madder than a wet hen don't half cover what I was that night, and it don't excuse a thing about what I did with it.

That's the night the quiet started. Neither one of us was fixin' to be the one that backed down first, you were too proud for it and Lord knows I was worse, and that quiet just never did break on its own after that. I remember cutting the lights on in that kitchen afterward and standing there by myself a long while, and even then some part of me knew the quiet was already set. Quiet like that don't heal itself. I used to think it might, given enough time. Time ain't done a thing but let it sit, same as a mess of greens left too long on the back of the stove, it just goes cold and nobody wants to be the one that touches the pot first.

So the years went on by. What used to be calling regular got down to occasional, and occasional got down to holidays, and even the holidays got to where we were managing around each other, you'd come by after I'd already left, or I'd time it so we weren't in the same room too long, the both of us being right careful, right polite, ma'am and sir with our own blood like we were strangers at a counter somewhere. Mama always did raise us to say yes ma'am and no sir to everybody, she just never figured we'd need it for each other. Whole seasons went by, all y'all's whole side of the family and mine, without one word passing between us that meant a thing.

There was one time I ain't never told a soul. It was coming up a cloud that evening, sky gone all green and ugly the way it does right before, and I had the phone in my hand. I liketa dialed it. Something about that kind of weather always did make me think of you. And I didn't call. I set it back down. Pride had ahold of me, and some part of me was scared that opening it back up would only make everything more cattywampus than the quiet already had, that maybe finding out you couldn't forgive it would be worse than just not knowing. So I let it be. I let another year stack up on top of the rest.

What's brought me to say it now, finally, I reckon it's just that the distance got to where I couldn't carry it no further. It ain't one single thing that happened. It's that I got older, and you got older, and I looked up one day and seen we'd let more years go by apart than we ever spent together, and I could not stand the thought of that number growing one bit bigger before I opened my mouth.

So here it is, plain, with no dressing on it. I was wrong. Not the fight itself, fights happen, that's just people being people, don't be ugly about it mama would say, but what I said inside it, that part was mine and mine alone. I ain't gonna tell you I was provoked, even though I could probably build a case for it. I ain't gonna tell you I was just mad, even though I was. None of that softens one thing about what I did. I said it to hurt you and I meant for it to hurt you and it did exactly what I meant. That's the whole truth of it and I'm not fixin' to hide behind nothing else, not tonight.

And I understand now, clearer than I ever let myself understand it back then, what that cost you. It wasn't just the two of us and whatever we used to have, it was you, carrying around, all these years, a thing I put in your head about yourself that you didn't put there and never deserved to carry. I took the one thing you were already unsure of and I told you it was true, and I know now you have carried that a long, long time, maybe you're carrying some of it still. That's mine to answer for. Not the fight's, not the years', mine.

Sug. I ain't called you that in longer than I can stand to think on. Feels strange saying it now, coming out of my mouth after all this quiet, feels near about like a stranger's word, but it's the truest one I got for you and I wanted you to hear it said plain, once, before anything else.

I ain't asking you for nothing back. Not forgiveness, not tonight and not ever if it comes to that. I'm not asking you to say one word. I just needed you to have heard the whole of it said plain and complete, nothing held back this time, nothing left for later.

Let that sit a minute. However long it needs to.

I know good and well them years are gone. Ain't no getting em back, whatever happens from here on, and I'm not gonna sit here and act like saying sorry buys em back neither. It don't. What's lost stayed lost.

But I'll tell you what I'm hoping for, and that's all it is, is a hope. I'm hoping we get to be kinfolk again the regular kind, the kind that comes by of a Sunday for supper and don't have to manage a single thing about it, sweet tea already made and setting in the icebox waiting. That's all I'm hoping. What you do with that is yours to decide, in your own time, God willing and the creek don't rise.
