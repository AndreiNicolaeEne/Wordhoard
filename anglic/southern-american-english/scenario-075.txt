I was fixin' to wrestle a buggy loose outside the Piggly Wiggly, one of them cattywampus ones that won't roll straight, when I heard somebody holler clear across the lot like they'd seen a ghost.

The friend: Lord have mercy, is that you?

Me: I know it is! Get on over here, don't be shy about it.

The friend: I have not laid eyes on you in I don't know how long, child.

Me: It has been over ten years, easy. Closer to fifteen if we're bein' honest, and don't you lie to me neither.

The friend: Fifteen. Plumb fifteen years and you ain't changed a bit, ma'am, 'cept you got taller somehow.

Me: I done told everybody that's just the shoes. What you been doin' with yourself all this time?

The friend: Workin' over at the hospital now, on my feet all day. Married, got two young'uns of my own runnin' me ragged. What about you?

Me: Still at the same job, might could get promoted directly if they ever quit draggin' their feet. Mama an' them are good, thank the Lord. Hey, you remember that time you liketa died fallin' off the back porch swing tryin' to show out for them boys?

The friend: Don't you bring that up! I was pitchin' a fit for a week, my mama was madder than a wet hen.

Me: I laughed till I cried. Bless your heart, you never did learn.

The friend: You seen anybody from back then? I ain't heard a word from our old friend in years, don't even know where she's at now.

Me: No ma'am, not a peep. I reckon she moved off somewhere, yonder past the county line maybe.

The friend: Well listen, gimme your number this time. I mean it, I ain't fixin' to lose touch again like last time.

Me: I promise you I'll actually call. We'll get some sweet tea and catch up proper before supper one evenin'.

The friend: You better, or I'm comin' to find you myself. Gimme some sugar 'fore I go.
