The mother set them down on the couch, close in beside her, both of them, before she said one word.

The mother: I need y'all to listen to me a minute. Right here, both of you.

The eldest: What's wrong?

The mother: Your granddaddy passed this morning, baby. He's gone.

The eldest: But like... gone gone? Like he ain't anywhere no more, anywhere at all?

The mother: His body's done, honey. It quit working and it ain't ever gonna work again, not in this life. But what made him him, the part that loved on y'all and told them fool stories and sat out yonder half the night fishing for something that wasn't never gonna bite, I don't believe that just stops. I can't prove it to you. I just believe it, down in my bones.

The youngest: Is he cold?

The mother: No, shug, he can't feel cold nor nothing else no more. That part of him is done too. He ain't hurting. I promise you that.

She pulled them both in then, one under each arm, tight.

The mother: Come here to me, shug. Come on now.

The youngest: Are we gonna see him again?

The mother: Not the way you mean it, not him calling on the phone or showing up at the door. But you'll see him plenty, in how your brother laughs just like him, in how you cain't stand to lose at cards neither, one. He's stitched into y'all. That don't go nowhere, ever.

The eldest: Okay.

The mother: Y'all hear me now, cause I don't want this catching you off guard again. Tomorrow the kinfolk gonna start coming through, and it's gonna be a whole lot of hugging and a mess of crying, and that's alright, that's just love with nowhere to go. Couple days after, we'll go on to the funeral home and say goodbye proper. It's gonna be hard. But ain't neither one of y'all going through it by yourself.

The eldest: Yes ma'am.

The youngest: Yes ma'am.

The mother: Alright then. Gimme some sugar, both of you, and go on back to playing.
