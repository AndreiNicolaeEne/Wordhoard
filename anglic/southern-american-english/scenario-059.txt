Lord, I still think about that night. Storm come up out of nowhere, coming up a cloud so fast the sky went that green color, and the wind got to whipping the trees around something awful. Right after dusk the whole house just went black, quiet-like, no pop or nothing, just gone.

Me: Y'all hold up, I'm fixin' to find something.

The eldest: I done stubbed my toe on the coffee table twice already, somebody move it.

We went digging through every drawer in that kitchen looking for a lighter or matches, and all we come up with was one candle, just a stub of one left over from who knows when, and a flashlight with batteries about dead. Mama an' them kept a whole box of candles somewhere but couldn't nobody remember where for the life of us.

The mother: There ain't but this one candle and it ain't gonna last, so don't nobody go burning it up running around the house.

We tried the flashlight on the fuse box a minute, but wasn't nothing tripped, so we reckoned it was the whole street out and there wasn't a thing to be done about it that night. Give up on it right quick, honestly.

So we just gathered up around that one little candle stub at the kitchen table, all of us close together, passing it around when somebody needed to see something. The young'uns thought it was kind of fun even, giggling in the dark, and somebody knocked into that same table leg a third time and started fussing.

The eldest: I'm fixin' to bust that table up, I swear.

We sat there sharing what sweet tea was left, talking low, telling old stories mama an' them used to tell, till the candle went down to nothing. Lights never did come back on. Wasn't no use sitting up in the pitch dark, so we all just felt our way to bed and figured the light'd be back on by morning, God willing.
