I love you. Ain't no better way to open this, so there it is, first thing, plain as I know how to put it.

I keep going back to one Tuesday evening, wasn't nothing to it at all, you was just at the stove with a mess of greens going, hummin' under your breath to some old church song, not even looking my way, and I liketa dropped clean through the floor standing there in the doorway. It hit me quiet as anything, that this was it, this was serious now, and wasn't no going back from it. Didn't need a big to-do. Just you and them greens and the window all fogged up.

You get madder than a wet hen if anybody so much as breathes on your kitchen while supper's near done, and you drum two fingers on the counter the whole time like you're counting something only you can hear. Ain't a soul else ever took notice of that but me. I don't reckon anybody else ever will, and I love you all the more on account of it.

You done ruined me for anybody else, and I mean that as the finest compliment I got. My days run different with you in em. Even the plain ones, the ones where ain't nothing happens but the porch light and mama an' them calling about nothing much, got a center to em now they didn't used to have.

The days we're apart have gotten harder than I let on when I tell you on the phone I'm doing fine. I ain't. I get to missing you plumb down to the bone, and I still push a buggy round the Piggly Wiggly out of habit like you're fixin' to be there at the end of the aisle, and you ain't, and that gets me every time.

Here's the one thing I ain't never said out loud to a soul, not even you till right now: some nights I lay there right worried I ain't fitted for somebody as good as you, worried one day you'll see clean through me and go on. I don't say it 'cause saying it feels like calling it up. But it's there, and you ought to know it, even if paper's the only place it can come out.

Here's my promise, and I aim to keep it: I ain't going nowhere, come what may cattywampus in this life. When the time's right I mean to marry you and grow old right alongside you on whatever porch we land on, God willing and the creek don't rise.

Gimme some sugar when you're done reading this, even if it's just the page you're kissing, darlin'.
