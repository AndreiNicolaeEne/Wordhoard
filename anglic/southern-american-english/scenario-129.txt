I was out back hanging up wash when I first seen it, right around one, two o'clock, just a thin gray smudge way off yonder past the tree line, and I reckon I stood there a good ten minutes watching, not thinking nothing of it. Looked kindly like the sky was coming up a cloud, only it wasn't fixin' to rain, it was smoke, and it was still a fair piece off. My neighbor hollered over the fence.

Neighbor: You seeing that?

Me: I see it. Ain't close enough to worry over yet, I don't reckon.

Wasn't no time at all before that wind kicked up something cattywampus, blowing every which way, madder than a wet hen, and inside of an hour that smoke had come right on up close, close enough my eyes commenced to sting and water something fierce. I was right scared by then, if I'm honest. That's when I knew watching time was done, and I ain't fixin' to sit here and find out the hard way what comes next. Looking back, I might could have left the minute I smelled it good, but you don't know till you know.

I done grabbed what mattered, the papers, a few pictures, a jug of sweet tea off the counter, the dog pitching a fit the whole time, mashed the door lock and was down the road in under twenty minutes, plumb the fastest I believe I ever moved in my life. My throat had gone raw as a scraped knee, felt like I'd swallowed gravel, and that heat was pressing right up against my skin the whole way to the truck like something alive. I looked back once, just the once, and that light against the sky was something else, something I ain't fixin' to forget.

Stayed with kinfolk two nights, not knowing a thing, missed supper both nights worrying, mama an' them calling every hour wanting word, and their young'uns asking was the house burnt down.

Mama: You reckon it made it, or made it past?

Me: I don't know, ma'am. We'll find out directly. God willing and the creek don't rise.

When they finally let us back, I liketa cried right there in the yard, place hadn't been touched, not a lick of damage. Bless the Lord for that, and bless the ones who fought it back. Gimme some sugar, I told the dog, and went on in to cut the lights on.
