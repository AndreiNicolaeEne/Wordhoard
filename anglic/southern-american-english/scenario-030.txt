The niece: I'm fixin' to get this recipe outta you before you go and take it off to glory with you.

The aunt: Lord, don't talk like that, mash that door shut, you lettin' all the cool out.

The niece: I mean it though. Grandmama's tea cakes. I want it wrote down so all y'all grandbabies got it after we gone too.

The aunt: I ain't never wrote it down, that's the whole trouble of it. Alright, let me think a minute. It starts with your butter. Two sticks, soft, not melted, naw, it's three. I keep sayin' two but it's three, she'd fuss me good for shortin' her recipe like that.

The niece: Okay. Three sticks.

The aunt: Cream that with your sugar till it's light, a cup and a half, maybe closer to two, I go by how it looks more than what it says. Your eggs next, two of 'em, one at a time. And don't you let me forget the vanilla. I like to leave that clean out of it and it just ain't right without it, so put you a good glug of vanilla in there too.

The niece: Butter, sugar, eggs, vanilla.

The aunt: Then your flour, and here's the part she'd get on me about every single time. You do not rush foldin' that flour in, child. She used to say you rush that dough, it'll fight you the whole rest of the way, and she was right, so you go slow, a little at a time, till it comes together soft but it'll hold its shape.

The niece: How you tell it's done bakin', with no timer or nothin'?

The aunt: Ain't no clock to it, baby. You watch till the bottom edges just barely go gold, they'll set up right when you pull 'em. You wait on the top to brown, you done ruined it already.

The niece: And the buttermilk, how much of that?

The aunt: That's the one part I never did measure. I just pour till the dough feels right in my hand, couldn't give you a number if you paid me.

The niece: Could I use regular milk 'stead of buttermilk?

The aunt: You could, I reckon, it'll do you in a pinch. Just won't be near as tender, so if that's all you got, go on and use it.
