The one new to the street: I heard y'all and them next door don't speak. What in the world happened there?

Me: Well, sit on down a minute, I'll tell you my side of it, cause Lord knows there's more than one floatin' around out here and half of 'em ain't worth the breath. I ain't gonna tell you what they'd say. I'm only good for my own.

You gotta understand, it wasn't always like this. Me and that household, we go back further than this street's had that stop sign on the corner. Kinfolk practically. Our young'uns run in and out both them houses like it was one house, didn't matter whose kitchen the sweet tea was in, they'd pour theirself a glass and nobody said a word. Mama an' them used to sit out on the porch of an evening with the mother from over there and just talk till the lightning bugs come out, didn't nobody watch the clock. If it come up a cloud fast and my clothes was still on the line, that woman'd have 'em down and folded before I even got home from work. That's the God's honest truth. Years and years of that. Borrowed sugar, borrowed ladders, watched each other's houses when the other'n went to see kinfolk out of town. I ain't makin' none of that up to make myself look good either, it was just how it was.

Then it started with the shed. He went and built that little tool shed of his right up near the line, close enough there wasn't hardly room to run the mower between it and the fence, and it was leanin' just a hair, sittin' cattywampus if you really looked at it, but I let that go a good while. Didn't say nothin' for near on a year. Then his water started runnin' off that roof straight onto my side ever' time it rained, washin' out a good bit of my flower bed, and I thought, well, I'll just ask him nice, he's fixin' to move it anyhow probably, he just ain't got around to it.

So I asked. Polite as you please. Said, whenever you get a chance, no rush, might could you scoot that shed back a foot or two, or at least get you some gutter on it so it ain't runnin' onto my bed like that. He said sure, sure, he'd get to it directly. Directly come and went about four times that summer. I let it lay. Asked again come fall, same answer, same nothin' happened. I want you to know I asked with a smile both times. Third time I asked, I'll admit it, I had an edge on it. I was done bein' patient about a durn shed.

Well, word got back to me, and this is where it gets ugly, through the one that lives between us, she carries tales both ways whether you want her to or not, bless her heart, and by the time it come to me through her mouth, it wasn't "the neighbor asked about the shed" no more, it was "the neighbor's been over there fussin' and carryin' on and threatenin' to call the city on us." I never once said the word city. I never once raised my voice at that man in my life up to that point. But that's what got told, and that's what he heard, and I reckon that's what he believed, cause when I went over there myself to straighten it out, he stood right there on his own porch and told me to my face he ain't never once ignored a request I made, not one time, that I was imaginin' it, that I was just lookin' for something to be ugly about. Flat denied it. Like them two years of directly never happened at all.

I ain't proud of what I did next, I'll say that plain. I didn't say a word back to him. I just quit lettin' his cat come through my yard on its way wherever it goes, started shooin' it off soon as I seen it, little bitty thing, wasn't even doin' nothin' to me. Petty. I know it was petty. But I was madder than a wet hen and that's the size of what I had in me to give back right then.

And then the comment come back to me, through her again, that stung worse than the shed, worse than the denial, worse than any of it. He'd told somebody, and it got to me word for word, I believe it did anyhow, that our young'uns had always been "a mess" over there and he reckoned we never did raise 'em right. Now you can talk about my shed, you can talk about my fence line, you can talk about my flower bed till the cows come home. Don't you say a word about how I raised mine. That one sat with me a long, long while.

I did try, though, I want that known. Come around Christmas that same year I carried a plate of tea cakes over, knocked on the door myself, said I didn't want this hangin' between us into a new year, said let's just let it go. Door didn't even open all the way. Plate got took, said thank you kindly, and that door shut. Didn't invite me in for the first time in I don't know how many years. That was the peace offering and that's what become of it.

Rest of the street took sides after that, and it split about down the middle, I'd say, near as I can figure. The ones on this end of the block sided with me, the ones on that end sided with them, and the ones in the middle just tried to stay out of it best they could, which I don't blame 'em one bit for. It all finally come to a head out in the street itself, if you can believe that, both of us hollerin' where anybody on the whole block could've heard it, and did, cause half of 'em come out on their porches to watch. That's when the accusation come out about my dog gettin' loose and diggin' up part of his garden, tore up a whole row of his greens he'd been growin' for a mess of collards, and I told him then and I'll tell you now, my dog ain't never been loose a day in his life, that dog don't leave my yard, and whatever got into that garden, it wasn't mine. He said it was. I said it wasn't. Neither one of us backed down an inch.

Been about six years now since either one of us has said one word to the other, six years of not speakin', not wavin', cuttin' the lights on of an evenin' and not even lookin' that direction no more. Little thing shows itself near every day if you know to look for it. I still cut my grass right up to that line and not one inch over, and he does the same on his side, and there's this little strip right along the fence where neither one of us'll touch it, grows up wild as anything, ain't nobody weeded it in years.

And I'll tell you somethin' else, since you asked, and since you was decent enough to just listen instead of pickin' it apart like some would. Much as I've told you my side, and I do believe my side, some nights here lately I lay there and I ain't a hundred percent sure no more who really started all this. Might could've been the shed. Might could've been before that and I just don't recollect it right. That's the honest truth of it, and I ain't gonna pretty it up for you or nobody else.
