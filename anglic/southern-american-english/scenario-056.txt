Lord, where do I even start with that week. My sister and her husband took the young'uns down to the coast for their vacation and asked would I mind the house while they was gone, seven whole days, feed the dog and the cat, water everything, bring in the mail, all of it. I told her sure, ain't nothing to it, how hard can watching one house be. Well.

First night already had something not right about it. I'm laying up in the guest room and the house is making noises a house I don't live in has got no business making, popping and settling, and that dog wouldn't quit pacing at the back door like he heard something out yonder in the dark. I got up and cut the lights on in the kitchen and stood there a minute just looking, and wasn't nothing there, but I did not sleep good that first night, not one bit.

Second day the cat got out. I opened the door to bring the paper in and that thing shot past my leg like it had someplace to be, straight out across the yard and gone up under the neighbor's fence before I could even holler after it. I liketa had a heart attack right there on the porch. Spent near about an hour crawling around under bushes calling it sweet as I could manage while cussing it something fierce in my head, and directly it come strolling back out from under the porch like it hadn't done a thing in this world, looking at me like I was the one who'd lost my mind. Caught it up and held it like a baby the rest of that afternoon, I was so relieved.

Then it was the plants. My sister's got a whole row of tomatoes and peppers out back and a mess of greens besides, and I was watering them same as she showed me before she left, but along about Wednesday I noticed the leaves on them tomatoes going yellow and droopy, and I did not know was that too much water or too little or some bug done got in there. I just kept on doing what I'd been doing and hoping for the best. By Thursday they looked plumb pitiful, and the stakes holding them up had gone all cattywampus in a little wind we had, leaning every which way. I ain't got the first idea about gardening, never have, so I was fixin' to just let it ride till they got home and let them sort it, figured they'd know better than I would what to do with it.

Wednesday evening the garbage disposal quit right under my watch. I mashed that switch on the wall same as always and it just hummed and wouldn't do a thing, wouldn't grind, nothing. Mashed it two or three more times like that was gonna fix something on its own, then finally give up and scooped what was down in there out with a spoon, praying it wasn't broke for good, praying I hadn't gone and broke it my own self putting something down there I shouldn't have.

Had to run to the Piggly Wiggly Thursday morning for more coke and bread, and I'm out there wrestling a buggy with one wheel that wanted to go its own direction the whole time, and I remember thinking, this week might could go a little smoother than it's going.

Thursday afternoon, out of nowhere, here comes her mother-in-law up the driveway, no call, no warning, just pulls in like she lives there. I about jumped out of my skin, thought for one second somebody was breaking in.

The mother-in-law: Well ain't you a sight. Where's my son at?

Me: They're down at the coast, ma'am, won't be back till Sunday evening. I'm just minding the house for 'em.

The mother-in-law: Nobody told me that.

Me: I reckon they meant to, ma'am, it happened kind of fast.

She come on in anyway and set at the kitchen table and drank near about a whole pitcher of my sister's sweet tea and told me all about her knee acting up, and how mama an' them used to could grow tomatoes twice that size back when she was coming up, and I just kept saying yes ma'am, no ma'am, till she finally got up and left an hour and a half later. Bless her heart, she didn't mean no harm by it, she just don't know how to call ahead about anything.

Friday I thought I had lost my sister's ring for good. She'd left it setting on the bathroom counter and told me before she left, don't let that thing go down the drain, and I moved it to clean and could not for the life of me find where I'd set it back down. Tore that whole bathroom up looking, my heart going the whole time like I was fixin' to have to call her at the beach and tell her I done lost her wedding ring. Found it, finally, in the pocket of my own housecoat where I'd put it without thinking so it wouldn't go missing on the counter. Lord, I could've laid down right there.

That same Friday I locked myself out. Went out to get the mail in my sock feet, door swung shut behind me on its own, and there I sat on the front step near about three hours till the neighbor come home from work and let me use her phone to call a man about a key. Sat out there and watched the sky start coming up a cloud, dark and low and mean-looking, and thought, well, ain't this just the icing on it.

The neighbor: You been settin' out here long?

Me: Long enough.

The neighbor: Don't be ugly, come on in and wait, ain't no sense you settin' on a step like a stray.

The man with the key, when he finally come, was polite as anything about it.

The man with the key: Happens more than you'd think, ma'am. Won't take me but a minute, sir, excuse me, I mean to say, won't take but a minute.

Saturday morning I went in the front bedroom and there was a puddle of water on the windowsill running down onto the floor, from a window I'd cracked open Tuesday for the air and never shut back before that cloud finally broke loose Friday night. Ruined a spot on the carpet, I believe, though it don't show too bad if you don't know to look for it. I mopped it up best I could and set a fan on it and prayed the good Lord it would dry out fine.

And the noise in the night, I've got to tell about that too, because Saturday night, close to two in the morning, something banged around out in the carport loud enough to wake the dead, and me and that dog both went stiff as boards. I got up, heart going, grabbed the only thing close to hand, which was a broom, and went and cut the carport light on, and it wasn't nothing but a possum done knocked the garbage can clean over, rooting through it. Madder than a wet hen at that possum, standing out there at two in the morning in my nightgown holding a broom like it was gonna do me any good.

By Saturday, which was the next-to-last day, I finally got that disposal sorted. Turned out there was a spoon handle jammed down in there, must've fell off the counter sometime that week without me noticing, and once I fished it out with the pliers it run smooth as anything. Felt like I'd won something, I tell you.

Sunday morning, they was due in that afternoon, and I plumb near went crazy trying to get that house looking like nothing had happened at all. Vacuumed that carpet spot within an inch of its life, put the ring back exactly where it needed to sit, hosed down the porch, straightened the tomato stakes best I could, fed both animals extra so they'd act sweet and glad instead of showing out or pitching a fit when everybody walked in the door. Stood there in the kitchen soon as I heard the car in the driveway, trying to look like a woman who'd had a real easy, quiet week of it.

When they come in the door the young'uns run at me hollering, wanting some sugar, gimme some sugar, gimme some sugar, and my sister looked all around and said the house looked just fine, real nice even, and I said well, it was quiet, y'all, nothing much happened around here. Her husband asked about the tomatoes and I said I believe they might should get a little more shade come next summer, that's all, they got a mite droopy in this heat, nothing to worry over. My sister asked had anybody come by and I said her mother-in-law had stopped in a spell on Thursday, and she just laughed and said, Lord, I should've warned you, she does that to everybody, all y'all included. I never did tell her about the cat, or the window, or the possum, or me setting on that porch three hours in my sock feet. Told her the disposal had acted up but I got it fixed, and left it right there.

They asked me to stay on for supper, and we had a good one, ham and them same greens off the counter, and I ate like I hadn't eaten in a week, God willing and the creek don't rise I never house-sit again anytime soon. My sister thanked me over and over, said she owed me one, and I told her wasn't nothing to it, glad to do it for kinfolk, and went on home after and slept about two days straight.
