Me:

I'm goin' to tell you'uns the whole of hit now, straight through, so you have hit same as I do.

Along about the last hour hit was just me and her in there, wadn't nobody else, and I'd thought she'd done drifted off to sleep for good. But she come to, and the first thing out of her mouth wadn't nothin' about dyin' a-tall. She said the fireboard needed dustin' right smart, said hit plain and easy like she'd just now noticed hit settin' there, and I liked to laughed and cried both at the same time.

Then she got to studyin' on somethin' else. Said they was a little debt owed down at the store in the holler, and she wanted hit seen to before anybody had to fret over hit theirself. Said see that hit's paid, clear as anything, no mistakin' what she meant.

She called me her old buttercup, same as she'd called me since the day I was borned. I don't reckon I'll ever hear that again this side of the ground.

Then she up and asked me somethin' I wadn't lookin' for - asked was I afeared of windin' up plumb wore down and hurtin' like her - and I told her the truth of hit, the best I might could. Told her I wasn't afeared of that a-tall. Told her I was only afeared of the house without her in hit.

The last whole sentence she managed to get out was, tell 'em I loved every one of 'em, and don't let the young'uns forget how to put up beans. I've kept that whole ever since, word for word, same as I heared hit.

After that they wadn't no more talkin'. Just her hand in mine, and me a-holdin' on, her breathin' gettin' slower and further apart, till there just wadn't no more breath to hit, and she went on.

That's the whole of hit, plain as I can give hit to you.
