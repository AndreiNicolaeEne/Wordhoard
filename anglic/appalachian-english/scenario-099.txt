Me: Well I'll be. Look what the cat drug in.

Old classmate: Lord have mercy, is that you? I ain't laid eyes on you since I don't know when. You'uns still livin up past the holler there, or did you move on down?

Me: Still up there, still a-fightin that garden every summer like it owes me money. How's your people?

Old classmate: Cain't complain. Mama's gettin on, her hip's been botherin her right smart, but she's still puttin up beans like she's forty. Daddy passed a few winters back now.

Me: I heared that. I'm sorry, I meant to get down there and I never did make it.

Old classmate: Well, you know how it is. Everybody means to. How's your own young'uns?

Me: Growed and gone, every one of em. One's over the mountain, one's clean up north somewhere, don't ask me to say where at, I cain't never keep it straight.

Old classmate: Lord. Time do go, don't it.

Me: It does. Say, you remember the schoolhouse? I was just thinkin on it the other day, don't ask me why.

Old classmate: I was fixin to say the same thing. You mean over where we went at, with the teacher that like to scared us all half to death?

Me: That's the one. I still get a little cold feelin just sayin it.

Old classmate: Lord, that teacher. If you talked out of turn one time, just one time, that was it for you. Didn't matter if you was fixin to answer a question right or wrong, you did not speak lessen you was called on, and you did not get up out of that seat for nothin.

Me: And if you broke it, you was writin lines. I don't mean five or ten neither. A hundred lines, every single time, didn't matter what you done, talkin, chewin gum, droppin a book too loud. A hundred times, "I will not talk without permission," till your hand like to fell off.

Old classmate: I still remember the sound of it. Whole row of us a-scratchin away after school while everbody else got to go on home.

Me: I can hear that teacher sayin it right now, plain as day. "This is my room and there is one rule that matters, and it is that you will not speak until you are spoke to." Said it the same ever single time, like it was fresh out the teacher's mouth that mornin, even though we'd all heared it a hundred times over.

Old classmate: Oh Lord, I can hear it too now that you said it. Gives me a chill.

Me: You remember the time that teacher made the one boy stand up in front of everbody and hold them books out at arm's length till his arms was shakin?

Old classmate: I do. I do remember that. We all just set there and watched, didn't nobody dare laugh out loud, but you could feel everbody wantin to.

Me: I still get that same froze-up feelin sometimes, even now, if somebody calls my name out sudden in a room full of people. Like I'm fixin to get called on and I ain't got the answer.

Old classmate: Same here. Same exact thing. Ain't that somethin, all these years later.

Me: You remember the one time that girl talked back? Lord, I still cain't hardly believe it happened.

Old classmate: I think about that all the time. Teacher was fussin at her over somethin, I don't even recall what now, and she just looked right up and said, plain as anything, "Well maybe if you'd have explained it right the first time I wouldn't be askin." And I swear to you, that whole room went dead quiet, we was all waitin on her to get sent to the office or worse.

Me: And she never did get in no trouble. Not a lick. Teacher just stood there a second lookin at her, and then went right on with the lesson like nothin happened.

Old classmate: I ain't never seen the like before or since. We used to talk about that for years after, like it was a legend or somethin.

Me: It might could been the bravest thing I ever seen a young'un do.

Old classmate: You remember that little habit that teacher had, with the chalk? Tap tap tap on the board three times before ever sayin a word?

Me: Lord yes. We used to mock that something awful behind the teacher's back, tappin our pencils on our desks three times when the teacher wasn't lookin, gigglin about it.

Old classmate: I forgot all about that till just now. Three taps, ever single time, regular as clockwork.

Me: But you remember the time that teacher was kind to the boy whose daddy had just passed? Come right up to his desk, quiet like, and just laid a hand on his shoulder and told him take however long he needed on his work, wasn't no rush on him at all. Whole different person right there for about a minute.

Old classmate: I do remember that. Surprised the fire out of me at the time. Didn't seem like the same teacher a-tall.

Me: You ever hear whatever become of that teacher? I heard tell the teacher retired and moved off somewhere, but I don't rightly know if that's so.

Old classmate: I heard the same thing, that or passed on, one or the other, but I couldn't tell you which for certain sure. Nobody I know's kept up.

Me: It's a funny thing, lookin back. We was plumb terrified of that teacher the whole time we was in there.

Old classmate: We was. But when I think on it now, it ain't fear no more, not really. It's more like respect, if I'm honest with you. That teacher meant for us to learn somethin and by God we did.

Me: I've come around to thinkin the very same. Wasn't a mean bone in it, just strict past all reason.

Old classmate: Lord, but strict don't half cover it.

Me: No it surely don't. I still get that little flutter in my stomach thinkin about it, even now, grown as I am.

Old classmate: Me too if I'm bein honest. Some things just stick with you.

Me: They surely do. Well, listen, how's that garden of your'n comin along this year?

Old classmate: Doin real good, tomatoes come in early this time. You still puttin up your own beans?

Me: Every fall, same as always. Got a right smart of squash too, more'n I know what to do with.

Old classmate: I'll take some off your hands if you're givin it away.

Me: I'll bring some by. How's your husband keepin, he still up at the sawmill?

Old classmate: He is, though his knees is givin him fits. Talkin about retirin here directly.

Me: Good for him. Tell him I said hey. And your youngest, she still singin over at the church?

Old classmate: She is, got a right pretty voice on her too, takes after her granny that way.

Me: Well I'm proud to hear it. It was good runnin into you, I mean that.

Old classmate: It surely was. Don't be no stranger now, you hear?

Me: I won't. You take care now.

Old classmate: You too, hon. Tell your people I said hello.
