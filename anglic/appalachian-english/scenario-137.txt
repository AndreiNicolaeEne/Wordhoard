Hit was already a mess before anybody said word one to me. The hogs had done got out and gone through the beans like they hadn't been fed in a week, rooted up a right smart of the corn my grandmaw had put by, and my uncle come up from the barn a-hollering before I even had my boots laced.

Uncle: Which one of you'uns left that gate open.

He looked straight at me when he said hit, though.

Uncle: You left that gate open again, didn't ye.

Me: No sir.

Uncle: Hit was you down here last, I know hit was.

Me: I never touched that gate.

Uncle: Same thing ever' time. You get to a-hollering on that phone and forget what you're about, and now look at hit.

Me: I never was down here.

The two boys who'd actually left it swinging stood back by the fence post, quiet as posts theirselves, studying the dirt like hit had something wrote on it. Neither one opened his mouth the whole time.

Uncle: I'm asking you plain. Did you leave that gate open.

Me: I swear before God I did not.

I didn't say hit again. Wadn't no use in it, and he could stand there restating hit till the sun went down, weren't gonna make it true. I've heared him get onto folks before and it never done no good talking wilder than he was, so I never raised my voice to match his. My face didn't give him nothing back neither, no matter how red he got nor how loud he got. I just stood there plumb still and let him say his piece.

Uncle: Well, somebody did.

Me: Reckon so.

Wasn't no more to it. Nobody said sorry, and I didn't look for it. I turned and walked on back up toward the house, and what I'd told him stood right where I'd left it.
