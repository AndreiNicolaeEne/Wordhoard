Me: You reckon we just do it up at the holler same as always, round seven?

My friend: Seven ain't gonna work for me, I got to run mommy to her sister's and won't be back till good on toward dark.

Me: Well hit don't matter none, we can push it back a bit. Eight suit you better?

My friend: Eight might could work. Let's go on down the list and see who all's even free. What about the one that works down at the plant?

Me: I seed her yesterday at the store, she said she might could get off early but weren't sure yet.

My friend: And her brother, is he a-coming?

Me: He's a-working a double at the mill, cain't make it a-tall this time.

My friend: What about the cousin with the truck?

Me: He's in for sure. Said he'd bring a poke of chips and something to drink.

My friend: Good, we'll need that. And the one up past the fireboard shop, the little store there?

Me: She heared about it already off her sister, said she'd be there.

My friend: What about the young'un's daddy, is he coming this time?

Me: No, he cain't. Says he's back on third shift again, been running him plumb ragged all week, ain't hardly home before midnight.

My friend: That's a shame, he's always good company when he can get loose.

Me: I know it, but hit is what it is. So does eight still suit, now we've pushed it back?

My friend: Eight's fine, but might could we do half past instead, give her time to get her young'uns settled first?

Me: That works fine by me. Half past eight up at the holler then.

My friend: Alright, I'll pass the word on to the rest so it ain't all falling on you to call everybody.

Me: I'd 'preciate that right smart. So we're looking at, what, six, maybe seven if the cousin's girl decides to tag along?

My friend: Right smart of a crowd for a Tuesday night. Six or seven sounds about right, though there ain't no telling exact till we're all standing there.

Me: Hit'll work out fine either way. See you'uns up there.
