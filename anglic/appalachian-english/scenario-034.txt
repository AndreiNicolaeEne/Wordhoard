The stall sets right where the road bends down toward the holler, and the owner's already got the griddle going before the regular's boots hit the gravel good.

The owner: Well look what the cat drug in. Fried bologna, extry mustard, no onion, same as always?

The customer: You know me too good. Onions today though, I'm a-feeling brave.

The owner: Onions it is. Set yourself down, hit won't be but a minute.

The customer: Ain't in no hurry. Ground don't grow no faster if I stand over you a-watching.

The owner: How's things up your way? Heared your boy's been a-hollering about school again.

The customer: Lord, he don't want to go, but he's got to. We was leveling with him last night, told him hit don't matter if he likes it or not, some things just get done regardless.

The owner: Ain't that the truth. Business been alright? Y'all still coming three times a week or has that dropped off on me?

The customer: Might could make it four here directly, way my wife's been sending me off to fetch supper of a evening.

The owner: Ha, well I ain't complaining none. Been right smart slower round here since the weather turned, but weekends still bring folks down off the ridge.

The customer: Sigogglin as that table's setting, I'm surprised it don't scare em off first thing.

The owner: Hush now, that table's held up plumb near ten year, longer'n some marriages I could name.

The customer: Longer'n mine, I reckon. Don't you go telling my wife I said that.

The owner: Your secret's safe with me. Here you go now, hot off the griddle.

The customer: What I owe you today?

The owner: Same as ever, five even.

The customer: There you go, keep the rest.

The owner: I'll see you Thursday then, same time.

The customer: Same time. You'uns take care now.

The owner: You'uns too, and tell that boy of yourn I said mind his mama.
