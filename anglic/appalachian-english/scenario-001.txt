Looking back on it now, hit was fourteen hours near on the nose from that first catch in my back to when she finally cried out, but you don't know that whilst you're down in the middle of it, all you know is it don't stop.

Hit started up along about suppertime, nothing but a little catch low in my back, and I taken it for having stood over the stove too long. Didn't think nothing of it, went on and cleared the supper dishes same as always. But it kept on, and by full dark it was coming regular, and I knowed then, sure as anything, this was it.

The other one was already over to her granny's for the night, so it was just me and my man in the house, waiting on it. He used to could sleep through a thunderstorm and never stir, but they wasn't no sleeping in him that night, up and down like a jack-in-the-box, a-pacing and a-fretting whilst I set by the fireboard trying to breathe through each one. Hours went on that way, slow and grinding, nine o'clock, ten, on toward midnight and past it.

Somewheres past the middle of the night is when it turned hard on me, the kind of hurting that don't let up between one and the next. I was a-hollering some, I won't lie about it, and my man lit out to fetch the granny woman from down the holler. She wasn't there from the start, but I seed her headlights come up along about the worst of it, and she come in calm as anything and knowed just what to do.

The granny woman: Bear down now, hard as you can, this next one's the one.

I pushed a right smart while, felt like plumb forever, and then come this quiet, just a few seconds of it, but I never heared such a quiet in all my life, and I like to went out of my mind waiting on it to break. Then she cried, and inside of a minute they laid her up on me, wet and hollering both, my second one, borned into the world at last, and I might could tell you I was tired, but glad don't half cover it neither.
