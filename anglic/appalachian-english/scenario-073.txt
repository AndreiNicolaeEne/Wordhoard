Well set down there and let me tell hit to you proper, since you're wanting the whole history and not just the short of it. You'uns'll be part of this now, so you might as well know where it come from.

Started about ten year back, give or take, up at my cousin's place on the holler road, the one whose daddy kept the store before it burned. She's the one drug the rest of us together, and I mean drug, none of us would've knowed one another otherwise, not proper. Her and me was already close on account of blood, but she'd went to school with the peacemaker's sister and worked the register alongside the organizer's mother, and she just kept a-pulling folks in like she was fixing to feed the whole holler at once. Had us all up at that house for a fish fry that first summer, said "you'uns come on, I ain't asking twice," and hit stuck. That's the whole of how hit got borned, right there on that porch with the fireboard still black from the woodstove behind her.

Now the late one. Lord, the late one almost never stayed on. First few times we got together she'd show up an hour behind, or not at all, and folks was fixing to quit inviting her, said hit weren't worth the trouble. But then her granny took sick that first winter and she had nobody else to set with her of an evening, and the peacemaker just started going and setting with her, no fuss made of hit, and after that the late one seed we meant hit when we said we was there for one another. She's still late to everything to this day, might could tell you hit's a curse on her, but ain't nobody thought of leaving her out since.

You'uns'll want to know about the falling-out too, since it's where the joke come from. The organizer and the quiet one had a set-to, oh, must've been the second year, over something so foolish I near forget the shape of it, a truck, a favor not returned, words said sharper than meant. They was both a-fussing and a-fighting for near a month, wouldn't hardly look at one another at gatherings, and the rest of us was leveling with each other that it couldn't go on thataway. What mended hit was the quiet one showing up at the organizer's place with a poke of tomatoes off her own vine, set hit on the step, and said nary a word, just left. Organizer et every one of them tomatoes and never mentioned hit again. To this day if either one of them gets prickly over nothing, somebody'll say "well, go on and bring the tomatoes," and that's the whole of the joke, you'll hear hit plenty, don't worry on understanding it all at once, just laugh when we do.

We lost one along the way and all. The schoolteacher's girl was with us the first four, five year, right smart of a laugh she was, but she up and said the group had gotten too settled for her, said we'd all quit growing and she needed something else. That's the reason she give, anyhow. Some of us still think hit was more to do with a fella she took up with that none of us cared for, but she never said that part out loud and we never pressed her on hit. She's gone on to wherever she's gone, and we don't speak ill.

The one who filled that gap in some ways come by way of her brother, who'd been in the group from near the start. He vouched for her hard, said she was solid, but we didn't just take his word plain, put her through better part of a year before anybody called her one of us proper. Not on purpose, mind, weren't no test set up a-purpose, just the natural way of it: you get invited to the little things first, the porch sitting and the small suppers, and if you keep on showing up right, keep on being decent, then one day you're just there at the big ones too and can't nobody remember when that changed. That's how hit works. That's how hit's fixing to work for you too, only you're ahead of the curve since I'm a-telling you all this now instead of making you piece hit together yourself.

The thing that bonded the rest of us past all reckoning was that trip up the mountain the year the weather turned on us quick, got caught out in hit good, soaked plumb through, had to hole up in an old hunting cabin that weren't fit for nothing, and we was there near two days before anybody could get up to us. Cold don't half describe hit. But we laughed more them two days than most weeks put together, and there ain't a one of us don't bring hit up still. That's the trip we mean when we say "the cabin," and if you hear that said you'll know now what hit's pointing at.

We lost another one too, though not the same way, the one who used to could keep every one of us laughing till we cried, moved off for work, clean across two states now, and there's a hole there still. We talk to her when we can, but hit ain't the same as having her setting right there, and I won't lie to you and say we don't feel hit at every gathering, on account of we do.

These days everybody's got their fixed part to play. The organizer's the one calls hit all together, sends word round, picks the place, wouldn't nothing happen without her, and we all know hit. The late one, well, you know about her now. And the peacemaker's the one that steps in quiet-like whenever voices start climbing, never raises her own, just says something that takes the wind clean out of whatever's building, same as she done that first winter.

There was a low point, I'll not pretend there weren't. Bout four year in, money got tight all round and some hard words got said over who owed who what, and for a spell there we near quit meeting altogether, went the better part of a season without nobody calling nobody. What mended hit was the organizer, same as ever, just setting a date and saying come if you're coming, no mention of the trouble at all, and enough of us come that hit healed on its own, the way a lot of things do if you just let folks be in a room together long enough.

One tradition's held since that very first porch, and hit's this: whoever's place we're at, first thing done is somebody sets out a poke of something homegrown on the table, don't matter what, tomatoes or beans or whatever come in that season, and we eat that before anything else gets served. Been that way since the fish fry and ain't never not been that way.

Now you, you already done your part toward belonging, small as hit seems. You brought your own chair last time 'stead of waiting on somebody to find you one, and you helped clean up after without being asked, and hit was seed, don't think it weren't. That's near about all hit takes round here. So welcome on in, proper. You're close as kin to us now, and that's the truth of hit.
