Me: Nine more days till this month turns over. Nine days and look what we got laying here.

The roommate: Twenty dollars and some change ain't laying, hit's drowning.

Me: Well count it again, maybe hit growed some since supper.

The roommate: Twenty-two fifteen. That there's it, that's the whole of it till the check comes.

Me: Beans then. We'uns get us a big pot of pintos going tomorrow and hit'll carry us clean through. Cornbread to go with, long as the meal holds out.

The roommate: Beans ever day for nine days.

Me: Beans and taters some nights, beans plain other nights. Hit ain't but two dollars of a bag if we're careful, and we already got the fatback in the freezer.

The roommate: I was fixin to run down to the dollar store for some of that good coffee, the kind I like.

Me: You ain't buying no coffee. We got a can of the cheap kind setting right there, half full.

The roommate: Hit tastes like dishwater and you know it.

Me: Don't matter what hit tastes like, hit's paid for already. You spend three dollar on coffee and we're eating beans with no cornbread come day seven.

The roommate: Fine. Fine, I won't. Lord, you'd think I asked to buy a truck.

Me: What about the water bill, it due this week?

The roommate: Due Friday. I'll call and ask can they push it, tell em I'll catch it up next check. They done it before.

Me: Might could work. So we got the twenty-two, minus what goes to gas for your truck getting to work, that leaves right smart little for anything else.

The roommate: We'll make hit stretch. We always have.

Me: Mama called while ago, said she's got a mess of green beans put up from the garden she wants shed of, said come get some whenever.

The roommate: Well there. That's supper for two nights right there, free and clear.

Me: See, we'll make it. Plumb tight, but we'll make it.
