The wife: You ain't touched that coffee and it's fixin to go plumb cold on you. Where'd you get off to.

The husband: Hit ain't nothing. Just a dream I can't quit turning over in my head.

The wife: Well turn it over out loud then, I ain't got nowhere to be till the eggs need gathered.

The husband: Alright. Started off ordinary as anything. I was just a-walking home, same road I've walked a thousand times, nothing strange about it at first. But it kept a-going. Kept on and on past where home ought to have turned up already. I'd walk and walk and the holler just kept stretching out longer, like somebody was a-pulling the road out ahead of me the whole time I walked it.

The wife: Lord. Did you get worried, or was it still easy-feeling.

The husband: Still easy at first. That's the thing about a dream, hit don't tell you to be scared till it's good and ready. I just figured I'd took a wrong turn somewhere and kept on. And then all of a sudden there the house was, right where it was supposed to be the whole time, like the road had been funning with me. Only when I went in, they was a room in it I ain't never seen in my life. Not in that house, not in no house. A room that don't exist waking.

The wife: What kind of room.

The husband: I'm gitting to it. Hold your horses.

The wife: Well I'm asking. What kind.

The husband: Sitting room, I reckon you'd call it, off the back, past where the kitchen ought to end. Had a fireboard in it and everything, like it'd always been there. And setting right there in the chair by it was my daddy.

The wife: Oh Lord.

The husband: Calm as you please. Same as he ever set of an evening, hands folded, like he'd just got up from dinner. Not a day passed on him. And he was a-talking to me, plain as I'm talking to you now.

The wife: What'd he say.

The husband: That's just it. I can't call it back. I know they was words, I know he said something to me and I know I heared it and understood it right then, standing there. I just can't git the words back out now. It's like reaching for something in the dark and your hand keeps closing on nothing.

The wife: That'll unsettle a body.

The husband: Hold on, let me try. He said something about... naw. It was something like... naw, that ain't it neither. I keep about to have it and it slips right back off. I reckon I ain't going to git it. Just the feeling's left, like I was told something I needed telling, and now I can't carry it out the door with me.

The wife: That's an awful thing, to be told something and not git to keep it.

The husband: Well it don't stop there neither. Whole thing shifted right after, quick as a dream'll do. I was a-hunting for something then. Tore that house up looking, and the yard both, drawers and the smokehouse and under the porch, everwhere a thing could be hid, and it weren't in nary a one of them.

The wife: What was you a-looking for.

The husband: That's the thing of it. I don't know. I couldn't tell you right now and I couldn't tell you then neither, in the dream itself. I just knowed I had to find it and I knowed it was gone and I kept a-hollering for it same as you'd holler down the ridge for a young'un that's wandered off, except I never had the name of the thing to holler.

The wife: That'd about drive a body out of their mind.

The husband: It got up in me till I couldn't hardly stand it. Searching faster and faster and nothing turning up nowhere, and the longer it went the worse the feeling got, till I was near about running through that house, and my heart was a-going like I'd run the ridge twice. And then hit just broke, the whole thing, and I come up outta sleep with my heart still a-slamming, laying there stiff as a fence post not knowing for a good minute which one was real, that dream or this bed I was laying in.

The wife: Well you're setting here now, so I reckon we know which.

The husband: I know it now. Didn't know it then. Laid there a right smart while just letting my heart settle down and telling myself, this here's the real one, that room ain't real, before I could hardly move.

The wife: I don't know what to make of him a-setting there so easy about it. You'd think if your own daddy come to you in a dream he'd be all tore up or trying to warn you off something, not just setting there calm as Sunday.

The husband: I've heared it means different things. Old folks used to say it means he's alright, wherever he's got to. Setting easy because he's easy.

The wife: Could be. My own grandmother always did say a dead person a-talking calm in a dream was them letting you know they're settled, and you ought to settle too.

The husband: Could be that's it. Or it could be it don't mean nothing at all, just my head a-doing what heads do of a night, stirring up whatall's in there.

The wife: Might could be both at once. I don't reckon there's a rule to it.

The husband: If they was a rule I never learned it. I just know I'd give a right smart to have them words back. Whatever he was telling me, I believe it mattered.

The wife: Well maybe that's the whole of it right there, that some things git told to you and you ain't meant to carry the words out, just the knowing that you was told.

The husband: That's a sight more comforting than I was making it out to be, setting here fretting over it.

The wife: I ain't saying I know. I'm just saying it same as you'd say it, turning it over same as you.

The husband: Well. Coffee's gone cold now sure enough.

The wife: I told you it was fixin to. Drink it anyhow, cold coffee never killed nobody, only a dream'll do that, and even then it never has yet.
