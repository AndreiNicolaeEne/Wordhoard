Well, I told my workmate I'd never do it again, and I mean it this time, but let me tell it from the start so you get the whole mess of it.

They was going down to see kin for a week, the folks up past the second creek crossing, and they asked would I stay on the place and mind it. Feed the goat, water the garden, keep an eye on the house. I said I would, hit didn't sound like nothing. Seven days. How bad can seven days get.

First night already had something not right about it. I got in about dark, put my bag down in the spare room, and the house was making a sound in the walls I couldn't place, kindly a ticking, a-going tick tick tick like something walking real slow behind the plaster. I laid there a good while a-listening to it before I figured it was just the house cooling off, the way an old house will talk to itself once the sun's off it. Still didn't sleep right. Kept thinking I heared a step on the porch and there wasn't nobody.

Second day the goat got out. I don't rightly know how, the latch on that pen looked fine to me, but I come out to feed her of a morning and she was gone, just gone, and I liked to had a fit. Went up the holler hollering her name, well, not her name, just a-hollering "come on, come on now" the way you do, and a neighbor woman two places up seen me going by and hollered down.

Neighbor: What're you after, up here a-running like that?

Me: The goat's out. She ain't yours, is she?

Neighbor: Lord no, but I seen something white a-going through my beans not ten minutes back. Might could be down past the fence line yet.

And sure enough she was, standing in the fence corner looking at me like she'd done nothing at all, and I got a rope on her and had her back in the pen inside of an hour, but my heart was going the whole time. That's a bad way to start day two of seven.

Garden trouble I didn't see coming on all at once, it crept up. First it was just a few leaves gone yellow on the beans, I figured that was the heat, we hadn't had rain in a spell. Then I noticed something had been at the squash of a night, chewed right down low, and by Thursday half the row looked plumb ate up, wilted and holes everywhere, and I never did catch what was doing it, coon or groundhog or what. I watered extra and I picked off what bugs I could find and I still handed it back to them Sunday looking rough. Right smart worse than when I got there, if I'm honest, and I dreaded telling them that part most of all.

Wednesday the water heater quit. Not all the way, just quit heating, so you'd turn it on and get about a minute of warm and then it'd go to ice water on you right in the middle of washing up. I don't know a thing about water heaters. I went down in the basement with a flashlight and looked at it like looking at it was going to fix something, and it never did. Cold showers three days running. I texted the man of the house, careful not to make it sound worse than it was, and he texted back not to worry with it, said it done that sometimes, there was a reset switch on the side you had to push in with something skinny, a pencil or the like. I never would've found it my own self.

Thursday I had a visitor I wasn't looking for at all. I was out back hanging up wash when a truck pulled in the yard, and it was the man from the far side of the ridge, come by to borrow a chainsaw he said was always kept in the shed.

The man from up the ridge: You're staying on the place this week, are ye?

Me: I am. They're down seeing kin.

The man from up the ridge: Well I ain't after you, I'm after that saw of his. Tree come down across my drive last night in that wind.

I let him have it, wasn't nothing else to do, but it give me a start, a truck just pulling in like that with nobody having said a word to me about it beforehand.

Friday was the day I like to lost my mind. I went to water the tomatoes and set my phone down on the porch rail, and when I come back for it, it wasn't there. I looked and looked, under the rail, in the grass, in my own pockets three times over, and I got that sick drop in my stomach thinking I'd gone and lost the thing or it had fell off into the weeds never to be found, and worse, thinking how do I even tell them I lost my own phone on their property, like that's their fault to hear about. Found it a half hour later, it had slid down behind the porch swing cushion. I set right down on the steps after that, my hands still shaking a little from nothing at all.

Saturday I locked myself out. Went to get the mail down at the road and the door swung shut behind me on its own, the wind caught it, and there I stood with no key, nothing, in my house shoes, for near four hours till the man of the house's brother come by to check the mail his own self and let me back in with the spare he kept under the flowerpot. I set on that porch steps a long while, feeling about as foolish as I've ever felt, watching the shadows move.

And it was that same evening, after all that, I forgot I'd left the bedroom window up a crack for the air, and a storm come through in the night, one of them quick mountain storms that don't give you no warning, and by morning the sill was standing in water and the rug under it soaked clean through. I mopped what I could and set a fan on it and hoped for the best.

Then that night, near two in the morning, something got to banging around outside, a real racket, and I got up with the flashlight my heart just a-pounding, thinking it was a bear at the trash cans, and it was, or something was, I never did lay eyes on it, just heared it go crashing off through the brush toward the tree line. I didn't sleep the rest of that night either.

By the next-to-last day I'd had it with that water heater quitting on me every other shower, so I finally set down and read the little sticker on the side of it proper, and it turned out the pilot was low, not the reset switch at all, and once I got that flame built back up right it run fine the rest of the time I was there. Should've done that the first day and saved myself the misery.

Last day before they was due back I cleaned like the house was on fire. Mopped that rug spot again, aired the room out good, swept the porch, put the goat's pen back to rights, picked what I could save out the garden so it'd at least look tended, and hid the evidence of my own carelessness best I could where hit couldn't be helped no further.

When they got in I told them the whole of it, near enough. Told them the goat got loose but I caught her quick, told them about the water heater and how I'd fixed it my own self by the end, told them the garden had took a beating from something I never caught in the act. Didn't say much about the phone, or the door, or laying awake half of two different nights listening for something in the dark. Some of a week like that you keep to your own self.
