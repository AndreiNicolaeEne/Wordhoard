The eldest: Lord have mercy, my knees. Been a-fighting me nigh on twenty year now, ever since I went down that bank behind the barn. Cain't hardly get up off the porch swing of a morning, they pop like green kindling, and of a wet day hit's a plumb misery just to walk to the mailbox.

The next eldest: Knees. You want to talk misery, I got one three, four night out of ever month keeps me from sleeping a wink, right down in the small of my back and into the hip. I'm just a-laying there staring up at the fireboard till it's near about daylight, cain't get comfortable no way I turn, and up I get the next morning like nothing happened, 'cause what else is they to do.

The youngest of the three: Well now. You two ain't studying real misery. I was borned with this trouble in my chest, hit's been with me longer than either one of you all has knowed me, and that's a right smart while. My own mother used to say I come into this world a-wheezing and never did quit. Heared the doctor tell her the same thing hisself, said hit might could ease up when I got grown, and it never has.

The eldest: Well who's got it worst then, if we're counting?

The next eldest: Hit ain't me, I reckon, long as she's talking.

The youngest of the three: Might could be me, might could be neither one of you'uns, we're all three sigogglin by now anyhow, bent every which-a-way.

The eldest: We was better built when we was young'uns.

The next eldest: We used to could climb that ridge without stopping to blow. Now I'm wore slap out just getting to the garden gate.

The youngest of the three: Ain't a one of us fit to say who's worst off. We'll all three go home and complain to the cat.

They all three laughed at that, and didn't none of them give an inch.
