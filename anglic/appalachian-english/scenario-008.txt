Child: Mommy, can we get the four-wheeler now? You said maybe.

Mother: I know what I said, but hit ain't happening right now, and I ain't gonna dance around it with you. We're plumb out of money after the truck note and your paw's doctor bill. Your paw used to could pull overtime regular at the mine, but they cut that clean back. That's the truth of it, no dodging.

Child: I could do extra chores for it. Get the eggs of a morning, split kindling too, without you a-hollering at me twice about it.

Mother: I thank you for offering, but chores don't put cash in the jar overnight. Now if your paw gets steady work again, or the garden comes in right smart this year and we get caught up on what we owe, we might could talk on it again then. Not before.

Child: But I seed it myself, up at his place. The boy up the holler already got one, and his people ain't got no more than us.

Mother: I don't care what's setting in his yard. I heared what his people give up to get it, and what they're still owing besides. What's over there don't settle nothing about what's here. We go by our own well, not his'n.

Child: I reckon.

Mother: I've been leveling with you the whole time, and I ain't fixing to stop now. Saturday I'll take you down to the crick with your paw's old pole, see can we catch supper. That's what's on offer today.

Child: I'd rather have the four-wheeler.

Mother: I know it. Take it or leave it.

Child: I'll take it.
