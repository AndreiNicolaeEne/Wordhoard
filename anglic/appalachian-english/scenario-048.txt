I come up on my friend's porch of a Saturday evening and we set there rocking a good while before I ever got round to what I come for.

Me: Evening. You'uns get any rain up your holler today?

My friend: Just a mist of it. Enough to wet the tobacco and not much else. How's your garden holding up?

Me: Plumb wore out, if you want the truth of it. Beans is coming in right smart though, more'n I know what to do with.

My friend: That's good to hear. You look like you got something a-weighing on you, setting there a-picking at your galluses like that.

Me: Well. I reckon I do. I hate it worse than anything to ask, but I'm needing to borrow some money off of you. Four hundred dollars, if you could spare it.

My friend: Four hundred. What's it for, if you don't mind me asking?

Me: Truck's acting up some. Ain't nothing too bad, just needs looked at is all.

My friend: Nothing too bad, and you're needing four hundred dollars for it?

Me: Well. Might be the transmission's fixing to give out on me. I heared it a-grinding the other day, plain as anything. Didn't want to make more of it than it is.

My friend: Hit's more of it than you're letting on, I expect, knowing you. I can let you have it. But I'm needing it back by the end of the month, when my own note comes due.

Me: I'll have it to you first of the month, I give you my word on it. Might could even get it to you sooner, if the truck holds together that long.

My friend: That'll do fine, then.

Me: I swear, I don't know what in this world I'd do without a friend like you. You're the best friend a man ever had, I mean it, I'd give you the shirt off my back and then some.

My friend: Lord, hush now, you're liable to make me cry setting here. Just don't you go and wreck that truck on the way home, or you'll owe me for two things instead of one.

Me: I'll drive her plumb careful, don't you worry none about that.
