Guest: Well look here, how's it going with that young'un? Y'uns getting any rest a'tall?

The mother: Oh, we're doing fine. Making do, you know how it is.

The father: Now don't let her tell you that. Hit ain't fine, not by a long shot.

The mother: Well he's right. Baby's up four, five times of a night, and that's on a good one. Three hours in a stretch if I get that much, and it ain't even three hours together, it's forty minutes here and an hour yonder and then he's a-hollering again.

Guest: Lord have mercy, that'd wear plumb anybody down.

The father: My back's took to hurting something awful from sleeping half sat up in that chair, and my hands has took to shaking on me here of a morning, I don't know if it's the coffee or just tired plumb through.

Guest: Bless your hearts, I hate that for you'uns, I surely do.

The mother: And I'll tell you what I done the other day, I was that far gone I set the baby's bottle down in the fireboard 'stead of the icebox and didn't miss it till it had gone plumb warm. Stood there looking at it a good minute 'fore I even figured what I'd done.

Guest: That there tells me all I need to know. Now listen here, I ain't asking, I'm telling you'uns. I'll come set with him of an afternoon this week, give you two a chance to lay down proper, and I'm a-bringing supper over Thursday, don't you fuss about it none.

The father: You don't have to do all that.

Guest: Hush now, I'm doing it anyhow.

The mother: Well... Lord, I ain't got the strength left to argue with you. We'd be much obliged.
