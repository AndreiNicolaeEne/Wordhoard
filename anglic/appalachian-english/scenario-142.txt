Woke up this morning and knowed exactly what day it was before I even opened my eyes good. Laid there a minute just a-dreading it, wishing I'd never opened my mouth back in the spring when I swore up and down I'd give this whole day over to clearing that fence line on the ridge above the holler, the one growed up plumb solid in briars and locust saplings since Daddy passed. I'd been asked one time too many about it, and I said hit right out loud in front of everbody, I'll do it, I swore hit, and now here hit was.

Didn't want up. Laid there a-hollering at myself in my own head about what a fool I was to ever say such a thing. Got up anyway, ain't no way around a sworn word.

Walked up that holler road grumbling the whole way, muttering to myself about how far it was, how the sun wasn't even up good and I was already wore out just thinking on it. "Whole day," I kept saying. "I said a whole day, like a fool."

Started in on it around seven, and they weren't ten minutes in before I was fussing about the length of hit, said I'd clear it and I meant every foot of that fence, clean to the far corner where the ground goes sigogglin off toward the crick.

The wife: You gonna gripe the whole day or you gonna cut?

Me: Might could do both.

Kept on. Griped about the timing of hit too, right in the heat of the year when I could be up at the springhouse instead. Griped about my own bad judgment worst of all, said out loud more than once I don't know what fool part of me ever agreed to hit.

Along about midafternoon, arms burning, briars had done tore my galluses half loose, I thought right plain about quitting. Studied on it a good minute. Then I just told myself, flat: you swore hit. Didn't let the thought go no further than that.

Gritted through the worst stretch, the tangle down where the fence sags toward the crick bank. Finished hit proper, every foot, right at dusk. Grumbled the whole way home too. But hit was done, and I hadn't quit oncet.
