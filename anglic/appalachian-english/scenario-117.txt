White: Now see, right there. That first move you made, settin' up thataway right off the bat, I knowed then we was in for a long night of it. You could a went a different route with that opening and this whole thing plays out different from the jump. I've studied on it since and I still say hit.

Black: I stand by what I played. Weren't no crystal ball settin' on that table tellin' me how the rest of it was fixin' to go. I played what I knowed right then and it was sound, borned outta what I could see, not outta what happened three hours on down the line.

White: Sound don't mean a whole lot when you look back on where it got you.

Black: Looking back on ever'thing is easy. Any fool can win a game three days after it's done and the board's done put away in the closet.

White: Well the early part of it, I'll give ye, it run about even. Back and forth, tit for tat, neither one of us give up much of nothin' for a good long spell there.

Black: That's about the one thing we might agree on, I reckon, and don't get too used to it.

White: Then we get on down to about halfway through, and right there's the move that turned the whole thing over. You know the one I mean, you don't have to ask me twice.

Black: I know the one you mean because you bring it up ever' single time we get to talkin' on this game.

White: 'Cause it was a plain mistake. Wasn't nothin' fancy behind it, wasn't no deep trick a man could point to and say well look what he was up to. It was just a mistake, plain as the nose on your face.

Black: It wasn't no mistake with what I could see settin' right there in front of me at that minute. You're a-judgin' it off what come after, and that ain't fair to how a body plays a game while he's playin' it. At the time hit was sound as anything else I done that whole match, and I'll go to my grave sayin' so.

White: Sound. Hit like to cost you the whole game right then and there, and you know it did.

Black: Didn't cost me nothin' by itself. What cost me was what come a little on down the road, that trade we made, and I'll tell you plain, that one's on your play, not mine. You forced that exchange on me and I weren't give no good choice in it a-tall.

White: I forced nothin' on you. You had ways around it and you didn't take nary a one of 'em. That whole mess sits square on your side of the board.

Black: We ain't gonna agree on that one neither, might as well quit tryin'.

White: By the time we got on past that trade, the game had done gone south on you. I could about tell you the move number where it went from even to lost. Wasn't hardly no hope left in it after that, not a lick.

Black: Lost. Hit weren't lost, not by a long shot. You're callin' it lost a good ten, twelve moves before it was anywhere close to that. I had chances yet, and I took some of 'em too.

White: Chances. Alright then, let's talk on that comeback you was tryin' to make, 'cause you did try, I'll give you that much credit.

Black: I did try, and I've studied on whether it come in time ever since that night. Some nights settin' here I think it come too late, and other nights I think it might a worked if you hadn't seed it a-comin' from a mile off.

White: It come too late. Weren't no way around that fact, however you want to dress it up.

Black: There's a move right there, late in the thing, that if I'd a played it instead of what I actually done, I believe to my soul it saves the whole game. I've run it back a hundred times in my head laying up at night.

White: You always say that about that same move, ever' single time. I don't believe it saves a thing but I've heared you say it so many times now I could about say it for you before you get it out your mouth.

Black: Well I still believe it, and sayin' it a hundred times don't make it any less true.

White: Fine, fine. Now the last move, the one that actually ended the whole business, ain't no argument between us on what that move was. We both seed it plain as day when it landed.

Black: No argument on what it was. The argument's on what it means, and that's a different thing altogether.

White: What it means is I had the better game the whole way through.

Black: What it means is the game come down to one move at the end and it fell your way. That don't make the whole thing yours from the very first move on.

White: The whole match run to the same count either way we tell it, though. That much we don't fight over, and I don't reckon we ever will.

Black: That's right. Same count, both of us say the same number, and that might be about the only other thing in this whole conversation we don't argue on.

White: At the time, right after, you shuck my hand and said good game and that was the end of it. Weren't no arguin' then, not a word of it.

Black: Weren't nothin' to argue then. A man shakes your hand and says good game, hit's done, hit's over, ain't no sense a-hollerin' about it right there in front of folks at the table.

White: But now, settin' here a good while on, a-goin' back over it move by move, you want to say you had the stronger game the whole way through regardless.

Black: I do say that, and I'll keep on sayin' it regardless of how that last move fell out. A man can play the better game the whole way and still lose it at the very end. Happens plumb all the time, ask anybody that's played long as we have.

White: That's a right smart of a claim comin' from a man that lost.

Black: Losing don't tell the whole story of a game and you know good and well it don't.

White: You remember that other match, from a good while back now, before you moved on down the holler, the one where you tried near about that same thing in the middle of the board?

Black: I remember it fine.

White: Well that's a pattern right there if I ever seed one. Same trick, different night, different year even. You've got a habit of it, and I believe this game proves the same thing that one did.

Black: I don't take that comparison a-tall, not one bit of it. That game and this game ain't got nothin' to do with one another. Might could look the same to you settin' on the outside lookin' in, but they ain't the same, not even close, and you know it.

White: I say they are the same.

Black: And I say they ain't, and we could set here till the fire goes plumb out and you still wouldn't talk me around to your side of it.

White: Might could be so. I ain't givin' up my side of it neither, I'll tell you that much right now for free.

Black: Then I reckon we're right back where we started from.

White: I reckon we are. Same as ever' single time we get to talkin' on it.

Black: Might could be we just leave it there for tonight and let it lie.

White: Might could. Don't mean I've changed my mind on a single bit of it, not one bit.

Black: Nor me neither. Not one bit, and don't you go thinkin' otherwise.
