The foreman: Afore ever'body scatters, let's get tomorrow settled whilst it's still in our heads.

The eldest: Lord, don't start on that yet. What hour you fixing to have us up there?

The foreman: Six.

The eldest: Six? Hit ain't even light good by six this time of year.

The young one: Seven'd suit me a heap better anyhow.

The foreman: Seven, then. But don't nobody go dragging in at half-past.

The driver: Where we headed at, the barn up the holler or back down to the crick job?

The foreman: The barn. I seed that roof again Sunday and hit's worse than we thought. We was up there leveling the low end just last fall, and now she's gone sigogglin all over again on the far side.

The eldest: I'll bring the ladder and the come-along, same as always.

The young one: I got the nail gun, and a poke of finish nails left over from the last job.

The driver: I'll fetch more tar paper from the store, if they got any. Might could be plumb out, the way things been going.

The foreman: That's what's a-worrying me. I heared on the radio it's fixing to rain by evening, and if we ain't got her covered by then we'll lose what's left of them boards.

The eldest: Well somebody's got to work that high side where she's sagged the worst. Hit's the roughest part of the whole job.

The foreman: That'll be you again.

The eldest: Might could talk one of you'uns into taking a turn at it for once.

The foreman: Used to could. Not no more.

The eldest: Figured as much.

The foreman: All right. That's four of us needed - me, you, the young one, and you.

The driver: About that- I got to carry Mama to town for her doctoring in the morning. Cain't make it a-tall.

The foreman: Well that leaves us shorthanded on the ground, and somebody's got to fetch that tar paper now too.

The eldest: Ain't gonna be me, I already got the roof.

The young one: I'll fetch it on my way up. But you owe me now, plain and simple.

The foreman: Owe you nothing. You owe ME, after I covered your part of the Tuesday job.

The eldest: Just get there by seven, the both of you'uns, and we'll call it square on the roof.
