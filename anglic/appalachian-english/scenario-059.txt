The lights went out right at dusk, quick as you please, no storm nor nothing to blame it on, hit just quit on us. I was setting on the porch and the whole house went dark at once, and my wife hollered out from the kitchen, "Hit's out!" like I couldn't tell for myself. I looked out and seed the neighbor's porch light still burning down in the holler, so it wasn't the whole mountain out, just us up here on the ridge. Come to find out later a limb had let go and took the line down with it, but right then we didn't know narry a thing, just that we was setting in the black dark.

We commenced a-scrambling after that, feeling along the fireboard where I keep the matches, and my wife caught her hip on that old corner cupboard that has always set a little sigogglin in the corner, not once but twice, a-hollering both times loud enough to wake the dead.

Findin light enough was the real trouble. We had one candle stub left in the poke under the sink, and the flashlight the youngest had done wore the batteries plumb dead in it a week before, so it come down to that one stub for the whole household. I hooked my thumb under my galluses and studied on it a minute, then lit hit and set it in a jar lid on the table, and everbody drawed up close around that little light same as if it was a fire.

I used to could fix that fuse box my own self, but I wasn't fixing to go messing with it by lantern and get myself kilt, so we give up on it for the night.

The mother: You'uns doing all right in there?

Me: We're fine, just setting in the dark.

The eldest: I bet hit comes back on 'fore we go to bed.

Me: Might could. Might not.

Hit didn't. We was a-setting there a good while yet, right smart close together, the young'uns a-whispering low like it was some kind of adventure, till the stub burned down near to nothing. Then we blowed it out and went on to bed dark, ever one of us, and daylight had done come 'fore the power ever did.
