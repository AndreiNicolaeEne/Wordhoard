There was a right smart of wood needed splitting before dark, stacked up sigogglin against the shed same as always. The old hand rolled a round up onto the block without saying nothing yet, set his feet wide, looked down at the grain, and let the maul drop. It popped clean in two and fell away on its own like it wanted to.

The old hand: There now. You see where I hit it, right on that seam where it's already fixing to open. Not the middle of it. Try you one.

The new hand swung hard and caught the round dead center. It bounced off and near about took his own foot with it.

The old hand: No, no. You're plumb a-chasing it with your arms. Let the head do the work and aim for that line, not the middle of the thing.

The new hand: Wait, so am I hitting it on the side, or straight down through the top of it?

The old hand: The line. I just showed you the line, right there where it wants to split. Watch again.

He split another one slower, pointing at the spot before he ever swung.

The new hand swings again, glances off the same as before, and the round wobbles clean off the block.

The old hand: Lord have mercy, I done seed you do that same thing twice now. You heared me say the line and you're still a-swinging at the middle. Hit ain't hard.

The new hand: I'm trying. This maul's heavier than it looks and it don't want to go where I aim it.

The old hand: It ain't about how hard you swing it, hear. You're overpowering the thing. Set the head down on the line first, just resting, no swing at all. Now lift it straight up off that same spot and let it fall. Don't aim it. Just drop it back where it already was.

The new hand does that, and the round splits clean down the seam.

The old hand: There. See it now? Hit ain't about how hard you hit it. It's about where.

The new hand: I see it now.

The old hand: 'Bout time. Roll up another'n.
