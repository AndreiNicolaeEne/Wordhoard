My friend: I ain't slept but a hour or two, if that. He's gone, hon. Left out this mornin' 'fore daylight, took the truck and ever'thing off the fireboard was ever his to take, and said he'd been unhappy a good while and just couldn't do hit no more.

Me: Mm.

My friend: We'd been a-fightin' near on ever' night here of late, over money mostly, and I keep a-studyin' on it, tryin' to figure what all I done. I reckon I nagged him too much, and I never did keep house like his mama does, and I heared him on the phone actin' funny for weeks and didn't say nothin' about it, just let it lay...

Me: Mm-hm.

My friend:, and he used to could make me laugh over the least little thing, and somewhere along in there I quit tryin', and might could be that's when he quit too.

Me: Now you hush all that right this minute. Hit ain't a thing you done, you hear me? That man's been runnin' around with some woman over in the next holler since spring, ever'body but you heared tell of it, and he was too sorry and too sigogglin to ever say hit to your face 'fore he lit out. That's the whole of it, plain, and hit don't say one thing about what kind of a woman you are. You're near 'bout the best one I know, and I mean that.

My friend: I just don't know what I'm gonna do with myself in that house tonight.

Me: You ain't a-settin' in that house alone, shug, I'll not hear of hit. Grab your poke and come stay on my couch, or we'll load up and ride out a spell, get you clear away from amongst all his mess. Either way you ain't doin' this by yourself.

My friend: Lord. You sound plumb like my mama right now.

Me: Well, somebody's got to, shug.
