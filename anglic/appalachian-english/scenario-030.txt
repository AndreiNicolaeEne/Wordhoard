Me: You gonna give me Granny's stack cake recipe or take hit to your grave with you?

The aunt: Lord, don't say that. Set down here and get you some paper.

Me: I'm ready.

The aunt: Well now, first off you take you some flour, about four cup, maybe a right smart more if the weather's damp, hit soaks up different ever time.

Me: Four cups.

The aunt: And soda, don't forget the soda now, I like to left hit clean out just then. If you leave hit out them layers won't rise a lick, so a teaspoon of soda goes in with your flour, and your ginger too whilst you're at hit.

Me: Okay. Flour, soda, ginger.

The aunt: Cut your lard in with your fingers till hit's like cornmeal, then work in your sorghum and your buttermilk, just enough to hold hit together. Roll hit out thin as you can get hit, plumb thin, and bake your layers first, six or seven of em if you'uns are feeding a crowd.

Me: What about the apples?

The aunt: You stew them dried apples down whilst them layers is baking, low fire, and don't you never rush that part. I heared Granny say a body could ruin a whole cake just a-hurrying them apples. You let em set and cook down slow or they won't spread right when you go to stack.

Me: How do you know when they're ready?

The aunt: Hit don't take no clock. You know hit by the smell coming up out the pot, and the apples'll go dark and thick, hold together when you drag your spoon through. Layers is the same, they'll spring back when you touch em, no need to time hit.

Me: How much sorghum you put with the apples?

The aunt: Now that I ain't never measured. I just go by eye, however dark and sweet hit looks to be right, that's how much.

Me: Could I use plain milk instead of buttermilk?

The aunt: You might could, if you put a little vinegar in hit and let hit set a spell. But hit won't taste the same, I'll tell you that plain.
