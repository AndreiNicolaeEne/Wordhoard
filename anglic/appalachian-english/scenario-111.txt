Ten o'clock now, and the eldest ought to been in by seven. I said it right out loud, like the room might answer me back. Three hours. Three hours overdue and not a word out of nobody.

I gone and worked it on my fingers like a young'un would, seven to eight is one, eight to nine is two, nine to ten is three, and hit come out the same every time I done it. Three hours, plain as anything.

Probably got to jawing with somebody down at the store, I told the room, and that truck wouldn't start again neither, hit's done that before. I near about believed it my own self, saying it.

But I couldn't set still with it. Up and to the window, back to the door, a-listening for tires on the gravel, and nothing out there but the crickets a-hollering same as always. Done that four or five times before I caught myself at it and made myself set back down.

Lord, don't let it be a wreck on that road, I said, and hated myself the minute it was out my mouth, for saying such a thing plumb out loud, like hit could call the thing up just from naming it.

Put me in mind of the time we was leveling out same as this, waiting on the daddy, and it got on toward midnight before he come walking up that holler. The granny had said then, don't you fret none, this holler's swallowed grown men before and spit em back out come morning, tired but breathing. You'uns wasn't even borned yet, and I seed that same wait before, and it didn't comfort me then neither, not all the way.

I might could walk down as far as the bridge and look, I told myself, and then talked myself right back out of it, for what if I was gone from the house when they come in, and nobody here to open the door.

The clock on the fireboard said past four hour now. Right smart past.

Something moved out past the window, a shadow crossing the yard, and my heart come up in my throat before it was just the dog, barking at nothing the way he does.

Then I heared it. The truck, for real this time, gravel and the door and boots on the porch, and it was relief first, pure relief, flooding up in me before the mad even got its turn to speak.
