Lord, I'm plumb tore down, but hit's done now, so let me tell you'uns how the day went.

We was up at the fence line before the sun had cleared the ridge good, and my body weren't hardly ready for hit yet. Cold too, hitching up my galluses and my hands already stiff before we'd even started.

First job was digging post holes, and that set the pace for everything after. Ground up there is rock near two foot down, so we was leveling that auger against stone half the morning, a-fighting hit the whole while. My hands took the complaint first, blistered up quick off the bar, and then it was my back's turn once we got to setting them posts plumb.

We took us a short break round eleven, thirty minutes with a biscuit and some coffee out of a poke, and that weren't near enough, but hit was what we had time for.

Afternoon was the hard stretch. I seed that sun get up over the ridge hot and knowed we was in for it. Hauling rock up that bank for the underpinning, right smart of rock too, more than I'd figured on, and I got to thinking I might could just quit early and nobody the wiser for hit.

But the wire fence itself went up faster than I'd have guessed, clean and straight, not a bit sigogglin like the old one had stood. That was the one bright spot in a grinding day.

He owed me forty for hit and heared me holler when I called it done, paid square right there.

Tomorrow he says might could use me again, same kind of work, or I might just lay up and rest my back a day 'fore I go again. Either way, I'm fixing to set my boots up on the fireboard tonight and not move.
