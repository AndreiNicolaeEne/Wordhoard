Me: I ain't lying to you, hit happened just exactly like I'm fixing to tell it.
My friend: You was probably half asleep, wore plumb out from all that walking, and got to dreaming on your feet without knowing it.
Me: I was wide awake. I'm leveling with you, I was thinking on my own business clear as anything, same as I'm thinking right now, looking at you.
My friend: Alright then, go on and tell it.
Me: I was borned and raised in this holler, I know this ground good as my own hand. I was coming down past where the old church used to stand, right smart after dark, and I heared something a-hollering way back up in the laurel, didn't sound like no bobcat, no owl, nothing I ever heared in my life. Then I seed a light, low down, about the size of a lantern globe, a-moving through the brush sigogglin like, all crooked, where they ain't even a path cut through there.
My friend: That there's foxfire, plain and simple. Rotted wood glows of a damp night, everybody knows that.
Me: Foxfire don't move. Hit sets right there on the one log till kingdom come. This light kept right up with me the whole way. I sped up, hit sped up. I slowed down, hit slowed down too.
My friend: Well then it might could been some fool a-coon hunting, trying to scare you with his headlamp.
Me: Wasn't no dogs a-hollering with it, and you ever knowed a coon hunter to go off without his dogs the whole way? Hit crossed the crick right along with me too, not one splash, not one sound of water, and they ain't no bridge there, my friend, you know that good as I do.
My friend: They's always some explaining for a thing like that, if a body looked hard enough.
Me: I'll swear it on my granny's grave, every word I've told you is true. I was standing right there in my own two boots and I seen what I seen.
My friend: I still say you was dreaming it plumb up, wanting to believe it that bad.
Me: Believe what you want to then. I know what walked that ridge with me, and it wasn't nothing that ever belonged to this world.
