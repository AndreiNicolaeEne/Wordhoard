My friend: Now don't you leave nothing out. I heared it plain as day through that wall, the both of you a-hollering fit to wake the dead, and then not a peep out of neither one for the better part of two days. What in the world got into you'uns?

Me: Lord, I reckon you did hear it, didn't you. I'm liable to be embarrassed the rest of my life over it, but I'll tell it to you straight since you already got half of it for free.

My friend: Start from the front of it.

Me: Well hit wasn't nothing to start with, that's the awful part. He'd gone into town to sell a load of stove wood at the flea market of a Saturday, said he'd be back by supper, and supper set there getting cold plumb near two hours. I don't mind saying I get short when I've stood over a stove for nothing, and by the time I heared the truck door I was already worked up.

My friend: So you was the one commenced it.

Me: I was, I'll own it. He come in smelling like he'd stopped off somewheres, and I don't even recall exact what I said first, something ugly about him not caring was there food on the table or not. And he didn't just take it neither, he come right back at me, said I always got to make a mountain out of him being a hour late. Then it come out he'd took some of that wood money and bought hisself a used chainsaw off a fellow there, hadn't said a word to me about it beforehand, and that's when we was really a-fighting, not just a-fussing.

My friend: What'd you say that you didn't mean?

Me: Lord have mercy. I told him he didn't care if we all froze plumb to death this winter long as he had his toys to play with. Which hit weren't fair and I knowed it even while it was a-coming out of my mouth, he'd been out there all day trying to scrape together enough to cover the propane bill, that's half why he was late, dickering with folks for a better price. But when you're hollering you ain't studying on being fair, you're studying on winning.

My friend: Did he say anything back he oughtn't?

Me: He always could reach back for the one thing he knows'll land. Brought up my granny's quilts, how I'd sold three of em to that antique man over in the next county without asking him first, back when the doctor bills was piling up on us bad. I ain't never forgive myself over them quilts and he knows it, and he throwed it up at me right then just to have something to throw. Said I hadn't got no room to fuss at him over one chainsaw when I'd sold off my own granny's handiwork for pennies on the dollar without a word to him.

My friend: That's a sore one.

Me: Hit surely is. We don't neither one of us bring that up, ever, it's an understood thing to leave be, and he knowed exactly what he was doing when he said it.

My friend: What got broke?

Me: Nothing got broke exactly, but he went out slamming that side door so hard it come near off the top hinge, it's set sigogglin on there ever since, hanging crooked as a dog's hind leg. I like to jumped out of my skin when it hit. And I recall snatching his coat off the peg by the fireboard and pitching it out on the porch after him, which weren't necessary, but I was past caring by then.

My friend: And then?

Me: And then wasn't nothing. He stayed out to the barn a long while, and when he did come back in he didn't say two words to me, and I didn't say none back to him. We eat, if you want to call it eating, setting at the same table not looking at one another. That went on the whole rest of the evening and clean through the next day. He'd be out working on something in the barn and I'd be in the front room, and if we passed in the kitchen it was like passing a stranger. I might could've said something a dozen times over but I was too stubborn, and I reckon he was the same.

My friend: Who broke it first?

Me: He did. Come the second evening he set down at the fireboard where I was mending, and he didn't look at me, just commenced stirring up the fire, and he says, "It's fixing to come a hard frost tonight, you best bring that basil in off the porch before it's kilt." Wasn't nothing about us at all, just the weather, but I knowed what he was doing, he was easing his way back round to talking.

My friend: Then what.

Me: I brung the basil in, and when I come back through he says, "I hadn't ought to said nothing about them quilts. That's done and gone and you didn't have much choice that year with them bills stacking up, and it wasn't right of me to throw it at you like I did. I'm sorry for it." Plain as that, no dancing round it, said exactly what he was sorry for.

My friend: And you?

Me: I told him I was sorry too, for saying he didn't care about us none, cause I know good and well he cares, he was out there all day in the cold trying to get us through the winter and I flung it back at him like it wasn't nothing. Told him I was sorry for pitching his coat out on the porch and all.

My friend: Did things ease up after that?

Me: They did. He hadn't called me honey in I don't know how long, not since we was first courting near about, it'd gone to just plain old woman most days the way you get to talking after you been together a right smart while. But that night he says "you want some coffee, honey" like it wasn't nothing, and I heared it and I know he heared hisself say it too, and that was the first true sign of it, before either one of us said sorry twice over.

My friend: What sealed it, then?

Me: He took the screwdriver to that door the next morning before he even had his coffee, had them galluses hanging loose off his shoulders and everything, and got that hinge set true again so it swung right instead of sigogglin ever which way. Didn't say a word about it, just fixed it. And I knowed then we was square, better than any amount of talking could've told me.

My friend: Well I'll say this, you'uns give me a show through that wall I didn't ask for.

Me: I know it, and I'm sorry you had to hear all that hollering. But I reckon you got the whole of it now, fight and mending both, so you ain't got to wonder no more. We're right as rain since, better than we was before if the truth be told. Just don't go telling the rest of the holler you got front row seats to it.
