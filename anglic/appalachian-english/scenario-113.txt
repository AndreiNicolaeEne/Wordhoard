Well I got to tell you about Friday, since you wasn't there and you missed the whole mess of it start to finish.

Four of us set down at the table, right there on the sigogglin old card table my brother-in-law's daddy built, the one leg always was shorter than the rest and we chock it up with a folded matchbook same as ever. Me, my brother-in-law, the neighbor from up the holler, and his wife. We hashed out the stakes before anybody dealt a single card, quarters in and a nickel a raise, small enough that losing wasn't going to hurt nobody's feelings nor their poke either one.

First few hands went by ordinary as you please. Nobody clearly ahead, cards passing round the table even-steven, little bit of talk about the garden and the neighbor's fence line and whatnot. I mean hit was a plain night, nothing to write home about.

Then my brother-in-law's luck turned. He taken a hand, and then another, and then two more right on top of it, four hands running he was a-winning, and along about the third one you could feel the table getting quiet in that particular way. His wife laughed it off first, said he was born lucky, but by the fourth hand even she wasn't laughing the same.

The neighbor set his cards down easy and said, kindly quiet like he didn't want to make nothing of it...

The neighbor: That's an awful clean shuffle you got there.

Nobody said much to that. My brother-in-law just grinned and said he used to could shuffle blind back when he ran cards for the fellows at the mill, and everybody let hit go. Laughed it off. Dealt again.

But then it come his deal a second time, and I was watching his hands, wasn't even meaning to, just where my eye landed, and something about how them cards was a-leaving off the bottom of the deck instead of the top caught me square. Not fast, not slick like in a picture show, just wrong somehow, like his thumb knowed something his face didn't.

This time didn't nobody brush hit off.

The neighbor: You're dealing off the bottom. I seen hit plain as day.

Flat out said hit, named him right there at his own kitchen table. And my brother-in-law's face didn't so much as twitch.

The brother-in-law: I never done no such a thing.

Me: I got to say, I seen something funny in it too.

The brother-in-law: You'uns can say what you want. I dealt them cards fair and I ain't taking that back.

No give in him at all, not a inch. His wife come in on his side quick, said she'd watched him deal cards her whole married life and he wasn't never light-fingered about nothing, and the neighbor's wife come back with how she didn't like the look of it neither, and before long the whole table had done split down the middle, two and two, and we was going back over hands already played and done, trying to reckon whether such-and-such a hand had went crooked too, and hit settled nothern. You can't unplay a hand. You can just set there and argue about hit, which is exactly what we done for a good little while, going round and round, hit don't matter how many times you lay the same three cards back out on the table and study on them, they don't confess to nothing.

We kept on playing anyhow. Wadn't like anybody was fixing to get up and go home over a nickel a raise. But I'll tell you, the ease was gone plumb out of that room. Where before there'd been talk and laughing between hands, now everybody dealt quiet and watched everybody else's hands close, even mine, even though wasn't nobody accusing me of nothing. That's just how hit goes once the accusing starts, everybody gets to watching everybody.

We played on out to eleven hands total for the night, which is about where we generally stop, on account of the neighbor's wife has got to be up early for her hens. Last hand went to the neighbor's wife herself, if you can believe hit, she come in with three of a kind right at the end and swept the pot, and there was a little bit of a laugh over that, first real laugh since the trouble started, on account of hit wasn't him that took it.

But when we tallied up who was ahead and who was down for the whole night, my brother-in-law was still setting pretty, up two hands over anybody else at that table, even with that last one going against him. Two hands is two hands. Don't sound like right smart, and in money hit wadn't nothing, few dollars total, but in the telling of it hit was everything, on account of now there's a number to point at.

And that's about the size of it. We finished out, put the cards up, everybody said their goodnights civil enough, but hit ain't laid down since. Every single time that same four of us sets down to play, and we still do, near about every couple weeks, ain't nobody quit coming, hit comes back up, somebody'll say something about a clean shuffle or watch the deal a little too close, and my brother-in-law will say the same thing he said that night, that he never done no such a thing, flat as a fireboard, and don't nobody ever get no further with hit than we did that first night. Might could be he did do hit. Might could be he didn't and just had him a run of luck same as anybody gets once in a while. I don't reckon we'll ever know for certain, and I don't reckon that's going to stop none of us from setting back down at that same sigogglin table next time and dealing hit all out again.
