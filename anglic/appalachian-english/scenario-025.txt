My husband: Well. That went on a good while.

Me: It weren't that bad.

My husband: Forty-five minutes at the door, and her still a-hollerin down the steps after us about did we want the rest of the beans.

Me: She just don't like to see us go, is all.

My husband: And that cornbread comment. Right in front of ever'body, sayin hers turns out better'n yours ever has.

Me: She's said that my whole life. Don't mean nothin by it no more.

My husband: Hit meant somethin to me. I was settin right there watchin your face.

Me: ...I reckon it did sting some. I been makin it her way for years and she still says it like I never learned.

My husband: See, now you're sayin it too.

Me: I ain't complainin, I'm agreein.

My husband: And your daddy. That truck-in-the-crick story again. Third time this year he's told it, word for word, right down to the dog a-runnin off up the holler.

Me: He tells it like we ain't heared it. Like we ain't seed that truck pulled out ourselves.

My husband: Might could tell him we know how it ends.

Me: He'd just tell it again next time regardless. He's plumb set on that story.

My husband: And forty-five minutes. Us standin there in the cold, her still a-talkin, your daddy out on the porch with his galluses hangin loose, hollerin one more thing to remember.

Me: I used to could stand it better'n I do now.

My husband: Right smart of complainin for a woman said it weren't that bad.

Me: Well. It weren't, till you got me started.

My husband: I didn't have to work hard.

Me: No. You surely didn't.
