The cousin: Hey, it's me. Mama said you was feeling poorly and I like to worried myself sick over it. How bad is it, really, don't sugarcoat it none.

The sick one: Aw, it ain't killing me. Started up three days back, just a ache in my joints and my throat all scratchy like I'd swallowed gravel. Then yesterd'y a fever come on top of it, and I felt plumb wore out, a-hurting all over. Today's a right smart easier than yesterd'y was, but I ain't gonna lie, I'm still far from right.

The cousin: Lord have mercy. What all you been doing for it?

The sick one: Well, first thing I done was gargle some warm salt water for my throat, three, four times a day. Fever wouldn't break on its own so I laid a cool rag 'crost my forehead of a night, kept changing it out ever hour or so, wringing it out fresh. Been keeping to broth and weak tea, nothing heavier, and sleeping whenever my body'd let me, which weren't much the first night, I was a-tossing and a-turning something awful.

The cousin: You need to rest proper, you hear me? Don't you go pushing through this like you always do. Hit don't matter what all needs doing round the house, it can wait.

The sick one: I hear you. I done set my own mind on it anyhow, if this fever climbs up any higher, or my breathing gets to where it's hard, I'm going in to be seen. Not before that though. I ain't paying good money to set in some waiting room for what a rag and some broth'll cure.

The cousin: You want me to bring you something over? Soup, or just come set with you a spell, hour or so?

The sick one: You don't need to be doing all that, you got your own place to tend to. But... I reckon I wouldn't turn you away neither, if you got the time.
