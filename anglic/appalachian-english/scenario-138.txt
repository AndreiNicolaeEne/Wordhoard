The neighbor: ...and they said he was drunk as a biler when he run that truck off Rich Mountain, hit was so bad they had to pull the whole thing out with two log chains, folks a-hollering up and down the holler about it half the night, and the frame left sigogglin like a fireboard somebody took a axe to...

The uncle: Now hold on right there. That ain't what happened. Not even close. I was down that bank myself, first one to him. That boy hadn't touched a drop of nothing, hit was his brakes went out on him coming off the ridge, plumb gone, and he rode her down best he could till she caught that stob and turned. He might could have been killed over it, was banged up right smart, but drunk he never was, not a lick of it in him.

The neighbor: Well I'll be. That's a far cry from what got told to me.

The uncle: Hit's traveled a good ways by the time it got here. Started as a boy that near about died and by the time it come round to you he's out drunk-driving off a mountain of a Saturday night. That's plumb backwards from the truth of it, and it ain't fair to the boy neither.

The neighbor: Where'd you even, where'd I hear it at, I mean to say. I believe it was told to me over at the store, whoever was keeping it that evening.

The uncle: And he heared it off whoever was up at the wreck and couldn't leave it be, had to season it some for the telling. Goes on down the line like a poke with a hole tore in the bottom, spills a little more out of it every hand it passes through.

The neighbor: I reckon it does.

The uncle: Well now you know it proper. Was his brakes, plain and simple, and the boy nearly lost his life over it. Tell it that way from here on, whoever asks you. He don't need the other hung on him on top of what he's already been through.
