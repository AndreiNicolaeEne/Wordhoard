Alright now, set down here a minute and let me tell you this whole thing proper, on account of you wasn't there for a lick of it and it don't do to hear it in pieces from ever'body else, they'll all tell it a little different by now anyhow.

Hit started over checkers. Just checkers, up at the cookout at my wife's daddy's place, down in the holler where the creek bends round under that big sycamore. We'd et already and was just a-setting round on the tailgate and the picnic table waitin on it to cool off some, and my brother-in-law drug out that old checkerboard his mama's had since before he was borned, and somebody said set it up, and it went from there.

Now me and him have played that board a hunnerd times if we've played it once, and I used to could take him two outta three without hardly tryin, so when he wanted to play I laughed at him some, just funnin, and I said something like, well, what're we playin for then, and I never meant nothin by it. It was a joke. That's the whole pitiful truth of it, hit was a joke that growed a life of its own before either one of us meant it to.

The brother-in-law: You talk mighty big for a man about to lose his shirt.

Me: Shoot, I'll play you for a poke of them boiled peanuts and call it done.

Well that's when the rest of 'em got ahold of it. His sister was a-standing right there and she hollered out make it worth somethin, and one of the young'uns piped up and said make it twenty, like twenty dollars was the biggest sum a body ever heared tell of, and ever'body laughed and it just stuck. Twenty dollars. Small enough it wasn't gone sting nobody bad, but enough that a man had to mean it now. We shook on it right there before a single checker got moved, twenty dollars, plain and flat, and the whole crowd was a-hollering about it like it was a prizefight.

Game itself went on a good while. Back and forth, back and forth, him jumpin two of mine, me jumpin back, neither one of us clear ahead of the other for the longest time, and the crowd standin round the table gettin louder ever' time somebody made a move, like they had money on it theirselves, which some of 'em I reckon did by then, side bets and all. I was settin there thinkin I had him, I really was, right up until I seed it. You know that feelin when the whole board just turns on ye all at once? Hit was one move, just one, and I watched it happen like watchin a truck slide on ice, cain't do nothin about it but watch. He had a double jump comin three moves out and I never seen it till it was too late to head it off. Lost before the game was even done playin itself out, and we both knowed it, him grinnin like a possum and me just settin there.

When the last piece come off the board I groaned loud enough they heared it up at the house I bet, and I said hit right out, wasn't no use pretendin.

Me: Alright, alright, you got me fair and square, I owe ye the twenty.

And that's where it got strange, on account of he didn't want the twenty. His sister spoke up first.

The sister: Twenty dollars ain't no fun for nobody but him. He's gotta earn it.

And the whole bunch of 'em just latched onto that like a dog on a bone, right there at the table, deciding amongst theirselves what I was gonna owe instead of money, and they didn't leave one bit of it vague neither. I had to sing the whole thing, ever' verse and the chorus too, not just hum through it, standin up on top of the picnic table where ever'body could see me plain, wearin the aunt's Sunday church hat with the big silk flower on it and a pair of the young'un's rubber boots, and I had to dance whilst I sung it, not just stand there like a fence post. Somebody said galluses too, so I had to wear a pair of his daddy's old galluses over my bare chest for good measure, on account of it'd look funnier that way, they said. I tried to bargain my way outta it right then.

Me: Come on now, just let me pay the twenty and be done with it.

The brother-in-law: Not a chance. You're doin the show.

They wouldn't hear a word otherwise, just laughed harder the more I tried, so I knowed I was stuck. We set a day for it, the next time the whole family was gonna be together again, up at that same place, and a Saturday come round for it proper, and by then it'd got round to near about ever'body, cousins and all, so there was a right smart crowd there waitin on it when the day come.

That old picnic table's sigogglin anyhow, always has been, one leg shorter than the rest since it was built, and it rocks somethin awful if you get much weight to movin on it, which nobody thought about till I was up there.

So there I stood on top of it in the aunt's church hat and them little rubber boots and his daddy's galluses hangin loose off my shoulders, and I sung "Rocky Top" straight through, ever' verse, whilst tryin to dance a jig on a table that was a-rockin under me the whole time like a boat in high water. I was hollerin the words more than singin 'em by the second verse, and the crowd was a-laughing and a-hollering right along, but the loudest of it, the part that still gets brought up ever' single time, was when that table rocked hard on one side and one of them rubber boots flew clean off my foot mid-jig and sailed off the table and landed square down in the middle of the potato salad bowl, splash, right in front of ever'body, and I never even stopped singin. Didn't miss a word of it, just kept a-hollering out that song on one boot with my bare foot slappin the table, and that's when the whole holler about come apart laughin. His sister said she like to have fell outta her chair. The aunt didn't even care about the hat no more, she was laughin too hard to fuss over it.

And that's how it ended, twenty dollars never did change hands, not one cent of it, and that potato salad went in the trash but nobody minded on account of we was all laughin too hard to eat anyhow.

Ever since then, whenever the whole bunch gets together, somebody brings it up again sooner or later, and it don't never come out the same way twice. Ever' telling the table gets more sigogglin than it really was, the boot flies further, my singin gets worse, and here lately somebody's took to sayin there was gravy in that bowl too, which there wasn't, but I don't fight it no more. Some things just keep on growin their own life ever' time they're told, same as that bet did in the first place.
