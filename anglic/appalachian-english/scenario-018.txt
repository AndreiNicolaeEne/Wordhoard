Me: Well, since you'uns are all a-hollering for it, and my partner over there's just a-grinning and won't say a word, I reckon it falls to me to tell it right, start to finish, so don't nobody go correcting me halfway through.

It didn't start out no big to-do. Wasn't no setup, wasn't no blind date somebody's aunt cooked up. I was working the counter at the feed store, weekdays mostly, and my partner come in on a Tuesday for a poke of scratch feed on account of some baby chicks that had just hatched out. Ordinary as that. I remember it was raining a little, not hard, just enough to make the tin roof talk. And I recall exactly what she said when I went to carry that poke out to the truck for her, on account of it was heavy and she'd already got mud plumb up to her knees. She looked at me kindly sideways and said, "You tote feed for every stranger that walks in here, or just the ones whose truck looks like it won't start?" And sure enough that truck wouldn't start. Had to get my daddy's jumper cables off the shelf and do it right there in the gravel lot. I've thought on that line a hundred times since. Still don't rightly know how she knew that truck was fixing to give out before it did.

Now I'll tell you plain, it wasn't me that was hesitant, it was her. She'd heared already, before we ever properly spoke, that I had a young'un, my little girl, from before, and she was scared to get in the middle of that. Scared she couldn't love a child that wasn't borned to her, scared she'd do it wrong, and I don't fault her one bit for thinking on it hard instead of jumping in blind. That's the kind of thing worth being careful over. What wore it off was seeing her with my girl at the church dinner on the grounds that fall. My girl spilled a whole plate of banana puddin down the front of herself, commenced to bawling like the world had ended, and my partner just knelt right down in the grass in her good dress and cleaned her up and told her it wasn't nothing a rag and a laugh couldn't fix. I seed that and I knowed. And I believe she knowed something too, watching herself do it.

The first time we properly spent time together, not just passing at the feed store, was a gospel singing over at the church up the holler from her mama's place. I asked would she ride with me instead of taking her own truck, and she said she might could, if her mama didn't need her for nothing. Well, that was the trouble right there. Her mama did not care for the notion, not at first. Thought I was too much trouble for a young woman to take on, what with a child already and working two jobs to boot. It wasn't hateful, her mama wasn't ugly about it, she just went quiet and cool whenever my name come up, and that's its own kind of cold. It took a right smart while to wear off. What did it, honestly, was time and patience and me showing up regular, at Sunday dinner, at hog killing, at whatever needed another set of hands, and never getting short with her mama no matter how she tested me. Eventually her mama seed I wasn't going nowhere and quit fighting it.

Now some of you already know the joke, so I'll just say the words and you'uns can laugh where you're used to laughing. The porch step. That first evening I come up to get her for the singing, her front porch step was so sigogglin it near about pitched me off into the yard, and I hollered loud enough her mama come running thinking somebody'd been shot. Her daddy never did fix that step the whole time we was courting, and to this day, that man is up in years and that step is still sigogglin, and every single time I go up there I say the same thing and everybody rolls their eyes. Some jokes don't need explaining. That's one of them.

The moment I knowed it had gone serious was the week she started leaving her good boots by my door instead of carrying them back and forth. Didn't say nothing about it, just left them there like they'd always been there. And how she found out I'd caught on? Her mama told her. Her mama had commenced to calling me "the one that's staying" instead of using my name at all, and my partner heared that from her own mama before I ever worked up the nerve to say it myself.

The asking itself wasn't no big show. Wasn't no ring hid in a cake or nothing like you see on the television. We was up on the ridge behind her daddy's place, where you can see clean down into the holler when the leaves are off, and I just asked her plain, standing right there in my work boots with mud on them same as always. She said she'd been wondering what was taking me so long. That was the whole of it. It was about a year, near enough, between that day and the wedding. Her mama, once she come around, come around all the way and near about took over the planning of it. My own kin took to her from the first day, didn't need no wearing down at all.

The thing I still see, the thing no photograph got, was during the vows a hard little rain come up out of nowhere, quick and warm, the kind that don't last but a few minutes, and not one soul moved off that porch. Nobody run for cover. The preacher just talked a little louder over it and kept right on, and by the time we was pronounced it had done quit and the sun come back out like hit had never happened at all. Her granddaddy said after that a wedding rain is good luck, and I don't know if that's true or just something old folks say, but I've chose to believe it.

We've had our hard stretch same as anybody does, and I won't dwell on it at this table, but there was a spell a few years back when work went bad for both of us the same year, and money got right smart tight, and tempers got short more than once. What carried us through it was mostly stubbornness, hers more than mine if I'm honest, and this family right here that never once let us go without, whether that was a truck to borrow or a poke of groceries left on the porch with nobody claiming credit for it.

What's stayed exactly the same since that first rainy Tuesday at the feed store is this: she still looks at me sideways like she's working something out, and I still can't never tell what that truck of hers is fixing to do next. So if you'uns will lift your glasses with me, here's to her, and to all of you that carried us this far, and here's to whatever comes next, however sigogglin the road up to it might be.
