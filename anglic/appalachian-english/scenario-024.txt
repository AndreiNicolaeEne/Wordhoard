Bride: We got to git this list cut down some, hit's over by nigh on thirty and that place up the holler won't seat one more chair than what it'll seat.

Groom: Well start with your mama's cousins we ain't seed since the funeral, and them fellers off the dock I work with, I don't hardly talk to half of 'em no way. Trim all them out and that's fifteen right there, might could get us more'n halfway.

Bride: You can cut ever last one of 'em you want to, but you ain't touchin her name off there. She's stayin on that list if I got to give up my own seat to do it.

Groom: Well if she's stayin, somebody's got to come off your side then. Hit ain't right, your people always gets more chairs than mine has, been that way since we first started this whole mess of plannin.

Bride: Here you go again with that old argument. My side ain't the one a-hollerin for thirty extra folks to be fed.

Groom: What if we was to have us a little gatherin after, out at the house, for the ones that don't fit in yonder? Coffee and cake, nothin fancy, poke of nuts on the table.

Bride: No sir. I ain't havin two weddins and two lists of hurt feelins on top of it. That notion's dead right there, don't bring it back up on me.

Groom: Fine, fine. Let's just count it again.

Bride: One, two... still a dozen over, near about.

Groom: I reckon I could let two of them dock fellers go. That's all I'm givin up tonight, though.

Bride: Twelve down to ten ain't but a start. Lord, we'll be plumb wore out fore this list ever comes out even.

Groom: Might could be. But we ain't done arguin it yet, and we ain't done cuttin neither.
