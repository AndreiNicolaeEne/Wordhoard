Well, I'll tell you, this was the best harvest anybody left alive around here can call to mind, and I don't mean just me a-saying it, the whole holler agreed on it. Right from the first pulling you could tell hit was fixing to beat everything we'd ever put up, and time we got done we'd took in nigh on twice what we generally do in a good year, no lie about it.

Now the tomatoes, they was the one that just outdone theirself. We usually get a right smart mess off them vines, enough to can up and share around, but this year they come in so heavy the stakes was leaning sigogglin under the weight of it, and we like to never got done picking before they turned on the vine. My uncle's the one keeps count from year to year in that little book of his, and he said the last time we come anywhere close was away back, must have been going on fifteen year prior, and even that one didn't touch this here, not by half.

We had to call in extra hands off up the road just to get it all in before it spoiled on us, my cousin and two of the neighbor boys come a-hollering and a-hoeing from sunup, galluses and all, and we was leveling them baskets full from dawn plumb to dark. Whatever we didn't need ourselves we put up in pokes and jars for the cellar, till even the fireboard had jars lined up on it, and the rest my daddy hauled down and traded off for what we was needing through winter.

The oldest grower down here has seed more summers than anybody left, and he set this year against every one he could call back.

Oldest grower: I have not seed the like of it in all my born days. Hit was the rain, coming right when we needed it and not a drop wasted, and a mild spell clean through that never let up on us. We might could get close again sometime, but I doubt we'll ever beat it.

They still talk on it today. Any year that comes up short, somebody'll say, well hit weren't nothing like that year, and everybody knows just which one they mean.
