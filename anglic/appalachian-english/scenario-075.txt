Me: Well I'll be dogged. Is that you standing there?

The old friend: Lord have mercy, it is! I like to walked right past you, I did not look for you here of all places.

Me: I never in this world thought I'd run up on you at a place like this. How long has it been, ten year? More'n ten, I bet.

The old friend: More'n ten easy. Got to be twelve, thirteen year since we was in school together. Time gets away from a body plumb before you know it's went.

Me: It surely does. What all you been doing with yourself?

The old friend: Working over at the plant, third shift mostly. Got married, that didn't take, so it's just me and the young'un now up in the holler where my people's always been. What about you?

Me: Married a woman from past the ridge, we got two young'uns of our own. I'm at the sawmill now, used to could do construction but my back give out on that.

The old friend: Say, you remember that time we was a-fishing down below the falls and you fell in right up to your galluses?

Me: Lord, don't. I heared about that for a year after, everybody in the holler a-hollering about it.

The old friend: I like to died laughing right there on the bank.

Me: Say, whatever come of that old friend of ours, the one always a-hollering and carrying on? Ain't heared a word from him in years.

The old friend: Not me neither. Somebody said he might could be down around Knoxville but nobody rightly knows.

Me: Well look here, give me your number this time. I mean to call, not like last time.

The old friend: I promise you I will answer if you do.
