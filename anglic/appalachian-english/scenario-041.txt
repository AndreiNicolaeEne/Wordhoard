I studied on quittin that job a right smart while before I ever done it. Two year, near about, I put in there, turnin them rental cabins up and down the ridge for folks that come through in June and again in October thinkin the mountains was theirs to have for a week at a time. Two year of strippin beds and haulin poke after poke of trash off decks so sigogglin you'd swear a good wind was fixin to finish what the builder started wrong. I stayed on account the money come regular and the boss, he wasn't mean, not outright. He was just the kind would smile in your face of a mornin and then pile your list twice as long as daylight allowed for.

What finally done it, what pushed me clean over, wasn't nothin big. That's the way of it, hit never is. Wasn't the pay, though the pay was low as a snake's belly. Wasn't him a-hollerin over that little radio he give us, "you'uns got two more units to turn before four," like we'd been standin still all mornin when we'd been a-scrubbin since seven with nary a break. No. What done it was a cooler somebody's guests left settin on the deck a whole week in July, forgot and rottin, and when I opened the lid on it the smell like to knocked me down the steps. I hollered up to the office was somebody comin to help me with it and the answer come back, plain as anything, that it was on my list same as the rest. I stood there with that mess crawlin in front of me and I thought, well. That's about enough of that.

I rehearsed what I meant to say for near on a week. Said it to the bathroom mirror, said it drivin to work with the radio off, said it layin awake longside my husband who'd just grunt and say go on and do it then if you're goin to. I wanted it to come out smooth, not like I was throwin a fit, not like two year of bein run ragged had got the better of me even though it had. I wanted to sound like a grown woman handin in her notice, not somebody stompin off.

I went in and asked could I speak with him a minute when he wasn't busy, and he said sure, come on back, thinkin I reckon it was about the schedule or some such. I set down across that desk from him with my hands in my lap so he wouldn't see how they was shakin, and I said my piece, careful as I'd practiced hit.

Me: I appreciate you havin me on, I surely do, and I want you to know this ain't about any one thing in particular. I've just got some things in my own life I need to tend to.

That was a lie and it wasn't. Weren't about any one thing, that part was true, hit was about two year of things stacked up. But it was about that cooler too, if I'm honest, and about him hollerin over the radio, and about him never once sayin thank you unless a guest complained and then he'd holler that too.

What I didn't say, what I held back right then settin across from him, was the real reason under all of it. Not the workload. What I couldn't bring myself to say was that I didn't respect him no more, hadn't for a good while, and there weren't no way to say that and still walk out with a decent word to put on my next application. A body's got to eat. You don't burn a bridge you might could need to cross again, my mother always said, and she was right about that even when it galls you to admit it.

He was surprised, or he done a fair job actin like it. Set back in his chair and said well, he hated to hear it, was there somethin he could do. Said he could bump my pay some, maybe knock a cabin or two off my list on the heavy days, said he thought I done good work and he'd hate to lose me. And I'll grant you, some part of me wanted to take it, because two year is a long time to build up a routine and the money he offered would've helped plenty. But I told him no, I appreciated it, I'd already give my word elsewhere and I needed to see it through. That was the polite reason. The true one was I didn't believe he'd change a thing past the first month, and even if the money changed the way he talked to us never would.

We settled on two weeks, same as anybody does it right, and I walked out to my car feelin like I'd done somethin brave. That feelin lasted about as far as the end of the driveway. By that evenin I was layin awake again, only this time worryin had I gone and done somethin foolish, quittin steady work over a cooler and some hollerin, when times is hard and jobs up here ain't exactly fallin off the trees. Plumb near talked myself into goin back in the mornin and askin could I take it back. Didn't, but I thought on it hard.

Them two weeks went by regular as any other, cleanin same as always, only every day felt like it was already halfway to bein a memory. The last day, he shook my hand at the door and said he wished me well, said if things didn't work out where I was goin, come on back, there'd be a spot. Hollow as a dead tree, that was, and we both knowed it, but you say them things anyhow, it's just what's done. My workmate, the one I'd trained in the year before, she hugged my neck out in the parking lot and for a second there she got right honest with me.

Her workmate: I don't blame you a bit. I'd go too if I had somewheres to go.

That was the one true thing said all day, and it near broke me more than anything the boss said in two weeks of goodbyes, because I knowed she meant it and I knowed she was stuck.

It's been the better part of a year now. I'll tell you true, the relief has outweighed the regret by a right smart margin. I sleep better, I ain't dreadin that radio no more, and I don't carry that tight feelin in my chest drivin up the mountain of a mornin like I used to. There's days I miss the easy parts, the quiet cabins before the guests come, the view off some of them decks that'd take your breath. But if you was to set me down and ask would I go back and say it different, say the true thing instead of the polished thing, I don't know as I would. I reckon there's a kindness in leavin a man his dignity even when he ain't earned yours. I got out clean, I got my reference, and I got to keep the real reason for myself, which is more than most folks manage when they walk out a door for good.
