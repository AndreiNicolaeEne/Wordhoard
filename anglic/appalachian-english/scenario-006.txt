The eldest: Now listen here afore the rest of them comes down off the holler, I'm a-fixing to learn you this right so you don't go messing it up in front of everbody.

The little one: I ain't gonna mess it up.

The eldest: You might could, first time out, and that's fine, hit don't matter none. Now you got to have four at the least to even start a game, five or six is better, more'n that and it gets plumb foolish, cain't nobody keep track. The line runs from the big rock yonder by the fireboard-colored stump clean over to the fence post, and t'other side goes from the porch steps down to where the ditch starts. That's your boundary, all the way round.

The little one: And if I get runned outside of it?

The eldest: Then you're safe, but you cain't come back in till you're tagged or till the chaser calls you dead, one. Most of us just play it you got to stay in, keeps it fair.

The little one: How do we pick who chases first?

The eldest: We used to could just holler "not it" and last one hollering was it, but now we do fists on three, whoever throws different gets to be it first round. Now a tag ain't no shove, hit's just a touch, two fingers is plenty, right smart of you'uns try to near knock a body down and that don't count for nothing.

The little one: What if I get touched?

The eldest: You freeze right where you're at, don't move a lick, like you was a statue. Somebody still running has to come reach you and slap your hand loose afore you can go again. Round's over when everbody's been froze at once, and then whoever got tagged last is it next.

The little one: Am I gonna do it wrong?

The eldest: Won't matter none this first round, I ain't counting nothing against you. Come on now, they're a-hollering for us already.
