Me: Come set with me a minute, love, don't say nothing, just let me talk it out, I been needing to say it to somebody who'd understand and not think I'm going on.

Them first days, I don't hardly remember 'em straight, and that's the God's truth of it. Hit was people a-coming and a-going the whole time, casseroles stacked up on the counter till they was falling off, folks I ain't seen in ten year showing up in the yard, and every one of 'em needing something from me, a hug or a story or just for me to say I was doing alright. There wasn't hardly a minute to feel nothing direct. You're too busy signing papers and picking out what he's to be buried in and telling the preacher what to say, and underneath it all hit ain't hit you yet, not really, because there ain't no room for it to land.

Then everbody goes on home. That's the part nobody tells you about. The casseroles quit coming, the phone quits ringing near so much, and one evening you're standing in that house by yourself and hit's quiet, quiet like hit's never been quiet before, and that silence comes down on you like the roof itself dropped a foot. I stood right there in the kitchen and couldn't move, couldn't hardly breathe, because there wasn't nothing left to distract me from what was actual true.

My body just quit on me for a good while after that. I'd go two, three days and never think about food, not because I was trying not to eat, hit just never crossed my mind hit was time to. And sleep, when hit come, come in little pieces, an hour here, forty minutes there, wide awake at three in the morning staring at the fireboard like hit was going to tell me something. Never a whole night. I got to where I forgot what a full night's sleep even felt like.

That first month was the strangest of all of it, because the world just kept right on a-going. Folks back at their jobs, kids back in school, the mail still coming same as ever, and I couldn't figure how everthing outside kept moving when everthing inside me had stopped dead still. I wasn't nowhere near ready for the world to move on and hit didn't ask my permission.

Weeks in, I got blindsided by a Tuesday. Wasn't nothing marked on it, wasn't his birthday nor nothing like that, just an ordinary Tuesday, and I don't even know what set it off, but hit undone me completely, right there in the middle of the grocery store, had to leave my buggy sitting in the aisle and go set in the truck a good while before I could drive.

And months on from that, hit'd still come out of nowhere. Somebody'd walk by wearing the same aftershave, or I'd find a poke of his in the closet with something in it, or I'd see a pair of galluses hanging on a nail same as his used to, and a wave'd hit me square, no warning, no pattern to it at all. You never know what's fixing to set it off till hit already has.

The middle stretch, though, that's the part that near got me, because can't nobody warn you proper about the fog of it. Days that ought to been good days, a nice supper, somebody's birthday, a pretty morning up the holler, and hit was all just gray through it, dulled down like looking at the world through a dirty window. I wasn't sad exactly, most days, just far off from everthing, going through it without being in it.

Somebody said something to me right around then that cut deeper than they meant it to, I reckon, something about how I ought to be doing better by now, how much time had gone by already. Didn't mean no harm by it most likely, but hit stung something awful, stung worse than a thing that mean ought to. And right alongside that, I got to say, my neighbor woman, she never made a big to-do of nothing, just showed up one evening with a pot of soup and set with me on the porch a spell and didn't say hardly a word, just set there. That done more for me than all the planned-out sympathy in the county put together.

Then the anniversary come up on me. I dreaded it for weeks beforehand, plumb dreaded it, couldn't hardly think past it, had it marked in my head like a storm a-coming. And when the day finally got here, I got through it. Didn't do it graceful, didn't do it right, cried through half of it and burnt the supper I was trying to fix and forgot to call my sister back, but I got through it, and that was the whole point of the thing. Getting through don't have to look like nothing in particular.

Appetite come back slow, and uneven, same as the sleep did. Some weeks I'd eat regular three times a day like a person's supposed to, then I'd lose it again for no reason I could name. Sleep the same way, a good stretch of nights then back to them broke-up hours staring at the ceiling. Never come back all at once, more like hit leaked back in a little at a time.

I remember the day I caught myself a-laughing, really laughing, not the polite kind you do to be sociable, but a real laugh that come up out of me before I could stop it. And right behind it, quick as anything, come the guilt, like I wasn't allowed, like laughing meant I'd forgot him or didn't care no more. Took me a while to make my peace with that, that laughing don't undo the missing him.

By the time the year had run out, I was carrying it different than I started. Lighter in some places, I won't lie to you, some of the weight had shifted where I could stand up under it better. But hit ain't gone, and I don't believe hit's ever going to be gone, not all the way, and I quit expecting it to be.

So I'll tell you plain, love, same as I wish somebody'd told me plain: hit don't stay this hard forever. Might could take you a year, might could take you longer, ain't no fixed line for when hit eases up, and there ain't no sense you a-measuring your year against nobody else's, not your mama's, not your workmate's, and sure not mine neither. You just go on and grieve him the way you grieve him, in your own time, and don't let nobody, including your own self, tell you you're behind.
