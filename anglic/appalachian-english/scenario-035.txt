Me: That biscuit stand up by the ballfield's the best in this whole holler, and don't nobody need to tell me no different.

My friend: You're plumb wrong, and you know it too. Ain't nothing up this mountain touches the fry-pie stall down past the church, not by a country mile.

Me: Fry-pie stall? Lord, I got a pie there once hard as a fireboard, cold in the middle, and the woman didn't even look up from her phone whilst she handed it to me in a poke like she was ashamed of it.

My friend: One bad pie don't unmake a hundred good ones. You just caught her on a bad day, is all.

Me: A bad day? I heared the same complaint from three other folks besides me. That building's sigogglin as an old barn, leaning every which way, and so is her cooking.

My friend: Well your biscuit man took my last five dollars and give me back a biscuit tough as a boot heel. I like to broke my jaw a-chewin it.

Me: Now you're a-lying. Everybody knows them biscuits is light as a cloud, hit's a fact.

My friend: Might could be light for you. Wasn't light for me, and I ain't leveling with you about it neither, I mean every word.

Me: I'm telling you, right smart of folks drive down from three counties over just for them biscuits of a Saturday morning.

My friend: And I'm telling you that fry-pie's been made by that same family since before you or me was borned, and hit ain't changed a lick.

Me: Well what about the corn-dog stand down by the creek? Now there's one you'uns and me can't neither one call bad.

My friend: Hit's true. I ain't never heared a soul bad-mouth that place, not one time.

Me: Then let's settle it proper. We'll go back to both, mine and yourn, and eat till we can't hardly stand up, and see who's still wrong come Sunday.

My friend: Fine by me. I ain't scared of being right twice over.
