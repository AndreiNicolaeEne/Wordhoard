Set down here a minute, I got to tell somebody or I'll bust. Hit was that four-wheeler, is what hit was about, though he'll tell you different if you ask him. The propane bill had been setting past due since before Easter and I had done told him plain, don't you spend one red cent till that's paid off, we can't be a-fighting the cold and the gas man both. Well he come home a-grinning like a possum, and I seed it in his face before he ever opened his mouth. I says, where'd that thing come from, and he owned up he'd give a fellow from up the church four hundred dollars cash for hit. The whole trade was sigogglin from the get-go, buyer's remorse wrote all over him, and still he wouldn't back down.

That's when I brought up his brother's truck note, how he cosigned on it three year back and it went bad on us and cost us a right smart of money we never did see again. Had nothing to do with the four-wheeler, I know it, but out it come anyhow.

He said the sharpest thing, not me. He looked at me and said, "You act like I ain't never done a lick right in my life." Set me back on my heels, hit did.

We didn't speak two whole days. Ate supper across from each other like strangers, both of us a-picking at our plates and looking at the fireboard instead of each other.

He backed down first. Third morning he had the bill paid and said he might could sell the four-wheeler back if it come to it. I told him keep it, we'd manage.

My verdict, and I'll say it to you plain: he was wrong to spend it behind my back, but I was wrong to drag up that old truck mess that wasn't his fault to begin with. We was both hurting and looking for something to hurl.
