The aunt: Well I was up to the holler yesterday and I seed his mama at the store, and she was a-telling me how good he's doing over there at the mill, steady work, ain't missed nary a day since he started. And Lord, the temperament on that boy, gentle as they come, I ain't never heared him raise his voice at nobody, not once. And you know good and well what family he's out of. Solid people. Been up on that ridge three generations, church every Sunday, hardworking, not a one of them ever sigogglin in their dealings, if you take my meaning.

The niece: I hear you, Aunt. I surely do.

The aunt: Well then what in the world could be wrong with somebody like that? Tell me that, if you can.

The niece: Ain't nothing wrong with him. I just ain't got the time right now, is all. Between the garden and Mamaw's canning and the church supper coming, I'm plumb wore slap out as it is.

The aunt: Time. Lord have mercy, time. You know your cousin said that very same thing when we was a-leveling with her about her intended, said she wadn't ready, said she was too busy for such. Look at her now. Married eight years and glad as a lark she didn't win that argument. She'll tell you herself, plain as day, she'd have been a fool to let that man get away.

The niece: I know it. And I ain't saying you're wrong to bring it up. I just ain't studying on it yet, is all.

The aunt: Might could be you'uns will come around to it in time.

The niece: Might could.

The aunt: Well. Hit don't cost nothing to keep an offer open, and this one's staying open. Whenever your mind's right, you let me know. I ain't going nowhere, and neither is he, far as anybody can tell. He ain't the kind runs off soon as somebody turns him down.

The niece: I hear you, Aunt. Thank you kindly for thinking on me.
