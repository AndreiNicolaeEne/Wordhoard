Me: I come up here on account of that roof of ourn, hit's still a-leaking and I don't hardly know what else to try.

The neighbor: Which corner is it now, still that back one over the young'un's room?

Me: Same spot, right up under the chimney. Every time it comes a hard rain the water runs down inside the wall there and drips right along the fireboard. Ceiling's gone plumb soft, you can push your finger through it now if you was of a mind to.

The neighbor: Lord have mercy. How long has this been a-going on, and what all did he say to you?

Me: Going on five months now, since before Christmas. First time I seed him about it he said he'd get somebody up there directly, said he had a boy that done roofing and he'd have him call me.

The neighbor: And?

Me: Never come. I called back on him about it again a few weeks after that and he give me the same excuse near about word for word, said the weather hadn't been fit for it. That was the second time I asked, and here it is still a-dripping.

The neighbor: That's what one of mine told my aunt too, one whole winter, said the weather wasn't right, wasn't right, till her kitchen ceiling went sigogglin right down around the light. She had to get the county out on him before he moved a hand.

Me: I don't want it to have to come to that.

The neighbor: Well, you might could write him a letter this time, get it down on paper so he can't say he never heared it. Or there's the county you could call if it keeps on. Either way it drags out slow and you'uns are the ones living under it dripping.

Me: I reckon we are.

The neighbor: What I'd do first is get you some pictures of that spot, right smart of them, before you say one more word to him. Then whatever you decide, you've got it to show.

Me: That's fair. I'll get on it this evening.
