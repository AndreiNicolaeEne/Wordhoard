The customer: Now hold on just a minute here. I ordered me three buttons up the front and a straight hem, not this two-button, sigogglin thing you got laid out on the counter.

The tailor: That there is exactly what we agreed on, plain as day. Two buttons, angled hem, I recollect it clear as anything.

The customer: Hit don't matter what you recollect, I'm a-telling you what I said, and I said three buttons and a straight hem. I ain't paying good money for nothing sigogglin like that hem's a-hanging.

The tailor: Well, I heared it different, but let me go check my note. I wrote it down myself when you was in here.

The tailor: Here 'tis, right on the paper. Says three buttons, plain as anything. Reckon I must've misheared that part. But it says angled hem too, right here in my own hand.

The customer: Well I'll be. I could've swore I said straight, but if hit's wrote down angled, my mind's playing tricks on me, or maybe I changed it and just plumb forgot. Either way, them two buttons has got to come off and three put on.

The tailor: That I can do, easy fix, won't take but a few stitches. Give me two days and you'uns can come on back and get it, buttons and all, and it'll set right this time.

The customer: Two days is fine, long as it's done right. I ain't got but the one good coat for meeting and I aim to have it back and looking proper.

The tailor: You'll have it. Might could even have it done sooner if the boy ain't got me swamped with them other alterations, but two days for certain.

The customer: Reckon that'll do. I thank ye for checking, saved us both a right smart of arguing over nothing.

The tailor: Weren't no trouble a'tall. Better to know for sure than to guess and have you'uns come back a-hollering a second time.
