Landlord: Evenin'. Y'all get that porch swing hung back up?

Tenant: Did, finally. Took some doing, but hit's up and holding fine now.

Landlord: Good, good. Heared you'uns had a right smart of rain up your holler last week.

Tenant: We did. Crick got up plumb over the low bridge for a spell. We was watching it half the night, scared it'd come on up toward the yard.

Landlord: Glad it didn't. Well, set on down a minute, what's on your mind?

Tenant: Rent, mostly. Been paying on the first, every month, three years running now, never missed nor late once.

Landlord: I know it, and I'm obliged for it, I truly am.

Tenant: And that back step's still sigogglin, same as when we moved in, leaning off the porch like it's fixing to give way under somebody. Asked about it more than once.

Landlord: I hear you on the step. But rent, now, I don't know as I can be dropping that. Everything's gone up on my end too, taxes and all.

Tenant: I ain't asking for nothing wild. Was hoping you might could come down a fifth off what we're paying.

Landlord: A fifth. That's a heap of money, friend, more than I can carry.

Tenant: I know it's a heap to ask.

Landlord: I could maybe do about half of that. A tenth off, and the step gets fixed this month, I give you my word on it.

Tenant: A tenth don't hardly cover what a fifth would've, but I take your meaning on the money being tight.

Landlord: Let me set with it a night or two. Won't say no outright, won't say yes neither, not yet.

Tenant: That's fair enough. I ain't asking you to decide standing here in the yard.

Landlord: We'll talk again come Sunday, after church lets out. I expect we can land somewhere both of us can live with.

Tenant: Suits me. Obliged to you for hearing me out on it.
