Now I'll show you how it lays out up here, and you just listen, on account of they ain't nobody ever wrote a lick of this down, hit's just knowed.

You can leave your truck down yonder by the old pump, right where the gravel widens out plumb to the culvert, that's fine, been fine for fifty year. But don't you never pull up onto the Widow's turnaround, not even it it's setting empty of a Sunday with nary a car in sight. That ground's hers, and everybody up this holler's knowed it since before I was borned. My daddy heared his daddy say the very same.

See where the fence runs out sigogglin like that and there ain't nothing after but weeds? That's still the line. Wasn't nobody a-stringing wire out there in my lifetime, don't need to. Cross over into the next place's bottomland and you'll hear about it, quiet-like, but you will hear about it.

Come nine of an evening the whole street goes still, dogs and all. Ain't a soul gonna be out a-mowing or a-hollering across a yard after that, clean through to daylight. Ain't no sign posted nowhere, hit's just how it's kept and always was.

Passing folks on the road, you speak to the oldest first and work your way down, that's just manners, used to could get away without it in town but not up here.

Borrowing, now, you ask plain for what you need and you carry it back your own self, don't make them come a-hunting it. Somebody keeps a poke or a jar too long, ain't nobody gonna say a word to their face about it, but they won't be lent to again, not for a right smart while, and everybody up and down that holler knows exactly why, quiet as it's kept.

And the one thing, near about everybody does it sooner or later and won't a soul own up to it plain, cutting through the Preacher's side yard 'stead of walking clean round by the road when you're in a hurry. Might could take the long way. Everybody's done it anyhow. Ain't nobody said it out loud yet, and I don't reckon they will.
