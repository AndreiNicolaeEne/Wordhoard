The customer come in wi' the ticket in her hand and stood at the counter while the tailor went through to fetch it off the rail. He brought it back held up on the hanger, like it were owt to be proud of, and she clocked it straight off, before he'd even got a word in.

Customer: Eh now, hang on, that's not what I asked for. Three button and a straight hem, that were it, not two button and one with the angle cut in it.

Tailor: Nay, that's exactly what were agreed between us, chuck. Two button, angled hem, I remember it plain as owt.

Customer: You don't, though. I said three button twice over so there'd be no mistaking, and a straight hem an' all. I'm not stood here mithering thee for nowt, I know what I asked for.

Tailor: Well give over a minute then, I'll go and have a look at th' book, see what's written down.

Tailor: ...Reet. Here it is, and it says three button, straight hem, plain as day. That's my own writing an' all, not yours. I must have gone and cut it to the wrong measurements altogether.

Customer: Well there it is then. I weren't making it up.

Tailor: No, love, you weren't, and I'm right sorry for it. Give me two day and I'll have thee three button and a straight hem done proper, and nowt on the bill for the alter, that's on me for the mix up.

Customer: Ta. Two day's champion, I'm in no rush over it. Appen it'll be gradely when it's done right.

Tailor: It will an' all, I'll see to that myself this time.

Customer: Ta-ra then.

Tailor: Ta-ra, chuck.
