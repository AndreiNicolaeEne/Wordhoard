Ey, come here, chuck. Sit thi sen down a minute, I'll put wood in th'ole, it's parky in here. I know tha's not wanting owt said, and tha doesn't have to say nowt back neither, just wanted thee to know I'm here, that's all.

I keep thinking back to when it were me, first time round, and I never told thee half of it proper, did I. Appen it'll help thee to hear it, appen it won't, but I'll tell thee anyroad, and tha can just listen, no need to say owt.

First few days were nowt like I thought they'd be. I thought I'd be in bits, on floor, that sort of thing, but honest to God I were numb as owt. There were that much to sort, people coming and going, phone never stopping, in-laws and neighbours and t'undertaker and forms to sign, that there were barely two minutes to sit and let it be real. Someone'd put kettle on, someone else were making butties for everyone stood in kitchen, and I were just going through motions, saying ta to this one and ta to that one, and underneath it all summat hadn't landed yet at all. It were like watching meself do it from over by door.

Then everyone went home. That were the bit nobody tells thee about. House emptied out, last one give us a hug at door, said ta-ra, and then it were just me and quiet. And by God the quiet were loud. I stood in kitchen and there were nowt to do, no arrangements left to make, no phone ringing, and it all come down on us at once like roof caving in. I didn't skrike straight off even, I just stood there like a gormless article not knowing what to do with me own hands.

Weeks after that me body did some daft things. I couldn't eat, days on end, food'd just sit there and I'd look at it like it were a foreign thing. Then I'd sleep, but not proper sleep, more like little bursts, an hour here, forty minutes there, waking up at all hours not knowing where I were. It weren't restful. It were like being broke into pieces and dozing off between the pieces.

First month were strange because world just kept going. Bus still ran late, shop still shut same time Sunday, folk at work still moaning about same daft things they always moaned about, and I kept thinking, dun't they know? Dun't they know everything's changed? But course they didn't, why would they, life just carries on round thee whether tha's ready or not, and I weren't anywhere near ready, not by a long chalk.

And then it were a Tuesday, nowt marked on it at all, no anniversary, no nothing, just an ordinary day, and I went under completely. I still don't rightly know what set it off. Weren't owt special about that date, that were the thing that got us, that it could just come from nowhere on a day that meant nowt to anybody.

Months on, and it were still doing that. A smell'd catch us off guard, or I'd see summat daft, a coat on a peg, and it'd be like being hit again fresh, same as day one. No pattern to it at all, tha couldn't brace thi sen for it, it just come when it come.

Middle of it were the worst in its own way, if I'm honest, because it weren't dramatic, it were just grey. A fog, proper fog, over everything. Days that should've been good, a nice do, a bit of sun, our kid ringing us up made up to tell us summat, and I'd feel it all through this flannel, muffled, like it were happening to someone else two streets over.

I'll tell thee summat and all. Someone at work said to us, all breezy like, "eh well, time's a great healer, you'll be right as rain in no time," and I could've screamed at 'em, it were that thoughtless, like grief were a poorly leg that'd mend itself given a fortnight off. But then there were this other thing, small, nowt planned about it, someone just left a brew and a barm cake on side for us one morning without saying a word about it, didn't ask us owt, didn't mither us, just left it there and got on wi' their own work. That helped more than any of the official visits, any of the leaflets, any of the "let me know if you need owt." Sometimes it's the daftest little kindness that lands proper.

Then there were the anniversary of it. Weeks before, I were dreading it summat rotten, couldn't think past it, like walking towards a wall. And when it come, I didn't do owt gradely with it, I just got through, clumsy as owt, cried in car park, rang our kid, ate a barm cake I didn't even want. But I got through it, and that were the whole of it, that were enough. Nobody said I had to do it belting, just had to still be stood at end of it.

Appetite come back slow, and sleep and all, but not together, and not in a straight line neither. Some weeks I'd think I were sorted and then it'd all go bobbins again for no reason. That's just how it goes, I reckon, nowt smooth about it.

And then one day, God knows when exactly, I caught meself laughing, proper laughing, at summat daft on telly, and soon as it come out me guilt were right there behind it like it'd been waiting round corner. Felt like I weren't allowed. Felt like laughing were letting on I'd stopped caring, which I hadn't, not for one second.

By end of year I were carrying it different. Lighter in bits, I'll not lie to thee, some days were nearly gradely again. But it never went. It doesn't go. It just sits different in thee.

So that's me lot, love, for what it's worth. It dun't stay this hard forever, I can tell thee that much true, but there's no set time on it, nobody can tell thee when it'll ease off, and dun't thee go measuring thi year against mine nor anybody else's. Yours'll go how it goes. Give over thinking tha should be further on than tha is. Just get through today, and then get through t'morrow after that. That's all any of us are doing.
