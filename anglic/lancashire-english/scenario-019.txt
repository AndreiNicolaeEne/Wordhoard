My friend: Go on then, give over dodging it, I heard the pair on yous through that wall Thursday like summat off the telly. What were all that about.

Me: Aye alright, don't be laughing at us, I'll tell thee the lot.

Right, it started over nowt, honest to God, over a bag o' barm cakes an' all things. He'd gone to th' shop for us on his way back from work, an' I'd said barm cakes special, twice I said it, an' he comes home with cobs. Just plain cobs, off the wrong shelf, an' I know that sounds daft when I say it out loud now but it weren't really about the bread. It were that he never listens, not properly, not to owt I ask him, and I'd had a belter of a week an' I just went off at him the minute he put the bag on th' side.

I says, "I asked thee for barm cakes."

The partner: "They're the same flamin' thing."

Me: "They're not the same thing, they're cobs, and tha knows they're not what I asked for, tha never listens to a word I say."

That were me raising it first, no use pretending otherwise, I went reet loud straight off, and he give it me back just as loud, stood there in his work coat still on, going on about how he'd had a shocker of a shift and he weren't stood being shouted at over a bag o' bread like he were a naughty lad. And once it got going it didn't stop at the bread, course it didn't, it never does. I said summat I didn't mean, told him he never puts me first, not once, not ever, which weren't even true when I think on it properly, he does loads, but it come out of us like that in the heat of it and I couldn't take it back once it were said.

And then he brought up the one thing I don't let him bring up, the one we don't touch. He said, "and where were tha when my dad were badly, eh, tha never once came, not once," which weren't fair 'cause he knows full well why, and it's not summat either of us mithers on about normal, it's left well alone, and he only threw it at us 'cause he wanted it to hurt, and it did.

By that point he'd had enough an' all, and he went to th' back door for some air, and he didn't just shut it, he put wood in th'ole so hard the glass rattled in the frame and a mug come off the side and cracked on th' floor. Nobody meant owt by the mug, it just went, and neither on us picked it up for a good hour after.

After that it were just quiet. I stopped answering him altogether, didn't say another word, went up th' stairs and shut mysen in th' bedroom, and he stopped in front of th' telly downstairs not watching a thing on it. That were the better part of a whole day, him on th' settee, me up top, not a word passed between us, just footsteps on th' stairs when one on us wanted th' bathroom and nowt said even then.

It were him that moved first, in th' end. He come up about teatime with two brews, knocked on th' door dead soft, and he says, "I've put kettle on, chuck, can I come in."

That were it, that were the first sign, him calling us chuck again, 'cause he hadn't called us that since it kicked off, and I knew straight off he were trying.

Me: "Aye, go on then."

The partner: "I'm sorry for what I said about thi dad. That were out of order, that. I know that's not summat we bring up and I only said it to hurt thee and I shouldn't have. I am sorry, proper sorry, not just saying it."

That were the actual apology, not a quick sorry chucked over his shoulder, he meant it and I could tell he'd sat on it half the afternoon working out how to say it right.

Me: "I'm sorry an' all. For going off at thee over a daft bag o' cobs, and for saying tha never puts me first, 'cause tha does, I know tha does, I only said it to wound thee back."

And we sat on th' edge of th' bed with us brews going lukewarm in us hands, not really drinking 'em, just holding 'em, and appen that's when it properly turned, 'cause he give us a bit of a nudge with his shoulder and went and made us a barm cake, a proper one this time, split it right down th' middle and give us half, and that were what sealed it really, more than owt said. Sat there sharing one barm cake between us like a couple of kids, that were belting after a day like that, considering how bobbins the whole thing had been.

My friend: So what's this our friend was on about hearing through th' wall then, is she gonna get an earful off thee for grassing us up.

Me: Ha, give over, I've already told her, next time she hears it going off through that wall she can just mither us about scran instead an' knock the door, save us all the bother. She reckons she heard the mug go an' all, and I said aye well that were the only casualty, one mug and one bag of cobs nobody wanted. We're proper made up now anyroad, gradely as owt, better than we've been in weeks if I'm honest, so ta for askin', but don't be earwigging next time, ta-ra.
