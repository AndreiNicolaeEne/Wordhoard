The father: I still fancy the one off the telly, the soft one ,  dead lovely, that were.

The mother: Give over, chuck, that's bobbins, that. Kid'll get mithered summat rotten at school with a name like that, they'll skrike him for it.

The father: Well go on then, since you've got summat gradely up your sleeve.

The mother: What about summat plainer, like your gran's side, keep it a bit proper?

The father: Nay, that sounds like it belongs to a right miserable owld feller sat in't corner of a pub. Not having that for our lad.

The mother: Well what then, cause you're knocking down everything I put up an' all.

The father: What about after me dad, eh? He'd be made up, reet chuffed.

The mother: We can't, love, your dad's got it already, and so's our kid's youngest, there'll be three on 'em down at the christening answering to same shout, right confusing that'd be.

The father: Appen it don't matter, it's a family name, that's the point of it.

The mother: It matters when the school's ringing up asking which one they mean.

The father: So who gets final say round here then? Every time I open me gob you're straight in wi' a reason it's bobbins.

The mother: Nobody gets final say, we're meant to agree on it, that's how it works.

The father: Aye well we're getting nowt agreed tonight, are we.

The mother: No, we're not. Put wood in th'ole, there's a right draught coming under that door. He's asleep bless him, he dun't care what we call him yet.

The father: Ta for that. I'm off up, I'm knackered.

The mother: Ta-ra then. We'll have a brew in't morning and start again, appen we'll be less mithered by then.
