Eh, I've got to tell thee this one properly, sit thi sen down, cos it were a right do and I'm still not right ovver it.

By t'end of t'night me mate were gone, and I mean proper gone, not just merry, not just "had a few," gone gone. Started off gradely enough, few pints, bit of a laugh, everyone made up cos it were Friday and nob'dy had owt on in t'morning bar me, worse luck. But summat shifted round about pint six or seven and after that it were all downhill, and quick an' all.

First sign it had gone past a joke were when me mate stood up to go to t'bar and just didn't. Legs went from under like they were made of nowt, straight down onto t'stool, missed it, caught t'edge of t'table instead and nearly had the drinks off it. And when I said summat like "eh up, y'alright there," it were all:

My friend: "I'm fine, I'm fine, honest, I'm reet as owt, stop mitherin' me."

But the words were coming out all wrong, all soft round the edges like they'd been left out in t'rain, half a sentence then nowt, then a laugh that didn't mean owt. I knew then. You know when you know.

Took some doing to get 'em to actually leave, mind. Proper resisted it, arms folded, saying they were "just getting going," saying one more wouldn't hurt, saying they'd sworn off going home early since they were nowt but a bairn. I had to near enough drag t'coat onto 'em.

Me: "Right, that's it, we're off, give over now."

My friend: "I'm not goin' nowhere, I'm champion, look at me."

Weren't champion. Weren't within a mile of champion. But after a fair bit of coaxing and one workmate helping me get an arm under 'em, we were out t'door and into t'night air, which is when it properly hit 'em how far gone they were.

Whole way home I'd me arm round 'em, not letting go once, cos every few steps they'd list off to one side like a boat taking on water. Down by t'ginnel near t'old shops we had to stop, cos it were coming, no question, and there weren't no arguing with it. Held their head, held their shoulder, said nowt daft, just let 'em get on with it. After, they just sat down on t'kerb there for a bit, head in their hands, not saying much, and I sat with 'em, cos what else are you going to do.

There were a fella walked past just then, dog on a lead, gave us a right look, half disgust half pity, shook his head and muttered summat under his breath I didn't quite catch, but you knew what he were thinking, seen it a hundred times I'd guess. Didn't bother me. Not my business what he thought, my business were getting me mate home in one piece.

Got up eventually, got going again, and this is where I'll admit I went wrong an' all, cos in all the faff of holding 'em up and keeping 'em walking I took us down t'wrong turn, went past t'ginnel we should've cut through and ended up adding a good ten minutes on, both of us going "this in't reet, is it," half laughing, half knackered, before we sorted oursens out and got back on track.

Tried getting some water into 'em along the way and oh, you'd have thought I were trying to poison 'em.

My friend: "Don't want it, get off, I don't want owt."

Me: "Just a bit, come on, you'll thank me."

My friend: "I'm not thirsty, give over."

Took a good few goes, bottle held right up to their mouth like they were nowt but a babby, before they finally gave in and had a proper gulp, then another, and you could see it doing 'em a bit of good even if they wouldn't say so.

Then, right out of nowhere, walking past t'chippy all shut up for t'night, they went all soft and said, dead serious like, eyes proper glassy:

My friend: "You're me best mate in t'whole world, you know that, don't you. I love thee, I do. You're the only one who'd do this for me."

Which, drunk as they were, they'd have said that to a lamppost an' all, but it still got me a bit, I won't lie.

Getting to t'door were its own battle. Fumbling with t'key, my friend leant up against t'frame skriking with laughter over nowt at all, and me trying to get t'door open one handed while keeping hold of 'em with t'other, and it took a good minute of proper wrestling with it before it gave. Got 'em in, got 'em through to t'front room, and near enough had to steer 'em down onto t'settee cos there were no chance of stairs, not that night, not a hope.

Once they were down, coat off, glass of water left where they'd see it if they woke, they grabbed me arm, dead solemn all of a sudden, and said:

My friend: "Never again. I mean it. Never drinking like that again, not ever, I swear on it."

Me: "Aye, alright chuck, we'll see about that in t'morning."

Didn't believe a word of it even as it were coming out their mouth, and I don't think they'd have believed it either if they'd been sober enough to hear thersen say it. But you let 'em say it anyway, don't you, no harm in it.

Checked on 'em next morning, proper sorry state, head like a drum, could hardly look at t'kettle without wincing. Asked what they remembered and it were bits and bobs, patchy as owt, remembered t'pub fine, remembered summat about a chippy, nowt after that. So I filled it in for 'em, most of it anyroad, the ginnel, the water, t'wrong turn, all of it, and they just put their head in their hands and groaned.

My friend: "Did I really say that, about being best mates and all that soft stuff?"

Me: "You did that, word for word, near enough."

My friend: "Give over."

Me: "It's true, chuck, every word."

They just laughed, proper embarrassed but laughing all the same, and said "ta" for getting 'em back safe, and I said don't be daft, that's what you do in't it, and put wood in th'ole on me way out cos there were a right draught coming through. Made up they were alright in the end, state they were in. Ta-ra for now, love, I'll tell thee the rest of it another day when I've had me brew.
