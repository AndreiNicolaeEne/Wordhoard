Right, so I said I'd do it, didn't I, mind the house a week while they were off, and I thought to myself, easy money this, sat on me backside a week with somebody else's telly and somebody else's fridge, what could go wrong. Seven days. I should've known better, me, honest to God.

First night and already summat weren't reet. Not owt you could put your finger on, mind. I got in, put wood in th'ole behind us like you do, had a look round, made meself a brew, and there were this draught coming from somewhere I couldn't place. Whole house creaking away like it were talking to itself. I says to meself, appen it's just an old house settling, houses do that, give over being soft, and I went up to bed, but I didn't sleep proper, not that first night. Kept thinking I'd hear summat and then I did hear summat and it were nowt, just the boiler ticking over. Daft.

Second day the cat got out. Now I'd been told, dead clear, keep that door shut, it's a bolter that one, and I must've left it on the latch going out to the bins because next thing I know I'm stood in the ginnel round the back in me slippers shouting a name that isn't even the cat's proper name, just "here, here, come on then," like that's going to work on an animal that's decided it's had enough of indoors. Took us the best part of an hour, up and down that ginnel, under next door's hedge, till it come out for a bit of ham I'd nicked off the plate for us own tea. Caught it, held it like a baby, heart going ten to the dozen. That were day two and I thought, right, that's my one disaster, I've had it now, smooth sailing from here.

It weren't, obviously.

The garden started going wrong so gradual I didn't hardly notice till it were past noticing. There were these tomato plants in the back, in pots, and I were meant to water 'em, which I did, mostly, but appen not enough or appen too much, I couldn't tell you which, and by about Wednesday they'd gone all yellow and droopy looking, sorry state, and there were nowt I could do about it by then, it were done. I kept giving 'em water anyway like that were going to fix it. It weren't gradely, that garden, by the end of the week, and I knew it and there were no use pretending otherwise.

Then Thursday the kettle went. Their kettle, mind, not mine, packed in right in the middle of us making a brew, just clicked off and wouldn't click back on, dead as owt. Now that's a proper disaster in this house, no kettle, so I had to go borrow one off next door, which meant explaining meself, which meant admitting I'd already lost the cat once that week, and the neighbour give us a look.

Neighbour: You minding next door then?

Me: Aye, week, they're away.

Neighbour: Going well, is it?

Me: Oh, belting. Champion. Just the kettle's packed up is all.

She lent us hers anyway, bless her, and I never did get theirs working again, just left a note about it, which I'll come to.

Friday the visitor turned up. No warning at all, just a knock, and I open it not thinking, and there's some fella stood there wanting to know is she in, and I says no, she's away, and he looks at us like I'm the one that's not supposed to be there, which fair enough, I suppose I am a stranger answering somebody else's door. Turned out to be her workmate come to drop summat off she'd asked him to.

Workmate: She said leave it on the side if she's not in.

Me: Aye, no bother, I'll see to it.

Workmate: You sound like you've had a week.

Me: You've no idea, love.

Same day, later on, I had the fright of me life. I couldn't find the spare key, the one they'd give us in case, the one I were meant to be dead careful with, and I went through every coat pocket, every drawer, turning the place over, proper sick with it, thinking I've lost it, I've actually gone and lost the one thing they trusted us with, how am I going to tell 'em that. Found it in the end down the side of the sofa where it'd slid off the table. Nearly cried with relief, I'm not ashamed to say. Skriking over a key, imagine.

Then Saturday, and this were the belter, I locked meself out. Proper out, not a key-down-the-sofa job, gone to put the bins out again and the door's swung to behind us and there's no way back in, and me phone's on the kitchen table an' all, so I'm stood there like a lemon in the front garden for I don't know how long, hours it felt like, till I remembered the neighbour again, God love her, she let us sit in with a brew while I worked out what to do, rang a locksmith in the end, cost us more than the money I were being paid for the whole week, but there you go.

Somewhere in amongst all that it rained, proper rained, and I'd left the little window in the back bedroom on the latch for the cat, forgetting the cat weren't even meant to go up there, and come the Thursday or thereabouts there's a right puddle on the windowsill and down the wall and onto the carpet, gone all dark and squelchy under me feet. I mopped it best I could and put a towel down and hoped.

And there were a noise one night, proper had us up out of bed at some ungodly hour, banging away downstairs, and I'm creeping down in the dark with nowt but a rolling pin off the side for protection, heart in me mouth, and it were the cat again, knocked a picture frame off the shelf chasing nowt at all. Give over, I says to it, out loud, on me own, at three in the morning. Bobbins, that cat.

By the Sunday, second to last day, I'd had enough of the kettle business and went and bought 'em a new one off me own money, plugged it in, worked a treat, at least that were sorted before they got back, one thing off the list.

Last day were just tidying, flat out, hoovering, wiping, trying to get that carpet looking less sorry for itself, moving the tomato pots to where they weren't so obvious, straightening the picture the cat had had off the shelf, going round the whole house like a woman possessed with a duster, our kid rang us and I couldn't even stop to talk proper.

Our kid: You alright, you sound frantic.

Me: I am frantic, I've got about two hour to make this house look like nowt's happened.

Our kid: Has owt happened?

Me: Don't ask.

And then they were back, stood in the hall with their bags, and I did the account, more or less honest, though I'll admit I played some of it down.

Me: House is fine, cat's fine, kettle went so I got you a new one, there's a bit of damp on the back bedroom carpet from the rain, and the tomatoes didn't quite make it, sorry about them, and oh, I locked meself out Saturday but it's all sorted now.

Owner: Sounds like you've had a week.

Me: Gradely week, ta very much. Never again, mind.

Owner: Ta for minding it anyway.

Me: Ta-ra then. I'm off home for a lie down.
