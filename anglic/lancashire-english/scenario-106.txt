Eh, give over lookin at us like that, love, it's nowt to make a song and dance of. Tha's asked us three times now to tell thee about th' checkpoint so I'll tell thee straight, no bigger and no smaller than it were, and then we'll say no more about it.

They'd put it where the two roads met, just past where the old road narrows down to one lane, cause there'd been bother that way, folk comin through as shouldn't, or so they said, and it were easier to stop everybody at that one spot than try and watch the whole stretch. So there it were, been there a fair while by the time I come to it, sandbags and a barrier and a little hut, and a queue back down the road that had built up summat chronic. Best part of half an hour I stood there, shufflin forward a few feet at a time, watchin the folk in front get looked over one by one.

I'll not pretend I weren't nervous, cause I were, reet nervous, though there were nowt on us to be nervous about, nowt I'd done wrong, but tha knows how it gets thee, standin there in a line with nowt to do but think. Me heart were goin like nobody's business and I kept checkin me pocket for the papers same as if they might've walked off on their own while I weren't lookin.

There were a lad ahead of us in the queue, few places up, and they held him back longer than the rest, took him to one side by the hut and had him stood there while they went through summat of his. Nobody said owt about it, not the folk either side of him, not the ones behind neither, we just watched him out the corner of our eye and let on we hadn't noticed, cause that's what tha does, isn't it, tha doesn't stare and tha doesn't say nowt, tha just keeps thy own eyes down and waits for thy own turn to come.

And then it were mine.

The one at the barrier: Papers.

Me: (handed em over)

The one at the barrier: Where's tha headed then.

Me: Down to see me sister, she's poorly, I'm goin to sit with her a few days.

That were plain enough, said it straight out, no messin, cause it were true and there were nowt to dress up about it. But then he asked us summat else, and this were the one I had to think on a bit more careful before I answered.

The one at the barrier: And how long's tha plannin on stoppin there.

Now that could've gone two ways, cause say too long and it looks like summat more than a visit, say too short and it looks like tha's not tellin the whole of it, so I took a breath and I said it steady.

Me: Week, appen ten days if she's still poorly, no longer than that, I've me own work to get back to.

He looked at us a bit, then looked down at the papers again, and there were this beat where nobody said nowt at all, just him readin em over, turnin em, and me stood there with me hands still, tryin not to let on how me insides were goin. Tha knows that quiet, love, when a body's readin summat about thee and thy whole face has to just sit there and not move.

Then he asked us where I were stayin exactly, and I went to answer and for a second, just a second, it went clean out me head, the address, cause I'd only ever thought of it as "our sister's" and never had to say it plain to a stranger, and I had to stop meself, catch me breath, and dig it out proper, street and number and all, and get it out before the pause went on too long and looked like summat it weren't. I got it out in the end. He wrote it down.

I kept me voice level the whole while, though it took some doin, cause tha doesn't want thy voice to go, tha doesn't want it to catch or wobble, cause a wobble looks like guilt even when there's nowt to be guilty of, so I just breathed steady and kept me eyes on him and answered plain when he asked and didn't say more than were asked, cause that's the trick of it, isn't it, not sayin more than tha's asked.

And he weren't unkind about it neither, I'll say that for him. When I fumbled a bit findin the address he just waited, didn't rush us, said "tek thy time" quiet like, which were more than I expected off a man with a queue back down the road behind him and a job to do. Little thing, but it steadied us more than owt else that mornin.

Then he folded the papers back up, handed em over, and just nodded us through, said "on tha goes then" and that were it, done, papers back in me pocket and me walkin on past the barrier and the sandbags and the little hut, tryin to walk normal and not too quick, cause walkin too quick looks like summat and all.

It weren't till I were a good twenty minutes down the road, past where I could still see the checkpoint if I'd looked back, that it actually let go of us, love. Just went out me legs all of a sudden, right down into me shoes, and I had to stop a minute at the side of the road, get me breath, cause I hadn't known how tight I'd been holdin meself the whole time till it weren't needed no more. Made us feel a right state, standin there gettin me breath back on a bit of road with nowt round us, but there it is, that's what it took out of us, and I'd not known till it were over.

So that's why I tell it careful when I tell it at all, love, no bigger than it were, cause it weren't nowt dramatic, nobody shouted, nobody so much as raised their voice, it were just a man askin questions and me answerin em and a lad up the queue who had it harder than me and never a word said about that neither. But I'll not make out it were nowt either, cause it took summat out of us that took a fair walk to put back, and that's the truth of it, plain as I can tell it thee.
