Her workmate: ...eh, get this down thee first, barm cake's from t' good shop, anyroad, so our kid reckons it were three of 'em set on him outside t' ground of a Saturday, done him over proper, face like a burst tomato, and the ambulance had to come and everything, it were...

The neighbour: give over, that's not what happened, not even close.

Her workmate: eh?

The neighbour: right, I'll lay it out for thee, cos I were there. There were no three lads, there were no fight, nowt of the sort. He were larking about on t' wall by us ginnel wi' his mates after t' match, lost his footing, and cracked his head on th' kerb coming off it. That's the whole of it. Few stitches, bit of a graze, he's reet as owt now, more embarrassed than owt else.

Her workmate: bloody hell. That's a way off what I heard, that is.

The neighbour: it's some way off, aye. A lad falling off a wall's turned into three of 'em setting on him outside t' ground by time it's reached you. That's proper drifted, that.

Her workmate: I am made up he's alright though, I'll say that. Where'd I even hear it were three on him, appen I've got it all wrong end to. Who told you what I were saying?

The neighbour: nobody told me owt, love, your face said it all over your brew. Go on then, where'd thee get it?

Her workmate: off my sister. She got it off someone at bingo, I think.

The neighbour: aye well, that'll be your sister's mate, she's been mithering everyone with it all week and appen adding a lad or two every telling. Belting tale, shame it's bobbins start to finish.

Her workmate: I'll tell her straight, aye, I will.

The neighbour: do, chuck. Tell it the true way from now on, whoever asks. He fell off a wall, cracked his head, he's on t' mend. Nowt more to it than that.

Her workmate: right y'are. Ta.
