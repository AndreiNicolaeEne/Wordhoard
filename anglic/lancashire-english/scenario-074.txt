Me: Eh, you'll never guess. She's cried off again. Text come through not twenty minutes since, "summat's come up, so sorry."

My friend: Give over. Not again, for th' barm cakes and a brew we'd been on about all week?

Me: Aye, that were the plan an' all. Half twelve, that caff by th' ginnel. Nowt. Not a dicky bird since.

My friend: That's what now, third time she's done this to us?

Me: Fourth, our kid. I've actually counted. There were the do at the club she never showed for, then the walk we'd planned that Sunday, then her sister's thing she talked us into going to and then dropped out of herself, and now this.

My friend: Four times! I'm made up for her if summat's genuinely gone off in her life, but four times, that's takin' liberties that is.

Me: Excuse this time were "me head's mashed, I need a lie down."

My friend: Appen it's true. Or appen it's bobbins and she just weren't fussed.

Me: I dunno. It's never owt you can check on, is it. Always summat different.

My friend: I skriked laughing when I read it. Mashed head, at eleven in the morning, on a work day.

Me: I know. Do we even ask her next time though? I don't want to be mithering her if summat's really up wi' her.

My friend: But you don't want to be sat there on your own again neither, chuck. That's twice now I've stood in that ginnel like a lemon waiting.

Me: True enough. She's a gradely lass when she does turn up, mind, I'll give her that much.

My friend: Aye, when. That's the whole bother though, in't it.

Me: Reet, here's what we'll do. We ask her same as always, no hard feelings. But if she cries off again, we just go, the two of us, and put wood in th'ole behind us.

My friend: Belting. That's settled then. Ta for that, love, I were gettin' proper wound up over nowt.

Me: Ta-ra, see thee in a bit.
