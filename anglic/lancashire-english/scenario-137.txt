Dinner van come back wi' half the order still on it and the rest delivered to wrong end of town altogether, and before nob knew owt for certain the boss had gone and picked his man, landed straight on our kid, and he'd had nowt to do wi' loading that van, not a thing. He'd been out the back on the ginnel door taking scran off the wagon all morning. But he were the one standing nearest when the boss come through, so he were the one that copped it.

Foreman: You loaded that van didn't you. Forty boxes gone to wrong street and it's your name on the sheet.

Our kid: Weren't me.

Foreman: Don't gimme that, I've seen you on that bay all shift.

Our kid: Weren't me.

Foreman: Summat's gone reet wrong here and it wants an answer, and you're stood there wi' the sheet in your hand.

Our kid: I swear down, I'd no part in it.

He never said it twice. Just the once, flat as owt, and back to stacking like nowt had happened. The two that had actually loaded it were stood by the ginnel wi' their brews going cold and their butties half et, saying nowt, looking anywhere but at him. Nob give them a second glance.

The boss stood there a minute waiting for more, wanting him to skrike or shout back or give summat away in his face, but he give him nowt to work wi', just carried on like it were any other Tuesday.

In the end the boss just went, well, appen we'll say no more, which weren't an apology, just give over and off back to th' office. Nob said sorry. Nob owned up. Our kid just carried on, ta-ra to the ones by the door, and left it standing exactly where he'd put it.
