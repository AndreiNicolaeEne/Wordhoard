Workmate: Eh up, tha looks knackered. How'd tha get on last night then, wi' the dog?

Me: Don't ask. Reet bad do it were. Come teatime she were off her feed, wouldn't touch owt, and she were movin' all wrong, sort of stiff like, wobblin' when she stood. I weren't leavin' her on her own all night wi' that, so I said to our kid, I'm stoppin' down here wi' her, you go up.

Workmate: Aye, tha would an' all.

Me: So I'm sat theer from about seven, watchin' her for owt changin', and for hours theer's nowt. Absolute nowt. I got a bit o' water in a bowl and were tryin' to coax her, dippin' me finger in and puttin' it to her mouth, 'cause she wouldn't drink off her own bat. Then must've been gone midnight she took a right bad turn, shakin' summat awful, and I thought, have I done wrong keepin' her home, should I have been on the phone hours back. Proper frightened me that did.

Workmate: God, chuck. That's no way to spend a night.

Me: I stopped anyroad. Kept her wrapped up warm, talkin' to her dead low the way you would to owt frightened, just nattering on about nowt in particular. Near dawn I catches her ear twitch at a noise, normal like, and her breathin' had changed, evened out. By full mornin' she were steadier. Not reet, mind, but steadier. And only when I were sure o' that did I go all in on mesen, proper wiped out, could've slept standin' up.

Workmate: Well tha's done gradely by her, that's for certain. Get thesen a brew down you.

Me: Aye, go on then. Ta. I'm made up she's on the mend, honest I am. Ta-ra, I'm off for me scran, I've earned it.
