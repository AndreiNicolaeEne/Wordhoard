Eh, this year were the one, no messing. You could tell early doors it were going to be summat special, way before owt were even in off the fields. Normal year we'd get happen twenty ton off that top field, this year we were looking at going on forty afore we'd even started counting proper, near enough double, and everybody saying so.

It were the spuds what did it, mind. Every other crop were gradely enough in its own road, but them spuds were bobbins compared to what came out that top field, sack after sack past owt we'd ever had. Best before that were donkeys years back, when me dad were still working the land himself, and even that were nowt against this one.

We'd to get extra hands in an' all, cos it were turning quick and we'd not enough of us to lift it all before it went off. Our kid's mates from the ginnel, neighbours, anyone who'd stand still two minutes got put to work.

Me: Ta for coming, love, we'd never have got it in without thee.
The one from next ginnel: Give over, it were nowt, glad of the day out.

Once we'd covered what we needed ourselves and what were owed round about, rest got stored down the barn, and what wouldn't keep got traded on to the market lot for a fair price.

The oldest grower there, who's seen every harvest since he were a lad, stood looking at it and said it were the best he'd ever clapped eyes on, gradely start to finish, nowt to touch it in his whole time on the land. Credited it to the rain coming reet when it were wanted and a mild stretch running clean through the summer, no shocks, no scares.

Old grower: Appen we'll not see its like again.

He weren't wrong an' all. It's still what gets talked of now, every bad year and every middling one measured against it, brew in one hand, barm cake going round, and someone always asking, "aye, but were it as good as that year?" Ta-ra.
