Me: Did you hear her, at the table? "I'd have done it different, but there y'are." Barefaced, that were.

My other half: She's only saying, love. Give over, she means nowt by it.

Me: She meant summat by it, she always does. Not that she'd say owt straight out. I could've skriked, honest, sat there with me fork halfway up.

My other half: You're mithering over nothing now.

Me: I am heck. And then your dad's off with the same tale again, third time that were, word for word, laughing at his own joke before he's even got to the end of it.

My other half: Aye, he does love that one.

Me: Gradely, he thinks it is. I'm nodding like a nodding dog, thinking appen he'll stop if I don't laugh.

My other half: He never does though.

Me: Never. And then the door, chuck. Forty-five minutes at that door. "Ta for coming, ta-ra, mind how you go, ta-ra, oh and tell our kid I said..." I were stood there with me coat half on the whole time, and by the time we got down th' ginnel to the car I were froze through.

My other half: ...Alright, I'll give you that one. That were a reet marathon and no mistake.

Me: See, I'm not making it up.

My other half: No, you're not. And she never once said she were made up for us, about the news, did she, she just went on about the gravy.

Me: The gravy! On and on about the gravy.

My other half: Give over, don't start me off an' all.

Me: Too late. Barm cakes were belting, mind, not cobs, not rolls, barm cakes, and she does 'em gradely.

My other half: They were, aye. Rest of it were bobbins though.

Me: It were a bit bobbins.

My other half: Appen it were. A bit.
