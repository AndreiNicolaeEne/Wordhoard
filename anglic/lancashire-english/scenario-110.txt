Our kid: Eh, put wood in th'ole, there's a right draught off that door.

The mother: Go on then, tell us. What's it like down there, this London.

Me: It's alright, it is. Big though. Proper big. You'd not credit it till you're in it.

The eldest: Bigger than Preston, aye, I'd guessed that much meself.

Me: Bigger than Preston's not even sayin' owt. It's bigger than owt round here put together and then some. You could put the whole of Preston in one corner of it and nob'dy'd notice it'd gone.

The mother: Go on.

Me: Honest to God, some days it takes the best part of an hour just to get from one side of it to t'other. Not miles and miles neither, just across, like. You're stood on this tube train packed in like scran in a tin, and it's rattlin' and screechin' and some feller's got his elbow in your ear, and you think, I could've walked to Longridge and back in this.

The eldest: Noisy then.

Me: Noisy in't word for it. Round here you notice a car goin' past. Down there it never stops, day nor night, sirens and drills and folk shoutin' and the trains under your feet even, you can feel 'em through the pavement. First few weeks I couldn't sleep for it. Now I don't hear it, which is worse in a way, if you think on it.

Our kid: So where do you actually live, like? Proper live.

Me: Little flat, share it with two others. It's not what you're picturing, I'll tell you that now. You're all sat there thinkin' some grand place with a view, aren't you. It's about the size of this kitchen and the bathroom, and that's your lot. Costs a fortune an' all. What I pay for that box a month, you could nearly buy a house round here with it.

The mother: Never.

Me: I'm not messin'. Rent alone'd have your dad havin' a fit. And then everything else on top, a pint's daft money, a barm... well, they don't even call it a barm down there, that's summat else altogether.

The eldest: Go on then, what do they call it.

Me: A roll. Or a bap, if you're really unlucky. I asked for a barm cake in a shop first week and the lass just looked at us like I'd asked her summat in Chinese.

The mother: Well I never. That's not right, that isn't.

Me: It's a different world, Mother, honest.

The eldest: Are they friendly, the folk down there? Or are they all up their own end like you hear?

Me: Now that's not a straight one, that. Some are lovely, dead friendly, help you shift a wardrobe up three flights without blinkin'. Others'll not look at you on the tube if you were on fire. It's not that they're unfriendly exactly, it's just, everyone's mindin' their own, keepin' their head down, gettin' where they're gettin'. You get used to it. Then you'll have a neighbour bring you round a brew and a bit of cake for no reason at all and you think, oh, appen it's alright after all.

Our kid: Where do you get your shop, like, your food and that?

Me: There's a market near us, a proper big one, all sorts on it, fruit and veg and fish and every kind of spice you'd never heard of. Cheaper than the supermarket if you know which stall. And there's a corner shop does everything else, open till all hours. It's not like poppin' to t'ginnel and round the back to the shop like here, it's a right trek some days, but you make do.

The eldest: And work? How've you gone on findin' work down there?

Me: Took a bit, I'll not lie. Sent us CV out to that many places I lost count. Got took on eventually through an agency, and now I'm three days in an office and two workin' from the flat, which suits us, if I'm honest, saves us the crush on that tube of a mornin'. It's a different way of workin' altogether to here. Nob'dy stops for a proper dinner break half the time, they're eatin' at their desk, sandwich in one hand and typin' with the other.

The mother: Sounds like no life at all, that.

Me: It's just how it is down there, Mother. You get on with it.

The eldest: Did you get lost much, when you first went?

Me: Lost? I were lost more than I were found, first month or two. Three, four times a right proper lost, not just turned round, I mean stood on a corner not knowin' which way were which, phone dead an' all one time, had to ask a stranger and even then I couldn't follow half what he were sayin' back at us. Took us a good while to get the layout of it in me head. It's not like here where you know every ginnel and back street from bein' knee high. There it's all changin' train lines and streets that look the same as the last three you walked down. I've got it sorted now though, mostly. Mostly.

Our kid: What about the scran, is it any good?

Me: Best and worst of both, that. Best in that you can get owt you fancy, any hour, any sort of food goin', stuff you'd never even heard the name of round here. Worst in that half of it's not a patch on a proper chippy tea, and don't get us started on what they try and pass off as gravy. And the bread's just wrong somehow, I can't say how, it just is. First time I come home and had your chips, Mother, I could've cried.

The mother: Aye well. So is it better then, down there, or is it worse? Straight answer now.

Me: Give over, there's no straight answer to that one.

The mother: Go on, you must have an opinion.

Me: I'm not sayin', it's neither, it's just different, in't it. Some things down there are belting, proper belting, and some of it's bobbins, and some days I think this and some days I think summat else altogether. You can't just say better or worse like that, it's not that simple.

The eldest: Give over yourself, you're avoidin' it same as ever.

Me: Appen I am an' all.

The dad: So come on then, are you comin' back for good, or what? None of this dodgin' about now.

Me: I dunno, honest. Might do. Not yet like, there's things keepin' us there for now, but appen one day, aye. I'm not sayin' never.

The mother: Well. As long as you're made up down there, that's all that matters, in't it.

Me: I am, mostly. Gradely, even, some days.

Our kid: Gradely. Get you.

Me: Give over.

The mother: Right, who's puttin' kettle on, I want a brew before owt else gets said.

Our kid: I'll go. Ta.

Me: Ta-ra to sittin' here all night talkin' about London and all, though, isn't it grand just bein' back.

The eldest: Get the brew in first before you go all soft on us.
