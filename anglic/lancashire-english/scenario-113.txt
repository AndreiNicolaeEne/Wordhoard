Eh now chuck, sit thi sen down a minute, I've got to tell thee about last neet, you'll not credit the half of it.

Right so it were me, our kid, the neighbour and her feller, round ours like always, four at th' table, cards out, kettle on. We'd sorted the stakes before a card were even dealt so nob'dy could come back after moaning about it, nowt daft, mind, just coppers really, enough it stung a bit losing a hand but nob'dy were walking home skint over it. I'd got barm cakes in, not cobs, not rolls, barm cakes, and a brew going round and a bit of scran laid out an all, so it were set up gradely before we'd even cut for deal. Somebody shouted put wood in th'ole, there were a right draught off the ginnel, and then we were away.

First few hands went by ordinary like. Nob'dy up, nob'dy down much, a hand each about, our kid moaning he'd nowt in his hand again, the neighbour laughing at him for it. Nothing in it. You'd have looked at that table after three hands and thought, aye, this'll be a quiet one, everyone'll break about level and go home. And then it turned.

Her feller took a hand. Fair enough, somebody has to. Then he took the next an all. Then a third. By th' fourth running I looked at our kid and our kid looked at me and nowt were said but you could feel it, that little shift where a room goes quieter without deciding to.

Me: Shuffle's looking reet clean tonight, that.

I said it dead light, like a joke, half smiling when I said it. And it were brushed off first time, no bother at all...

Her feller: Appen I'm just having a night, aren't I. Give over.

, and we did, we gave over, played on, had another brew, our kid went on about summat off the telly, and it looked like it'd blow over.

Then it come round to her feller dealing again. And this time our kid were watching proper, not chatting, just watching the cards leaving th' bottom of that deck, and summat about it caught his eye that he didn't like the look of at all. He put his hand flat on the table, right in the middle of the deal, and he said it straight out, no messing:

Our kid: You're dealing off the bottom. Plain as owt. I just watched thee do it.

No appen about it that time. Named him to his face, sat right across from him.

And the answer come back flat, no give in it whatsoever:

Her feller: I am not. Don't talk daft, I've dealt like that thirty year.

That were it, then. Table split straight down the middle. The neighbour were up out of her chair near enough, saying our kid wanted his eyes tested and her feller had never cheated at owt in his life, and our kid weren't backing down neither, saying he knew what he'd seen and he weren't going to sit there and be told different. I sat there in the middle of it not knowing which way to jump, if I'm honest with thee, because I'd seen summat funny in that fourth hand an all, only I couldn't have sworn to it in a court, like.

So then we're going back over the hands. All four of them, the whole streak, who'd led what, who'd had what left in their hand at the end, whether the cards coming off suited a man who could see the bottom card or whether it were just a run of luck like happens to everybody once in a way. And honest, chuck, we got nowt out of it. Not a thing settled. Round and round it went, this one saying that proves it and that one saying it proves nowt of the sort, and after twenty minutes of that we were further from an answer than when we'd started, only more wound up about it.

But we played on anyway. That's the daft part. Nob'dy got up and went home, nob'dy chucked the cards down and said right, that's me done. We just... carried on, dealt the next hand, and the one after that. Only the ease had gone clean out of it. Where before it were laughing and moaning and taking the mick, now everybody were watching everybody's hands a bit too close, and the deal went round with folk practically holding their breath till it were done and nowt untoward had happened. Made a long neet feel a lot longer, I'll tell thee.

We got to the last hand, eleventh of the neet all told, I counted after, and do you know who took it? Our kid did. First hand he'd won since before all the bother started, and he slapped it down on the table like it were the World Cup he'd won, dead made up with himself, and even the neighbour laughed at that, first proper laugh in an hour. Bit of the ease creeping back in right at the death, like.

But here's the thing. When we come to tot it all up at the end, eleven hands played, her feller were still two hands up on whoever were nearest to him. Won the argument for nowt, in a manner of speaking, because whatever anybody thought of that middle streak, the numbers at the finish had him out in front regardless.

We packed the cards away, had one more brew, and everybody said ta for having them and went off down the ginnel in ones and twos same as always. But it's not left it, chuck, I'll tell thee that for nowt. Every single time the four of us sit down to play since, it comes up. Somebody'll say summat about a clean shuffle, dead sarky, or our kid'll watch the deal a bit too hard and her feller'll say give over, not this again, and round we go. Nob'dy's ever proved a thing either way and nob'dy ever will now, it's that far back, but it's there every time regardless, sat at the table with us like a fifth player. Belting neet in the end, if I'm honest, whatever else it were. Anyroad. Ta-ra for now, I'll tell thee how the next one goes.
