Me: Eh up! I don't believe it, is that thee?

The classmate: Bloody hell, it is an' all. I were just stood here trying to work out if it were you or not. How long's it been, chuck?

Me: God knows. Must be twenty year if it's a day. You've not changed a bit, mind.

The classmate: Give over, I've changed plenty, I'm just better at hiding it. What you doing round here anyroad, you still living this end o' Preston?

Me: Aye, still same house near t' ginnel behind t' old shop. Just been for a barm cake and a brew, thought I'd treat mysen. You?

The classmate: Ha, a barm cake, listen to thee. I were brought up right then, none o' this cob nonsense. My mother'd have skinned me if I called it owt else.

Me: Too right. Cob's what they call it if they've no taste. Anyroad I've just been for scran and I'm sat here made up wi' misen, and then I clap eyes on thee.

The classmate: Small world in't it. Our kid still lives round t' corner from where we grew up, he says he sees half the old school knocking about if he waits long enough.

Me: How is he, your kid?

The classmate: Champion, ta for asking. Wed now, two little ones. Time flies dun't it.

Me: It does that. Appen we're getting on ourselves and all, if we've kids old enough for school.

The classmate: Don't. Speaking of school, were you just thinking what I were thinking?

Me: Go on then.

The classmate: I walked past t' old building last week, they've done summat to t' front of it, all glass now, but I stood there and I thought straight off of him. You know who I mean.

Me: Oh aye. Him. I don't need to ask which one, there were only ever one that put the fear o' God in everybody.

The classmate: There were only one rule and all wannit. If he were talking, you didn't talk. Didn't matter if it were a whisper, didn't matter if you were only asking to borrow a rubber off the lad next to you, if his mouth were moving and yours were an' all, that were it, you were for it.

Me: And it were always the same and all. Lines. A hundred of 'em, every time, no matter what you'd said or not said. "I must not talk when the teacher is talking." A hundred times, in your best writing an' all, or he'd have you do it again.

The classmate: My hand used to ache just thinking about it. I can hear him now, clear as owt, "When I want silence I'll ask for it, and I haven't asked yet." Word for word that, I could say it in my sleep.

Me: That's it exactly, that's him to a tee. I've not thought of that in years and now I can hear it plain as day.

The classmate: D'you remember when he had that lad stood at t' front, must've been all o' twenty minutes, holding that big dictionary out at arm's length, and every time his arm dropped an inch he'd tell him to hold it up again. Whole class just sat there dead quiet, watching, too scared to laugh even though it were dead funny really.

Me: I do an' all, I were sat right at t' back and I remember thinking thank God it's not me. And then not two minutes later he's asking me a question and I went that cold I couldn't even remember me own name never mind t' answer. I still go like that now if someone puts me on t' spot, honest to God, straight back to being eleven year old.

The classmate: Ha, same here, I'll be sat in a meeting at work and someone says "any thoughts?" and summat in me just freezes solid, and I'm thirty summat year old, not a kid.

Me: It never leaves you, does it. But do you remember the one time, and I still can't believe it now, thinking back, when that lad gave him cheek and got away scot free wi' it?

The classmate: Oh God, aye, I know exactly which one you mean. He said summat back to him, dead cool as you like, and we all just froze, thought right, he's had it now, he's for the dictionary and lines and all sorts. And instead the teacher just stood there a minute, and then he laughed. Actually laughed. We couldn't believe it.

Me: We talked about that for weeks after. Nobody could work out how he'd got away wi' it. If it'd been anyone else they'd have been in that office before you could blink.

The classmate: Gradely bit of cheek that were, whatever he said. I wish I could remember it exact.

Me: Me an' all. Do you remember his little habit though, the thing he always did, tapping the chalk twice on t' board before he wrote owt? Tap tap, every single time, we used to do impressions of it behind his back at break.

The classmate: Ha! I'd forgot that till just now. Tap tap and then off he'd go. We reckoned it were some kind of ritual, like he couldn't write a word without it.

Me: Belting impression our kid used to do of it and all, though he never had him for a teacher, just heard us going on about it that much he picked it up.

The classmate: Do you remember though, the time he were actually kind, proper kind, when that lass were crying in t' corner, summat had happened at home I think, and everyone thought he'd just tell her to get on wi' it. And instead he sat down next to her, dead quiet like, and just talked to her for a bit till she were alright. Never saw owt like it before or since off him.

Me: I do remember that. Threw us all completely, didn't know what to make of it. Made you realise there were more to him than just the shouting.

The classmate: Do you know whatever happened to him, after? I heard off someone he'd retired years back, gone to live over Blackpool way.

Me: I heard that an' all, though I also heard he'd passed on a few year since. I don't know which is reet, to be honest, might be neither.

The classmate: No, nobody seems to know for certain. Appen we should try and find out one of these days.

Me: Appen. Funny looking back though, isn't it. At the time we were all proper terrified of him, and now, thinking on it, it were more respect really, wannit, than fear.

The classmate: It were. He just wanted us to do reet, in his own way. Bit rough round t' edges about it, mind.

Me: Just a bit. But you look back now and you think, gradely teacher really, whatever we called him at t' time.

The classmate: We did call him some names an' all, didn't we, behind his back.

Me: Give over, don't remind me.

The classmate: I'm not lying though, if I walked past him now, tomorrow, in t' street, I reckon summat in me would still stand up a bit straighter. Just a flicker of it, like.

Me: Same here, honest to God. Some things don't leave you.

The classmate: Anyroad, listen to us, going on about school like a couple of owd biddies. How's everything else with you, how's work?

Me: Can't complain, bit mad this last month but nowt I can't manage. How's yours?

The classmate: Bobbins at minute if I'm honest, they've changed the whole shift pattern and mithered on about it for weeks before they even told us proper. Still, mustn't grumble too much, roof over me head and that.

Me: True enough. And your mother, how's she keeping?

The classmate: Champion, still nattering on same as ever, still telling me to put wood in th'ole every time I open t' back door in winter.

Me: Ha, some things never change do they. Give her my best won't you.

The classmate: I will an' all. We'll have to sort a proper brew out sometime, catch up proper, not just standing in t' street like this.

Me: Aye, go on then, I'll get your number off our kid, he'll still have it.

The classmate: Do that. Right, I'd best get on, things to be getting on with.

Me: Aye, me an' all. Made up I bumped into you though, honest.

The classmate: Same here, chuck. Ta for stopping and having a natter.

Me: Ta-ra then, look after yourself.

The classmate: Ta-ra, love.
