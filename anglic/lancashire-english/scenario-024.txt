The bride: Right, sit thi sen down a minute, we've got to sort this list, we're over by thirty near enough, happen more.

The groom: Thirty? Give over.

The bride: I'm not lying, count 'em thi sen. So we start wi' your lot from work, the ones you don't talk to from one do to the next, and my mam's second cousins twice removed, that'll bring it down happen fifteen, near enough half.

The groom: Fine, fine, do what you want wi' them, only don't you dare touch our kid off that list.

The bride: I'm not on about our kid, I know she's stopping, it's the other one I meant.

The groom: She's stopping an' all. She has to be there, no matter what, she's family, end of.

The bride: Well if she's stopping then someone off your side has to go, cause your list's twelve more than mine already and that's not reet, it never is, it's always your side gets more seats, every time, every do we've ever had.

The groom: Here we go again.

The bride: I were just saying.

The groom: Appen we could do a smaller do after, like, for th' ones that don't fit. Bit of a brew and a butty round mine, nowt fancy.

The bride: No. We are not having two weddings, give over wi' that, it's bobbins, absolute bobbins, someone'll skrike about being asked to the second do and it'll be worse than now.

The groom: Just a thought.

The bride: Let's count it again then, proper this time.

The groom: Go on.

The bride: ...still a dozen over. A dozen, after all that.

The groom: Right. I'll take two off mine, our kid's mate and the one from th' warehouse I never see. That's summat, any road.

The bride: It's a start, I suppose. Not gradely, but a start.

The groom: Give it here, I'll have another go at it tonight. Stop mithering me now, I've had enough for one afternoon.

The bride: Ta. Put wood in th'ole, it's parky in here.
