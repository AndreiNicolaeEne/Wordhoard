Eh, chuck, gi' me a minute, I'm fair done in. Sit thi sen down, I'll tell thi, only don't be mitherin' me for owt else after this, I've nowt left in me.

That ash at top of th' street, the big un that's been leanin' since March gales, well it come down proper in th' night. Right across th' ginnel and half into th' end house wall, roots and all stuck up out o' th' pavement like summat off telly. Weren't no way one household were shiftin' that on their own, not wi' a trunk thick as a barrel and branches gone right across both pavements. Too big a job for one door to knock on, that were plain to see soon as it were light.

It were the young lad from th' end house what saw it first, gettin' up for work, and he were bangin' on doors up and down before seven, shoutin' the street were blocked and could anyone lend a hand. Word went up that street faster than owt I've seen, faster than the ice cream van on a Friday. Twenty minutes and there were folk out in their old coats, wi' rakes and bow saws and one wi' a proper chainsaw under his arm like he'd been waitin' on it.

And more than anybody reckoned on turned out too. We thought appen a dozen, half th' street, but there were near thirty of us by nine o'clock, folk from streets over an' all, once they heard. Work got shared out how it fell, really, whoever come with what tool got put where it were needed. Th' joiner from three doors up took the heaviest end of it, mind, cuttin' through the main trunk section wi' his big saw while the rest of us dragged brash and stacked branches out th' road. He were at it hours, that lad, sweat comin' off him summat shockin', and nob'dy grudged him a rest when he took one.

Course it weren't all smooth. Partway through, draggin' one o' the big limbs clear wi' a tow rope, the rope slipped its knot and the whole thing swung back and put th' front window of the end house straight through. Almighty crack, glass everywhere, and for a minute nob'dy said owt, just stood there like gormless. Then there were a right to-do over whose job it were to've tied that knot proper, and another lot sayin' they should never have been draggin' whole limbs at all, should've been sawin' 'em down first like the joiner said from th' start. Voices went up summat fierce for a bit, two on 'em nearly at it, but the eldest woman there, must be gone eighty, she just stood there wi' her rake and said give over, the pair of yer, there's a job to do, and that were that. Sorted itself out after, sawed the rest down to size first like he'd said all along.

There were brews goin' round all day, mind, two households brought their own urns out, and butties comin' out on trays, ham and cheese and one lot doin' egg. Barm cakes an' all, proper barm cakes, and I heard somebody from over th' road callin' 'em cobs and got told in no uncertain terms that round here they're barm cakes, love, not cobs, not rolls, and don't be startin'. Scran enough for twice the number of us, honest, nob'dy went hungry.

Kids got put on gatherin' up all th' little bits, twigs and bark and that, into bin bags so nob'dy went over on it, and they took to it like it were a game, racin' each other up and down th' ginnel wi' their bags. One of 'em come skrikin' to her mother over a splinter in her thumb, made a right song and dance of it for two seconds and then were straight back to it once it were out.

And the eldest woman, she weren't havin' any of sittin' it out on a chair either, not her. Kept sayin' she'd tell folk where to put things and mind the little uns weren't near th' saw, and nob'dy were about to argue wi' her twice in one mornin'.

We did have one right laugh, mind, proper stitches, all of us. Th' joiner, bendin' down to shift a big ring of trunk, his welly come clean off in th' mud and he went arse over, sock foot and all, right into a puddle, and he just sat there swearin' wi' his other welly stuck fast an' all. Whole street were creased up, even him after a minute, and somebody said he'd best not tell folk at work how he come to lose a welly in his own street.

Anyroad, we reckoned it'd be two hour, three at most, done by dinner. Weren't done till gone six, three hour past when anybody thought, and we were all knackered by then, backs gone, hands full o' splinters. But by th' end we'd th' whole road clear both ways, wall propped up safe till somebody proper could look at it, window boarded over, gutters cleared out an' all while they were up there anyroad, and a right mountain o' logs stacked up for whoever wanted 'em for their fire, three ton if there were an ounce. When th' last of it were dragged clear and th' road stood open, somebody give a shout and the whole street went up cheerin', proper cheer, like we'd won summat.

And it were a gradely feelin', chuck, honest. That street felt different all evenin', doors stood open longer than usual, kids out playin' past their time, folk sat on their steps havin' another brew instead of goin' straight in. Only for th' one evenin', appen, but it were there. Made up, the lot of us, even wi' backs like they were.

Right, that's me done, I'm off up. Put wood in th'ole on thi way out, there's a draught. Ta for listenin', love. Ta-ra.
