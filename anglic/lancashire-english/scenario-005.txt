Friend: Now then, how's tha getting on, you two? He's growing summat gradely, look at him.

Mother: Oh we're alright, ta. Tired, like, but who isn't.

Friend: Aye, course you are. Sleeping through yet, is he?

Mother: ...Nay, go on, I'll not kid thee, I'll tell thee proper. He were up five times last night. Five. I've had three hour kip if that, and it were broke up an' all, half an hour here, forty minutes there, not one proper stretch in it.

Father: And me hands won't stop shaking, tiredness that is, nowt else wrong with us. Been rocking him that long me arms have gone to jelly. Me back's bad an' all, off that chair we've had him on half the night.

Friend: Eh, chuck, that's no good that, is it. You look reet done in, the pair of you, honest to God.

Mother: I put his bottle in th' airing cupboard this morning 'stead of the kitchen. Found it two hour later wondering where it'd gone off to. Skriking with laughing at meself once I clocked it, could've cried an' all if I'm honest.

Friend: Give over, that's bobbins that is, all that broken sleep, no wonder you're mithered to bits, the both of you. Tell thee what, I'll have him Thursday afternoon, give you two a proper kip for once, and I'll bring scran round an' all, summat easy, a few butties, save you cooking owt.

Father: You don't have to do that, honest.

Friend: I know I don't have to. I'm offering, aren't I. Put wood in th'ole, sit thysen down, I'll sort a brew before I go.

Mother: ...Go on then. Ta, love. We'll not say no again, we're past arguing, we're that done in.

Friend: Course you are. Ta-ra for now then, see thee Thursday, and get thi head down when tha can.
