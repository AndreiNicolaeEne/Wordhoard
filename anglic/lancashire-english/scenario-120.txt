Me: Eh, sit thi sen down a minute, I'll tell thee the whole of it, right from t' start, cos tha weren't there for none of it and it's about time somebody heard the lot in one go and not just the tail end of it.

So. I decided, one Tuesday, dead casual like, over us tea, that I were going to run a marathon. Never run further than the corner shop and back in me whole life, and I says it out loud to the entire table, to the mother and our kid and everybody sat there, "I'm doing a marathon, me." And once tha's said a thing like that in front of thi own mother tha can't very well go quiet on it after, can tha, cos she'll ask thee every single Sunday how it's coming on, and if tha's nowt to tell her it's worse than if tha'd never opened thi gob in the first place. So that were it, decided in front of witnesses. No training done, not one day of it, and already there were no quiet road out.

My friend: Bloody hell. Straight in then, no messing.

Me: Straight in, aye. And I'll tell thee, them first few weeks were a right shock to the system, nowt like I expected. I thought I were fit enough, walking about all day at work and that, but the first proper run damn near finished us. Legs like lead, chest going like a pair of bellows, and I were sat on the kerb after ten minute getting me breath back thinking, what has tha gone and done here. Every session after left us more wrecked than the one before it, not less, which weren't how I thought this were meant to work at all. I thought it got easier as tha went on. It got harder before it got owt else.

But I kept at it. Settled into a routine, out most mornings whatever were doing outside, rain or shine or that flat grey nowt-weather we get more of than either. Mother stood at the door some mornings shouting to put wood in th'ole behind us, letting all the warm out into the ginnel, and off I'd go, half asleep still, up past the backs in the dark. Didn't miss many days. Told mesen that were the only thing kept the whole business honest, cos anybody can say they're doing a marathon, it's the getting up on the wet Tuesday when tha'd rather not that proves it one road or t'other.

Then it all near stopped dead before it were barely started. Went over on me ankle coming down off the moor, daft thing, nowt dramatic to it, just a wrong step on a loose bit of path, and it swelled up summat rotten by that night. Physio took one look and pulled a face like I'd said summat stupid to him. Weeks off it, he says, not days. And for a good while there I weren't sure I'd get back to it at all, if I'm honest wi' thee. Sat about the house feeling sorry for mesen, watching the weeks tick past that I should've been building up in, thinking the whole idea were bobbins from the start and I'd been a fool to say owt about it to anybody.

My friend: But tha did get back to it.

Me: I did, eventually, appen more careful than before. Cut the plan right back when I started again, no showing off, no trying to make up all the lost weeks at once the way some daft beggars do. Just steady. And for a good stretch after that our kid ran alongside us most mornings, which made more difference than I can rightly say. Some days I'd nobbut have gone back to bed if it were down to just me deciding, but knowing our kid were stood waiting at the end of the ginnel meant I got mesen up and out. The days it were hardest going were the very days having company mattered most, funny that, and we'd get back and have us a brew and a barm cake apiece, proper scran, none of that other stuff, and put the world to rights before either of us had even had us shower.

There were one day though, one training day out of the whole lot of them, that near ended the whole attempt outright. Long run, further than I'd ever gone, and about two mile from home everything went at once, legs and head both. I were skriking, I don't mind telling thee, proper skriking on the towpath like a bairn, sat on a wall not able to go another step forward nor manage the walk back neither. Rung our kid to come and get us in the car. That were the closest the whole season come to just stopping there and then, and it weren't the ankle that near did for it that time, it were us, plain and simple.

The middle of the whole season were the worst for doubt and all, worse than any one bad session. Training going along same as ever, week after week, but the finish still felt that far off it barely felt real, like I were running toward summat that only existed on paper somewhere. That's the bit nob'dy tells thee about, the middle, cos it's neither the start, which is exciting, nor the end, which is the whole point of it, it's just miles and miles of grey nowt with the finish never seeming to come a step nearer for all the effort going in.

Then, past the worst of that, summat small happened. Nowt dramatic, no fireworks to it, just an ordinary Thursday session that went better than any before it, legs felt reet, breathing easy for once, and I come home made up wi' mesen in a way I hadn't been in weeks. Belting, that session were, and daft as it sounds how much one gradely run can put back into thi head after all that grinding away at nowt.

Toward the end I tightened everything right up. Watching what I were eating proper, getting to bed at a sensible hour instead of putting it off for one more programme, and then the last week I did the opposite of all that hard work and eased right off it. Barely ran a step that final week, which felt all wrong after months of the other thing, but tha has to trust the months already sat in thi legs, the physio kept saying, and for once in me life I listened to him.

Night before, I barely slept a wink. Lay there going over the whole route in me head hours before there were any need for it, going over what I'd eat and when and what if this went wrong and what if that did, mother shouting up the stairs at one point to give over and get some kip, love, it'll come whether tha sleeps or not.

My friend: And the day itself, then?

Me: The day itself. I'll tell thee, the worst of it, the proper worst, came right near the very end, not the middle like tha'd think, when every bit of us wanted to stop and just wouldn't. Legs gone, head gone, and still somehow one foot going in front of the other, and I couldn't tell thee to this day where that come from, only that it did, from somewhere.

And then it were done, and I stood there and it were nowt like I'd pictured, not one bit. All them months I'd imagined summat grand, arms up, dead made up, the whole works. What I actually felt were just tired, tired past owt I'd ever known, and a bit hollow if truth be told, and somebody putting summat round us shoulders asking us a question I couldn't rightly answer. Took days after to feel right again and all, days before me legs felt like me own legs and not somebody else's borrowed off us, hobbling about the house like an old fella, our kid stood laughing at us going down the stairs sideways of a morning.

But I'll tell thee this for nowt. I don't look at owt hard the same road now, not since. Used to think there were things I just weren't the sort to do, and I know different now, don't I. Ta for listening to the whole of it, I know it's a long tale for one sitting.

My friend: Ta-ra, chuck, I'm made up for thee, I really am.
