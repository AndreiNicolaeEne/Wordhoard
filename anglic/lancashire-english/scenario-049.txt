I'd been putting it off for weeks, mithering meself more than I ever mithered him about it, but a fella can only watch his own purse go thin waiting on somebody else's memory. We were sat in his kitchen, a brew going cold between us and half a barm cake still on the plate, when I finally said it plain.

Me: I don't like bringing it up again, chuck, but it's forty quid now, and it's been since March.

My mate: Aye, I know, I know. It's just been mad busy, what with work and everything.

Me: You said that last time an' all. You said you'd have it me by payday, the one before Easter. That's been and gone twice over now.

My mate: Give over, will yer. I'm not made o' money, y'know, I've me own stuff going on.

Me: I'm not saying tha is. I'm saying you said a date and it came and went and I heard nowt off yer.

He went quiet then, proper quiet, staring at the table like it had said summat to him. When he spoke again his voice had gone tight, like I'd caught him out at summat worse than owed money.

My mate: You make it sound like I'm trying to do one on yer. I'm not. I've just had nowt spare, that's all it is.

Me: I know tha's not skint on purpose. I'm just saying forty quid's forty quid, and it's been long enough now that appen I stop asking so nice about it.

He dug about in his pocket after that, pulled out a twenty and pushed it across the table, said he'd sort the rest end of the month, definite this time.

My mate: End of the month. I mean it, reet.

Me: Aye. Well. We'll see, won't we.

Ta for the twenty, any road. But I'll believe the rest when it's in me hand and not before. Ta-ra.
