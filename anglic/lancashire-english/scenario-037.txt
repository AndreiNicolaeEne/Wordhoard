Dad: Come on, get thi sen up, we're losing daylight stood here nattering.

Me: I'm up, I'm up, give over shouting.

The youngest: I'm helping today. Proper helping.

Dad: Tha's on the forking, our kid, tha knows tha are, no use putting owt else on thi.

Our kid: Aye go on then, might as well get warmed up.

First hour's always the worst, cold in thi hands, but once tha's moving it goes.

We cleared the top section by mid morning and everyone shifted round then, like we always do, the ones who'd been forking went onto stacking and the ones stacking went onto raking the leavings, keeps it fresh, stops folk seizing up doing the same thing four hour straight.

It were round about then my auntie started falling behind a bit, not saying owt, just going quieter, and her rake going slower and slower. Nobody made a thing of it, the mother just drifted over like she were checking summat else and started raking alongside her, not a word said, just fell in next to her and matched her pace till she'd caught back up without anyone having to say she were struggling.

The mother: Y'alright there.

My auntie: Aye, just me back, it'll pass.

The mother: Aye well, tha's not on thi own with it then.

Come about eleven the mother went back for the scran, and that were a right relief, everyone stopping where they stood and drifting toward the trailer where she'd laid it all out, barm cakes with ham in, a flask of tea going round, biscuits for the lad. Dad: Tha's forking like tha's stirring soup, put some back into it.

Our kid: Reet, watch this then.

Sky started turning while we were still sat there, that flat grey coming over from the west that everyone round here knows means rain within the hour, not appen rain, definite rain. Dad were up on his feet before anyone else clocked it proper.

So we swung it round, everyone shifting down to the low field at a fair old pace, and it held off longer than we thought, just spitting a bit then nothing, and by early afternoon it had cleared right off and gone almost warm, so we pushed on hard through the low field while it lasted, making up for the time we'd lost swapping about.

Dad: Come on, faster now, we've not got the light we did an hour since.

We finished as the light were properly going, that blue coming down over the field, and Dad stood counting the trailer loads out loud.

Dad: That's nine off the top and seven off the low, that's a gradely day that, best we've had in a few year.

The mother: Right, before anyone sits down proper, somebody needs mucking the gate shut and the trailer sheeted over case it rains in the night.

The youngest: I'll do it.

Dad: Go on then lad, tha's earned first go at owt today.

Ta to everyone Dad said, going round shaking hands like it were a match won, and we all just sort of nodded, too knackered for owt else, and headed in for a proper brew before ta-ra and off home.
