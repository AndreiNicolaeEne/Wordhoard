Three on 'em sat in t'caff off the ginnel, barm cakes going and a gradely pot of tea between 'em, and it weren't long before the talk went where it allus goes.

The eldest: Eeh, me knees, chuck. Been at me now going on ten year, summat chronic when it's parky like this. Feel like two rusty gate hinges going up them stairs of a morning.

The middle one: Ten year, give over, that's nowt. Try this hip o' mine, three or four nights a month it has me up and down summat rotten, can't get comfy whichever road I lay meself. I'm that mithered wi' it I've been near skriking into th' pillow some nights, straight up I have.

The youngest: You two want to hear yerselves, honest. I've had this back since before either of yer knew me, since I were a lass on the looms, that's how far back it goes. Nowt either of yers has said comes anywhere near it.

The eldest: Appen you're only after the sympathy, love.

The middle one: Reet, who's got it worst then, out wi' it, no fibbing now.

The youngest: Me, no question, and that's me final word on t'matter.

The eldest: Bobbins, mine's the belting one, worst in Preston I'd say.

The middle one: Yer both wrong, it's mine and I'll not have it argued.

None on 'em budged an inch, and in th'end they were all laughing fit to burst, tea gone stone cold in th' pot, and the one nearest the counter got up and put wood in th'ole, for the draught were creeping in off the ginnel something shocking.

The eldest: Right, I'm off then. Ta for the scran, chuck.

The middle one: Ta-ra, love. Mind them knees.

The eldest: Give over.
