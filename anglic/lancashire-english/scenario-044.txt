Someone's left the yard door swinging and there's flour dust lifting off every surface.

The baker: Put wood in th'ole, will tha, before all this scran's covered in muck. Right. Watch us first, don't go wading in wi' both hands.

She splits the barm careful, not reet through, butters her to th'edge, lays the bacon down the middle, presses her shut in one go.

The baker: See that? Not through, just open her up like a book. Have a go thysen.

The new lad: splits his clean in half, two flat halves sat on the board.

The baker: Nay, chuck, tha's cut her clean in two now, look. Through the middle, not to th'bottom. Try again, gentle this time.

The new lad: Is it same for a cob, or...

The baker: There's no cob in this shop, love, it's a barm. Split it, don't halve it. Reet down the middle. That's twice now I've showed thee.

He butters it fine, then piles bacon right out to the edges so it won't press shut.

The baker: For God's sake, how many times, look at state of that, it won't shut. Tha's put nowt in the middle and everything at th'edge, that's same mistake dressed different.

The new lad: Sorry, sorry, I were only tryin' to get it full, I thought...

The baker: No need to skrike over a barm cake. Give over apologisin' and watch us again, proper this time. There's nowt hard about it, and I don't want owt else off thee but watchin'. Butter to th'edge, filling stays in th'middle, not spread to buggery.

She shows it again, slower, hand over his.

The baker: Now you. Middle. Not edges.

He splits it right, butters to the edge, keeps the bacon to the centre, presses her shut clean.

The baker: There. That's gradely, that is. Them first two were bobbins but that one's belting. Appen tha just needed showin' proper instead of askin' about cobs.

The new lad: Ta.

The baker: Ta-ra wi' that. Get us a brew on while there's a lull, and we'll get through rest of 'em same road.
