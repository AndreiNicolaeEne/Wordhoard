Me: Eh up, hang on... is that thee?

Old friend: Give over, I don't believe it! What are you doing stood in this queue?

Me: Getting us barm cakes for us dinner, look at state of it in here. I knew there were summat familiar about that face soon as you walked in.

Old friend: I know, honest to God, must be over ten year since I've seen thee. Last time were before our kid's wedding, and he's divorced and wed again since then.

Me: Get away, ten year? More like twelve knowing us. How's tha been keeping, what's tha up to now?

Old friend: On the vans, over at the depot, it's alreet, keeps us busy. Two kids now, eldest starts high school come September, God help us.

Me: Bloody hell. I'm still at same place, the warehouse, worse luck. Married now an' all, and chuck's expecting us second in the autumn.

Old friend: Get away, that's belting news that, proper made up for thee.

Me: Ta. Ey, does tha remember when we used to skive off through that ginnel at back o'th' school, the one by the chippy?

Old friend: Don't! We got caught by the caretaker that time and he marched us straight back in, and I were skriking I were that scared, and you just stood there laughing at us.

Me: I were an' all, couldn't help it, state of your face. Proper belting days them were.

Old friend: They were that. Ey, has tha heard owt from that lad who used to knock about with us both, always getting us into bother?

Me: Nowt, not a word in years. Someone said he'd gone over Wigan way but I don't know if that's even true. Appen he's still about somewhere.

Old friend: Shame that, he were a gradely lad. Anyroad, give us your number before we lose each other again.

Me: Go on then, put it in. I'll not lose it this time neither, I mean it.

Old friend: Aye well, we said that last time an' all, and nowt came of it, so mither me if I don't message thee.

Me: I will an' all. Right, best get this scran home before it goes cold. Ta-ra then, chuck, look after thaself.

Old friend: Ta-ra, love. Good to see thee, proper made up.
