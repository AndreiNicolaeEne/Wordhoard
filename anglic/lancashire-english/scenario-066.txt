New one: Go on then, everyone keeps mitherin' on about some animal moved in down the street, what's all that about?

Me: Ooh, chuck, sit thi sen down, I'll put wood in th'ole, it's draughty in here wi' that door open, and I'll tell thee the whole of it, cos it's a tale and a half and I'm made up somebody's finally asked me proper instead of getting it in bits off everyone else.

Right, so it were back end o' last year, and it started wi' nowt really. The lad two doors up, he says he's seen summat big moving through the gardens at back, middle o' the night, and everyone just laughed it off. His mother were mortified. "Give over," she says, "tha's been at the telly too long, our kid, there's nowt out there but t'cat from number nine." Everyone waved it off as him mekkin it up, cos he were allus one for a tale, and nob'dy thought owt of it. Bobbins, the lot of it, that's what they said.

Then not long after, the woman over the ginnel, her that nob'dy argues wi' cos she's got a head on her like a lawyer, she seen it an' all. Broad daylight, mind, not some half-asleep middle-of-the-night business. She says it were sat bold as owt on top of her wall, looking at her like it owned the place. Well, once she'd said it, that were that, cos if she says summat's true, it's true, end of. Nob'dy argues wi' her.

After that it were seen reet reg'lar. Poking about in the bins of a night, tipping 'em over, having a rummage through folk's gardens, digging up what it shouldn't. Proper mess some mornings, bin bags all up the ginnel, and everyone blaming it before they'd even looked prop'ly.

Street split down the middle over it, dead quick an' all. Half of 'em said leave it be, appen it's not doing nowt but what animals do, it'll shift on its own, no need to mek a fuss. Other half were dead set it wanted dealing wi', proper dealing wi', before it got worse. You could feel it every time folk stood talking at the shop over their barm cakes, one lot rolling their eyes at the other lot. Somebody even said cob instead of barm one morning and got looked at like she'd sworn in church, but that's another story.

Then the cat went missing off next door but one. Well. That were it then. Nob'dy had a shred of proof, mind, nowt at all, but everyone had already decided, hadn't they. "It'll have had that poor cat," they were saying, like they'd seen it wi' their own eyes, when really they'd seen nowt but an empty basket and a woman skrikin' on her step for two days. Proper row it caused, folk arguing summat rotten over whether it were even the animal's doing or whether that cat had just wandered off same as cats do.

All that came to a head at the do they had at the community room, meant to be about summat else entirely and ended up being an hour and a half of shouting about this animal. One lot stood up saying it were dangerous, wanted rid of it proper, and other lot saying give over, it's not bothered nob'dy, it's just trying to get by same as owt else. Voices going up, folk talking over each other, somebody's brew going stone cold on the side table while they argued the toss and never touched their butty. Nowt got settled, but ooh, it were a belter of a row, the sort folk were still on about at the shop a week after.

Meanwhile, the young couple at the end, they decided they'd sort it their sen, didn't want no fuss, no meetings, nowt. Bought some contraption off the internet, some trap thing, reckoned they'd have it caught in a night and that'd be that, gradely job done and nob'dy the wiser. Well. Woke up next morning, trap were empty, fence panel down, and a right mess in their garden an' all, worse than owt they'd had before. So that put paid to owt being sorted quiet like, and gave the other lot plenty to say about it an' all, mitherin' the poor couple summat shocking every time they showed their face.

Then come the night that settled it, near enough. The widow at the top, on her way in from her sister's, comes face to face wi' it right there in the ginnel, close as I am to thee now. Give her the fright of her life, she said her heart were going like a hammer for an hour after, and she near enough skriked wi' the shock of it. Now, whether it were owt more than startled, nob'dy could say for certain, cos it never so much as growled at her, just looked and were off, but try telling that to folk once word got round. That's when the whole dangerous-or-not argument really took hold proper, one side saying it were a danger to folk now it'd come that close, other side saying give over, it were just unlucky where it wandered that night same as she were unlucky standing there, it never done owt to her.

So in the end, wi' everyone at loggerheads and gettin nowhere, somebody had the sense to bring in a fella who knows about this sort of thing prop'ly, does it for his living, not just somebody's opinion down the shop over a brew. Come and had a proper look round, told 'em straight what they were dealing wi' and what weren't nowt to worry about, and what would help and what wouldn't. Street settled on it in the end, more or less. Bins to be kept locked up prop'er, nowt left out to tempt it, gardens tidied a bit so there weren't so much for it to poke about in, and somebody keeping half an eye on things of a night without mekkin a song and dance of it.

And that's about where it's sat since, chuck. Bins are all sorted now wi' the lids strapped down reet tight, the odd sighting still comes in now and again but it's nowt like it were. It still gets seen every so often, mind, but folk just say "ooh there it is again" and carry on wi' their scran, not a bit bothered like they were. It's got to be more of a tale now than owt else, summat folk tell over a butty at the bus stop for a laugh, "eh, remember when it had the cat" and all that, even though nob'dy proved it had owt to do wi' that cat at all. Gradely thing really, how it all calmed down in the end. Anyroad, that's thi lot, that's the whole of it. Ta for listening, love, get that brew down thee before it goes cold. Ta-ra for now.
