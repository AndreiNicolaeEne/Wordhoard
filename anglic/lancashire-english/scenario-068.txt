Me: Now listen, love, cause I'm only saying this once and I'll not be mithering thee about it again after.

There's this fella goes round, tha'll clock him quick enough. Always got a brew in his hand off somebody else's kitchen, always chatting up whoever's just moved in down th' ginnel, dead friendly, matey as owt, calls everyone chuck an' all, like he's known thee years. He'll tell thee he's got a deal on, a phone, a van, a bit of work cash in hand, tickets for summat that's sold out everywhere else, and it's dirt cheap, too gradely to be reet if tha thinks on it two minutes. That's the whole trick, love, it's built to be too good to question. He'll want a bit up front, just to hold it like, and once tha's give him owt, that's it. He's gone. Tha'll never clock him again, not down that ginnel, not at th' bus stop, nowhere.

Our kid's mate fell for it two winters back. Gave him fifty quid for a phone he swore were still in't box.

The one it happened to: I thought I'd got a right bargain, didn't I. Made up with myself an' all.

Never saw him nor the money again. Skriking her eyes out by teatime when she twigged, and nowt left over for scran that week neither.

Signs, love, he's too matey too soon, he's always got somewhere else to be right after tha's paid, and he'll not give thee a proper number nor say where he lives. If he comes at thee, tell him ta-ra and give over, don't hand over a penny to nobody new like that. Go and check with the one at the corner shop first, he knows every face round here and he'll tell thee straight if it's bobbins.

Ta, love. Put wood in th'ole on thy way out.
