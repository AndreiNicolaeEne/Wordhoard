Me: Eh, sit thysen down a minute, I'll tell thee this one proper, cos tha missed a reet belter and no mistake, and it's still doing the rounds now.

It started over nowt, like they always do. We were sat in ours of a Tuesday, me and my mate, half a lager in and a chip barm cake going cold on the plate between us, darts board up on the wall doing nowt for nobody. He turns round to me, casual as owt, and says...

My mate: Bet I'd have thee at darts.

Me: I laughed right in his face. Told him he'd not hit a barn door from six foot, never mind a double top, he's never been reet at darts, not once in his life, arm like a broken gate. He were made up with himself though, wouldn't let it drop...

My mate: Go on then, put thy money where thy mouth is.

Me: And before I knew where I were we'd shook on it right there over the barm cakes. I want thee to know summat an' all, I went into that game thinking it were done before a dart were even thrown. Our kid were sat next table over, heard the whole thing, and he's straight in...

Our kid: Twenty on it then, and I'm holding stake.

Me: Twenty quid. Small enough not to sting, that's all it were at that point, twenty on who won, nowt else said about it. Word got round the pub quick as owt though, way it does round ours, and before we'd even chalked the first leg there were six or seven stood round the board with pints in hand giving it the big commentary, like it were the Embassy. One of the lads at the back, giving me all sorts...

One of the lads: He's shaking, look at him. Get 'em in, lad.

Me: And it went back and forth a fair while, I'll give him that much credit. He got a nowty run of doubles I never saw coming, I got mine back, nip and tuck the first two legs, proper contest. Third leg, decider though, and I could feel it going wrong before it were properly gone wrong, if that makes sense to thee. He needed forty, stood there cool as owt, first dart, double top, bang, done. I just stood there looking at that board like it had personally wronged me. Whole place roared. I groaned summat terrible, put my hands right over my face, and said it straight out, no point stood there pretending otherwise, aye, alright, he'd won it fair.

Now here's where it gets daft. The landlady, God love her, weren't having the twenty at all...

The landlady: Nay, forget the twenty love, we'll have summat better than that off him.

Me: And before I'd even got my head round what were happening, the whole lot of them were stood there deciding out loud, right in front of me, exactly what I owed instead. Not a penny of it. A performance, they said, right there in the middle of the pub in front of everyone, and they weren't vague about it neither, they'd it all laid out plain, up on the little stage where the karaoke sits, full Tina Turner, wig and dress and the leg kicks, proper leg kicks an' all, not half-arsed. I tried it on, like tha does, said I'd sooner just gi' 'em the twenty and be done with it, no fuss for nobody. Landlord just laughed straight in my face...

The landlord: Nay chuck, tha's had thy chance, tha's dancing now.

Me: And that were that, decided, no way out of it, everybody creased up about it. Set it for the Friday after, same pub. I thought about nowt else all week, dreading it summat rotten, and our kid mithering me every single day down the phone...

Our kid: Has tha practised thy kicks yet?

Me: Come the Friday the place were packed, word had gone right round the street by then. Landlady had scran going round an' all, bacon butties and that, forfeit night or not. She'd found a wig from somewhere, God knows where she'd kept a Tina Turner wig behind that bar all these years, and a sequin dress off some hen do gone by. I got changed round the back by the barrels, felt a right pillock, come out past the ginnel door into the main room and the whole place went up before I'd even opened my gob.

Music went on and I gave it everything, I'll say that much for myself, proper kicks, strutting up and down, lads banging the tables. Door went at the back halfway through, some poor soul just trying to get a pint in, freezing draught right down the room, and the landlord's bellowing over the top of the music without even looking round...

The landlord: Put wood in th'ole, we've a show on!

Me: That got its own laugh an' all. But the moment, the one that's stuck, right, is this. Big kick, high one, right on the chorus, and the wig, the whole wig, flies clean off my head and lands, plop, straight in some fella's pint at the front. Dead centre. He just looks down at it floating there in his lager, looks up at me, looks back down, picks it out with two fingers like it's summat crawled out the ginnel, holds it up dripping, deadpan as owt...

The fella at the front: I think this is thine, love.

Me: Place come apart. Proper grown men, doubled right over, one of the lads laughing that hard he were near enough skriking, had to be told to give over. Our kid couldn't breathe. I just stood there bald as a coot in a sequin dress with the whole room in bits, and even I were laughing by then, couldn't help it, it were that gradely a moment tha couldn't stay narked about it if tha tried.

And that's the thing, see. The twenty quid, nobody even remembers there ever were a twenty quid on it. Some nights are bobbins and some are belting, and that were about as belting as they come. What's stuck is the wig in the pint. Every time we're all in together since, somebody brings it up again, and by now it's grown legs of its own, last I heard someone were telling it that I did the splits an' all, which I never did, not once, but that's how it goes, a bit more added on every time it gets told. Appen in a year or two I'll have wrestled the landlord for the wig back an' all, the way it's going.

Anyroad, that's thy lot, that's the whole of it start to finish. Ta for sitting through that, love, I'll get thee a brew in. Ta-ra.
