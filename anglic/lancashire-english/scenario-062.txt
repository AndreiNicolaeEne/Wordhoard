Me: Come in out of that, love, and put wood in th'ole behind thee, we don't heat the whole street. Sit thee down a minute and I'll tell thee, but I'll only tell thee my side, because that's all as I've got, and appen it's not the whole of it neither, not any more.

We were belting, them years, best years I've had on this street if I'm honest. Them next door and us, we were thick as owt. Not just a nod over th' fence, I mean in and out of one another's kitchens like they were our own. Our kid used to play with their lad up and down that ginnel every night after tea till it were nigh on dark, and their mother'd have him in for his scran if I were working late, and I'd have theirs in for a brew and a butty if their mam were poorly, which she were, more than once, and glad of it an' all. She were made up, I remember, the day our kid come through the door with news of his first proper job, near as made up as if it were her own lad. That's the sort of close we were. Cards through the letterbox any day but Christmas an' all, just because.

It started with nowt, really, that's the daft thing about it. There were an old fence going, rotten as owt, and he put a new one up, fair enough, tha can't argue with that, only he put it up near a foot over onto our side, and stood a shed against it an' all, right up close to where the line used to run. I never made a fuss straight off. I just said to him, ever so pleasant, over th' fence like we always talked, "when tha's a minute, appen tha could shift that back a bit, only it's sat right on our path and I can't get the bins round." Polite as owt. He said aye, no bother, he'd sort it.

He didn't sort it. Weeks went by. I asked again, same as before, no edge on it at all, just a reminder, because folk forget, don't they, and I gave him that much. Then more weeks. Then I asked a third time and I'll not lie to thee, chuck, there were an edge on it that time, because three times is three times, and I'm not the sort as likes asking twice let alone three, and I said summat like, "I've asked thee this before now, tha knows," and that were the first time I heard owt back that weren't just aye-aye-no-bother.

After that it starts coming to me sideways, through her that lives between us, she's always been the one as carries word up and down that ginnel, bless her, though I've come to think she enjoys it a bit too much. She comes round and says he's saying I never asked him at all, not the first time nor the second, that I just come out shouting one day for no reason. Now that's not gradely, is it, that's not owt like what happened, but by the time it's gone through her and back it's turned into summat else altogether. So I go straight round and say it to him myself, I says, "I asked thee twice before, polite as owt, tha knows I did," and he stands there bold as brass and says it never happened, not once, that I never said a word to him about it till I come round shouting. Flat denial, straight to my face. Now I don't mither about much, but I don't forget being called a liar in my own street either.

So I'll not pretend I were a saint about it after that. I never did owt daft, mind, nowt tha could point to, but I stopped putting his bin out with mine of a Tuesday when the wagon come early, which I'd done for years without a word said about it, and I never said why, and he never asked. Small thing. Petty, appen, but it were mine to be petty with.

And then it come back to me, through her again, what he'd said about it round his own kitchen table, and this is the one that's stuck with me all this while, worse than the shed, worse than the fence, worse than any of it. He'd said I were all mouth and nowt gradely to show for it, meaning our kid, meaning what our kid does for his living, and that cut deeper than owt about a shed ever could, because that's not about a fence any more, is it, that's about somebody tha's known since he were in short trousers. Our kid come home skriking that day when he heard, and I'll not forget that in a hurry either.

I did try, chuck, I want thee to know that, I did try, once. Made up a batch of proper barm cakes, not cobs, not rolls, barm cakes, because that's what his mother always liked best off me, and I sent our kid round with them, a peace offering, plain and simple, no note, nowt said. They come back within the hour. Not eaten, not even opened far as I could tell. That were that, really. After that I give over trying.

Rest of the street's gone about how tha'd expect. Some's with us, some's with them, and it's split near enough down the middle, and there's one or two poor souls caught in between as won't say a word either way, and good luck to them, I say. It all come to a head proper one Saturday dinnertime, out in the street where anybody walking past could hear it, both of us going at it hammer and tongs, him saying his piece and me saying mine at the top of our voices. It were bobbins, the whole thing, nowt gradely about it at all, folk stood at their windows watching. That's when the shed come up again, and the fence, and then he says summat about damage to it, says I'd been at it with summat, knocked a panel clean off, and I told him straight, reet to his face, I never touched his shed, never laid a finger on it, and I'll say that to my dying day, because it's true.

It's been the better part of four year now, this silence between us. Four year of not a word said either way, four year of both of us pegging washing out and looking anywhere but at next door's yard. Ta and ta-ra to everybody else on this street, mind, just not to them. It shows itself in daft little ways, day to day, if tha wants to know. Our kid still goes the long way round to dodge that ginnel rather than pass their gate. I still catch myself of a Tuesday, hand on next door's bin, and stop myself before I've thought owt of it.

And I'll say this to thee, chuck, and I'll not say it to many, because I've told this tale a fair few times now and I always come out sounding the wronged one in it, that's just how the telling goes when it's thy own mouth doing it. But between thee and me, this once, I'm not right sure any more who started it. I know how it went after, every bit of it, I could tell thee that all day and reet accurate an' all. But who moved first, whose fault the fence were, whether he really did forget or whether it were meant all along, appen I knew it once, and I've told the tale that many times the true version's got lost somewhere in among all the telling. That's the honest truth of it, for what it's worth. Ta for listening, anyroad.
