Reet, I'll tell thee it proper, start to finish, cos I've only told bits of it to folk and none of it straight.

Two year I were there. Two year on the despatch floor, same shift pattern, same locker, same walk down th' ginnel at back to have me break away from everyone stood by the bins. Two year, and I'll not say it were all bad, cos it weren't. First year were even gradely, if I'm honest, decent enough money, decent enough folk on me line, and our supervisor at the start were sound as owt, always got the brews in.

It weren't one thing that did it. Nowt ever is, whatever folk tell thi after. It were a long line of little things, each one you could shrug off on its own, each one you did shrug off, and then one day there's nowt left to shrug with.

The final straw, though, were daft when you say it out loud. They brought in this new form for toilet breaks. You had to sign out on a clipboard by the door, time you left, time you came back, like we were kiddies. Five minutes over and it went to the supervisor, and the supervisor had to log it and all. That were it. That were the whole of it. Not the pay, not the shifts they'd already messed about with twice that year, not the Christmas bonus that quietly stopped being a thing nob'dy mentioned again. A clipboard by the bog door. But it were the last one on top of all t'others, and summat in us just went, right, that's me done.

So I spent about a week and a half rehearsing what I were going to say. In the car, in the shower, walking down to get a barm cake at dinner, going over it in me head like I were revising for an exam. I wanted it dead polite, dead measured, nowt that could come back on us for a reference. I practised summat like, I've really valued my time here and I've learned a lot, but I feel it's time for a new challenge. Practised it that many times it stopped sounding like owt I'd actually say, more like summat off a leaflet.

I asked to have a word with the manager on the Thursday. Not the supervisor, mind, went straight over her head, cos this needed to be said to whoever were actually in charge of the site. Popped me head in the office, asked would she have five minutes, and she said aye, sit thi down, love, what's up.

And I sat down and said me opening line, the one I'd polished up nice and diplomatic. Said it's not about any one thing in particular, I've just been thinking a lot lately and I feel like it's time for a change. Which were half true and half a right lie, cos it were about one thing in particular, that clipboard, only I couldn't say that, could I, cos it'd have sounded so small next to two year of work. You can't hand your notice in over a bit of paper by the bog. You'd sound like you'd lost the plot. So I dressed it up as summat bigger and vaguer, a change, a new challenge, and left the real thing sat there behind me teeth where it belonged.

The manager weren't that surprised, if I'm honest. She sort of nodded like she'd half expected it, said she were sad to hear it, said I'd been a good worker, reliable, never any bother. Then she went into the counter offer, like they do.

The manager: Is it money? Cos we could maybe look at bumping you up a band, I'd have to check with upstairs but I reckon we could sort summat.

Me: No, no, it's not the money, honest, the money's been fine.

The manager: We could change your shifts round an' all, if it's the nights doing your head in, we've had a few ask for more days lately.

Me: Nah, it's not the shifts either, it's just, I think I need summat different for meself, you know, growth-wise.

Growth-wise. Where that came from I don't know, never used that word in me life before or since. But I turned it all down, every offer, and give her the nice packaged reason instead of the true one, cos the true one were a clipboard by a bog door and I weren't stood there saying that to a manager's face after two year.

We settled on two weeks' notice, which were the normal amount, nowt unusual there, and I signed summat and shook her hand and went back out to me line feeling like I'd just done a job interview backwards.

That night, though, lying in bed, I had a right wobble. Kept thinking, have I done summat daft here. Two year of steady work, chucked over a bit of paperwork I could've just ignored half the time anyway. Appen I'd been hasty. Appen I should've just kept me head down another few month and let it blow over like it usually did. That doubt sat on us the whole two weeks, tell thi the truth, more than any excitement did.

Last day come round quick enough. Everyone said their bits. Take care, keep in touch, we'll miss thi round here. Most of it were hollow as owt, said the way you say ta to the bus driver, nowt behind it, just the shape of a nice thing cos it's what you say. Our kid off the next line brought us a card everyone'd signed, half the names I could barely make out, and a couple of the lads said we'd have to get a pint sometime, which we both knew were never going to happen.

But there were one bit that were honest. Right at the end, going out the door with me stuff in a bag for life, the old supervisor, the sound one from that first year, caught us by the ginnel and just said, straight, not dressed up at all.

The supervisor: Don't blame thi. I'd have gone an' all if I could afford to.

That were it. Six words and it near enough said the whole thing I'd spent a week and a half not saying to the manager. I just said ta, and give over before I said summat back I'd have regretted, and went home.

And how's it felt since. Mostly relief, if I'm honest with thee, more relief than owt else. No clipboard, no five minutes over logged like a naughty kid, and I sleep better not thinking about it of a Sunday night. There's a bit of regret and all, mind, cos the new place isn't gradely, it's just alright, bobbins some days if I'm honest, and I do miss our kid off the line and the brews and the daft carry-on we used to have. But put it this way, if I had that same conversation to have again, I'd still say the same polished nowt-in-particular line to the manager's face, cos that's just how you do it round here, you don't tell a manager it's about a clipboard by the bog, you tell her it's about growth. But I'd have gone in sooner. Two year were appen a year too long, and I knew it a long while before I said owt.

Ta-ra to that place. It weren't bad, it just weren't for us no more.
