The aunt: Now then, chuck, sit thissen down, I've just wet t' brew, there's a barm cake if tha wants it.

The niece: Go on then, ta.

The aunt: I've been havin' a word wi' our kid at t' bakery, and hoo tells me t' lad hoo were on about is a gradely sort, reet gradely. Works all hours down at t' depot, never late, never skives a shift, and his gaffer thinks world of him, belting worker by all accounts.

The niece: Oh aye.

The aunt: Aye, and temperament wi' it an' all, mild as owt, hoo's never known him say a wrong word to anyone in his life. And his family, tha knows 'em, decent stock, allus put summat by, never a bit o' bother from that lot. Work, temperament, family, what more's a body want?

The niece: I've nowt against him, honest.

The aunt: Well what's up then, chuck? What could tha have against someone like that?

The niece: I'm just... I've too much on at minute wi' work an' all, I've not the time to be thinkin' on courtin' proper.

The aunt: Time. Thy cousin said same exact thing about t' one I put forward for her, said hoo were too busy an' all, give over hoo said. Married ten year now and made up wi' it, tells anyone who'll listen hoo were glad hoo didn't win that one.

The niece: I hear thee, I do, but it's not summat I can decide over a butty and a brew, is it.

The aunt: No, appen not, I'll not mither thee no more about it today. Don't want thee skrikin' to me in a year sayin' tha's let a gradely one slip through thy fingers, mind.

The niece: I hear thee.

The aunt: I'll give over, for now. But t' offer stands, love, whenever tha changes thy mind, tha knows where I am. Put wood in th'ole on thy way out, there's a draught, ta-ra chuck.

The niece: Ta-ra.
