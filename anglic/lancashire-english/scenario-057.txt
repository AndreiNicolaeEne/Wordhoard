Landlord: Now then. Come in out o' that, it's fair chucking it down.
Tenant: Ta. I won't stop long, just wanted a word about summat.
Landlord: Sit thi sen down first, chuck, I'll put wood in th'ole, this room's like a barn. Brew?
Tenant: Go on then, if kettle's on. Owt to eat wi' it, a butty or owt?
Landlord: There's a barm cake in the tin if tha wants one, none o' this cob nonsense in my house.
Tenant: Ta. How's your lot keeping?
Landlord: Grand. Our kid were saying he saw thi lad down th' ginnel Sunday, kicking a ball about.
Tenant: Aye, he's made up wi' that new school, belting it is, by all accounts. Right, well, it's the rent I've come about, if I'm honest.
Landlord: Oh aye?
Tenant: I were only saying to the wife, we've been here four year now and never missed a week, not one. And that damp patch by th' ginnel wall in the back bedroom, I've mithered thee about it since I moved in and it's never once been sorted.
Landlord: I hear thee, but I can't just start knocking money off. Costs go up, not down.
Tenant: I'm not asking for nowt daft. A fifth off is what I'm after.
Landlord: A fifth? Give over, that's a lot to find. I could appen manage a tenth, and that's me being gradely wi' thee.
Tenant: A tenth's bobbins, if I'm honest, after all this time.
Landlord: It's not nowt though, is it.
Tenant: No, it's not nowt. But it's not what I'm after either.
Landlord: Tell thee what. Let me think on it proper, sleep on it, and I'll come back to thee with a fair number by week's end. That damp an' all, I'll get someone round to look.
Tenant: That'll do me. Ta for that.
Landlord: Ta-ra then, mind that step again on thi way out.
Tenant: Ta-ra.
