There were nowt in the tin what should've been there. I counted it twice over, thinkin' me eyes had gone doolally, then again over on th' windowsill where it goes sometimes an' all, but it weren't there neither. Money for th' leccy and the shop, gone lighter than it had any right to be.

I asked round mild enough at first, over us brew.

Me: Has anyone been in the tin at all, only there's summat missing off it.

The eldest: Not me, no.

The middle one: Don't look at me, I've been at work all week.

Our kid: Never touched it.

Same flat answer off each on 'em, like they'd all agreed it between 'em before I'd even asked.

Only one on youse were in this house of a Tuesday though, weren't they. Everyone else were out, at work or college or wherever. So it come down to askin' proper, not mild no more.

Me: Reet, straight up now, it were only thee in this house Tuesday. Where's th' money gone?

Our kid: I've told thee, I've not touched it.

Me: Don't gi' me that, there's no one else it could've been, is there.

Our kid: I'm not lyin' to thee, I never went near it, I swear down.

Me: Appen tha thinks I'm soft in't head. Summat's gone missin' and thee were the only one in this house all day!

Our kid: I don't care what tha thinks, I'm tellin' thee straight, I never touched thy money!

Me: Don't raise thy voice at me in my own house!

Our kid: Tha's the one shoutin', give over!

Neither on us give an inch, and it just stopped there, hangin' in th' air, nowt settled, nowt sorted, and both on us still stood our ground.
