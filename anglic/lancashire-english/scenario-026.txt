Eh, our kid, appen tha's heard bits of it off someone else already, but I want thee to have it straight off me, not off t'grapevine.

It were the settee that started it. Six hundred quid, and she'd gone and ordered it off her own back while I were on nights, never said a word till the van were stuck fast in't ginnel trying to get it up to t'door. I come home Tuesday to this great cream thing wedged half through t'front room, and I could've skriked, honest. I says, "chuck, what's this then, when it's at home?" That were me brought it up first, right there on t'doorstep, weren't mithering, just asking.

She says I've no room to talk, not after t'caravan money. Five year gone now, that were, three hundred quid deposit I put down and never saw again when the fella did one, and it's got nowt to do wi' a settee but back it come anyroad, same as it always does.

Sharpest thing said were hers. She stood theer wi' her arms folded and says, "tha's never had a head for money since, so give over lecturing me." That went in deep, that did.

After that neither on us said a dickie bird. Two full days, our kid, me in t'front room wi' t'telly on low, her upstairs, and the only talking in t'house were "is there owt for scran" and "aye, in th'oven."

Third morning she come down, put wood in th'ole behind her, and made a brew and a barm butty wi'out being asked, put it down next to me and said nowt, just sat. That were her sorry. I took it as such, said summat daft back about the settee happen being gradely really once tha gets used to it.

My verdict, our kid, she were wrong going behind me, and I were wrong dragging it two days ovver it. Her reckons that settee's belting, I still reckon it's bobbins, but we'll appen be sat on it in ten year regardless. Ta for listening, love. Ta-ra.
