The eldest: Reet, well summat's got to go first, so we'll start wi' this un. Yon little clock off t' mantel, that's mine. She said.

The younger: Since when? Nob'dy's said nowt to me about no clock.

The eldest: Cause she said it to my face, di'n't she, not to thine. Sat right there in that chair, she did, and said "that clock's for you when I'm gone." Plain as owt.

The younger: Aye, well appen she said it, appen she didn't, only funny how it's allus thee that heard the promises and never me.

The eldest: Give over, I'm not mekkin it up.

The younger: I never said tha were. I'm just saying, is all. Give over mithering me about a clock.

The eldest: What about t' money then, while we're at it. Three and a half thousand in that account and it were down under two afore t' funeral were even paid for.

The younger: I paid for t' funeral out of it, tha soft ha'p'orth. Where's tha think that went.

The eldest: Funeral don't run to fifteen hundred quid and tha knows it. Tha's had summat off it already, our kid, don't tell me tha hasn't.

The younger: I've took nowt off that account that weren't mine to take. Not one penny.

The eldest: It's not about t' clock though, is it. Nor t' money.

The younger: No. It's not.

The eldest: I just weren't there. When it happened. Tha were there and I weren't, and I can't get past it, that's all it is.

The younger: I know, chuck. I know.

The aunt: Eh, give over, the pair of you, she'll be turning in her grave listening to this. She were a gradely woman and here's you two scrapping like a couple o' kids over t' clock. Sit down, I'll put wood in th'ole and mash a brew, get some barm cakes in an' all, you're neither of you thinking straight.

The eldest: Sorry. I don't want to fall out over her things.

The younger: Nor me, if I'm honest.

The eldest: We sound a right pair of bobbins, don't we, stood here scrapping over what's left of her.

The younger: Aye. We do that.
