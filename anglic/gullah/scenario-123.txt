The younger: Tittuh, ya ain't touch ya coffee. E gettin cold. Wa mek ya so far off dis mornin, enty somet'ing wrong wid ya?

The elder: Ain't nothin wrong wid me, I jus can't shake dis dream I bin have. Since day clean I settin here da turn e over and over in my head, can't put e down.

The younger: Well tell me den, don't leave me hangin. Wa kind a dream keep ya dis tie up?

The elder: E start out plain plain, nothin strange bout e at all. I jus walkin home, same road I walk since I small, same as always. But de road jus keep on, tittuh. E ain't stop where e spose to stop. I walk and I walk and de house ain't come up where e spose to come up, e jus keep on further and further, past de bend, past de field, past everyt'ing I know fa be dere. I ain't skeered yet, mind ya, jus wonderin how come de road so long dis time.

The younger: Enty dat funny how de road do dat in a dream. Go on, wa happen when ya git dere?

The elder: When I finally reach de house, e look like home from de outside, same porch, same tin roof, but inside e had a room I ain't never see in my life. Not in dat house, not in no house. A room dat ain't have no business bein dere.

The younger: A room? Wa kind a room, tittuh? Wa e look like?

The elder: I can't rightly say wa e look like, everyt'ing bout e soft, like e ain't have no hard edge to e. But I gwine tell ya who was settin in dere. Daddy. Settin right dere calm as anyt'ing, like e ain't never left dis world at all, like no time ain't pass since e gone.

The younger: Lawd have mercy, tittuh! Daddy? Settin dere plain so?

The elder: Plain so. Calm face, cut e eye at me soft de way e always do, and e da talk to me. E mouth movin, I shum plain as day, but I can't tell ya wa e say. I yeddy somet'ing, I know I bin hear e voice, but de words ain't stay wid me. I jus have dis feelin like e done tell me somet'ing I spose to carry, somet'ing important, but e slip right out my hand soon as I try fa grab holt of e.

The younger: Try again, tittuh. Try fa remember jus one word.

The elder: I bin try since I wake up. E say... e start wid somet'ing bout... no. No, e gone. Every time I t'ink I bout to catch e, e slide off like water off a cootuh back. I can't call e back, no matter how I try.

The younger: Dat's alright, don't fret yaself over e. Wa happen after dat?

The elder: After dat de dream jus turn again, de way dey do. I lookin fa somet'ing den, huntin all through de house, through every room, and nothin dere where e spose to be. I ain't even know wa I lookin fa, but I know I got fa find e, and de longer I search de worse e git. I da pull open drawer after drawer, movin t'ing round, and my heart start beatin fast fast, like somet'ing bad gwine happen if I don't find dis t'ing.

The younger: Wa was e, dough? Wa ya was lookin fa?

The elder: Dat's de t'ing, I can't tell ya, cause I ain't know myself, not even in de dream. I jus know I got fa find e and I ain't have no idea wa e even look like. Dat's wa mek e so bad, searchin fa somet'ing wid no name, no face, nothin.

The younger: Enty dat de worst kind, when ya can't even say wa ya lookin fa.

The elder: De worst kind. De panic jus keep climbin and climbin, till e bust right t'rough de dream and I wake up wid my heart still racin like I bin run all de way from de field. I lay dere still a long time, tittuh, not even movin, jus tryin fa settle myself. And fa true, dere was a minute, jus a minute, when I couldn't say which one was de real one, de room wid Daddy settin dere, or dis room I wake up in.

The younger: Dat give me chill jus hearin bout e. Ya alright now, dough?

The elder: I alright. Jus can't shake e off me yet, e still settin heavy.

The younger: Oona know wa dey say bout dat, when somebody gone come back in a dream so calm, sweet mout and all, like ain't nothin ever happen to dem. Some say e mean dey want fa tell ya somet'ing, dat's how come ya feel like e done tell ya wa'somever e was.

The elder: Some say dat. My gran use to say when de dead come dat calm, e ain't come fa trouble ya, e come fa comfort ya, dat's how come e ain't never look sad nor strange, jus like e settin down fa Sunday dinner.

The younger: Maybe so. Or maybe e jus come fa remind ya e still round, watchin how ya do.

The elder: Could be dat too. I don't rightly know wa fa believe bout e. All I know, I ain't never bin so glad fa hear somebody voice and so vex I can't call de words back, both at de same time.

The younger: Dat's how dream stay, tittuh. Dey give ya jus enough fa keep ya wonderin, and dey hold de rest back fa demself.

The elder: True e stay so. Pass me dat benne cake, den, since I done talk myself hungry tellin ya all dis.

The younger: Here, eat. And drink dat coffee fore e go stone cold on ya, ya been lettin e set dere too long already.

The elder: I hear ya. I hear ya. Jus give me a minute more fa come all de way back.
