The bride: We done pass de number by thirty, e got fa come down some kind a way.

The groom: Start wid dem cousin oona ain't call in a year, and dem folk from oona job we ain't hardly talk to since. Dat right dere gwine cut e by fifteen, sixteen easy, half a what we need.

The bride: Oona can cut all dat oona want, but don't oona touch my tittuh name on dis list. E got fa be dere, enty, no matter what come.

The groom: If e stay, den somebody got fa go off oona side too. Every time we da do dis same ting, e always oona family got more seat dan mine. Same ole argument since de first day we start dis whole business.

The bride: Don't gimme dat cut-eye, I ain't hidin' nobody. Oona people done multiply since we bin talk 'bout de numbers de first time.

The groom: What if we fix a lil second gathering, out de yard, fa de one dat ain't fit? Some benne cake, some fish fry, keep e small an sweet.

The bride: No, suh. Ain't no leftover-wedding for my kin, dat gwine hurt dem worse dan not askin' at all. Dey gwine yeddy 'bout e and feel e too.

The groom: Awright, awright, forget I say e. Let we count again den.

They count de list over from de top, slow, one name to de next.

The bride: Still over. By a dozen or more.

The groom: E ain't move much a-tall. Slow as a cootuh, dis list.

The bride: We ain't finish yet.

The groom: I'll pull my two workmate off, dem I ain't yeddy from since dem left. But my tittuh stay right dere, don't come back sweet-mout me on dat one no more.

The bride: Fine. Dat ain't settle e, but e a start.
