Since day clean I been up frettin bout dis money. E stay fold up in a sock on top de wardrobe, low hundred, for grocery an light bill dis month. I gone fa git some dis mawnin an de sock feel light in my hand. I count um, count um again, still short by a good piece. I check under de mattress too, where I done move um one time before, but I ain shum de money dere neither.

I call everybody to de kitchen, easy at first.

Me: Oona seen de money I keep on top de wardrobe? I ain fussin, jus ax. Somebody move um, borrow um, e alright, jus tell me so.

The husband: I ain touch dat money, ain even know exactly where you keep um at.

The eldest: I ain went near dat wardrobe, Ma. Ain got no reason to.

The youngest: I bin at school all day, how I gon touch anything.

Tittuh: I ain shum nuttin. I ain even come in your room dis week.

I stand dere an t'ink, an den I remember e was de one alone in de house one day dis week, everybody else bin gone to church.

Me: Tittuh, you was de only one home dat day. Enty you was here by yourself all day?

Tittuh: I ain touch de money. I don't know why you lookin at me like dat, wid dat cut-eye.

Me: I ain lookin at nobody else cause nobody else bin here! You da lie to me right to my face, you yeddy me?

Tittuh: I ain lyin! Don't come sweet-mout me first den turn roun an call me thief in dis house.

Me: Ain nobody sweet-mout you, I ax you plain, an de money gone!

Tittuh: Jus cause I bin here don't mean I done took um. You believe what you wan believe.
