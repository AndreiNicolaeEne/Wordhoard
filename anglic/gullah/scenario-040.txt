Me: Oona settle down now, cause I gwine tell oona de whole of it, start to finish, since oona want know.

I lef out de house 'fore day clean, nerve all tie up in me chest like something bad bin gwine happen, and nothing ain't even start yet. Walk down to de road wid me lunch pack tight, praying de ride don't leave widout me.

Mother: E ain't leave you, enty?

Me: No ma'am, e ain't. But soon as I git dey, de buckra dat run de place, e stand up front, look me up and down, ain't say much, just point and say "you dis way." Show me where fa put me thing and where fa stand at de line.

First task e gib me, e easy, easier den I bin brace fa. I done tink all week e gwine hard my first day, but e just show me fa stack de crate right and keep de counter clean. I do it fine a while, but den, right in front everybody, I drop one whole crate and e scatter cross de floor. Lawd, everybody stop and look at me one time. Me face hot like fire.

Tittuh: Enty everybody drop something dey first day, though.

Me: Dat what de one help me say too. E ain't fa ax, e just come over quiet and start fa sweep e up wid me, say "dat happen to all we, don't mind it none." Dat de one I gwine remember out de whole bunch.

On de break I set back and yeddy de rest of dem talk, size dem up good, who da laugh loud, who da cut-eye de buckra soon as e back turn, who sweet-mout de customer den roll dey eye de minute dey walk off.

De work, e come faster den I brace fa, and nine hour run straight through, I ain't hardly set down de whole time.

Walk home I run e over and over in me head, every piece of it.

The eldest: So how e stand, den, all together?

Me: E stand alright. E rough on me feet and me pride bof, but I can do dis.
