Day clean and de water already at de door, jes ankle deep, sittin quiet under de sill like e ain mean no harm yet. I stand dey and watch um a minute, tink maybe e go stop right dey. An hour after dat I check back and e done rise good, higher now, movin quick. Enty I say e got a mind of e own. I start haul t'ing up off de floor while dey still time, de pot, de basket, de likkle stool, all go up on top de table.

By midmorning water done reach my knee, cold, full of yard trash, leaf and stick, and one cootuh paddlin cross de yard like e own de place. "Oona come help me move dis benne sack off de bottom shelf 'fore e spoil," I call out. De mother, e say, "I comin, hole on, I comin." We move ebryt'ing we can fa move, up on de bed, up on top de wardrobe, whatsoever stand highest we put e dey.

But de water ain done. E keep da rise, keep da rise, till early afternoon when e reach past de highest t'ing we bin raise, past de wardrobe top, past all we hope for. Tittuh say, "Enty I tell oona e go come past de top?" I ain got no answer fa dat, jes stand and yeddy de water knock and slosh 'gainst de wall, hour after hour, like sump'n big want in.

De buckra on de radio bin all sweet-mout bout it dat mornin, call e a little high water, nuttin to fret. We fret plenty. We wait through de worst, nobody sleep, jes listen to de wall. Hours after e peak, e start fa go down at last, slow slow, but e going down.

Next day I gone back fa shum wid my own eye. De line e leave stand dey high over my head, dark and long where de water bin. I cut-eye at um and don't say nuttin.
