Le me tell you how de whole day gone, cause you ain't been dey and somebody got to carry it fa you.

We start at day clean, cyar de truck down de sandy road, park um under de big oak wuh bin dey since fore anybody livin could member. De house belong to we gran, and e done pass las winter, and after dat de taxman and de buckra from de bank keep sendin paper, sendin paper, till de family set down and say we cyan hold on to um no mo. Ain't nobody livin dey to keep de roof patch and de wood dry, and de young ones done move off to town fa work. So we decide, all of we together, dat dis de day we clear de house out fa good and turn de key over.

Wasn't just we close family come neither. Tittuh come from cross de creek, and de cousin dem from up de road, and one ole neighbor man wuh bin knowin gran since dem was pickney together, e come too, say e ain't gwine miss dis. Dat how e go round yah, de whole yard full a people fore de sun even good up.

We start in de front room, cause dat where de door open. My auntie de one wuh find de first ting. E pull back a old sheet off wuh look like nuttin but a beat-up sewin basket, and soon as e see de wood handle e stop still. E say, dis de same basket gran bin larn me fa plait sweetgrass wid, right dey on dat porch, when I ain't no bigger dan a knee-high. E start fa cry right dey holdin um, and don't nobody say nuttin, we jes let um stand dey wid um a minute.

Then in dat same room, back behind de old settee, we shum, a little tin box, rusty, wid a piece of iron inside look like a hook and a curve of wood ain't fit nuttin we know. Nobody in de family could call wuh dat ting for. De cousin turn um over and over, say, oona ever see dis befo? And ain't nobody had a answer. Somebody guess e a tool fa mendin net, somebody guess e come off a boat, but we ain't never gwine know fa true, so we jes wrap um back up and set um wid de keep pile.

Next room, de back bedroom, dat where de argument start over de rockin chair. De uncle say e de one should keep um cause e de oldest son and dat de chair gran always sit in of a evenin. But de cousin say no, e de one bin sit at gran foot every night e was small, and gran always tell um dat chair gwine be his one day. De two of dem stand dey talkin loud, near bout raise de roof off wuh already half gone. Tittuh de one settle um. E say, oona both hush now, de chair go to de one wid de littlest house and de biggest porch, cause dat where e gwine set outside where folk can see um and member gran by um. Dat mean de cousin, and once tittuh say um so plain, don't nobody argue no mo. E true, enty.

Same room, we come on de old leather strap hangin on a nail behind de door, de one gran use to use on de chillun back in de day. Some of de older ones want fa keep um, say e part of de house history, e show how tings bin. But de younger ones cut dey eye at um and say burn it, say ain't no reason to hold on to a ting like dat, e ain't bring nobody good memory, only fear. Went back and forth a while, but in de end we let de youngest have dey way and toss um on de burn pile out back. Nobody cry over dat one.

Den we move to de kitchen. Dat where somebody find wuh nobody bin lookin fa. My auntie pryin up a loose board by de hearth to see if de wood good under dey, and e come up wid a little cloth bundle, all tied wid string gone stiff. Inside dat, some ole bills, folded small, and a paper look like a deed to a piece a land wasn't nobody know gran still had. We all stand round dat kitchen table starin at um like e gwine talk. De old neighbor man say e yeddy talk long time back bout gran ownin ground down by de creek, but wasn't nobody sure e was true. Now yah e is, right in we hand.

De room wuh take de longest, dat was de back storage room, de one wid all de boxes and jars and old newspaper stack to de ceilin near bout. Every single ting in dat room got a story tied to um, so wasn't nobody able to move fast. Somebody pick up a jar of benne seed gran bin save from summer befo last, and dat lead to a whole conversation bout benne wafer and who make um best. Somebody find a cootuh shell gran bin keep on de windowsill fa good luck, and dat start another round a talk. We bin in dat room near two hour longer dan any other room in de house, and by de end everybody tired but ain't nobody mind, cause de talkin was half de reason we was dey.

Round midday we stop and eat, all of we, out on de porch where de breeze come off de water. Somebody bring rice and shrimp, somebody bring benne wafer, cold tea in a jug. We eat together right dey on de step and de floorboard, and fa a little while ain't nobody talkin bout clearin nuttin, jes talkin bout gran, bout who e was, laughin bout tings e use to say. Dat de best part of de whole day, fa true.

After we eat we go back to um, and de big wardrobe in de hallway, dat de one nobody want in de end. E too heavy fa cyar, too big fa fit in anybody house wuh dey got now, and de wood done start fa rot on de bottom anyhow. We try fa see who gwine take um, but ain't nobody step up, and finally we jes leave um fa de junk man come haul off. Sad to see um go, but wasn't no fight over dat one, nobody want um bad enough fa fight.

De family Bible, now dat different. E settin on de shelf in de front room de whole time, wid all de name write down inside, every birth, every marryin, every one wuh pass. Soon as somebody hold um up and ask who gwine keep dis, de eldest say, I got um, and don't nobody say a word against um. Everybody know dat right, e de one bin keepin de family record in e head long fore anybody write um down. Ain't no argument dey a-tall, jes a nod round de room and dat settle.

Last room to stand empty was gran own bedroom. We save um fa last on purpose, wasn't nobody say so out loud but we all know um. When de last box come out dat room and de floor stand bare, everybody get quiet fa a minute. Tittuh say, e still feel like e in yah, enty. And we all jes stand dey in de doorway, lookin in at nuttin, member everyting.

Wuh little bit left after dat, we spread out on de porch and divide um up amongst weself, some dish, some tool, de jar of benne seed split three way, a few quilt, some old photograph nobody could bear fa leave behind. Everybody take a piece, big or small, and ain't nobody leave empty hand.

By den de sun bin gettin low, and we cyar de last load to de truck. De old neighbor man de one shut de door, turn de key in de lock one last time, and e stand dey a minute wid e hand still on de knob. Den e turn round to we and say, well, dat do um. We all walk down de step together, de house standin empty behind we wid de light comin cross de marsh, and ain't nobody look back too long, cause some ting, once e done, e done.
