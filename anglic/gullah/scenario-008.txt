The child: Ma, you gon buy dat phone fa me now? Everybody got one but me.

The mother: Chile, I done tell you no already, and oona chillun act like I ain't say nutin de fus time. We ain't got de money dis week, enty you yeddy me?

The child: But I been ax since day clean dis mornin, Ma.

The mother: I bin yeddy you good, every time you ax. De trut plain: de light bill da pile up, de boat need work, and dat phone cost buckra price, mo den we got fa spen right now. Dat's de real reason. Ain't no dodgin roun it.

The child: I could do mo chore fa e, den. I could mind de net eb'ry mornin, clean de whole yard too, widout you ax me.

The mother: Chore good, and I glad fa e, but you do em slow as cootuh half de time. Chore ain't gon make de money come no faster dis week neither. Sweet-mout ain't gon change nutin round here.

The child: Den maybe fa my birthday, when e come?

The mother: Maybe so. If de crab run good dis fall and yo daddy catch plenty, and if you still doin right by yo chore come dat time, den we gon see bout e. Dat's how e stan fa now, and I ain't promisin no mo den dat.

The child: But tittuh boy down de road already got one! I shum wid it yestiddy, walkin roun showin off, cut e eye at me like e betta.

The mother: E people do e own way, we do we own way. Jes cause e mama and daddy buy one don't settle nutin fa dis house. We got we own bills, we own road fa walk. Enty you know dat?

The child: (say nutin, look down at e foot)

The mother: I ain't got de big ting fa you today. But I got dis. Take dis ol' phone yo uncle done fix up, and some benne candy on top. Fa now.

The child: Awright. I gon keep e. But I still want de real one, one day.

The mother: I know you do, baby. We gon see.
