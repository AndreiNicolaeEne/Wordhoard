Me: Look yah, I got to talk to you bout dis mud business, cause I ain playing no more.

My friend: Wuh mud business? I done dat every low tide since I small, ain nothing new bout it.

Me: Enty I bin right dere yestiddy when de tide come in on you? I shum, oona out to de creek bank fa dig dem oyster, an de mud done pull you down to de knee fore you even know e happening. E was pulling you further an de water da come up fast round you, an I stood dere my heart beating like a drum, my hand shaking so bad I couldn even throw de rope right de first time.

My friend: (give one cut-eye) I done got out ain I. Ain no big thing. Come on now, you know I too fas fa dat mud.

Me: You call dat ain no big thing? Member when tittuh husband break e leg right in dat same mud two year back? E couldn even pull esef free, had to holler til somebody yeddy him from de road. E still limp today, an e was a strong man too, stronger than you.

My friend: Dat different, e was careless out dere.

Me: E ain different, e de same mud, same tide, same current da pull anybody down e don care who. Next time e might not be a broke leg, e might be oona don't come home a-tall. Tide da rise, e don wait on nobody, an if oona get stuck when day clean still an hour off an ain nobody out dere fa see you, who gon yeddy you den?

My friend: (quiet)

Me: Who oona think left standing dere lookin out to dat water every day after? Oona mother done bury one already. I ain tryin fa preach at you, I just done shum too close dis time, an I scared, I still scared right now telling you dis.

My friend: I yeddy you. I ain gon lie an say I gon stop altogether, dat how we eat sometime, oona know dat. But I go watch de tide table closer, an I go bring somebody wid me stead of going by esef out dere. Dat much I can give you.

Me: Dat enough for now. Just don mek me shum dat again, enty.
