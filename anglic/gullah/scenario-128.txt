De sky bin still fus, still in a way I ain't like one bit. No bird da call, no dog da bark, e so quiet you coulda yeddy yo own heart. De air hang heavy, like e bin holdin e breath fa de longest, and I say to my husband, dis one comin fa true, enty? E jes look up at de sky and go on for de radio without a word.

De man on dat radio bin sweet-mout we all week, sayin e gwine curve off, gwine pass we by to de south, ain't gwine be nothin but a lot of rain. Enty e wrong. Wasn't but an hour after dat de wind pick up, and I mean pick up hard and quick, not gentle at all. We all run outside fa tie down what could be tie, fa bring in what could be bring. My husband da haul de crab trap up off de dock, de eldest da drag de porch chair into de shed, and I holler for oona to git de wash off de line 'fore it blow clean over to de mainland.

The auntie: Oona hurry now, dis wind ain't playin wid we today.

Me: I know it, I know it, jes let me git dis last sheet.

De youngest bin runnin round tryin fa help and jes gettin under everybody foot, and I had to holler fa e to git in de house and stay dey. By de time we done wid all dat, de wind already pickin up harder still, howlin round de eave like something alive and vex. We git everybody in close, de children, de old auntie, my tittuh who bin stayin wid we dat week, all of we gather up in de front room where de wall thick and dey ain't but de one small window. Wasn't long after dat de light gone out. Jes so, quick, dark like de inside of a pot.

Dat's when it start fa turn bad fa true. De wind git so loud we couldn't talk to one nother no more, not without hollerin right in de ear. I bin tryin fa keep de youngest calm, holdin e close, and I could feel my own heart da bang like it want fa jump out. Den we yeddy it, a big crack, den a boom dat shake de floor under we foot, a tree comin down not far off, close enough we could yeddy it land, close enough de whole house shudder wid it. Nobody say nothin for a minute. My tittuh grab my hand and I grab back, and de auntie start fa pray low under she breath, and I ain't shame fa say I bin prayin too.

Not long after dat we yeddy a next sound, a tearin, rippin sound up top, and my husband say, dat's de roof, dat's de roof gone. Part of it bin stripped clean off, and de wind come whistlin through de attic like it own de place now. De one window in dat front room, de old one dat bin dey since fore I bin born, crack straight cross wid a sound like a gunshot, but it hold, praise God it hold, it ain't bust clean through. We all move fa de far corner, away from dat window, and we jes stay dey, huddle up, waitin. I won't lie to oona now, I bin scared fa true dat night, scared in a way I don't like fa recall too close, but I ain't say it den. Nobody say it den. You don't say it out loud when de children right dey lookin at you fa see how bad it really is. You keep yo face still even when yo inside shakin like a leaf. Dat's what a mother fa do, enty.

We stay huddle up like dat, I couldn't even tell you how long, time don't move right when de wind da scream like dat. Den, all sudden, quiet. Not slow quiet, jes quiet, like somebody cut it off wid a switch. De wind bin dere den e bin gone, and de silence after bin almost worse, cause you don't know fa true if e done or if e jes catchin e breath fa come back harder.

We wait a good while fore anybody move. My husband finally say, I gwine go look, and e went and open de door slow, slow, like e half-fraid what e gwine see.

My husband: Come look. Oona come look at dis.

We all step out into it, and Lawd, de yard ain't look like we own yard no more. By day clean de whole island bin out lookin at what de storm bin leave. Dey bin a old cootuh crawl up in de yard from de flood tide, confuse and slow, and de children bin de first ones fa shum and go runnin fa look, glad fa somethin to laugh bout after a night like dat one. De benne field down de road bin flatten out too, every stalk lay over one way like somebody bin comb it down.

Days after dat bin all cleanup, all of we and half de neighbors out dey wid saw and rake and tarp, haulin limb, pullin dat oak apart piece by piece til de road bin clear again. Somebody come look at de roof and say what it gwine take, and it bin more than we want fa hear, but we bin grateful all de same, cause we bin dey to hear it. We patch what we could patch and wait on de rest.

Now when I tell it, I still can't help it, I still reach for de big words when I git to de worst part, cause it deserve dem. Lawd, dat wind! Lawd, dat tree comin down right dey by de house, close enough fa shake we teeth! I tell it and my hand still go up like I'm pushin de wind back even now, even wid de sun out and everything peaceful round we. My tittuh laugh at me for it, say I tell it worse every time, say I gwine have de tree fallin right through de roof next telling. Maybe so. But oona wasn't dey, wasn't dey in dat dark room wid de wind screamin and de children holdin on to you, so oona don't know how it feel fa come out de other side of dat and still have a house standin, even a broke one, even one gwine need work for months to come. We bin lucky. We know we bin lucky. But I ain't never gwine tell it quiet, not dat night, not never.
