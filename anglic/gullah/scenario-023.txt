My friend, I know oona been waiting on dis story since oona couldn't stand up dere wid we, so I gwine give you every lick of it, start to finish, don't leave out nothing.

I wake up way fo day clean, dark still heavy on de creek, and I ain sleep good all night no way, heart just da beat like a drum somebody left running. My mother put grits and a egg in front me first thing and I try, Lawd I try, but I ain able fa swallow but two bite. She cut she eye at me and say:

The mother: Gal, you got fa eat something, dis a long day fo we, you can't run on air.

I try again but de food just da set dere getting cold and I couldn't do nothing wid it.

Den right when everybody bout dress and de cars bout fa pull off, I look down and enty I ain got de little pin my gran left me to wear! Whole house turn upside down. My tittuh da run from one room to de next, de little flower gal start fa cry cause everybody hollering at once, and finally my mother shum, way back een de bottom of a drawer where I done put um fa safekeep two week before and done clean forget. We laugh and we bout fa cry de same time, right dere in de hallway, before we even step out de door.

When we get to de church yard, my husband family and my family, dey ain never all stand together in one place befo dat day, not once in my whole life. Dey come from every which way and dey all bunch up on de grass, sizing one anodda up, everybody polite and stiff, till somebody bin brave enough fa cross over and shake a hand, and after dat it loosen right up. My husband mother, she de one who cry first, soon as she see me come round de corner in de dress, she just broke, hand over she mouth, and dat set my own mother off, and by de time I reach de door half de yard was wet-eye.

Inside, de preacher da read out de vows nice and slow, and when it come my turn my mind just went blank, plain gone, I lost de line complete, standing dere wid my mouth open and nothing coming. My husband just squeeze my hand and wait, ain rush me none, and it come back to me like it bin dere all along waiting for me fa find it. Den we say de words dat seal it, de plain simple part everybody wait on, "I do" and de ring going on, and dat de part dat make it real, more real den de dress or de flowers or any of it.

After dat we sign de papers on a little table off to de side, one witness standing for my side and one fa his, both of dem trying fa hold de pen still cause dey hand shaking near bad as mine.

Den come de photograph, oh my friend, de photograph took forever. Somebody trying fa cluster de kin just right, dis row shorter one kneel, dat one move over, tittuh move here, uncle stand dere, and every time dey almost got it set somebody else da wander off or a little one run through de middle of it. We stand in de sun so long I start fa feel my feet swole up right dere in my shoes.

At de reception dey had a mix-up wid de seating, put my husband auntie and my mother workmate right next to one anodda when everybody know dem two ain speak easy since way back, and all through dinner it was cut-eye dis way and cut-eye dat way cross de table, nobody saying much of nothing to de odda but dey face saying plenty.

De benne wafer dat de old auntie made, gone in about ten minute flat, whole tray wiped clean before de music even start good. But de cootuh stew she bin insist on making, cause she say a wedding table need it, dat pot ain hardly touched, everybody sweet-mouthing her about how good it smell and how dey coming back for some later, and later just never come.

De toast, my friend, wasn't nobody could get through it dry-eye. De best man start off fine, joking, den halfway he just stop, throat closing up on him, had fa take a breath and start again, and by de end half de room was sniffling right along wid him.

And dat one dancer, oona know de one I mean, wouldn't sit down de whole night, not for dinner proper, not when dey call everybody fa de speeches, just kept moving out on dat floor by heself when de music good, and everybody just let her be, cause some people just built dat way.

Somewhere round dessert de old story bout de cootuh come back up, how de old folks used to go down de creek and catch cootuh for soup back when times was hard, and how one time a certain uncle bin so scared of a big one snapping at him he drop de whole bucket and run clean home, and dey drag dat story out and tell it to de whole table like it fresh, and everybody laughing hard even dem who done yeddy it a hundred time befo.

By de time de last guest walk off, night done come down good, and it was just me and my husband left standing dere in all dat mess, cups and plate and chair pushed every kind of way, de lights still on over an empty floor. We ain say much, just stood dere a minute looking at it all, den start fa laugh, quiet, tired kind of laugh, and dat de first quiet minute we had de whole day, just we two, married now, in de middle of all dat mess, and it was good, my friend, it was real good.
