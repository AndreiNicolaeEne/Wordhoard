De cold dat come dat year, e beat anyting dis house ever bin see, and e hold on leb'm day straight, no break, no let up one time.

From de fus day e bin cold pass wa anybody spec. Oona could yeddy de wind da blow off de water fore day clean even crack, and you know right den say dis one different from de reg'lar cold. We keep de fire da bun round de clock, night into day, feed em wid pine knot and oak log, cause if e die down one hour de whole house get cold same as outside de door.

De pump wa we bin draw water from since fa'ever, e froze solid clean through, de handle wouldn't budge for nothin.

The mother: Enty dis pipe done bust now, look how e swell up.

Me: I shum dis mornin, ice all down inside de spout, e ain't drip one drop.

De eldest done see plenty winter come and go in e time, and e set dis one against de cold back when de eldest bin small.

The eldest: I member de cold wen we lose de sweet potato right in de ground, but dis one beat dat one, e beat um bad, e beat um wors'n I ever tell oona bout.

De worst night bin de seventh, de sky clear and de stars sharp like glass, and dat clearness bin de real danger cause no cloud fa hold de heat down over we. De cootuh dem down by de pond bin froze in de mud, ain't one move fa week straight.

We lost de hog dat same night, e freeze right in e own pen fore daylight come, and dat hurt bad, cause we bin countin on em.

Tittuh: Don't cut e eye at me now, I done bank de fire high as e go, ain't nothin more to feed em wid.

Leb'm day e hold, and wen de thaw finally come, de drip off de roof soundin like music to we ears, and every soul in de yard walk out in de sun and jus stand dey quiet, lettin e warm we bones back slow.
