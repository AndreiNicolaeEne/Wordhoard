Tittuh, dis body done in, for true. We bin start 'fore day clean, e still dark when we walk out to de field, body ain't even ready fa move good yet, cold settle down in de bone. Buckra come round early and holla, "Oona git dem hoe and start on de row," and dat set de pace for de whole day, cause once you start dat row you cyan't slow down, everybody watching everybody down de line.

'Bout eight my back start fa holler at me. Dat hoe heavy in de hand and de row long, long, ain't seem like e ever gwine end. We git a lil break 'bout ten, ain't hardly nothing, just enough fa wipe you face and drink some water 'fore we yeddy de bell and back to it we go.

Den come de stretch in de evening, 'bout two, tree o'clock, sun beating straight down on you head, dat de hardest part of de whole day, enty. I shum coming down de row wid dat look, and I tink 'bout walking off more dan once, my hand blister, my back bend like a old cootuh shell. But you cyan't quit on people depending on you.

One good ting, do. De benne patch, we done finish dat faster dan I spec, ain't take but a hour, lil lift in a long grinding day. Buckra ain't cut-eye me one time, and I ain't have fa sweet-mout him neither fa get my pay. E count it out straight in my hand, forty for de day, fair and done.

Ten hour all told, sun to dark, and my body know every one of dem. Tomorrow e say maybe de same ting again, or maybe e let we rest one day. I pray e let de body rest, cause dis body done, tittuh. Done for true.
