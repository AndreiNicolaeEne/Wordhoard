The niece: Tittuh, e me, oona all right? I yeddy from Cousin say you ain doin so good.

The aunt: I ain gwine lie to oona, I been low three day now.

The niece: Wha happen, tell me straight.

The aunt: Started Sunday wid a ache in me jint, an me throat scratchy scratchy. Monday de fever come on top e all, lay me right down. Today little easier than yesiddy, praise God, but I still ain right, ain right atall yet.

The niece: Enty you oughta done gone see bout dat by now?

The aunt: Listen how I work um. First ting, I gargle warm salt water fa de throat, dat old remedy never fail. Fever ain broke off by e self so I lay a cool cloth cross me head, change um right through de night, clear on till day clean.

The niece: You bin eatin anyting?

The aunt: Broth an weak tea, dat all me stomach want fa hold. Ain no cootuh stew for me today. I sleep whenever de body leh me, don't fight um none.

The niece: Tittuh, you got fa rest proper now, you hear me, don't push tru dis like you does.

The aunt: (cut-eye) Who you tink you talkin to.

The niece: I'm jus sayin. Oona too hardhead fa oona own good.

The aunt: If de fever climb any higher, or if me breath start come hard, I gwine go be seen, dat I promise oona. But not before dat, I ain runnin to no buckra clinic over a lil ache an fever.

The niece: Le me bring oona some benne cake, or I could set wid you a hour, keep oona comp'ny.

The aunt: You too sweet-mout, chile, oona ain got fa do all dat.

The niece: I'm bringin de cake regardless. De settin, we go see how you feel later on.

The aunt: Well. If oona insist. I ain gwine turn away good comp'ny, but don't fuss over me too much, I done tell you I ain dat bad off.
