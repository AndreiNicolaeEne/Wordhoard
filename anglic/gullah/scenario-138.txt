Me: ...and dem say e bin out on de water when de storm ketch em sudden, and de boat done turn all de way over right by de creek mouth, and e nearly drown out dere fore nobody could git to em, enty.

Tittuh: Stop right dere. Dat ain't wha happen. Not even close.

Me: Enty?

Tittuh: Enty nothing, chile. I shum dis mornin wit me own eye, day clean, standin right dere on de dock. Ain't no storm out dere yestidy, de sky bin clear as glass all day long. Wha happen is de motor cut out on em halfway cross, and de tide bin runnin out under em, and e drift right on to de mud flat by de oyster bank. E da wait dere, dat's all, till de tide come back in and float de boat loose fa em. E wet up to e knee from standin in dat water so long, and dat's de whole of it. Boat never turn over, not one time, not a inch.

Me: But dem tell me e nearly drown out dere.

Tittuh: E ain't never nearly drown. E ain't even git wet past e waist. By de time dat story reach oona ear it done pick up a whole storm and a turnt-over boat and a man near dead, and none o dat true. Dat's how far e done drift, chile, all de way cross de creek and back again.

Me: Where I even yeddy dat from, den?

Tittuh: Who you yeddy it from first?

Me: De one at de counter down de store bin tell me. E say e yeddy it from buckra own son.

Tittuh: Mm-hm. Buckra son do love to sweet-mout a story till e twice de size e ought to be. Don't cut you eye at me neither, I ain't makin sport, I just tellin you straight. You go tell whoever ask you de true way from now on, oona hear? Motor cut out, boat stuck on de flat, e wait fa de tide, and de tide bring em home. Dat's all it ever was.

Me: I yeddy you.
