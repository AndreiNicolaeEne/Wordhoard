Oona know how e stay wid money and friend, e ain never easy fa mix de two. Since day clean I bin studyin how fa bring dis up wid my friend, cause we done know one anodda since we been pickney runnin cross de field togeda, e like me tittuh true true. E owe me eighty dollar, bin owin since fo Christmas, and I ain wan mek big ting outta um, but de mont jes keep pilin up on top one anodda.

Me: My friend, I ain tryin fa fuss wid you, but you know dat eighty dollar you bin owe me since fo Christmas. Dat's gone on seven mont now, mo dan dat.

My friend: Gal, I hear you, I hear you. Ting bin tight, de buckra down at de dock ain pay we straight dis week, e say next week fa sho.

Me: Enty you tell me de same ting back round Easter time? You say soon as de benne sell you gwine settle up wid me straight. Dat benne done sell and gone, and I ain shum a dime yet.

My friend: You keep countin up every little ting like I some kinda cootuh crawlin slow on purpose. I ain never cut-eye you bout nutin, all dem time I stand by you when you need.

Me: I ain cut-eye you neida. I jes state what plain fa see. Eighty dollar since fo Christmas, dat's all I say, I ain say no mo.

My friend: You mek me feel like a stranger to me own self, like all dem year we bin friend ain count fa nutin now.

Me: I ain mean um so. I jes need what mine, same as you would need yourn.

My friend: Yah, tek twenty now, dat's all I got on me dis minute. De rest comin, I swear fo God, fo end de mont.

Me: Enty you swear de same befo? I gwine tek de twenty. But I ain sweet-mout bout de rest no mo till I shum settle in me hand.
