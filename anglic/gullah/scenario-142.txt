Day clean ain't even broke good when I wake and remember what I done swear to do. Back before Christmas, when Tittuh keep axing me one time too many bout dat old dock plank, I say plain out, "Awright, I gwine give oona one whole day and finish e, ain't no way round e." And today de day.

Soon as I open my eye I feel de regret set down on my chest like a wet sack. I ain't want fa git up. I ain't want fa look pon dat dock atall. But I done swear e, so I git.

I mutter de whole way down de path, grumbling bout how I ever let my mouth run so loose. "Why I bin say a whole day," I say to myself, "why I ain't just say I go help lil bit." De sun ain't even clean de sky good and I already tired a de sound a my own voice complaining.

When I reach, de eldest already dey, waiting.

The eldest: You slow dis marnin, enty?

Me: I slow cause I ain't want fa be yah atall.

The eldest: Cut-eye all oona want, de plank still rotten.

We start in, and every board we pull up I find something fa fuss bout, de nail bent, de wood too heavy, de sun too hot for so early. Round midday, my back done give out near bout, and I stop and think, maybe I just leave e half done, come back tomorrow. But den I yeddy my own self say I bin swear it, and dat shut de thought right down fore e go anywhere.

Workmate: You want fa rest lil bit?

Me: Enty me swear I go finish e today. I ain't studying rest.

I grit through de worst part, hand blister and all, and by de time de sun low I done haul de last rotten board off and set de new one down solid. De eldest shum de finish work and don't say nothing sweet-mout, just nod.

The eldest: Oona keep oona word.

Me: I bin grumble de whole way, but I keep e.

And I did.
