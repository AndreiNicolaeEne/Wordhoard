Me: Since oona new to dis street and oona done yeddy people mention de critter dat move in round here a while back, set yourself down good, cause dis one got a story to um, and once I start talking I ain gwine stop till e done.

It start real quiet, back when de days still bin hot for true. De young one down by de corner house de first one fa shum. E come running up to de porch one morning, say e see sumting big, gray, wid a ring round e tail, poking down by de bin. But nobody pay um no mind. E only a chile, and chillun always seeing t'ing in de dark. De old folks just cut-eye and laugh, say e must bin dream, say ain no such critter round dis part of de island, e must bin de neighbor cat or de shadow off de moon. So dat first sighting get wave off, plain and simple, like it never happen a-tall.

Enty a week pass, maybe two, fore de second one come. Dis time e ain no chile talking, e de elder from up de road, de one everybody go to when dey need to know if de weather gwine turn or if de fish running good. When dat one say dey shum too, de whole street sit up different.

The elder: I ain lying to oona. I bin standing right dere by de wash line when e come out from under de porch, big as day, and e look me dead in de eye fore e gone back in de bush. Dat ain no cat. Dat ain no dream neither.

After dat, ain nobody laughing no more. Cause if de elder say e true, den e true.

Well, once people start looking, dey start seeing plenty. E da tip over de bins mostly, come night, digging through for whatever scrap bin left. Den e move on to de gardens, da root round de benne patch out back one yard, knocking down what somebody bin growing all season. Some folks say e da poke round de chicken coop too, sniffing at de feed, though ain nobody catch um in de act of dat.

Dat when de street split clean in two. One side say leave de critter be, e just doing what critter do, e ain hurting nobody real bad, e gwine move on once e find um ain nothing worth staying for. De other side say no, e got to be dealt wid, cause e getting too bold, coming closer to de house each time, and if oona let um keep coming e gwine end up somewhere e ain suppose to be, like inside de house itself.

Den de garden torn up bad one week, whole row of collard pulled up and left to rot in de dirt, and everybody just blame it on de critter straight off, no proof, nobody shum do it, just figure who else e could be. Folk argued over dat for days, some saying e must bin de critter for true, others saying could bin a dog running loose, could bin anything, oona cant just blame a t'ing cause e convenient to blame.

Dat argument really bust wide open at de gathering dey have out in de yard one evening, when half de street come together to talk it through proper.

The one with the garden: I done lost near a whole row and nobody wanna do nothing about it! Dat critter got to go, one way or de other!

The elder: Oona want to go killing sumting ain done nothing for sure yet? E ain no proof e was even dat critter tore up your row. Could bin plenty t'ing.

The one with the garden: Enty everybody shum round here lately? What else it gwine be?

The elder: Cootuh move slow and steady, dis critter move quick and quiet, dat don't make um guilty of every t'ing gone wrong on dis street.

De talking got hot dat night, voice raised, some walking off mad, and wasn't nothing fa settle a-tall.

Round dat same time, one household decide dey gwine handle de matter dey self. Dey set a trap dey rig up out of a crate and some string, bait um up nice, sit up half de night waiting. Come morning de bait gone, de crate knock over, and de critter nowhere to be found. Dey try um again de next night and same t'ing happen, and after dat dey give it up, say de critter too smart for a homemade trap, and dat was de end of dat household doing.

But de real turn come de night de critter come right up close to de house, near de back step, and startle one of de women so bad she scream loud enough de whole street bin yeddy um. She was just going out to check on sumting and de critter come round de corner sudden, hiss, and she near bout fall down de step backing away.

The neighbor: I ain never move dat fast in my life. E was right dere, right up on me, fore I even yeddy um coming.

The elder: Tittuh, oona alright? Oona look like oona shum a haint.

After dat night, ain nobody on de fence no more. Even de ones who bin saying leave um be start saying sumting got to change, cause a critter dat bold round de house at night ain sumting fa just wave off.

Still, dey argued over whether e was truly dangerous or just unlucky in where e wander to. Some say a critter dat size, dat close to people, bound to bite sooner or later. Others say e ain never bit nobody yet, e just wandering where e shouldn't, poor t'ing probably lost e own home when dey clear dat lot over yonder, and now e just looking for someplace to be.

Dat when somebody finally say dey gwine call in a buckra man dey know from de county, one who deal wid dat kind of t'ing for a living, done seen every critter dis island got to offer. E come out, walk de whole yard, look at de bins, look where de garden bin tore, look under de porch where de critter bin denning up. E tell de street straight: de critter ain sick, ain rabid, just a young one looking for easy eating, and easy eating was exactly what de street bin giving um wid open bins and scrap left out.

De terms dey settle on after dat: secure every bin good, lid tie down tight, no more scrap left where e could reach um, and if e come round still, leave um be long as e keep e distance, but if e ever come inside a house, den dat a different matter. Nobody gwine hunt um down for sport, but nobody gwine feed um neither.

Since den, t'ing done quiet down considerable. De bins all got string or a brick weigh down de lid now, whole street done dat. De elder still keep half an eye out come evening, mostly out of habit more dan worry. De sightings still happen time to time, somebody shum crossing de road come day clean, or yeddy um rustling round de garden fence late, but ain nobody run screaming no more.

Truth told, e done turn into more a story dese days dan a threat, sumting to tell round de yard of an evening, sumting to sweet-mout up a little bigger each time somebody tell it. Oona gwine yeddy it different every time depending who telling um, but dat right dere, dat de whole of it, straight as I know how to tell um.
