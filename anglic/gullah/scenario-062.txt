Pull that chair round, since you want to yeddy how it start, and I gwine tell you my side, cause dat's de only side I got fa give. Just so you understand fore we start. I ain't gwine tell you nothing bout nobody else mouth, only what I know and what I see wit my own eye.

Now you got fa understand, de two house wasn't never like dis. Bin close, close like family, closer than some blood family I know, enty. We share one well fore de county run de pipe, share one dock too, and de chullun bin da run in and out one another door so much it ain't have no line between de yard at all far as dem was concerned. I call de woman next door tittuh, and she call me de same, and dat wasn't no manner of speaking neither, dat was true. Fifteen, sixteen year we bin dat way. Benne cake back and forth every Christmas, gone over de fence so much you couldn't say who plant de shrub and who bring de plate. E watch my chullun same as e own when I bin out on de boat, and I done de same for e when e was down sick one whole winter. Dat's how it bin. You have fa know dat fore you know de rest, cause everybody wan tell you bout de feud like it always bin so, and it ain't. It ain't been so mos of de time we bin neighbor.

It start small, de way dese ting always start small. E husband put up a shed for e tool, right up close on de line, close as e could set um without stepping clean over. Must bin done fore day clean too, cause I ain't shum um going up at all, I just wake one morning and dey e stood, already finish. Eave hang out over my side, and every rain de water da pour off dat tin roof straight down on my collard bed. First year I ain't say nothing, figure e ain't notice. Second year I say something, but I say um soft, de way you talk to family. I say, "when oona get a chance, move dat eave a little, e da flood my bed every time it rain." E say awright, awright, e gwine see to um. All sweet-mout talk, cause time pass and nothing move. Slow as cootuh bout it. I ask again round Easter, same soft way, and e say de same ting, awright, gwine see to um. Time pass again. De collard rot two year running from all dat runoff, and by de third time I ask, I done lost my patience and I ain't try to hide um no more. I tell em straight, I say oona need fa move dat shed off my line, I done ask nice twice already.

Well. Dat's when de trouble start for true, cause word don't travel straight, it travel through whosoever carry um. De one who tote message between de two house dat time, e live right cross de road, and e mean well, I still believe e mean well, but by de time my words reach de next house dem wasn't my words no more. What come back to me was dat I bin cuss de whole family out front de church, dat I say dem people ain't got no manners and ain't got no respect for a line dey own family draw fifty year back. I ain't never say none of dat. I ain't never raise my voice de first time nor de second. But dat's what dem yeddy, and dat's what dem believe, and when I try fa straighten um out, e husband come to my gate and tell me straight to my face dat dem never ignore no request, dat I never even ask till dat day, dat de whole ting bout de shed bin news to um. Flat like dat. Like I bin trouble myself over rotten collard for nothing.

I ain't say nothing back dat day. I just turn round and go in my house. But I ain't forget um neither. Next benne season, when de time come fa share out like we always do, I ain't carry none over. I ain't make no big show of um, I just quiet stop. And I let my goat graze right up on dat line same way dem let dey water run on my bed, figure what good for one good for de other, enty. Small ting, I know. Petty, I know it, I ain't gwine pretend I'm proud of um, but a body can only bin push so far fore e push back a little too.

Den de comment come back to me, de one dat cut worse than anyting else in de whole business. It come through de same road, same kind of carrying, and what dem say bout me was dat I bin jealous my whole life of what dat family have and never had myself, dat de real reason I make trouble bout a little tin shed was I couldn't stand fa see nobody do better than me. Dat one stay wit me. Still do, if I'm honest wit you. All de rest of um I could shake off like water off duck back, but dat one dem aim right at someting I ain't never say out loud to nobody, and somebody musta known um well fa aim so true.

I try one more time fa make peace after dat. I carry a plate over myself, ain't send it by nobody else dis time, okra, rice, little bit of de benne cake I ain't share de season before, set um right on de step my own self. Left um dey. By evening de plate bin back on my step, everyting on um still dey, ain't touch, ain't even a note. Dat tell me all I need fa know bout where dat family head bin at.

By den de whole street done pick a side, near enough half and half. De ones down toward de landing side wit dem, say I bin de one carry a grudge too far over a piece of tin. De ones up my end side wit me, say a body got a right to dey own line and dey own collard bed. It got loud too, one evening out by de dock where near everybody bin standing round waiting on de boat, e husband and my own self, hollering back and forth so de whole street could yeddy every word, no hiding um no more. Somebody even say if it come to blows dem gwine call buckra on we, dat's how loud and how far it bin done gone. Dat's when de real accusation come out, right dey in front of everybody: e say e shum um missing right after our trouble start, e cast net off e own dock, and ain't nobody else coulda took um but me. I deny um flat, same as e deny me, and I still deny um today, cause I ain't never touch dat net, I ain't never want dat net, I got my own.

Dat bin near twelve year now. Twelve year we ain't shared a word past what manners make you say when you pass close enough can't help it. Twelve year I walk de long way round to de landing so I ain't have fa pass dat gate. E still cut-eye me every time we cross path at de store, and I know I do de same back, can't help it no more, it come natural now. De chullun grown and gone off, and even dem don't tote word between de two house no more, dat road done close up complete.

And I gwine tell you someting else too, since you set dey and yeddy me out to de end wit not one question, and I respect dat. Some night, late, when I think back on de whole ting real careful, I ain't even sure no more who start um first. Bin de shed, or bin de way I ask about de shed, or bin someting before dat I done forgot bout complete. Twelve year is a long time fa hold onto a straight story, and mine done bend some too, same as everybody else. But e still my side, and e's de only side I got.
