Half pass nine da clock say now, and e tell oona all fo e lef dis mornin, not fa wait supper, e be back fo six sharp. Dat mean e gone free hour pass da time e say a'ready, and I ain't shum since day clean when e tek out wid da boat.

Maybe da buckra man down to da landin hold em up, talkin bout dat motor need fixin again. Dat could be it, e always stop and jaw too long wid dat man, enty. I gwine believe dat one for now, cause I ain't studyin bout nothin worse.

I git up and look out da window, den I go to da door and look down da road, den I set back down. Da clock tick and I git up again, look out, ain't nothin dey but da dark and da dog sleepin on da step. I done dat so many time a'ready I lost count.

Maybe some gal down dey sweet-mouthin em to stay on for supper, dat's all it is. E do like dat kind a attention. I try fa laugh bout it but it ain't sit right wid me tonight.

Den da worse thought come, whether I call it or not: s'pose da water tek em. S'pose da boat swamp out dey and ain't nobody round fa know it. I say hush out loud to my own self, like sayin it make it not so.

I member one time, me and tittuh wait just like dis for Daddy, when we been cotchin cootuh in da crick all day and e ain't come home till way after dark, mud up to e knee, laughin bout wuh e catch, and tittuh give we benne cake fa hold we quiet till e walk through dat door. Dat mem'ry warm me and scare me both da same time.

I studyin fa go out look for em my own self. I git up, put on my shoe, den I set right back down. Not yet. E bin know dis way home since e a boy, enty.

Da clock say pass one now. Four hour gone, more.

Someting move outside and my heart jump clean up in my throat, but ain't nothin dey, just da wind in da palmetto.

Den I yeddy footstep on da step for true, and da door, and e da come in right now, and all dat worry wash off me fore da mad even git a chance to rise up.
