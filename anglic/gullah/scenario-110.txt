The eldest: E home! Come een, gal, mek I look at you good. How e stay up dey, dat city?

Me: E stay awright, Auntie. Busy for true. Busy like oona ain't never see.

The mother: Busy how? Talk to we, we ain't gone nowhere, oona know we ain't.

Me: Well, e big, first ting. E big pass anyting oona could picture from settin yah. Home yah, you kin walk from de landing to de church 'fore de kettle done bile good. Up dey, oona got fa cross town and e teck de best part a hour some day, jus fa git from one side to de oder side. Traffic da back up like ants on a log dat somebody done poke.

Tittuh: Hour? Fa true? Jesus my Massa. Round yah you kin git anywhey you need to be time you finish smoke half a cigarette.

Me: Dat's what I da tell oona. And de noise! Lawd, de noise never stop, day nor night. Car horn da blow, train da run under de ground like some big animal, somebody siren da holler all hour. Yah, night time e so quiet you kin yeddy de marsh talkin to itself out dey. Up dey e ain't never quiet, not even day clean.

The eldest: Enty I tell oona city life ain't restful. Buckra done build dat place fa move fast, not fa live good.

Me: Some a dat true, Auntie, I ain't gone lie.

The mother: Where oona da stay now, den? Show we de place, tell we bout um.

Me: E small, mo small dan oona gone picture. One room fa sleep, one lil kitchen ain't big as dis table yah, dat's bout all e is. Oona could put my whole place een de yard back home and still have room fa de chicken coop and de fig tree too. But e cost. Lawd, e cost. What oona pay fa a whole month lot down yah, I pay dat much fa two week a room, and dat's if de landlord ain't raise um again.

Tittuh: Two week?! Fa a room dat small? Cootuh could live better dan dat, and e drag e own house on e back everywhey e go.

Me: (laugh) I know, I know. But everyting cost so much up dey. Rice, greens, even benne, you got fa go dig fa find real benne, and when you shum um on de shelf e cost like gold dust.

The eldest: Enty. Buckra done teck de land and now oona got fa buy back de dirt.

Me: Something like dat, Auntie.

The mother: Well, de people, dey friendly, or dey ain't? Straight answer, now.

Me: Dat ain't no plain yes or no kind a question, Mama. Some a dem sweet-mout you to de face and don't know oona from Adam de nex day dey pass you on de street. Some a dem, dough, dem good people, dem show up when trouble come, no matter oona ain't kin to dem a-tall. And some, Lawd, dem give oona cut-eye jus fa walkin slow on de sidewalk, like time ain't fa wastin round dem.

Tittuh: Cut-eye fa walkin slow? Enty e never see nobody walk right den, up dey.

Me: (laugh) Dat's de truth of it, tittuh. Ain't nobody up dey walk slow. Everybody da rush someplace, head down, like de devil hisself da chase dem.

The eldest: How oona buy oona food, den, wid all dis rush goin on?

Me: Dey got a market, big one, ain't like we lil market wid two table and de fish man and de truck full a watermelon. Dis one got a hundred stall, oona kin buy anyting under de sun dey sellin, but ain't nobody know oona name dey, ain't nobody know your mama nor your gran nor who your people is. You jus one mo face in de line, movin fast as de nex one.

The mother: And how oona find work up dey? How e done, dat kind a ting?

Me: E done different from yah. Yah you know who fa ax, oona kin walk to somebody kitchen or somebody boat and dey put you on dat same day. Up dey oona got fa write up paper, send um off, wait fa somebody to call oona back, and sometime dey never call a-tall, like de paper done fall in a hole. I bin walk round three, four week 'fore I find de work I got now.

The eldest: Dat sound hard, chile, walkin round dat long.

Me: E hard sometime, Auntie, I ain't gone lie bout dat neither. But once oona in, e steady. Dey pay come regular, every two week, no waitin on de weather nor de tide nor whether de fish bitin.

Tittuh: Oona ever get lost up dey? Big place like dat, e sound like e could swallow a body whole.

Me: Gal, don't ax me dat. First month I bin lost three, four time, easy, maybe mo. One time I walk near two hour tryin fa find my way back and end up right where I start from, standin dey like a fool. E teck me long time fa learn de streets, dey ain't like yah, yah you kin find your way by de creek an de big oak an where de road bend by de church. Up dey every street favor de nex one, same brick, same light, nothin fa mark um by. Now dough, I shum um clear. I know which corner fa turn, which one fa pass right by.

The mother: What bout de food, den? Oona eatin good up dey, or oona sufferin?

Me: Some ways better, some ways worse, Mama, e ain't one thing straight through. Dey got food from all bout de world up dey, tings I ain't never taste befo I lef home, and some a dat sweet fa true, I ain't gone say e ain't. But ain't nobody down dey kin cook rice like oona, and don't even talk bout gumbo, dem call it gumbo and e ain't got no okra in um a-tall, e jus soup wid a name borrowed. I miss cootuh soup somethin terrible, and de field pea, and de benne wafer oona make at Christmas.

The eldest: Enty I tell oona, ain't no place cook rice like home.

Tittuh: So which one better, den? Yah or dey? Jus say um plain.

Me: (laugh) Oona ax me dat every time I come home, tittuh, and I ain't gone answer um straight neither one a de time.

Tittuh: Come on, now. Jus say.

Me: Dey good in dey own way and yah good in e own way. E ain't de same kind a ting fa put side by side and call one better.

The mother: Mm-hm. Look like she ain't gone tell we nothin straight today, same as every time.

The eldest: Well. Oona ever comin back fa good, den? Dis yah still oona home, oona know dat.

Me: I know um, Auntie. I tink bout um. I tink bout um mo dan oona know, mo dan I tell anybody up dey. But I ain't got no answer fa dat yet neither, not a real one. Maybe one day, when de time right. E ain't a no, I kin tell oona dat much for sure.

The eldest: Dat's a start, den.

Me: E a start.
