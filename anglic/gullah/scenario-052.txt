Nine day left fo dis month done turn, and we sit down to de table countin out what we got left. Twenty dollar. Dat's all e come to, spread out flat like e might multiply if we stare on um long enough.

My tittuh: Nine day, and twenty dollar to cyar us through. E ain't gwine stretch far, enty.

Me: E haffa stretch. We ain't got no choice in de matter.

My tittuh: Bin thinkin on um all day. Rice and grits gwine have to do us, straight through, wid a little field pea in um when we got um. Fry a piece a fish Sunday and mek de rest a de pot last off dat same fish, warm um over, warm um over again, till e gone.

Me: Rice every day again.

My tittuh: Oona rather we go hongry? Dat's de plan and we stickin to um. If e come down to it we could ketch cootuh outta de creek fo protein, but let we not get dat far.

Me: I ain't say no to de plan, I just say I tired a rice already and de month ain't even start good.

My tittuh: What dis I shum in de bag, den? Benne wafer? Oona spend money on benne wafer wid we down to twenty dollar?

Me: E was one dollar!

My tittuh: One dollar today, one dollar tomorrow, cut-eye all de way down de line, dat's how e go. Buckra at de store don't care oona sweet-mout yaself, e still take yo dollar and gi you nothin back.

Me: I yeddy you. I ain't buy nothin else, I promise.

My tittuh: We gon push de light bill back a week too. Dey ain't cut we off dat quick fa one week.

Me: Twenty dollar, nine day. Dat 'bout two dollar a day if we split um even.

My tittuh: Some day cost more, some less, long as e balance by de time de month turn over.

My tittuh: My cousin say e got extra field pea from e garden, e gon bring some by day clean, fo e go to work.

Me: Dat help. Dat help a whole lot.

My tittuh: See, we gon mek it. Nine day ain't nothin.
