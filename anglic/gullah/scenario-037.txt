Day ain't even clean good when I yeddy Mother knocking on de wall, saying oona git up, git up now, de corn ain't gwine cut ehself. I lay dere a minute more in de dark listening to de rooster carry on like e own de whole island, den I put my foot on de floor cause I know how Mother stay when she call twice.

Out in de yard e still mo dark than light, dat gray time fore day clean proper break over de water. Daddy already up walking round wid de lantern, checking e tools, and Mother da move round de kitchen house da wrap up grits and fatback in a cloth for later, cause once we start we ain't stopping till de sun say stop.

Mother: Oona listen now. De little one gwine tote water and keep de birds off de corn dat still standing. Dat all e able fa do dis year, and e plenty.

De youngest one clap e hand like e done win something, running off fa find de gourd we use for de water, happy fa be call on for anything at all. Nobody grudge um dat, cause e small yet, and every hand count on a day like dis, even a small hand.

Daddy: My eldest gwine come wid me to de heavy row. Dat corn stand tall dis year and somebody got fa cut and haul um to de cart, and dat ain't work for no light-boned somebody.

De eldest just grunt and pick up de cutting knife, cause e know dat e de one wid de back for it, big through de shoulder like Daddy own self, and ain't no argument in um dis early. Dat de heaviest part of de whole day and dey two carry um together, cutting and hauling, cutting and hauling, till de sweat done soak clean through dey shirt fore de sun even clear de tree line good.

De rest of we go to work on de sweet potato bed and de okra, digging and picking, moving down de row steady. By de time de sun stand up over de water proper, we done clear de whole first section of corn, and Daddy call out fa shift we round.

Daddy: Awright now, dat section done. Oona come round to de benne patch and start pulling. E dry enough now, e ready.

So we move, everybody shifting task, somebody go help spread de benne stalk on de cloth to dry in de sun where de seed can fall out proper, somebody go tie up de corn already cut into shock.

Now de old man, my granddaddy, e bin working steady all morning too, but along about midmorning I notice e falling behind on e row, moving slower, stopping more, wiping e face more than e working. Nobody say nothing to um about it, nobody call um out or shame um for going slow. My tittuh just ease over quiet and start working de other end of e row toward um, so by de time dey meet in de middle e ain't even notice how much of de work done get take off e hands. Dat just how we do. Ain't no need fa make a thing of it.

Round about when de sun stand straight up, Mother come out from de kitchen house wid de food, grits and fatback and some cold cornbread and a jug of water, and everybody stop right where dey stand and come set down under de shade tree for a spell. Dat de best part of de whole day, dat little rest wid food in de belly and de shade on we back.

Me: Oona shum dat cootuh crawling cross de potato row dis morning? E move slower than de old man!

De eldest: Ha! Oona best not let Granddaddy yeddy you say dat.

Everybody laugh, even de old man e own self, cutting e eye at me playful-like but laughing too. De youngest one want fa keep de cootuh for a pet, say e gwine name um, but Daddy tell um cootuh soup taste mighty good come Sunday, and de youngest cut e eye at Daddy hard for dat, don't want fa hear it.

Daddy: Oona cutting dat corn too slow, oona know dat?

De eldest: Slow? I done cut more dan you dis whole morning!

Daddy: Sweet-mout ain't gwine get you out of de rest of dat row, no sir.

Dey go back and forth like dat a good while, half serious half play, bout who cutting faster and who doing um right, till Mother tell dem both hush and eat.

Fore we even done finish eating good, I notice de sky done change over toward de sound, dat dark color coming up quick like it do sometime, wind starting fa pick up and rustle de corn leaf hard.

Mother: Storm coming! Oona git dat benne up off de cloth fore it get wet and spoil!

Everybody jump up and run for de benne patch, gathering up de drying cloth and folding um quick, carrying baskets under de shed roof, Daddy and de eldest running de cart under cover too. De wind blow hard for a spell, de sky look mean, and we all stand round holding we breath waiting to see if it gwine break open on we.

But e pass. De wind die down, de dark cloud move on off toward de mainland, and de sun come back out hot and steady like nothing happen. Daddy wipe e brow and look up.

Daddy: E holding. We got de afternoon yet. Oona back to work now, we lost time enough.

So we push on through de afternoon, everybody back at it, digging and cutting and hauling, de heat pressing down hard now dat de storm done clear off, sweat running steady, nobody talking much, just working.

Bout mid-afternoon I yeddy a crack and a holler from de sweet potato bed, and I look up and shum de old digging fork done broke clean off at de handle in de eldest hand, right in de middle of a good row.

De eldest: Dis fork done broke! How I fa finish dis row now?

Daddy come over, look at it, shake e head, but don't waste no time fussing. E send de youngest running to de shed fa fetch de other fork while de rest of we shift over and finish digging dat row by hand, pulling de potato up out de dirt wid we own fingers till de tool come back. Ain't nobody stop working long over it.

By de time de light start fa lean long and gold cross de field, Daddy call out loud so everybody yeddy um clear.

Daddy: Oona move now, de light going fast! We got fa finish dis last section fore dark catch we!

And everybody come together on dat last row, de old man and my tittuh and de youngest and all, everybody working de same patch side by side, cutting and pulling and hauling all together till dat last stalk of corn come down and de last potato come up out de ground.

Mother stand by de cart counting out loud while Daddy call de number.

Mother: Dat make ten shock of corn, six basket of sweet potato, four sack of okra, and de benne pan full besides. Enty dat a good day work?

Everybody just nod, too tired fa talk much, standing round de cart in de last of de light. Den one by one we all sink down right where we stand, on de ground, on de cart wheel, wherever, arms heavy, back aching, nobody able fa move much more.

But fore anybody get too settled, Mother look over at de youngest, who done already stretch out flat on e back in de grass like e own self done finish de heaviest work of de day.

Mother: You. Go shut up dem chicken fore de fox get um, and bring in dat wash off de line fore de dew fall on um. Den oona can rest same as everybody else.

De youngest groan loud but get up and go do um, dragging e foot cross de yard while de rest of we set in de gathering dark, too wore out fa even laugh at um good, just glad de day done, glad de crop in, glad fa be still.
