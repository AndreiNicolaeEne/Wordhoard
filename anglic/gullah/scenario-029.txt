Me: Oona want to know how it go of a morning in dis house? Alright, siddown dey, I gon walk you through de whole ting, start to finish, but don't look for me to make it pretty, cause it ain't pretty, it just what it is, same as it bin yestiddy and same as it gon be tomorrow.

Me: First one up, every single day, rain or shine, is de grandmother. E up 'fore day clean, 'fore any rooster, 'fore any alarm clock in dis house ever think to ring. Nobody teach she to do dat, e just always bin so. E don't even turn on de big light, e just move through de kitchen in de dark like e got eye in e feet. And every mornin, first ting, 'fore e even put water on for de coffee, e go out to de step and check on e benne, pinch off whatever leaf done turn brown overnight, talk to it soft like it gon answer she back. Five minute, no more. Den e come in and de day proper start.

Me: Second one up is de mother, and e wake to de same grumble every mornin of e life, cause de daddy already in dat wash room and won't come out. Ain't matter what time e went in, ain't matter who was up first to knock, de daddy find a way to be in dat wash room right when de mother need it worse.

Mother: E in dey again, enty? Every blessed mornin. I yeddy de water runnin and I know right den I done lost.

Me: Dey been fussin over dat one wash room since 'fore I could talk good, and it ain't never gon get settled, not in dis house.

Me: Den come de children, and dey wake up in de same order every mornin, same as dominoes fallin. Eldest first, cause eldest sleep light and don't need but one knock. Den de second one, who need de knock, den de light turn on, den somebody pullin de cover clean off. Den de youngest last, cause de youngest could sleep through a hurricane comin.

Me: And de same three words git call out down dat hall every mornin, same order every time:

The mother: Git up! Wash up! Move now!

Me: Every day. Ain't never change. I could set my watch by it if I had a watch worth settin.

Me: And most mornin, right 'bout de time everybody tryin to git in dat one wash room, de same argument start up, near 'bout word for word:

The second one: Tittuh was in dey longer den me!

The eldest: E was not, oona always sayin dat.

The second one: Enty you was in dey since I first call you?

Me: And de second one cut e eye at de eldest walkin out all satisfy, like e won a prize, and don't nobody ever win nothin, dey just move on to breakfast still fussin under dey breath.

Me: We got a dog too, and e got e own part in all dis same as anybody. E wait right by de youngest door every mornin, and soon as dat door crack open e squeeze through and go stand by de table, tail goin, like e think today gon be de day somebody let um lick de plate 'fore it git wash. E slow like cootuh gittin up off de floor at night, but come mornin e da wait dey already, first thing, quicker den half de children.

Me: Breakfast de same too, every day, grits and egg and whatever meat we got, and de grandmother cook um de same way she cook um since I was little, don't never change de recipe for nobody. But de daddy, e de one who never vary. Same order every single mornin: grits dry, no butter, egg hard, not soft, and don't nobody put no pepper on it 'fore it reach e plate. E get quiet and serious 'bout it too.

Daddy: Dat egg soft again. I done tell oona how I take my egg.

Me: And de grandmother don't even look up, just say "mm" like e ain't hear nothin new.

Me: Now every mornin, without fail, somebody shoe done gone missin. Same shoe, near 'bout, or seem like it. And it always in de same three place: under de bed, behind de door, or somewhere de dog done drag it to. We look dey first now, don't even waste time lookin nowhere else.

The youngest: I shum! I shum de shoe!

Me: And e come runnin out wid it like e find gold, and everybody just keep movin like it ain't nothin, cause it ain't.

Me: You want to know de sound dat mean everybody finally proper awake in dis house? It ain't no alarm, it ain't no rooster. It's dat old screen door slappin shut when de daddy finally come out dat wash room and head out to de step for a minute, sayin e got to git to de dock 'fore buckra open de gate. Dat door slap, and right after it de whole house pick up speed, like somebody wind a clock.

Me: De mother de one always runnin behind, same few minutes late every mornin, no matter how early e say e gon start. E de last one dress, last one wid coffee still in e hand, and den it's all rush at de door. Coat grab off de hook, bag pass hand to hand, "You got you lunch? You got you book?" and don't nobody ever forget nothin, only cause e check three time 'fore anybody step foot out.

The mother: Y'all got everyting? Check again. I ain't turnin round once we hit dat road.

Me: Every mornin, six day a week, same rush, same fuss, same dog underfoot.

Me: But Sunday different. Sunday de whole house go quiet instead. Ain't no shoutin down de hall, ain't no fuss over de wash room, everybody just move slow and easy, like de house itself take a breath. De grandmother still up first, still tend to e benne, but ain't nobody rushin behind she. It's de one mornin de whole rhythm just let go.

Me: But six mornin out de seven, it's de same handful of word said right dey at de door 'fore anybody leave out:

The mother: Mind how oona go, ya yeddy?

Me: Same word, same door, same voice, every single time, and I swear it never once sound tired sayin it, even after all dese year.

Me: And dat's it, my friend, dat's de whole show, every mornin, over and over, and I laugh 'bout it near 'bout every day cause it so absurd when you set back and really look at it, all dat noise and fuss over de same shoe, de same egg, de same wash room, like we ain't done dis ten thousand time already. But I wouldn't trade it, not one mornin of it, even de fussy part, even de late part. You never bin see nothin like it till you live inside it, and now you know, least a little, how it go 'fore de rest of de world even wake up good.
