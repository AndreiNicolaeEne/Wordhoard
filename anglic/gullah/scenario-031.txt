Let me tell you bout the meal I dream on, since you ain't never had it and can't say boo bout it.

Come Sunday, when the tide low and e still day clean, that's when this meal call to you. You smell it fore you see it, benne toasting in the pan, that smell da come cross the yard fore you even reach the porch steps. My tittuh already up, done put the cootuh on since fore light, letting e stew slow, low fire, all morning, till the meat pull loose from the shell easy easy.

That's the dish that stand out, the cootuh stew. E got to be tender like that, fall-apart tender, and the gravy got to be thick enough to sit on top the rice and not run off to the side. If e thin, e ain't finish, and everybody at table gon yeddy bout it soon as e taste.

You ever shum the best gumbo they serve up at the buckra place in town? This beat that plate clean, and ain't no comparison, cause this one cook with time, not with no clock.

We eat off tin plate on the porch, oona all crowd round one table, no fork rushing, cause rushing show disrespect for who took time fa cook it.

Normal it feed bout eight, nine, the whole family, the neighbor tittuh, whoever pass by and get invite in.

The moment I wait on every time is when the eldest taste first, pause, cut e eye round the table, and say enty this good, and everybody laugh and dig in sweet-mout. That right there, that's the meal.
