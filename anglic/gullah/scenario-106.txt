I gwine tell you dis one careful, cause e ain't happen big and e ain't happen small neither, e jes happen de way e happen, and I ain't gwine mek it more than dat.

We bin lef from day clean dat mornin, fore de sun even good up, cause we know de line at de checkpoint gwine be long by de time we reach. We bin travelin off de island dat day, gwine cross over to de mainland fa see about tittuh chile, e sick in de hospital dey. De road dat lead off de causeway had a checkpoint set up on e, cause of de war time, dem soldier watchin de coast fa boat comin in dey ain't suppose to come, so nobody ain't cross widout dem look at oona paper first. Everybody know it stay so dat season, enty. But knowin ain't de same as standin in e line.

De line done build up long fore we reach de front, good half hour we stand dey in de sun, wagon and truck and people walkin, all bunch up waitin, de line movin slow slow, like cootuh cross de road. My heart bin da beat hard fore we even reach de front, I could yeddy it in my own ear, I ain't gwine lie bout it. I bin studyin what dem gwine aks, turnin e over and over in my head, and de more I study e de more my hand da shake.

When we reach de front, de one at de checkpoint hold e hand out fa de paper, and I pass um to um.

The one at the checkpoint: Where you come from and where you gwine?

Me: I come from de island, suh, gwine cross to see bout my sister chile, e sick in de hospital.

Dat one I ansuh plain, cause e true and e simple and I ain't haffa study on it.

The one at the checkpoint: What you carryin in dat sack dey?

Dat one I bin haffa study more careful. I know say ef I say de wrong thing e gwine hold me up longer, so I tek my time and tell um jes what stay in e, clothes and a lil food fa de road, nutin more. E look in de sack e self, move e hand through e slow, den e look back down at de paper.

E ain't say nutin fa a lil while, jes stand dey lookin down at de paper, turnin e over in e hand, and dat silence stretch out long past what e need fa jes read a paper. I stand dey and wait, my mout dry, my hand still on de side of de wagon.

The one at the checkpoint: You ain't got no year wrote on dis yuh line. When you say you bin born?

I bin nearly forget dat part, cause e jes stay written down somewhere all dis time and nobody never aks me fa say e out loud befo. But I study quick and I gee um de year, and e write e down e self on de line dat bin empty, and dat settle it.

Dey bin a man in de line fore we, and dem hold him back longer than de rest of we. Dem tek e paper off to one side and dem talk over it low, and e stand dey wid e hat in e hand, e face ain't show nutin at all. Nobody say nutin bout it, nobody look too long neither, but I shum out de side my eye, how e stand dey so still. We all bin watchin widout watchin, ef oona understan me. Wasn't none of we business fa comment on it, and we ain't.

I bin wuk hard fa keep my face steady and my voice from shakin de whole time we bin dey. E ain't easy, standin fore a buckra hold your paper in e hand, and e can send you back or let you pass jes as e please, and you cyan show what you feelin inside, cause dat only mek tings worse fa you. I breathe slow and I ansuh when I bin aksed and I ain't say one word more than dat.

De one courtesy e show me, e call me tittuh when e hand de paper back. E say, "Awright, tittuh, oona can go on through." E ain't haffa say dat, e coulda jes wave e hand and move we long, but e say de word kind like dat, and it stay wid me still, all dis time after.

E fold de paper back up and gee um to me, and e nod e head toward de road. Dat nod bin de whole of it, dat lil movement bin all e tek fa let we go on.

We walk on through, and I ain't feel nutin lift off me right away, no. We bin walk good twenty minute down dat road, past de checkpoint and till nobody dey couldn't shum we no more, fore de tightness in my chest finally let go. My hand start fa shake den, after, when e ain't matter no more, and I bin haffa stop and stand a minute fa let e pass through me. Dat de toll of it, e come after, e ain't come while you standin dey needin to be steady.

Dat's why I tell dis one careful, no bigger and no smaller than e stay. E ain't nutin happen to me dat ain't happen to plenty of we dat season, and nobody put a hand on me, and I bin let through same as anybody dey wid dey paper right. But I member how e feel, standin dey waitin, and I member how long e tek fore my body b'lieve e was over. Dat's worth tellin plain, jes so, widout mekin it into more than e was.
