Le me tell oona how de whole ting gone, start to finish, cause e bin a mess fo e get better.

Day clean when I walk out and shum. Wind bin da blow all night, howl round de house like someting ain't right, and when I reach de fence line I see de whole ting flat to de ground. De big pine from nex do yard done come down cross e, crush post and rail bofe, like de fence never bin dey a-tall. I stand dey a minute jus a-look, ain't even believe wa I da see.

First ting I try to work out, whose tree e was. Dat pine bin standin right on de line long as I remember, half e root in my yard, half in dey yard, growin dat way since fore either one of we move in. Nobody never call um my tree or dey tree, e jus bin de tree on de line, mindin e own business till de wind come fa take um down.

I gone knock on de neighbor do soon as I see de light on.

The neighbor: Mornin. Dat wind sure bin someting, enty?

Me: E bin someting fa true, but come look wa e done to de fence.

We walk round together and de neighbor look down at e own tree layin cross my fence, and e face change quick. E cut-eye at me, like e already know wa comin.

The neighbor: Well, dat ain't nobody fault. Wind blow, tree fall, dat all e is. God send de wind, not me.

Me: Dat tree bin leanin dat way long fore last night. E ain't jus fall out de blue, e bin da lean fa a good while.

The neighbor: Every tree lean some. Dat don't make um my doin.

Bout den my tittuh come out on e porch, cause e yeddy we talkin loud, and e call cross de yard.

My tittuh: E right bout dat tree leanin. Oona remember, e tell oona bout um at de fish fry, say oona need fa get somebody look at um fore e come down on somebody head or somebody fence.

The neighbor: I don't remember no such ting.

My tittuh: E do so. I bin standin right dey when e say um.

Well dat put a chill in de air fa true. De neighbor ain't say nothin more, jus gone back inside e house, and I gone back in mine, and fa de nex few day we ain't hardly speak, jus a nod when we pass, cut-eye and keep movin.

De question bout who gon pay fa de fence, dat sit dey like a hot coal nobody want pick up. I feel like since e e tree, e should pay all of um. De neighbor feel like since e wind, ain't nobody fault, and if anybody pay, oughta split um even, or better yet ain't nobody owe nobody nothin a-tall. We gone back and forth bout um near a week, short word here and dey, neither one budgin a inch. I ain't want be de one back down first, cause den e go round tellin everybody e win. I reckon de neighbor feel de same bout me. Wasn't neither one of we lookin fa sweet-mout de udda, we jus dig in.

Den one evenin de old man from down de road, e done live on dis island longer dan either one of we, e come by like e jus out fa walk, but I know good e ain't jus out fa no walk.

The old man: I yeddy oona two havin trouble bout dat fence.

Me: E tree fall on my fence and e say ain't e fault a-tall.

The old man: Well now. Dat tree bin on de line long as I can remember, growin half in one yard, half in de yudda. Root don't ask permission fo e cross a line, enty? Old buckra surveyor draw dat line straight through de tree e own self, so how oona gon say e belong to jus one side? Dat tree belong to bofe a oona, same as dat fence belong to bofe a oona. Always bin so.

E say um quiet, not takin no side, jus a-layin out how tings stand. Den e turn to de neighbor.

The old man: Oona ain't have fa say e fault. Storm done blow hard enough fa knock down tree bin standin fifty year and more. Ain't no shame in dat.

Den e turn to me.

The old man: And oona ain't have fa say e ain't fault neither, cause oona right, dat tree bin talkin fa a while fore e fall. Somebody bin see de sign of um.

The old man: So le we jus call um a fence bofe a oona use, and split de cost even, and be done wid um. Ain't nobody lose face doin wa neighbor spose fa do fa one nudda.

Ain't neither one of we say much after dat, but I feel someting loosen in my chess, and I could see de neighbor shoulder come down a little too.

The neighbor: I reckon dat sound fair.

And dat bin de end of de fuss bout money.

De clearin start dat Sat'day mornin, and Lawd, de first hour or two bin stiff as a plank. We out dey wid saw and axe, cuttin up dat big pine, haulin de limb off to de side, and ain't hardly a word pass between we cept "watch you foot" or "pull dat end fa me." De neighbor son and e wife come out too, and my husband and my tittuh come help my side. Two crew workin de same job, but might as well bin two different job, dat how quiet e bin. E da get hot too, sun climbin, sweat runnin, and still ain't nobody sayin much.

Den bout mid-mornin de neighbor wife come out de house wid a plate of benne cake and a jug of cold water, and e ain't jus give um to e own family, e bring um cross de line and hold de plate out to we too.

The neighbor's wife: Oona must be thirsty, workin in dis heat. Take some, e still warm.

Wasn't nothin much, jus benne cake, but someting bout um break open wa bin stiff between we. My husband take a piece and tank e kindly, and after dat de talk start easy, back and forth bout how fa set de new post, who bring wa nail, somebody make joke bout de old cootuh dat live down by de creek, how e come up in de yard slow like e got all day and don't care nothin bout nobody fence trouble. Everybody laugh good at dat.

Later on when we runnin short a good rail, de neighbor son jus walk cross wid e own crowbar and e level, ain't nobody ask um fa um, jus hand um over.

The neighbor's son: Use um long as oona need.

Small ting, but e mean someting. E mean we ain't strainin against one nudda no more.

We work good together de rest of de day, de two household, all de way till de fence stand back up, and I tell oona, e stand sturdier dan de old one ever bin. We set de post deeper dis time, use better wood, and put a extra brace where de line bin weak fore.

Now when I walk out day clean and shum, I don't feel no bad feelin bout um a-tall. Me and de neighbor, we talk over de fence most mornin, bout de garden or de weather or wa de grandchildren up to. Ain't say we bin close befo all dis happen. But we closer now dan we ever bin, and dat ole tree dat cause all de trouble, e still make good firewood fa bofe a we, split even, same as de fence e used to hold up.
