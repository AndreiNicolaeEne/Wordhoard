Seven o'clock I clock een and right away I yeddy we two short, before anyting even start out de gate. De one who does de hall on de far side, e never call, e just never come, and de other one call in say e chile sick. So it left me and my workmate and de night nurse fa cover de whole building, three a we doing what six s'pose to do. I say to myself, Lawd, dis gwine be one long night, enty, and I ain't even know how long yet.

Eight o'clock everyting quiet-quiet, too quiet, and dat was de first sign, cause when it too quiet on dat hall somebody studyin trouble. De call light on room by de corner start fa flicker, off and on, off and on, like it can't make up e mind. My workmate walk down dey fa look and come back say,

My workmate: E ain't nottin, de bulb loose, I done fix um.

But e wasn't nottin small like dat, cause by nine o'clock de whole panel commence fa buzz and de light on de hall dim down low den bright back up, like de building itself breathin hard. I cut my eye at de panel and I say dis machine bin actin up for a week now and nobody fa come look at um till e ready fa fall out all de way.

And fall out e did. Nine-thirty, de backup unit dat run de oxygen line for two a de rooms just cut off, no warning, no sound, just gone. I run een dey and de old buckra man in de bed, e machine da click and click and no air da come. I yeddy my own heart before I yeddy anyting else. We had fa pull de little portable tank from de closet and run um by hand while my workmate try fa raise somebody on de phone.

I call de maintenance line and it ring and ring, nobody da answer. I call again. Nobody. I call a third time and leave word, say we got a resident on air and de backup down, come quick. And still nottin, for de longest while, like de phone itself gone to sleep.

The night nurse: Call de supervisor house line den, we can't wait on maintenance all night.

So we call de supervisor, and e ain't like being pulled out e bed one bit, e voice come on de phone all short and heavy.

The supervisor: Y'all can't handle a machine widout callin me every time it hiccup?

But e come. E come in e house clothes, cut-eye on e face de whole time, grumblin under e breath, but e come, and e get de backup unit going again with e own two hand while we hold de portable steady. By den it pass ten o'clock and we ain't even touch de med cart yet, and dat s'pose to bin done an hour ago.

So we scramblin now, tryin fa patch de air thing widout stoppin de rest, cause de med pass still fa do, de vitals still fa check, de whole building still there needin tend to. My workmate da run de cart one direction, I'm da run de hall de other, de night nurse split between de two a we like e cuttin em self in half.

'Bout eleven, right when we think we gettin ahead a it a little, de laundry room start fa flood. De old machine dat wash de bed linen, e hose bin bin weak for a time, and e finally give way all de way, water da run out under de door into de hall. My workmate step een fa shut de water off and e foot slide on de wet floor, e catch de rail just in time but e wrist twist bad. E ain't broke nottin, thank God, but e hand swole up quick and e couldn't hardly grip de cart handle after dat.

The night nurse: Oona stop right now, wrap dat wrist, we ain't need two a we down.

We had fa stop de laundry altogether, just shut de room off, pile de wet linen by de door, and deal wid it come mornin. Wasn't no way fa keep dat going and do everyting else too, so dat had fa give.

'Bout one o'clock, one-thirty, it settle a little. De air machine holdin, de med pass mostly caught up, my workmate wrist wrapped and e da manage one-handed. I sit down for de first time all night, drink a little water, and I even think, well, maybe we gwine make it through dis one after all. I remember dat little quiet, cause it ain't last.

Round two o'clock, just when it feel like it turnin, de fire alarm system start fa sound off, no smoke, no fire, just de panel itself gone bad and de whole building ringin like Judgment Day. Residents wakin up scared, some a dem confuse, callin out, and we had fa go door to door explainin ain't no fire, ain't no fire, while dat noise still da blare. Took near twenty minutes fore we could get anybody on de phone fa cut de system down, and by den half de building wide awake and frighten and we back to square one wid de comfortin and de settlin down.

By de time we tally it up, it was a night, I tell you. De linen from de flood, ruint, had fa be throw out, too much a de room soak through. De portable tank we bin usin, near empty, and we had fa scrounge for another one fore mornin shift. De med pass end up two hour behind where it s'posed to be. One resident e chart got mix up in all de runnin back and forth and we had fa go back and straighten it out piece by piece so nottin was give wrong. My workmate wrist still swole when day clean come, e ain't complain but I could see um favorin it.

Three, four o'clock we just pushin, one foot front de other, running on nottin but de smell a coffee somebody bin brew in de break room round back. Ain't no time fa benne cake nor nottin sweet dat night, just cold coffee and keep movin. Five o'clock come and we still checkin, still writin, still makin sure everybody breathin right and settle. De supervisor bin gone home hours before, still vex when e left, mutterin bout how dis kind a night shouldn't happen on e watch, like we bin de one call in sick.

Six o'clock de sky start fa lighten up out de east window, dat grey turnin pink slow slow, day clean comin on whether we ready or not. My relief walk een de door bout quarter to seven and I ain't hardly have de strength fa give um de full report proper, I just say, e bin a rough one, look at de log, it all dey.

I walk out to my car and de light was up good by den, birds already carryin on like nottin happen, like de whole night ain't just try fa take we under. I was too tired even fa feel glad it was over. I just sit dey in de car a minute, hand on de wheel, and let my breath come slow before I could even turn de key.
