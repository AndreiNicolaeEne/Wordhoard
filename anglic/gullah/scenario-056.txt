Seven day I bin mind dat house, and fore de week done I know why dey pay somebody good fa do it. First night already something ain right. I walk in wid de key dey lef under de cootuh shell dey keep by de step, dat de same old cootuh shell bin dere long as I remember, and de house cold like nobody live dere in a year, and de icebox making one sound, kata-kata-kata, like e trying fa talk to me. I stand in de kitchen and say to myself, well, dat ain how a icebox spose to sound, enty. But I too tired fa study it dat night. I jes shum, shrug, and gone to bed in de back room.

Second day de dog get loose. E da come round de side yard where I lef de gate cracked fa let de air through, and fore I even done drink my coffee e gone, tail up, straight cross de field like e headed to Beaufort. I holler, I run, my nightgown still on under my coat, barefoot in de dew. Neighbor across de way, e see me and come out on e porch.

Neighbor: Wuh you lost, gal?

Me: De dog! E get loose!

Neighbor: E ain gone fa. Dat kind always circle back round to where de food at.

And e was right, cause fore a hour pass dat dog come trotting back on e own, tongue hanging, like e jes bin out doing e business and ain thought nothing bout it. I grab de rope and tie um good dat time, and I say a little prayer of thanks, cause how I gone tell de owner I done lost dey dog first full day.

De garden trouble ain hit all at once, e crept. Monday de tomato look fine. By Wednesday I shum de leaf curling up on de bottom row, little yellow spot spreading like somebody bin dropping tea on um. I ain no farmer but I know when something ain right wid a plant same as I know when somebody ain right in de face. I water um extra, I pull off what look bad, but by Friday half dat row done give up de ghost. I felt bad bout it, real bad, cause dat de kind of thing a buckra owner notice quick when e come home and walk de yard.

De stove eye gone out on Thursday, right in de middle of me trying fa fry up some fish for my own supper. Click click click and nothing, no flame, jes de gas smell creeping. I bin scared to leave it be and scared to mess wid it more, so I cut de gas off at de valve and eat cold food dat night and de next, and I call my tittuh on de phone bout it.

Tittuh: You call de man bout de stove?

Me: I ain even know who de man is. Dis ain my house.

Tittuh: Well leave um a note den. Dey ain gone blame you for de stove breaking, dat ain your doing.

Me: Enty. But e gone look like my doing if I ain say nothing.

Den come de visitor, no warning at all, jes a truck pull up in de yard round midday Friday and a man get out like e own de place. Turn out to be de owner cousin, come through on e way somewhere else, ain call, ain write, jes come. I near bout jump out my skin when I shum him walking up to de door.

Visitor: You de one watching de house?

Me: Yes sir, I bin here all week.

Visitor: Dey ain tell me nobody be here. I jes come to get something out de shed.

Me: Well, come on den, e still where e always bin far as I know.

E got e box out de shed, stood round talking bout nothing for a good while, den gone off again same way e come, sudden. Lef me shaking a little after, not scared exactly, jes rattled, cause you ain never spect nobody when you mindin a empty house.

De jolt of alarm come dat same evening. I bin looking for de little wood box de owner keep on de dresser, de one wid e mama ring inside, cause I bin dust round it careful all week and now I ain shum um nowhere. I turn dat room upside down, pull out drawer, look under de bed, my heart beating like a drum, thinking, Lord, I done lost e mama ring, how I gone face dat woman. I bin near bout crying fore I finally find um, e bin move to de closet shelf where I myself done put um safe when I dust de dresser Tuesday and forgot. I sat down right dere on de floor and jes breathe.

De lock-out come Saturday afternoon. I step out to hang de wash on de line, door swing shut behind me in de wind, and I ain had de key on me, jes had it setting right dere on de kitchen table where I could shum it through de glass, laughing at me almost. I sat on dat step from two o'clock to near bout five, waiting on de neighbor to come home so I could use e phone, no way to reach nobody else. De sun beat down and de mosquito start in on me and all I could do was watch dat key through de window and cut-eye at de door like e done me wrong on purpose.

De rain got in Sunday night through de window I lef crack in de front room, cause de heat bin so bad I ain thought to shut it fore de sky turn. I woke to de sound and run in dere and de sill bin soaked, water pooling on de floor, curtain hanging heavy and wet. I mop it up best I could in de dark, prop de window shut, and lay back down mad at myself.

De noise in de night come Monday. Something bumping round outside, low and steady, and I yeddy it clear through de wall. I lay dere a long time telling myself e ain nothing, jes de dog or de wind, but e ain stop, so I get up and go out wid de flashlight, heart up in my throat. Turn out to be nothing but a old washtub done blow off de porch, rolling round in de yard, knock knock knock against de step. I stood dere in de dark laughing at myself, den gone back to bed.

De stove I finally fix Tuesday, next to last day. I ain call nobody, I jes take de eye apart myself, clean out de little hole where de gas spose to come through, cause I recall my own mama stove doing dat same trick years back. Put um together, strike de match, and de flame catch blue and steady like e never gone out at all. I stood dere proud of myself, I ain gone lie.

Wednesday, de last full day, I clean like de house on fire. Scrub de floor, straighten every cushion, cut fresh flower for de table, sweep de porch, even pull what I could save in de garden and set um back up best I could so de damage ain show too bad. By evening de house shine, everything back in e place, nothing left to tell on me but what I bin decide to tell.

Owner come in Thursday evening, bag in hand, and look round de room slow, and I stood dere waiting.

Owner: House look good. Everything alright?

Me: Everything alright. De dog got loose one morning but I catch um quick. De stove eye gone out but I done fix um myself. Some rain got in de front room one night, I mop it up good, ain no harm done past de curtain. Tomato took a little sick toward de end of de week, I done what I could for um.

Owner: Dat all?

Me: Dat all worth telling. De rest ain nothing.

E smile a little, e ain press me further, and I gone on home wid my pay in my pocket and my legs tired like I bin walking seven day straight instead of jes minding a house. My tittuh ask me later how de week bin.

Tittuh: So how e go?

Me: Sweet-mout on top, plenty trouble underneath, same as any week mindin somebody else business. But de house standing, and so am I, enty dat enough.
