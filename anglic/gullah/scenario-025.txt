We aint hardly clear de yard fo e start in.

My husband: E cut e eye at de rice soon as e set down. Enty you shum?

Me: E ain cut e eye. E just lookin close, dat all.

My husband: E lookin like e taste sumting rotten, den e say "mmm" like dat, and everybody roun de table know wa dat mean.

Me: You too tetchy bout um. Dat just how e stay.

My husband: And e daddy, e da tell dat same old story bout de storm and de boat again, third time e tell we since we bin come dis year. I done yeddy dat story so much I could tell um myself, backward, and never miss a word.

Me: E ain got but a few story e like fa tell. Leave um be, he old.

My husband: I lef um be, I lef um be good and long. Dat goodbye at de door gone on so long I tink e was gwine run clean to day clean. Oona daddy hold my hand and sweet-mout me bout de church and de garden and de weather, and every time I try fa step off de porch, oona mama call out one more ting.

Me: Mmm. She do go on, for true.

My husband: She do.

Me: And de benne cake she wrap up fa we, she gone back in de house twice fa find um.

My husband: Enty I say so?

Me: You ain say nothing bout no cake.

My husband: No, but I bin tinking um good and hard. Slow like a cootuh, dat woman, when she ready fa let we go.

Me: Slow like a cootuh, for true. Drive on, tittuh waitin on we fa call her soon as we get in.
