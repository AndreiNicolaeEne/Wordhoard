The eldest: Oona see how many we got so far? We need six for true, seven better, 'fore de rest of dem chillun come down. But we ga start wid what we got.

The little one: Where de line run at, tittuh?

The eldest: From de big oak clear down to de fence post, dat's one side. Pon de other side, from de porch step to de ditch by de benne patch. Step outside dat line while somebody da chase ya, ya out, done, finish, ya can't come back een dat round. Enty?

The little one: Enty we could hide 'cross de ditch den?

The eldest: No, chile. Cross de ditch, e out. Simple so. You ain't no cootuh, ya can run fast enough fa stay inside dat line.

The little one: Who gon chase first?

The eldest: We do finger-shoot, one-two-three, and whoever don't match, e da chase.

The little one: Wha' if I sweet-mout ya, put me second fa chase 'stead of first?

The eldest: Sweet-mout ain't gon work on me, chile. Finger-shoot decide e fair, dat's all.

The little one: Wha' count for a tag? Just touch light so?

The eldest: (reach and tap de little one shoulder) See so? Dat's a tag, good and firm, pon de arm or de back. Brush ain't nothing, buckra breeze could do dat much.

The little one: A'right, I yeddy ya.

The eldest: Now soon as ya bin tap, ya freeze right dere. Don't move, don't even shift ya foot, till 'nother one still runnin' loose come tap ya hand and set ya free again.

The little one: Wha' if everybody freeze and ain't nobody left fa tap we loose?

The eldest: Den round done, chaser win dat one. Whoever e tag first, e chase next round, fair so.

The little one: Wha' if I mess up de first time?

The eldest: First round ain't gon count 'gainst ya, ya hear? Don't gi' me cut-eye 'bout it neither, jus' run when ya shum comin' and freeze when e ketch ya. Ya gon learn e good 'fore day clean tomorrow, if we play dat long.
