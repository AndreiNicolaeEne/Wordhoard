Chile, let me tell you bout de worst trip me and my tittuh ever tek in dis life. I laugh bout it now, but Lawd, enty was no laughing dat day, not one bit.

We bin plan dat trip good, mont' befo' it ever happen. Ticket bought early, bag pack neat, everyt'ing fa go smooth like butter. My tittuh say, "We gon get up day clean, be on de road befo' de sun even good up, so we ain' rushing nobody." I say awright, dat sound fine to me. We had it all figga out, down to de hour.

Well e start bad from de jump. We ain' even lef' de island good when de ride dat s'posed to carry we to de station don't come. We standing dey by de road wid we bag, waiting, waiting, phone in we hand, and de man never show a-tall. Near a hour pass befo' a neighbor come by and carry we sheself, and we already runnin' behind fore de trip even properly start.

By de time we get on de big bus up in town, we tired and vex already. Den bout two hour in, de bus start to shake, and smoke come rolling out from under. Driver pull over side de road, cut e eye at de dashboard, and say e sorry, e got to call fa 'nother bus to come. Everybody groan and start climbing off, standing dey in de heat on de shoulder of de road.

Wasn't long befo' mo' folk gather. Look like every bus dat bin trouble dat day stop right dey too, till it was a whole crowd of we, strangers all mixed up together, some setting on dey bag, some pacing, some fanning deyself wid whatever dey had in hand. De driver sweet-mout we, say, "Oona jus' hold tight, another bus da come soon," but e own self ain' know when soon was.

One hour pass. Two hour. T'ree hour pass and still nobody could say when anyt'ing gon move. Folk start to grumble, some cut-eye on de driver like e de one mek de engine break in de first place. A little boy start to cry cause e hungry, and him mama diggin' in her bag fa somet'ing to hush him.

Den de sky, on top of all dat, turn dark. Cloud come rolling in low and thunder start to roll behind it, and befo' anybody could get shelter, de rain come down hard and cold. We all standing dey soak, no cover but de broke-down bus itself, and everybody trying to squeeze up under de little bit of roof it gi.

In all dat commotion, my tittuh gone from side me. I look round, I don't shum nowhere, not in de crowd, not by de bus, nowhere. I holler e name over de rain, my heart beating hard, t'inking all kind of foolishness. Bout twenty minute pass, feel like twenty year, befo' I find e done gone round back de station looking fa a bathroom, ain' tell nobody, and got sheself caught up talking to some ooman she never met befo' in her life.

Right bout dat same time, dat's when a stranger, a ooman standing near we wid her own umbrella, see we soak to de bone and shaking, and she just open e umbrella over we head and share it, no word needed. Den she dig in e bag and gi we some hot coffee out a thermos she had, ain' ax fa nothing back, ain' even know we name. I still t'ink bout dat ooman today, how e ain' have to do dat, but e done it anyway. Dat de kind of kindness you don't forget.

By de time de replacement bus finally roll up, we done spend near fifty dollar we ain' never plan fa, on food fa hold we over and on a lil room to change out we wet clothes, cause everyt'ing in we bag done soak through too.

We get back on de road, but now we good and behind, and dat's when me and my tittuh start to fuss.

Me: We need to get off dis bus at de next stop and tek a cab de rest de way, we never gon mek it in time otherwise.

The tittuh: A cab gon cost we mo' money we ain' got, and we already spend fifty we ain' plan fa. We stay on dis bus.

Me: Dis bus too slow, we gon miss de whole service if we wait on it.

The tittuh: And how you know a cab wouldn't get loss same as dis driver? You don't know dis road no better dan him.

Me: I know it better dan you, dat's fa sure.

We went back and forth good, right dey in front everybody on dat bus, till a man setting near we lean over and say, "Oona betta hush, oona giving me a headache wid all dat." We hush, but we ain' settle nothing between weself.

Turn out we never tek no cab, we stay right dey on de bus, but de driver, trying to get round some flooding from de storm, tek a wrong turn off de highway and don't catch it fa near two hour. We riding down road none of we ever see befo', turning back, stopping to ax direction from a man at a lil store who ain' even yeddy of de town we trying to reach. My tittuh ain' say "I told you so" but e face say it plenty.

By dat time I so tired I could bawl right dey in my seat. My feet swell up in my shoe, my eye heavy heavy, and I ain' had nothing but a piece of benne candy since day clean dat morning. Dat right dey was de bottom of de whole t'ing, setting in dat seat in de dark, t'inking we never gon get dey, t'inking de whole trip done gone to waste befo' it even start.

But den, jus' when I done give up in my mind, de driver spot de right sign fa de highway we need, and de rain done finally clear off, and traffic dat bin backed up from de storm start to move again, quick like somebody wave a hand over it. E say over de speaker, "Oona hold on now, we almost dey," and fa de first time all day, e was right.

We finally roll in nine hour pass when we s'posed to be dey, nine whole hour late. De service long over by den, but de family still setting round, waiting on we, and when dey see we come through dat door, wet-headed, wrinkle up, walking like we hundred years old, dey ain' fuss one bit, dey just get up and hug we, glad we mek it a-tall.

Me and my tittuh look like two drown cootuh time we walk in dat door, mud on we shoe, hair gone every which way, dress look like it been fought over. But we dey. We finally dey.

Now I laugh bout it good, cause what else you gon do wid a day like dat. It put gray hair on my head dat I ain' had befo'. But I tell you dis, I ain' never forget dat ooman wid de umbrella, not to dis day, and I ain' never gon let my tittuh forget how e wander off looking fa a bathroom and had me screaming e name in de rain neither.
