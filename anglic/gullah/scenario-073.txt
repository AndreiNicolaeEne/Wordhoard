Set you down easy, sugar, cause this here take a minute to tell right, and oona need for hear the whole of it 'fore you go any further into this family, cause that's what this been ten year now, family, plain and simple, blood or no blood.

It bin about ten year back, right after the big storm come through and tore up half the island, when the core of we first come together. Wasn't no plan to it. The one who really pull we all in. I gone call her the first one, cause she was, she the deacon daughter, and she the one who stand up in the yard behind the church with a pot big as a wash-tub and say everybody who lost they roof or they freezer full of spoil food, oona come eat crab and rice with we, ain't no charge, ain't nobody fa pay a cent. She da cook from day clean 'til dark, three day straight, and that's how it start. Wasn't but four, five of we at the first, but by the end of that week it bin the whole crew, near enough to what we is now.

Now the one who almost ain't stay, that's her workmate, the one who come by that first night straight off her buckra job still in her work clothes, kind of stiff, kind of holding back, like e ain't sure e belong round people who all done known each other since Sunday school. E almost gone right back out that gate. But the deacon daughter, she ain't let that happen. She shum standing there by the fence with a plate in e hand and nowhere to set, and she just pull up a chair and say, set here, tittuh, and don't get up till you done eat. That's all it take. E stay. E been staying ever since, matter fact e the one who bring the biggest pot to everything now, like e trying to pay back that first plate ten time over.

Now I got to tell you about the falling-out, cause you going to yeddy people bring it up sideways and you need the whole truth so it don't confuse you. Two of we. I ain't going say who, cause it long done patched and don't need dragging back up whole, but two of we had words over money, over who was suppose to pay back who for a truck repair, and it get ugly for a stretch, real cut-eye between them two for near about two month, wouldn't even set at the same table. What patch it was the peacemaker, and oona going to meet her, she still the peacemaker to this day, she made the both of them come help her can up benne and put up preserves one whole Saturday, shut up together in one kitchen with nowhere to go, and by the time the last jar was seal they was laughing again. And that's the same Saturday the joke get born, the one oona going to yeddy a hundred time before the year out, somebody ask who put the sugar in with the benne, cause one of them two put sugar in the savory batch being so vex, and to this day if anybody mess up a recipe, whole table hollering who put the sugar in the benne, and can't nobody explain it quick to a stranger, but oona going to feel it land soon enough.

Now the one who left, that was maybe four year in. E say e was moving on, fa take a job off the island, up toward the city, and ain't nobody push back on that reason cause it seem true enough at the time and e ain't owe nobody more explanation than that. Whether that's the whole of it, I can't say for certain, some things people keep for theyself and that's they right, enty? E still send a message round the holidays. It ain't the same as e being here, but the door ain't shut neither.

Not long after that a new one come in, brought by cousin to the peacemaker, who vouch for her hard, say she good people, she raise right. But we ain't just take cousin word for it right off. We tested her a while, if I'm honest with you, small thing, seeing do she show up when she say she going show up, do she carry her weight when it come time to clean up after, do she keep her mouth when somebody tell her something in confidence. Took the better part of a year 'fore she was properly counted, not tested no more, just family. That's about how long it take, generally, so don't be surprised if it feel that way for you too, sugar, ain't nothing personal in it, it just how we do.

The thing that really bond the rest of we deeper was the trip we took down to the water for three day when the boat motor die halfway out and we was stuck on a little spit of marsh with the tide coming in and no phone signal to speak of. We had to wade a good piece with the water round we knee, mud pulling e shoe clean off, mosquito thick as smoke, and one of we near step on a cootuh in the dark and hollered loud enough to wake the dead. We laugh about it now, but child, we was scared that night, truly, and when help finally come at day clean the next morning we was holding on to each other crying and laughing both at once. Ain't nobody who was on that boat ever forget it, and it draw the rest of we close in a way regular Sunday dinner never could.

I got to tell you too 'bout the one who gone from we now, moved off to be near her children up north a few year back. E still call. E still show up on the video when we do the big holiday gathering, propped up on somebody phone at the head of the table like e never left, and everybody still save her a plate out of habit even though ain't nobody going eat it. That empty seat still feel some kind of way, and I reckon it always going to.

Now here how it lay out these day, so you know who's who without me having to spell it every time. The peacemaker still keep the peace, same as she always done, anybody get to fussing, she the one who step in quiet and fix it 'fore it get big. The deacon daughter still organize everything, every last gathering, she the one texting oona a week ahead asking who bringing what, and don't nobody dare show up empty-hand to one of her things. Her workmate, the one who almost ain't stay, e always late, every single time, e got a reputation for it now, we tell people to give e a time hour before the real time and e still find a way to be behind, and don't nobody even fuss about it no more, that's just e part to play.

We did have a low point, I ain't going lie to you. 'Bout two year ago the whole thing near come apart, too much left unsaid for too long, feeling hurt that ain't nobody bother to say out loud, folk pulling back from the group chat, showing up less, that kind of slow quiet break where can't nobody point to one thing but everybody feel it. What mend it was the peacemaker again, calling a sit-down, plain and blunt, telling everybody put they cut-eye away and say what actually bothering them, no sweet-mout, no talking round it. Took a hard evening, some crying, some real talk, but we come out the other side of it closer than 'fore.

One thing ain't never change since the very first week, and that's the fish fry the Saturday closest to when the storm first hit, every year, rain or shine, same as that first pot in the deacon daughter yard. We don't miss it. Whatever else happen in a year, that Saturday we together, no excuse taken.

And you, sugar, you already started belonging 'fore you even know it. You the one who show up early to help set up chairs 'thout nobody asking you, and you brought your own dish that first Sunday 'stead of waiting to be told what to bring. That's how we know. So welcome, chile. You family now, close as blood, same as the rest of we.
