I gwine tell oona de whole of it, start to finish, cause plenty people done ax me how long e take and how many hand come help wid dis house, and I tell dem straight: eleven month, and six different pusson lay hand to it 'fore e done stand.

We bin livin in de old place down near de landing, me and de wife and de chirren, and dat house done get too tight for we. De eldest done grow big, done need e own room and cyan share wid e little brudder no more, and den de wife mother house flood out bad one season and e come stay wid we too. Six of we in a house wid three room, movin round each other like cootuh try turn in e own shell. De wife say to me one evenin after supper, e look tired to e bone.

The wife: "We cyan go on so. Oona gwine haffa build, or dis house gwine bust wide open from too much people in um."

So we set down at de table, me and de wife and my uncle who done build house all e life, and we reckon on it good. We figure maybe five, six month, and we count up who could come help, my uncle, my woman brudder, tittuh husband, de neighbor man down de road, and de two of we own self. Six hand all told, dat de number we settle on, though we ain't know yet how far off five month gwine be.

We start clearin ground at day clean one Saturday, and dat part gone sweet, everybody in good spirit, de sun just risin over de marsh. But den come de wrong turn. My uncle bin lay out de foundation e own way, and I follow behind wid de measurin, and near two week in, we shum de corner ain't square, e off by more dan a hand's width, and dat kind of off gwine throw de whole frame crooked. My uncle stand dere and cut e eye down at de string line long time 'fore e say anything.

My uncle: "Dis ain't right. We gotta pull it up and start over, enty?"

Enty indeed. Two week of diggin and settin block, all of it come up and done again. Dat de first hard lesson dis house teach we, measure twice, and don't let pride sweet-mouth you into thinkin you already right.

After dat we get steady on it, weekend after weekend, evenin after work when de light still hold. Den one Saturday mornin, 'fore we even barely start, three truck pull up we ain't call for. It was de church men, come cause dey yeddy from de preacher we bin strugglin, and dey just show up wid dey own hammer and dey own lunch pail like dey belong dere.

The neighbor man: "Oona think oona gwine build a house wid six hand and no help from de Lord people? Move over, we da come whether you ready or not."

Dat day we get more done dan de whole two week before it, and I ain't never forget how full my chest feel watchin dem men work on my house like e was dey own.

Den de weather come. Rain fall for de better part of a week straight, dat kind of low gray rain don't stop don't start, just set in and stay. We couldn't do nothin but set on de porch of de old house and watch water stand in de yard where de new frame post bin goin. De wife auntie bring over benne cake to keep spirit up, and we eat um slow, watchin de sky, waitin.

Somewhere past de middle of it, we run short on lumber. De buckra at de yard say de order ain't come in, gwine be another two week 'fore more come, and we couldn't wait dat long wid de weather already stole so much time. So we make do, pull good board off de old shed out back, straighten out bent nail wid a hammer and use um again, split what long board we had into shorter piece where shorter piece would do. Ain't pretty in every spot, but e hold.

Den come de scare. My woman brudder up on de roof settin rafter, and e foot slip on a wet spot we ain't see, and e go down off de edge, maybe eight, nine foot, land in de soft dirt we bin pile up for de foundation. Everybody run. E lay dere a minute, we all standin over um holdin breath, and den e open e eye and cuss and try to set up.

Tittuh: "Oona lay still, don't you move, I gotta shum if anything broke!"

My woman brudder: "Ain't nothin broke but my pride. Help me up 'fore I get dirt down my collar."

Nothin worse dan a scare, thank God, though e walk stiff a week after.

Den come de argument, and dis one nearly split de whole crew. It was over how to frame de porch, my uncle want um done de old way e always know, deep post set straight in de ground, and de neighbor man want um set on block like e seen in town, say e last longer against de wet. Dey stand out dere in de yard cuttin dey eye at each other, neither one gwine give.

My uncle: "I bin buildin house 'fore you was born. De ground know how to hold a post."

The neighbor man: "And I done seen dem old post rot out in ten year. Times change, de wood ain't gotta rot wid it."

We ain't settle it dat day, and dey ain't talk sweet to each other for near a week, but in de end we split de difference, post on block, set deep just de same, and it stand fine to dis day.

De worst of it come when we shum de back wall bin buildin crooked from de start, de sill under um rotten where water bin settin against it for who know how long. Wasn't no fixin dat wall, e had to come down whole and go back up new, framin and all, near a month lost right when we bin thinkin we close.

But den, oh, de day de frame finally stood up straight and true, all four wall and de roof line true against de sky, dat was a good day. We stand back and just look on it a long time, ain't nobody say much, just look.

De eldest: "E look like a real house now, enty?"

Me: "E do. E finally do."

De last stretch we push hard, four night runnin workin past dark, lantern hung on nail, truck headlight shinin on de wall, everybody so tired we hardly talk, just work, hangin door, settin window, nailin down de last board. My hand bin so sore I couldn't hardly hold a fork after.

And den one evenin, e was done. Just so, e was done. We all stand inside it together, me and de wife and de chirren and my uncle and my woman brudder and tittuh husband and de neighbor man, all six hand dat bin help plus we own self, and nobody say much cause nobody need to.

We tally it up after, eleven month start to finish, six different pusson lend dey hand along de way, not countin de church men who come dat one Saturday unasked. Longer dan we reckon, harder dan we reckon, but e stand.

I stand in um now most every evenin, in de room where de chirren sleep good and separate, where de wife mother got e own corner to set in, and I run my hand along de wall dat had to come down and go back up right, and I think on all of it, de wrong turn, de rain, de fall, de argument, de four night we push through dark to finish. E ain't just board and nail to me. E de eleven month and de six hand, all set down together, standin.
