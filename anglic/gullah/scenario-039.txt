The foreman: Oona hold up now, 'fore everybody scatter off home. We ain't settle t'morrow yet, enty.

The eldest: I say we start day clean, six sharp, and beat the heat 'fore e come down pon we head.

The driver: Six too early for my old bones, tittuh. Seven better. Ain't nobody fa be standin round in the dark waitin on nobody else.

The young one: Seven fine by me too. I bin up 'fore day clean every day this week a'ready, my body done tired.

The foreman: Awright then, seven e is, no more argue. Job t'morrow down to the old landing, the one the buckra been talkin bout since spring. E say e can't wait no more.

The eldest: I bring the come-along and the rope, same as always.

The driver: I got the truck, and I bring what tin left in the shed, but dat ain't much.

The young one: Dat's what I worried bout. We runnin low on tin, enty, and if e rain like the sky lookin, we lose the whole day anyway.

The foreman: We make do wit what we got. If e short we short.

The tittuh: And who fa climb up top and lay it down, cause my knee ain't fa dat no more, I tell oona true.

The foreman: Cut-eye all oona want, but he young, he strong. He fa do the ridge.

The young one: Every time oona stick me wit the hard part! I do the ridge last time too, my back still ain't right from it.

The driver: Oona sweet-mout him now cause e need him bad. Anyhow who else we missin? Where the other one?

The foreman: I call em. E say e boat done broke down again, e can't make it t'morrow.

The eldest: Dat leave the four of we then. Enough if the weather hold.

The driver: Somebody owe somebody now, cause I ain't climbin nothin and I ain't buyin the benne cake neither.

The young one: Oona all owe ME. But I be there seven, on time, oona yeddy me?

The foreman: We yeddy you. Seven, day clean job, don't nobody be late.
