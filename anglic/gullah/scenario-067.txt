Come on now, walk wid me slow, I gwine show you how e stay pon dis street, cause ain't nobody write none of it down, oona jus haffa know.

You can leave de truck right dere by de ditch, front of de old lot, dat fine, been fine long as I member. Buckra come through sometime, drive slow, gawk round, but dey don't park yah, and you don't park by de pastor house neither, wid de grass grow high like nobody use it. Look empty, enty, but e ain't. Somebody bin buried under dat corner long time back, and dis street don't forget dat.

See where de fence post lean, right past de tittuh house, where de benne bush grow wild, down to where de creek bend and de cootuh sun deyself on de log? Dat de line. Ain't no wire, ain't no mark, but every soul yah know not fa cross it. De old woman ground start right dere and e end at de ditch, and we leave it be.

Come day clean, dis whole street quiet, quiet till bout seven. Nobody run dey mower, nobody holler, nobody let dey dog bark loud. And come dark, same ting. You yeddy a mower start early some morning, dat de one gwine catch grief for it, sho.

Now when you pass folk on de road, you speak to de elder first, every time, before you even look at who standin next to um, e done live yah de longest, e get de first word. Den de mother of de house, den whoever else. Dat how e go, e always go so.

You see somebody da carry ting down de road, dat mean dey headin home, and you speak den too. You need borrow a tool, you ask plain, you don't jus take. And you bring um back clean, same day if you can. Somebody keep a borrow ting too long, don't nobody fuss, don't nobody say a word out loud, but dat person get de cut-eye every time dey pass, and next time dey ask, de answer sweet-mout um and dey don't get nothing.

And de one rule everybody break, sooner or later? Cutting cross de old lot fa save time 'stead of walking round. Everybody done shum somebody doin it. Everybody done do it deyself. Ain't nobody gon say nothing bout dat neither.
