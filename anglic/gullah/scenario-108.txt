Tittuh, I done owe you dis letter since de fig tree drop e leaf, and now e budding again, so you know how long I bin carrying it round in my head 'fore I set down and write um out. Every week I say today de day, and den de wash come, or de chirren need see 'bout, and de letter stay in my head 'stead of on de paper. But here e is now, finally, and I gwine tell you everyt'ing, so set yourself down good, dis gwine be long.

First t'ing, de house. Member how de bottom step on de porch bin rotted since 'fore you lef, how you daddy keep say he gwine fix um and never fix um? Well e finally fix um. Took him a whole Saturday and half a Sunday fa get um right, and e cuss dat wood de whole time, but de step solid now, ain't gwine give way under nobody foot no more. Enty dat someting. But soon as dat step done, de water pump start acting up, e da make dat groaning sound in de mornin' like e tired 'fore de day even start, so now we got a new trouble where de old one used to be. Dat's how e go round here, one t'ing mend, anudder t'ing broke, seem like de house keep score. We put up de benne by de fence dis year too, and de row doing good, gwine have plenty fa parch when de time come. Somebody done plant collard where de tomato used to be, and dey coming up thick.

De big change since you bin gone is de eldest move off de creek house into town, close by de church. I yeddy say e bin thinking on it a good while but e ain't want to go. De creek been eating de yard little by little, every big tide take a piece, till de shed 'bout to go in de water, and e finally say enough. E workmate get on wid a buckra outfit doing yard work in town round dat same time, and dat's how e come to have a truck fa borrow, de deacon lend one too, and de two of dem move all de furniture in two whole days. Now de eldest stay in a little place near de crossroad, e say e miss de water sound at night but e don't miss de worry every time de wind pick up.

You 'member de t'ing we don't say out loud, de t'ing 'bout de rooster and de Sunday shoes? I ain't gwine explain um here neither, I just leave um right dere so you can laugh to yourself wherever you at.

De neighbors done shift round some. De one used to live cross de road, e move off to be near e chirren up state, and de house stay empty a good while till a young family from up de island move in. Dey got a baby now, you ought to shum, e growing fast, already trying to walk 'fore e time. De old couple down by de landing, dey family done grow too, de daughter bin have another one dis spring, make t'ree chirren under dat one roof now, dey porch always got somebody sitting on it. De daughter-in-law give de mailman cut-eye something fierce de day e run over de flower bed, but dat done pass now, everybody friends again.

De one at de counter down de store ax 'bout you near every time I come in.

The one at the counter: How dat one doing, e still up dere?

Me: E doing fine.

The one at the counter: Tell um come by when e visit, I keep asking and nobody bring um round no more.

E always sweet-mout me for a little extra when I check out, but e mean well. You workmate ax after you too, de one used to fuss wid you 'bout who make better rice, e say e miss having somebody to argue wid.

Now, dis de part I couldn't say to your face, so I gwine write um instead. Dat mornin' 'fore you left, when you bin so busy packing you ain't have time to eat, I bin cook you dat plate you ax for and I eat half of it myself waiting on you, den when you finally come look for um I tell you I ain't touch it. I sorry for dat, small a t'ing as e be, e bin sit on me all dis time and now e off my chest.

Last season bin hard on de place, more storm dan usual, one blow through and take down de old tree by de gate, de one you used to climb. De garden ain't do too bad though, de okra come in strong even wid all dat rain, and de shrimping bin good for a stretch, de men bring in plenty and de whole street smell like e for a week. De cootuh dem been out extra dis year too, crossing de road in de evenin', chirren stopping traffic to move dem out de way.

Now le' me ax you someting, since I done gi' you all de news from here. How de work stay wid you, e treating you right, or e wearing you down? You eating good, or you living off dat quick food e sell round de corner up dere? And I hope oona all doing good in dat house, you and whoever stay wid you, oona keeping warm? Dat cold up dere, day clean must come different from here, enty? I try to picture you waking up to dat and I can't hardly see it.

De mother ax after you too, e stop me on de road just yesterday.

The mother: Him call you lately?

Me: E call las' month, e sound good.

The mother: Tell um write him old mother someting, not just call.

So dere e is, e want a letter too, don't leave her out when you finally set down to answer dis one.

Everyt'ing else 'bout de same, chirren growing too fast, church still meetin' same time, de old dog still sleepin' under de same tree like e ain't got a care in de world. I miss you more dan I let on when we talk on de phone, dem call too short to say half of what I want to say, dat's how come dis letter come out so long.

I gwine send you dat benne brittle I always say I gwine send, soon as dis batch cool and I get a box dat won't crush in de mail. Don't fuss at me if e take a minute more, you know how I stay. All my love to you, tittuh, till de next letter come, and dis time e won't take so long, I promise.
