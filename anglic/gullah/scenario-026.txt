Tittuh, lemme gi' you de whole account, cause I know oona gon side wid me anyway.

E start wid dat motor for de boat. E done gone and buy a new outboard, ain't say a word to me, and we done bin talk bout dat land tax de buckra county done raise, say we got to hold money fa dat fus. I shum de receipt fall out e coat pocket when I da hang up e clothes, and I ask e straight, right dey in de kitchen.

Me: Wuh dis? Oona spend all dat on a motor when de tax note settin right dey on de table?

E ain't even look up good.

The husband: De old motor bin fixin fa quit anyhow. I need one fa go out to work in de creek.

Me: Enty we say we gwine hold off till fall?

Dat when e drag up de ole ting, bout how I bin de one lost money on my cousin car note ten year back, like dat got anyting to do wid a boat motor. E say I ain't got no room to talk 'bout spendin, and dat cut me deep, cause dat mistake done and gone, we done pay dat debt off long time.

De sharpest ting e say, e look me dead in de eye and say, "Oona always da tell people how fa spend, but oona hand ain't clean neither." I ain't say nothin back, just cut my eye at e and walk out on de porch. E know dat cut-eye mean I done wid talkin.

We ain't hardly yeddy one another good fa three, four day, slow as a cootuh. Just "pass de salt" and dat kind o' ting. E try one sweet-mout ting come Thursday, bring me some benne cake e mama make, leave it by day clean 'fore e gone to work, ain't say sorry wid e mouth but I know wuh e mean by it.

I ain't back down first, I tell you dat much. E de one soften, e go fa pay half de tax note out e own pocket widout me ask.

So dat my verdict, tittuh: e wrong fa buy dat motor behind my back, but I ain't no saint 'bout money neither, and a marriage ain't worth all dat silence over one motor. E come round. Dat enough for me.
