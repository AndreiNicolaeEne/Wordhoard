Dis de story bout how we people come fa live yah on dis island, an I gwine tell um de way e bin pass down to me, den I gwine pass um on to oona same fashion, cause dat how it spose to go.

Before, we people bin live up de country, way back pass de forks of de river, where de ground neva did belong to we noway. We bin work oda man land, share crop for a buckra what own de whole stretch of it, an every year de crop come in, e come round wid e sweet mout talking bout how e gwine mek it right by we come nex season, but nex season come an we still owe um mo dan de season before. My great-grandfather bin a young man den, an e see how e gwine be, e see chillun coming an no ground fa call e own an no end to de owing. E say e not gwine raise no family on a buckra debt what neva get smaller no matter how e wuk. So dat de reason e lef.

E gone off by esef first. Nobody else. E daddy hoe head wrap up in a cloth an a lil sack on e back, dat all e carry, an e walk. Some of de way a man gwine to market let um ride on de wagon, but mos of it e bin on e own two foot, walking from day clean to dark, sleeping wherever bin dry, a barn, a church step, one night in de woods wid nuttin but e coat over um.

E ain lef by esef needcessary. E bin travel wid a cousin, a young man bout e same age, de two of dem grow up close as brother. But de cousin neva reach yah wid um. My great-grandfather neva talk bout um much, an when e did, e neva call de name, not one time. E jes say "my cousin," an e voice go down low, an oona know right den not fa ask no mo. Whatever happen on dat road, sickness, or de water, we neva rightly find out, e de only one what bin dey to see it, an e ain tell de whole of it to nobody, not even e own wife. Dat de one part of de story wid a hole in um, an de hole ain neva close up, not to dis day.

E come to dis place wid next to nuttin. De clothes on e back, e daddy hoe head, an one lil iron pot e mama put in e hand fore e lef, she say fa remember she by. Dat same pot right yah in dis house dis minute, up on de shelf, we ain cook in um no mo dese long years but we keep um jes de same. Every so often somebody tek um down an hold um a while, an dat enough fa know where we people come from.

Fus few month bin hard, hard fa true. E ain had no work steady tall. A family down by de creek tek um in, let um sleep in a lil room off de back of dey house, ain e own place, e bin borrow um, jes fa have a roof over e head. Dem people good to um, share what dey had, some day it ain much mo dan cootuh e ketch out de creek esef an whatever green bin growing. E tell how some evening all dey had bin a han full a benne parch up in de skillet, an dat suppose to carry a grown man through til morning. But e neva forget dem people kindness, cause dey ain owe um a thing an dey ain need to tek um in a tall.

E tek whatever work e could find, a day yah, a day dey. E dig for one man a week, cut wood for anoda, go out on de water wid some of de men when dey need a extra hand fa haul in de catch. Some man round bin give um cut-eye at first, wondering who dis stranger be, but e neva mind it, e jes kept e head down an e hand busy til dey come fa know um. Slow, slow, e save little by little, put by whatever bin left after e eat.

Take um mo dan two year fore e had enough fa sen for de rest, e wife an e chillun back up de country. Two year an some mont, dat how dey tell it, before e could sen word an de money fa bring dem down. E wife bin wait all dat time not knowing de day for certain, jes trusting whatever word e bin able fa sen back to her. When de day finally come dat dey bin due, dey say e bin down by de landing from day clean, waiting, waiting, an when e shum dem coming up de road at last e neva say one word, e jes tek de smallest one up in e arm an hold um dey a long time.

Once dey bin all together, e tek up de trade e gwine keep for good, e learn fa mek net off one of de men e bin wuk wid out on de water, an e got good at um, good enough dat people from all bout de island come fa buy e net or fa have one mend. Dat de trade e chillun learn off um after, an some of we still know de way of it today.

Wid in de fus few year dey find a lil piece a ground not too far from de water an dey build up a house right dey, small, but e dem own, an dat mek all de difference from borrowing another man roof.

An right dey, on dat same ground, de chillun start fa come, my grandmother bin de fus one born right dey, an after she, e brother an e sister after dat. Dat when e say de place stop bein jes somewhere e bin lan at an start bein home fa true, when e own chillun bin born right on dat dirt, when e could stand on e own step an say dis yah mine, an my chillun born right yah.

My grandmother bin de one tell me all dis, when I bin small, setting wid her of an evening.

The grandmother: Chile, oona know how we come to be yah? E ain always so. My daddy walk down yah from up de country by esef, wid nuttin but a hoe head an a lil pot. Enty I done tell you dis before?

Me: You done tell me, but tell me again.

The grandmother: Well den. E come by esef, e wuk day labor til e could sen for we, an e neva stop wukking a day in e life after dat. Oona remember dat, hear? Oona come from people what walk fa get yah, not people what jes bin born setting still.

I bin small den an I ain rightly understand all of it, but I yeddy every word, an I hold on to um close. Now I de one telling it, setting on de same step near bout, an my own grandchillun round me, an I tell um de same way e bin tell me, de walking, de cousin whose name nobody carry no mo, de lil pot still up on de shelf, de net we still know how fa mek in dis family, de house dat bin small an borrow at first an den dey own fa true once de chillun come.

Dat how we people come fa live yah, an dat how I pass um on, same as e bin pass to me. Oona hold on to um too, hear, cause once de one what carry um is gone, de story gone wid um, less oona tek um up an carry um on esef.
