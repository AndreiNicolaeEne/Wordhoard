That evening the light gone out right after dusk, and we ain't had no warning bout it, enty. One minute the bulb da shine over the table, next minute the whole house black like the inside of a pot. Wind bin blow hard the whole afternoon, and we could yeddy it still rattling on the tin roof after the light gone, so I reckon a line done drop somewhere down the road, cause the whole row of house dark that night, not just we own.

First thing everybody scramble. My tittuh run for the drawer where we keep the matches.

The mother: Oona find that flashlight fa me, the batt'ry weak but e better than nothing.

We look all through the house, and all we could find, after all that looking, was one candle stub no bigger than my thumb and a box of match half empty.

The eldest: That all we got in this whole house?

Nobody answer, cause everybody already know e true.

We light the one stub and set um on a saucer in the front room, and the whole household haul up close round that little light. I shum sitting there quiet in the glow, watching the flame like e was the only thing left in the world. My little brother bin knock into the same chair leg three time in the dark, and the third time he cuss soft under he breath, and the mother cut-eye him even with barely light enough to see by.

Nobody try fa fix nothing that night, wire or no wire. We just sit close together, tittuh telling old story from before, and we eat cold rice and a little benne candy for sweet-mout after. The light ain't come back till we all done gone off to bed, and by day clean the fan was running again like nothing ever happened.
