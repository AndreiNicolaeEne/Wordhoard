Me: Come yah, love, set down by me. I know oona don't want fa talk much right now, and dat alright, I ain need you fa say nottin. I just gwine tell you how e bin fa me, cause somebody oughta tell you straight, ain nobody tell me straight and I bin walk round like a blind cootuh dem first few week, bumpin into everything.

De first days, I ain even feel like myself was dere. People da come and go, phone da ring, dish da pile up on de counter cause everybody and dey mama bringin food, benne wafer and rice and I don't know what all, and somebody always axin me question bout de service, bout de plot, bout who gwine speak. Buckra from de funeral home call bout paper. My tittuh from up de road, e come set wid me every day dat week, e da hold my hand and I bin grateful, mighty grateful, but I tell you true, wid all dat crowd and all dat noise dey wasn't hardly no room fa feel nottin direct. E was like standin in de yard wid everybody talkin at once and you can't hear one single voice clear. I bin numb, and I bin glad fa de numb, cause I ain know what was comin after.

And what come after was de night everybody finally gone home. Dat first night I truly bin alone in dat house. Oona know how a house get, once de last car pull off and de last dish get wash and put away. De quiet come down on me like sumpin wid weight to it, like e could press you flat. I set in dat kitchen and de refrigerator hummin was de only voice in de whole world and I couldn't hardly stand it. Wasn't nobody left fa distract me from de fact of e. Dat quiet tell me everything de crowd bin coverin up.

De weeks after dat, my body ain even do right. Some day I couldn't eat nottin, food ain have no taste, I'd set a plate and just look at e. Den sleep, sleep ain come whole no more, e come in little piece, hour yah, hour dere, up at three, up at day clean starin at de ceilin. Never nottin like a real night rest, just dese little broke-up piece dat leave you more tired dan if you ain sleep at all.

De first month bin de strangest, cause de world just kept right on movin. People back at dey work, back to complainin bout traffic and dey grandbaby and de weather, and I couldn't understand how de world ain stop, how e ain even slow down, when my whole world done stop. Enty dat's a hard thing to swallow, watchin everybody else world keep spinnin when yours done froze.

It was a plain ordinary day, love, nottin marked on no calendar, nottin I bin dreadin ahead of time, and dat day undone me complete. I ain even remember what set e off. I was just doin sumpin small, foldin laundry I believe, and e hit me like a wave off de sound, knock me clean down on de floor cryin like de first day all over again. Dat's de one dey don't warn you bout, de day you ain see comin.

Even month after month, love, a smell could still do e. Somebody wearin de same cologne, or I shum a old shirt foldin up in a drawer, or just de smell of coffee a certain way, and e wave come right up outta nowhere. Ain no pattern to e, ain no way fa brace youself, e just come when e come.

De long middle of dat year, dat's de part ain nobody tell you bout. Dat's a fog, love, a real fog, and e don't lift for de bad day only, e sit right down on top de good day too. Sumpin dat oughta bin happy, a nice mornin, good weather, somebody laughin, and de fog still dere dullin e all down like lookin at e through dirty glass.

I got to tell you bout one thing somebody say to me dat cut deep, deeper dan e had any right to. Somebody da sweet-mout me at first, real kind soundin, den turn round and say I oughta be "movin on" by now, like grief got a due date. I ain say nottin back, just cut-eye and walk off, but dat comment stay wid me longer dan e should. And right next to dat, I gotta tell you bout de kindness ain nobody expectin. Wasn't nobody big, wasn't nobody plan e out. Somebody just show up one evenin wid a plate a food, ain say much, just set e down and set wid me a spell and den gone home. Dat lil thing help me more dan every arrangement dem official folk make put together.

Den de anniversary come round. Weeks before e, I bin dreadin dat day like nottin else, my stomach in knot just thinkin on e comin. And when de day finally come, I ain do nottin fancy, I just got through e however I could, clumsy and quiet, and dat bin enough. Dat's de whole point, love, you ain got to do e pretty, you just got to get to de other side of e.

My appetite and my sleep, dey come back slow, slow and uneven, not all at one time. Some week I'd eat good and sleep whole, den sumpin knock me back down again for a spell. Ain no straight line to e, e come in fit and start same as everything else in dis grief.

And den one day I catch myself laughin, real laughin, from somewhere deep, and right behind e come de guilt, uninvited, like e ain have no business followin joy round like dat. But e do. Dat guilt come right on de heel of de laugh, every time, for a good while.

By de time dat year bin done, I was carryin e different. Lighter in some place, I could set down and breathe widout dat weight crushin my chest constant. But e ain gone, love. E never gone. E just get to where you can carry e and still walk.

So dat's how e bin for me, and I'm tellin you cause I want you fa know e don't stay dis hard forever. But ain no clock on e, ain no set time when e ease up, and you hear me now, oona ain got to measure youself against nobody else year, not mine, not nobody's. You go through e in your own time, however dat time look.
