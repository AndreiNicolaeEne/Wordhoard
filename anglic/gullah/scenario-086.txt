Me: Friend, I got to tell you how dat night gone, cause you ain been dey and I done owe you de whole tale by now.

You know how my tittuh stay, when e mek up e mind to celebrate, e ain leave nobody out. E gran-baby been christen, and de whole yard done full up wid people fo de sun even down good. Rice and gravy, cootuh stew on de fire, fish fry, benne cake pile up on de table high as my hand can reach. Even de buckra man next door been holler over de fence say we was making too much noise, and we just laugh and wave and tell um come git a plate if e want, and my tittuh stand right dey by de pot dishing up, saying, "Oona ain done yet, enty? Come git one mo helping, dis food ain gwine eat itself." And I say awright, one mo, den one mo again after dat, cause how you gwine tell she no when e look at you so sweet-mout and e hand already reaching fo you plate.

Den de wine start pass round, and I ain mean to drink so much, but every time I put de cup down somebody done fill um back up. We been laughing, talking bout old time, and somebody bring out de radio and de yard turn to a whole dance. I member dat part clear, de music, de auntie clapping, everybody foot in de dirt kicking up dust. I told myself I gwine leave soon, I really did, but "soon" ain never come.

Last thing I member clear good is standing by de fire wid a cup in my hand, laughing so hard my side hurt, and somebody say something bout de preacher wife dat set de whole yard off. After dat e all come back to me in piece, like a net wid holes in um, a flash of we dancing by de road, a flash of somebody pulling me up when I done fall out de chair, a flash of de music still going when de moon been high up. Where de night end and how I got myself to bed, dat I cyan tell you, cause I don't yeddy nothing after dat.

Den day clean come, and it come mean. My head been pounding like somebody beating a drum right inside um, my belly turning over just thinking bout food, and de light through dat one little window been like de sun itself set up right in de room wid me. Every sound too loud, a rooster crow like a gun going off, a pot clang in de kitchen like it drop straight from de sky. Just to sit myself up in de bed take everything I got.

My tittuh already up, moving round de house normal like nothing happen, and soon as e shum, e couldn't help e self.

The tittuh: Enty I tell oona? Look how oona stay dis morning. Oona was dancing like a chicken wid e head cut off last night, and now oona cyan even hold oona own head up.

Me: Hush, tittuh, my head ain fit fa oona mouth dis morning.

The tittuh: I ain say nothing bad, I just say what I shum. Oona want some water, or oona want fa die right dey in dat bed?

E ain mean no harm by um, e just cyan help e self, and I ain had de strength fa argue back proper noway. I lay right dey still and I mek myself a promise, flat on my back wid de room still moving slow round me: I ain never gwine let a night git away from me so bad again. I say it out loud too, low, like God gwine hold me to um. And even while I saying um I know good and well dis ain de first time dat promise done cross my lips, and it prob'ly ain gwine be de last neither.

Small ting dat don't take nothing normal, just fa walk out to de pump and back, just fa sweep off de step, dat whole morning dem ting feel like more dan I got in me. I try fa walk out to de yard round mid-morning and I had fa stop and hold on de post like I been a hundred year old. De simplest chore in de world and my body say no, not today.

By and by I manage a little grits, plain, no butter, no nothing rich near um, just enough fa settle my stomach down off de edge. And I stay quiet, real quiet, sit on de step in de shade and let de worst of um pass over me slow.

And all dat morning, piece of de night keep coming back to me in flash, and one piece I keep turning over and over till I near wear a hole in um: I done say something to de deacon wife bout her singing dat I ain got no business saying, loud enough half de yard shum me say it. I cyan even member de exact word now, just de feel of um, and de way e face done change when I say it. I owe dat woman a real apology, and I know it, and I been putting um off cause shame is a heavy thing fa carry up to somebody door.

By evening, though, I done come back to myself. De light ain hurt no more, my stomach done settle, and I sit down and eat a real plate, rice and all, and think straight fa de first time all day. My tittuh give me one mo cut-eye when e pass by, just fa remind me e ain forgot, but e ain say nothing mo bout um. E ain need to.

So dat's de whole of um, friend, far as I can piece um together. I still got fa go by de deacon wife house and set it right, and I dread um, but I gwine do um. And underneath all dat, I got dis heavy little feeling setting on me, cause I know myself, and good as I mean dat promise I made flat on my back dat morning, some day, some celebration down de road, oona gwine hear dis same story all over again.

My friend: Enty I done tell you dat before.

Me: You done tell me, and I done know it, and I gwine do um again anyhow. Dat's just how e stay.
