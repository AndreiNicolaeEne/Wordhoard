Dis de year everybody still da talk bout. From day clean in de spring we bin know somet'ing different bin comin, de way de ground bin lay, de way de rain bin fall jus when e fa come, not too much, not too less, and de whole season stay mild, no hard freeze fa cut de young t'ing down early.

By de time we get to reap, oona could see am fa oona self, de whole yield done pass wa we used to git, near bout twice de usual amount. But e de benne wa really stand out above all de rest. Dat benne come in so heavy de row bin bendin under e own self, count way past wa e usually give, more dan wa dem git back in dat big year way back, de one de old folks used to measure by till now.

We bin haffa call in extra hand, cause benne fa turn quick once e ripe, and nobody wan lose um to de weather waitin. Cousin, in-law, whoever bin free fa come, everybody bin out dey in de field fa git um in fore e spoil. After we done store wa de fambly need fa see us clean through de year, we still bin have a heap left over, so some we put by fa nex season seed, and some we trade off to buckra down de road wa always want um.

The eldest: E de best I done see since I bin a chile, and I done see plenty year come and go. Enty rain come right on time dis one, and de season stay mild de whole way through, dat wa mek de difference, ain't nothin more to um.

Me: Oona ever shum a crop like dat before?

The eldest: Never. E pass everyt'ing.

Ain't nobody need fa sweet-mout dat harvest, de row done speak fa e own self. And now every year somebody bring in a good crop, somebody gon cut-eye and say, "Enty e good, but e ain't dat year good," and everybody yeddy dat and know zackly which year dey mean.
