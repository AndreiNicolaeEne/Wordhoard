I call oona all together dis evenin cause e time. I done set the table but ain nobody eat yet, and oona know when I do dat, something comin. Oona all settle down on the porch, the eldest in e chair by the rail, the middle one and e wife on the step, the daughter with e arms fold up like e already brace fa bad news. Nobody da talk. Oona just listen, waitin, cut-eye passin round the porch quiet like that, nobody sayin nothing yet but everybody know.

Me: Oona hush now, cause I got something I bin keepin back long time, and tonight e time fa let e go.

The daughter: Mama, what kind of talk dis.

Me: Le me finish fo oona start. Oona remember the year the boat gone. Oona remember how e was found broke up on the rock past the point, the net tore up, and the whole season lost right fo benne and oyster time. That was a hard year, oona know e was. We nearly lost the land dat year, the money so tight, and everybody had to answer for e somehow.

The eldest just look down at e hand. The middle one ain say nothing, but I see e jaw set hard already, like e know weh dis goin.

Me: Oona remember how the anger was. How everybody say e was the middle one fault, cause e was the one down by the dock mendin net dat evenin, and e was the one always tie up the boat. Oona call em careless. Oona ain never quite forgive em fa e, not all the way, not one of oona, and e bin carryin that ever since, all these year, that name foller em everywhere e go.

The middle one's wife look up sharp at that, and I see the old grievance rise up in e face already, the one she carry so long about how nobody in dis family never believe her husband, never once.

Me: But e wasn't em. I bin know the whole time, and I ain never say. It was the eldest.

The room gone still like the air befo a storm. Ain nobody breathe.

Me: I ain guessin. I shum myself. I was a girl, up past when I should a bin sleep, and I shum the eldest come back from the mainland dat night, walkin unsteady, smell of drink on em, and e leave the boat right where e drift, ain even check the line, and the tide was fallin. I shum em go on in the house and lay down like nothing happen. And come mornin the boat was gone to pieces on the rock, and everybody just start sayin e was the middle one, cause e was the last one people seen near em befo dark, mendin net like e always do. Nobody ask the eldest nothing. Nobody even think to.

The eldest: (low, not lookin up) Mama...

Me: Le me finish. I kept e back all dis time. I was scared if I tell, the anger dat had finally gone quiet in dis family would rise right back up wors than befo, and I ain want to be the one who bring more damage down on we head. I was tryin to protect the eldest too, I ain go lie bout dat. And I was scared, if I open my mout, e would break something in dis family that ain never go heal back right.

I see the daughter starin at me now, e face full of disbelief, like e can't fit dis new shape into what e already know.

The daughter: Mama, you sure. You sure bout dis.

Me: I sure. I shum with my own eye. I ain sayin e to stir nothing up, I sayin e cause e true, and I sure.

Me: I almost tell oona one time befo, years back, at the funeral when we was all together and everybody was talkin bout the boat again, sayin how the middle one still ain never live e down. I had e right on my tongue dat day, I was fa say e right there, and then I look at the eldest face and I pull back. I couldn't do e. Not dat day.

The middle one's wife: (voice shakin, half angry, half like e want to cry) All dese year I bin tellin oona, all dese year I bin sayin oona wrong bout em, and oona never listen to me, not once...

Me: I know, tittuh. I know oona bin right the whole time, and I let oona carry that fight alone, and dat was wrong of me too.

Me: E take a toll on me, keepin dis. I watch the middle one live under a name that ain never belong to em, watch em get cut-eye at church, watch people whisper when e pass, and I know the whole time e wasn't so, and I ain say nothing. I carry dat like a stone in my chest all dese year.

The middle one ain say much at first, e just sit there with e head down, and when e finally look up e face was a mix I ain never seen on em befo, some relief in e, like a weight comin off, but a fresh anger risin right behind e too.

The middle one: All dese year, Mama. All dese year I bin the one everybody point at. I lost friend over dat. I had people ain trust me with a boat since. And you knew.

Me: I knew.

The middle one: And you let em keep on believin e.

Me: I did. And I ain got no excuse fa e that make e right.

The eldest ain deny nothing. E just sit there quiet a long minute, and when e finally talk, e voice come out low and rough.

The eldest: E true. Every word of e. I took the boat out dat night to see a man bout some money I owe, and I stay too long, and I drink too much, and I ain check the line when I come back cause I could hardly stand up straight. I let the tide take em. And when everybody start blamin oona instead of me, I ain say nothing to stop em. I let em believe e. I bin lettin em believe e all dese year.

The daughter's face gone hard now, the old anger from back den flarin up fresh in the room, but dis time e got somewhere to land.

The daughter: All dis time we bin so hard on oona (turnin to the middle one) and it was you (turnin to the eldest) the whole time.

The eldest: Yes.

Nobody say nothing fa a while after dat. The middle one's wife reach over and take her husband hand, holdin e tight, e own anger still full in her face but pointed the right way now, finally.

Me: I owe oona an apology, all of oona, but the middle one most of all. I ain got no good reason fa keepin dis back so long, only fear, and I bin wrong to let fear run e that long. I owe oona dese year back and I can't give em back to oona, I know dat. But I ain go carry dis silent no more.

The middle one wipe e face with the back of e hand and don't say nothing fa a long time, just look out at the water gone dark past the marsh.

The middle one: I don't know weh dis leave we now, Mama.

Me: E don't leave we tidy. E don't leave we fix. But e leave we standin on the truth instead of walkin round e like we bin doin all dese year, and dat count fa something, enty.

Nobody answer dat right away. The crickets start up loud in the dark past the porch, and the wind come soft off the water, and we all just sit there together, the whole family, quiet now in a different way than befo, not the waitin kind of quiet no more, but the kind that come after something heavy finally get put down.
