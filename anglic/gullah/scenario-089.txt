My friend,

Oona know I bin talk bout dis for a long time, so leh me finally tell you how e really happen, dat whole season I gone learn fa swim.

I mus be de onliest grown woman on dis island who ain't know how fa swim. Everybody else round yah done learn when dem was pickney, splashin in de creek before dem could even walk good. Me, I bin scared of de water my whole life, and I keep put it off, put it off, till one day I just say enough, I gwine learn dis thing before I dead and never know how.

So I gone down to de rec center where dem hold de lesson, early, at day clean, before de heat come up and before I lose my nerve. De teacher dere, e patient but e ain't sugarcoat nothing. First day, e watch me try fa float and e just shake e head.

The teacher: E gon take you longer than de rest, at your age. Your body ain't as quick fa learn now. But e can still learn, ent nothing wrong wid dat.

I tell you, dat kind of talk sting, even doe I know e ain't wrong. And den fa see de little children dem, seven, eight year old, already da kick cross de pool while I still cain't even put my face in de water good, dat sting worse. Dem pickney pass me by in three day what took me three week fa do. I ain't gwine lie to you, I bin shame.

Dat whole first week my body hurt in place I done forgot I even had. Muscle in my back, in my shoulder, in de back of my leg, all of dem wake up and start complain, every time I move dem. I get up in de mornin stiff like a old board, and my tittuh laugh at me walkin cross de kitchen like I bin plow a whole field.

My tittuh: Look like de water done beat you worse than it drown you.

I ain't find dat funny at de time, but I laugh bout it now. E start bring me benne cake after every lesson dem week, say e for sweeten de bitter, and I ain't gwine lie, e help some.

Den come de fall. I don't know how else fa call it, cause dat what it was, even doe I was in de water. I bin try fa push off de wall and do what de teacher done show me, and I lose my balance and go down hard, smack, and de water knock every bit of breath clean out my body. I lay dere in de shallow end a good minute, not able fa get up, sure, sure as day, dat I was done wid de whole foolishness. I tell de teacher right dere, I say I ain't comin back, dis ain't for me, I too old for dis. My tittuh was dere dat day, watchin from de fence, and e ain't sweet-mout me bout it neither, e ain't try fa make it sound better than it was. E just say, come on back one more time, one more, and if you still want fa quit after dat, den quit.

So I gone back one more time.

And friend, I wish I could tell you it turn easy after dat, but e ain't. Progress bin slow, and humiliatin more time than not. One little thing gotten right after a whole month of gettin it wrong. And de whole time, a little crowd was watchin, old men on de dock, black children and buckra children both from round de neighborhood, some of dem cheerin me on, some of dem cut-eye look, some of dem outright laughin. I could yeddy dem talkin bout me low, thinkin I ain't hear, but I yeddy every word.

De onliest thing dat ever help me through was fa stop measurin myself gainst dem children or gainst anybody who done pick de thing up in one afternoon. I start measurin myself gainst myself, gainst how I bin do a month back. Dat me, a month ago, couldn't put e face in de water widout panic. Dis me, right now, can float on e back for ten second. Dat kind of measurin, dat de only kind dat ever kept me comin back to de water.

Round de fourth month, somethin click. I cain't tell you in word exactly what it was, e wasn't nothing I could reason out, e was somethin in de body, like de body finally understand what I bin tryin fa tell it all dat time. I was in de water one day clean mornin, and I just move, and e work. De thing dat bin impossible de whole time before dat, e just turn difficult, ordinary-difficult, de kind of difficult you can live wid.

I never did catch up to dem fast children, and by de end I make my peace wid dat, enty. I proud instead of what I can do now dat I couldn't do at all when I start: I can swim cross de little creek behind de house, I can float long as I want widout fear, I done even shum a cootuh swim right past me one mornin and I ain't panic, I just watch e go by, cool as anything.

Lookin back on de whole humiliatin season now, e sit sweet in my memory, sweeter than I ever expect it to. De fall, de soreness, de bein laugh at, all of dat done fold up into somethin I glad I do. My tittuh still tease me bout it sometime, still ask me, you member how you bin lay dere in dat shallow water lookin like a turn-over cootuh, enty? And I laugh wid e now, cause e true, and cause I here now, on de other side of it, knowin how fa swim same as anybody oona know.

So dat de whole account, friend, of dat whole season, start to finish. Late as I bin fa start it, I glad I ain't wait no longer than I already did.
