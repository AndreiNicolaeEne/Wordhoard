The pot ain't even on the fire yet and I already got my phone out taking note, cause every time I ask this woman for she red rice recipe she tell me she going to write it down one day and one day ain't never come.

Me: Auntie, for true now, give me the recipe. I want it wrote down before I forget how you say it.

The aunt: E ain't never been wrote down, chile. E grandmother never write nothing, e just bin teach me with e hand and e eye. But awright, I tell you. You yeddy me good now.

Me: I'm listening.

The aunt: First ting you do, you tek you salt meat, bout a good handful, chop um small. No, wait, hold on, that ain't first, I tell it backward again. Onion go in the grease first, then the meat go in after, let um brown up together.

Me: Onion, then the meat. Awright.

The aunt: And oona can't forget the green pepper, I nearly leave that whole part out on you just now and e grandmother woulda tan my hide, e never make red rice without the green pepper in it, say the dish come out too flat else. So e onion, e pepper, e meat, all together till soft.

Me: Then what.

The aunt: Then you tomato, the paste and the can both, little water, bring um up good. And this the part e grandmother always warn me bout, don't you rush that gravy, let um cook down slow, cause if you rush um the rice ain't going to take the flavor right. Then, and only then, the rice go in on top last, and you leave um.

Me: How you know when e done, you ain't using no timer.

The aunt: Naw, no clock for that. You listen, when the pot stop da talk to you, when the steam quiet down off the top, that when e getting close. You lif the lid and shum, the rice done drink up all the water, e open.

Me: And the salt, how much you put in?

The aunt: That the one part I ain't never measure, tittuh, not one day in my life. My eye tell me, my nose tell me, that's all.

Me: Enty you could use turkey neck 'stead of the salt meat?

The aunt: Yeah, that go too, e awright, just don't come out with the same sweet-mout to it, but yeah, you could do that.
