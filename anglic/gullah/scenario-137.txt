Day clean an de cootuh trap done pull up empty, de float line cut clean through, an de big net side a it tore besides. De boss come down to de dock fus thing an e see it fore anybody could tell em one word.

The boss: Enty you de one bin down yah last night fa watch dis? Look wa done to me trap, look wa done to me net. Oona gwine tell me you ain shum wa happen?

The one blamed: I ain do dat.

The boss: You yeddy wa I say? Ain nobody else down yah but you, tittuh. Line cut, net tear, an you da stan dey like you ain know nottin bout it.

The one blamed: I ain do dat.

De boss voice climb higher, e finger comin up close to de one blamed face.

The boss: You had de watch. You had de key to de shed. Ain nobody fa blame but you, enty?

The one blamed: Fore God, I had no part in it.

E ain say it again. E jaw set, e eye steady on de boss, no cut-eye back, no sweet-mout to soften nottin neither, jus dat one word standin plain where e leave it.

De two boy who bin out late de night fore, dey lean up gainst de shed post, one still chewin e benne cake slow, e eye down on e own foot de whole time. Dey ain say nottin. Dey ain look up. De buckra da come round bout dat cootuh money come Friday, an somebody gotta answer fa it, but dat wasn dey worry right now.

De boss cut-eye pon de one blamed one last time, wait fa some tear, some beggin. Nottin come. De one blamed turn an walk on down de dock, leave de denial settin right dey in de mornin air, same as e say it.
