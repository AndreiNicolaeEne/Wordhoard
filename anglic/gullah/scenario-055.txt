Le' me tell you bout dat moving day, my friend, cause you bin gone when e happen and I ain gwine spare oona nothing bout how it gone.

We bin spose to start at day clean, before de heat come down pon de yard, but nobody wake up till de sun already high. E ain nobody fault by e self, it jus everybody bin tired from packing all de night befo, boxes stack up to de ceiling and we ain even see we own bed till we bin fall in um. So de plan bin fa be gone by seven, and by de time I open me eye de clock already say half past nine. I holler fa de eldest fa get up, and e groan like e ain hear me at all.

Fus thing gone wrong befo we even outta de door good. De truck key ain nowhere to be find. We bin turn de whole kitchen upside down, look in every pocket, every jar, and come fa find out de littlest one done carry um off fa play truck-truck in de yard de evening befo and drop um down een de grass by de fence. Enty dat de way e always go? You look fa a thing in every place cept where a child done leave um.

While we still down on we knee in de grass, who show up but de workmate from down de road, de one ain nobody call nor ax fa come. E jus walk up wid e own gloves on e hand like e know we gwine need um. E say,

The workmate: I yeddy oona movin today, so I say le' me come see bout oona.

Nobody bin call um, nobody bin tell um de day nor de hour, but e stand dey ready fa lift same as family. Dat kind of thing still happen round here, thank God, cause dat day we bin need every hand we could get.

Now here go de part dat still hurt me to tell. We bin have dat big mirror, de one bin belong to de grandmother, wid de frame carve all round de edge, older than any of we standing dey. We bin wrap um good, blanket and all, but somehow between de porch and de truck bed e slip. I ain even see how e slip, I jus yeddy de crash and den de shout go up from everybody at once, loud like somebody bin hurt. De mother drop right down on de step and put e hand over e mouth. Wasn't nothing left to do but sweep up de glass and carry de frame on empty, no glass in um a-tall. E hurt bad, cause dat mirror bin see three generation of dis family fixing dey face in um befo church.

And den dat box. Oona know how it go, every move got one box dat just vanish like e never bin dey. Dis one bin have de good pots in um, de heavy iron one and de little one too, and de last time anybody shum it, it bin settin by de back step. By dat evening it gone. We look under de truck, we look in every room, we call de neighbor down de road ax if e see anybody carry off a box, and nothing. Dat box still lost to dis day, and I reckon somebody somewhere cooking rice in we good pot and ain even know how e come by um.

De heaviest piece bin de old wardrobe, solid wood, been in de family since I don't know when, and dat de piece dat start de argument. De eldest want fa carry um standing straight up, say e too tall fa turn on e side. My tittuh say no, e got to go on e side or e never gwine fit through nothing. Dey stand dey in de yard, hot sun beating down, one pulling one way and one pulling de other, till de mother come out and say,

The mother: Oona gwine break oona back arguin longer dan e take fa jus turn de thing.

So we turn um on e side, and wouldn't oona know it, de tittuh bin right all along, though e ain say I-told-oona, jus give a little cut-eye and keep on working.

But turnin um on e side ain solve de whole problem, cause den we come to dat doorway. Lawd, dat doorway. We push, we pull, we take de door off e hinge, we try um high, we try um low, we try um every way a wardrobe can go through a door and e ain gwine budge. Somebody say tek de legs off, somebody say tek de back off, and by de time we get done argue bout it we done lose near an hour standing dey sweating in dat narrow hall, de wardrobe stuck like a cootuh dat done pull e head in and ain studying bout coming out fa nobody.

And right bout when we finally work um loose, de weather turn on we too. De sky bin clear when we start, but round midday de cloud roll in dark off de water and de wind pick up sudden de way e do out here, and befo we know it de rain come down hard, straight through we open truck, straight through de boxes we ain get cover yet. We bin running back and forth wid tarp and sheet, anything we could grab, hollering fa each other fa hurry, and still some of we things come out de other side of dat storm wet through.

By den we bin so hungry we ain even studying bout de rain no more. Somebody bin bring a pot of rice and some fish already fry, and a little tin of benne wafer besides, and we ain even bother fa find a table, we jus stand right dey mongst de boxes and de wet furniture and eat off we knee, plate balance on top a crate. Dat little meal save de whole afternoon, I tell oona true. Ain nothing taste better than food you eat standing up tired, when you ain even sure you gwine make it to de end of de day.

De last few load, dat when de tired really hit. Not de kind of tired sleep gwine fix right away, but de kind where you arm shaking jus fa lift a lamp, where you legs feel like water going up dem step one more time. Nobody bin talking much no more by den, we jus da work, quiet, cause talking bin cost too much energy we ain have left fa spare.

Befo we lock de door fa de last time, I walk through de old place by e self, room to room, and every room bin empty and e sound different empty, e echo. I stand in de front room where we bin sit so many evening and I could still see where de furniture bin mark de floor, lighter square where de sun ain never touch. I ain cry, but e come close. Dat house bin see a whole heap of we life, and now e jus gwine stand dey empty fa somebody else.

We ain reach de new place till good after dark, headlight de only light we bin have fa see we way in wid de last of de boxes. And I tell oona, we bin come with less than we bin start de day thinking we'd still have, de mirror gone, de good pot box gone, half we things wet through from de storm, but we bin still standing, still together, and dat workmate still dey helping right up to de end, wid ain nobody even have to ax um twice.
