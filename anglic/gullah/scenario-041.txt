Tittuh, set down, cause I got to tell you de whole of it, start to finish, cause I ain never tell nobody de real of it, only de part I let people yeddy.

Two year I bin at dat place. Two whole year, from de summer de heat first come in bad till dis summer just gone, cleaning and packing and standing on my foot from day clean till de light done leave de sky. I ain lef fast. I bin sit on it long, longer than I should, telling myself e go get better, telling myself I too far in now fa quit. But de last straw, tittuh, e wasn even big. E was just a little thing, small like a shrimp head you flick off and don't even think on. De manager tell me fa come in on my day off cause somebody ain show, and when I say I already promise my mama I take her to de doctor, e just look at me, ain say nothing, just cut-eye long and hard and turn e back and walk off like I ain say nothing at all. Dat right there. Dat de one dat done it. Cause dat cut-eye wasn about dat one day. Dat cut-eye bin building two year.

So I start fa rehearse what I gon say. Say it in de car, say it in de mirror, say it low to myself while I sweeping. I want it to sound polish, tittuh, I want it to sound like somebody who done thought e through, not somebody who vex. So I practice till de word come smooth, till dey ain even sound like something I feel, dey just sound like something I decide.

I go in and I ask fa speak wid de one in charge. E in de back office, and I stand in de door and I say de line I done practice so much e ain even shake when e come out my mout.

Me: I just want to say, it ain about no one thing in particular. I just feel like it time for me to move on to something else.

And dat was true and it wasn't. Cause it was about one thing, tittuh, it was about a whole heap of one things stack up on top each other till dey make one big thing, but I ain say dat part. I hold dat back cause I ain want no argument, I ain want to stand there and watch e face while I list off every little cut and every little word dat done cut me all dese two year. I done seen how dat go before, how you say de true thing and den you got to defend de true thing, and I ain have de strength left fa dat fight. So I give de smooth line instead and I let e be enough.

De one in charge look surprise, or e make like e surprise, hard to tell wid buckra sometime, dey face don't always say what dey mean neither. E lean back and e say,

The one in charge: Well, we sho hate to lose you. You been here longer than near about anybody. What if we bump your pay a little, and you don't have to do no more weekend closing?

And part of me, tittuh, I ain gon lie, part of me want to take it. Two year, dat mean something, and money is money. But I already know in my heart dat more money ain gon fix a place where de manager cut-eye you for asking a fair thing. Money don't buy back two year of feeling small. So I turn it down, and I give de polite reason again.

Me: I appreciate that, I really do, but I think it's just time for a change for me.

Ain say: I done sick of how oona talk to we like we don't matter. Ain say: e enty right, how oona da come find fault wid we over every little thing and never once say thank you. I just smile and say time for a change, like I picking out a new dress and not walking away from two year of my life.

We settle on two week notice, same as everybody do, and I shake e hand and I walk out dat office feeling like I done something. But dat night, tittuh, I lay down and I couldn't sleep for doubt. I keep asking myself, enty you done throw away something good? Enty you shoulda took de raise, took de easier schedule, stay wid what you know steady? Rent don't care how you feel about cut-eye. I lay there till near day clean, turning it over and over, and I ain had no answer, just de dark and de doubt sitting on my chest like a cootuh sitting on a log, not moving, not going nowhere.

But I ain call back and take it back. I let de two week run.

Last day come and it was quiet, mostly. Everybody tell me bye in dat way people do when dey mean it a little and dey mean nothing a lot, dey say we gon miss you, dey say keep in touch, and I know good as I know my own name most of dem word gon be forgot by de time I reach de door. Hollow, tittuh. Sweet-mout and hollow, like sugar on top and nothing under it.

Cept one. My workmate, de one who bin stand next to me packing since near about de first week I come, e walk me out to de car, and e stand there a minute not saying nothing, just looking off toward de water. Den e say,

My workmate: You know e ain treat you right. I see it. I see it for a long time and I ain never say nothing cause what I gon say. But you was right to go.

And I almost broke right there, tittuh. I almost say de whole thing, how tired I bin, how small e make me feel, how I used to cry in my car before I even walk in de door some morning. But I just nod and I say,

Me: I know. Thank you for that.

And e nod back, and dat was it. Dat de closest anybody come to saying de true thing out loud, and even den we ain say it all de way, we just let it sit there between we, both of we knowing.

Since den, tittuh, it been more relief than regret, I tell you dat straight. De first week I still keep waking up early like I got to go in, body ain know yet e free. But now, oona ax me, I feel like something done lift off my back dat I ain even know was heavy till it gone. I sleep better. I ain flinch when my phone ring no more. Some day I do miss de money, and some day I do wonder how e doing back there, whether dey done hire somebody new to cut-eye. But I ain sorry.

If I had dat conversation to have again, I tell you what I do different. I don't think I change de word I say to e face, cause dat still de smart way to leave a place, smooth and no burning bridge behind you. But I woulda said more to myself, sooner. I woulda trusted dat first cut-eye when e happen de first time, stead of letting two year of dem pile up till I nearly bury under it. Dat de real verdict, tittuh. Not dat I was wrong to go easy on my way out. Just dat I was too patient on my way in, and patience like dat can cost you more than any raise gon pay back.
