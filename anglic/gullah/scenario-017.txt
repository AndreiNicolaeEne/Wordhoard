The aunt: Chile, I yeddy something bout dat young man wuk down to de dock. Dey say e da work steady since day clean to dark, and I shum wid me own eye last week, e da haul net till e back near broke, ain't stop for nothing.

The niece: Wuk hard don't mean nothing by itself, Auntie.

The aunt: Hush now, leh me finish wuh I got to say. First ting, e got da good steady job, ain't like some fool wuk all e life for buckra and got nothing to show for it come pay day. Second ting, e temper good, e ain't da fuss and fight and carry on like some. Tird ting, e family good people, e mama make benne cake for every fundraiser at de church, e daddy work de land, dey ain't never bin in no trouble round here.

The niece: I yeddy you, Auntie, I yeddy you good.

The aunt: Well den. Wuh wrong wid em? Wuh dis young man ain't got dat you need?

The niece: Ain't nothing wrong wid em, Auntie, nothing tall. I just ain't ready fa tink bout dat kind a ting right now, wid all I got on me hand.

The aunt: Enty dat de same ting yuh tittuh say bout de one I bin tell her bout, years back? She cut-eye at me too, sweet-mout me and say she busy, she ain't ready. Look how e turn out, married good, chillun grown and doing well, and she glad now, e tell me sef, glad she ain't win dat argument wid me.

The niece: Tittuh case ain't my case, Auntie. I respect wuh you say and I ain't fixin to disrespect none of it, but I got to know me own mind bout dis one.

The aunt: Oona young people too fast to say no fore oona even try. But I ain't gwine push you no more today. De door still open, ya yeddy me? Whenever you ready, dat offer still da stand right where I left it.
