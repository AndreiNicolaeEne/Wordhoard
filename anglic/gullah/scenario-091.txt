Me: Tittuh, mek I set down and tell you de whole ob it, since you couldn't come and dis all you gwine ever get, so lissen good, cause I ain't tellin it twice.

We yeddy bout we grandmother 'fore day clean bin even good and broke. Somebody phone somebody 'fore de sun stand up good, and by de time I self done yeddy, e feel like de whole island and haffa de mainland too already know. Phone ain't stop ringin de whole day. Two day we bin scramblin like ant when you kick de hill, cause funeral home say dey could only do de one day, and we had was fa git ready fast fa true. Folk cookin all night, folk callin folk, somebody drivin to town fa git de dress right, somebody else out lookin fa where de good black cloth still lef on de shelf. Ain't nobody sleep proper dem two night, I tell you dat.

De one dat live way off, e travel de whole day fa git yah, drivin since 'fore light, and e pull up in de yard wid scarce time fa change e clothes 'fore de house done full up. E say e ain't stop but fa gas de whole way, and I believe um, cause e face bin lookin like e ain't shut e eye since Sunday. But e mek it, and dat's what matter.

By day clean de mornin ob de funeral, de house done full already, tittuh, full like I ain't never see um full. Food da come in faster dan anybody call fa um. Rice bin dey, and fish, and dat big pot ob perloo somebody bring wid no name pin to um, and benne cake and benne candy stack up on top one anodder till nobody could find room fa put down one more plate. Plate on de table, plate on de counter, plate on de arm ob de chair, plate anywhere a plate could balance. Nobody eat much dat mornin, truth be told, but de food bin dey anyhow, cause dat's how we do.

Everybody wear plain dark clothes, nothin fancy, ain't need to say more bout dat, oona know how we do.

'Fore we walk out wid um, everybody gather in de one room, and I ain't never in my life yeddy a room dat full stand so quiet. You coulda yeddy de clock on de wall. Grown folk dat talk loud every day ob dey life ain't say one word. Even de little one dem hush up widout nobody hafta tell um hush.

We four carry um out togedder, slow, cause de room bin narrow and we ain't want to rush um, not dat day.

Partway through, one ob de aunt dem broke down sudden, ain't no warnin to um at all, e just gone, cryin out loud from somewhere deep, and nobody move fa stop um. Nobody say hush now, nobody rush to pull um back together. We just let um cry, cause enty dat's what de body want to do sometime, and who is we fa tell um no.

De preacher speak after dat, and e ain't come sweet-mout we wid no long word dat day.

The preacher: "E done rest now. Oona de one still got de work to do."

Dat one line, tittuh, I done yeddy it repeat from mos every mouth in dis family since. My own mama say it to me just yesterday, like e her own word now.

Den, right in de middle ob all dat heavy, somebody remember somethin bout we grandmother and a cootuh dat used to get in e garden every summer, how she used to chase dat cootuh wid a broom hollerin like e done stole somethin, and how e never once catch um, not one time in twenty year.

Someone: "E used to tell dat cootuh e gwine put um in de pot, and dat cootuh ain't never fraid ob um one bit."

And de laugh dat come out, tittuh, it come out sudden and loud, took everybody by surprise, even de one who let it out first, e clap e own hand over e own mouth right after like e couldn't believe e self. One ob de old aunt dem gi'e um cut-eye quick, but even she couldn't hold e face straight but a minute 'fore e bin laughin too. It ain't disrespect. It just come, de way it do sometime when de heart get too full fa carry only de one thing.

De walk to de restin place felt longer dan I remember um bein, and I done walk dat road plenty time befo. Sun bin hot, and de line ob us movin slow behind um, and every step felt like it stretch out longer dan de one befo it.

De words say out dey bin plain and short, nothin dressed up 'tall. Preacher say what e had was fa say, ain't drag it out none.

A handful ob dirt let go down, and dat bin dat.

De walk back felt shorter, way shorter, though it de same road we just come.

De meal back at de house after, whoever bin closest to de family jus put um togedder, ain't nobody organize nothin, it just happen, and it bin enough to feed everybody twice over and still had plenty lef fa send home wid people.

One relative nobody bin expectin show up anyway, right when we bin thinkin everybody done arrive already, and I tell you, tittuh, it mean more dan anybody say out loud. Nobody make a fuss over um comin, but you coulda see it in how people hold on to um a little longer dan a regular hug.

By evenin de house done empty out, and de quiet come down all at once, heavier dan all dat noise bin all day. Dat quiet, tittuh, dat's de part I wish you bin here fa feel, more dan any ob de rest ob it. Cause de crowd and de food and de walkin, dat part you can hear about and get de shape ob it fine. But dat quiet dat drop down after everybody gone home, wid de plates all empty and de chair all still warm from folk just settin dey, dat ain't somethin I can hand you over de phone. I wish you bin dey fa dat, and fa see we grandmother face one last time 'fore dey close um up, cause e bin lookin so peaceful, tittuh, like e just bin sleep good fa de first time in a long while.
