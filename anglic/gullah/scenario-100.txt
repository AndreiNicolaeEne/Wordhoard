Le' me tell you bout de year dat nearly bury me, cause I done live through it and I still yah fa tell it, so you know I ain lyin bout none ob dis.

It ain start wid nutton big. Jes one Monday de boat man tell we e ain need so much crew no mo, dem contract done dry up, and dat wuz dat. I bin work steady on dat water fa year an year, since I wuz young, and den one Monday e done. No warnin, no cushion, jes done. I member standin on de dock dat mornin wid my hand still smellin of bait an thinkin, well, sump'n gwine turn up by Friday. Friday come. Nutton turn up.

Fus thing to go wuz de small comfort dem. De little sweet t'ing you don't even think bout till e gone, no mo buyin benne candy off de lady by de road, no mo stoppin fa fish plate on a Friday when you tired an don't wanna cook. I stop dat quick, first week. Tell myself, dat easy, I don't need dat. An I ain, not really. But den de money still ain stretchin, an de bigger t'ing start to go. De truck insurance lapse cause I had to choose tween dat an de light bill. I stop goin to see bout my teet' when dey start to ache, jes live wid de ache. We give up de extra room we wuz rentin out to hep pay, move tighter in de house, everybody doublin up. Dat de kind of givin up dat change how you walk through your own house.

An den come de part I never thought I'd hab to do. I had to ax fa help. Enty dat a hard t'ing. I done work all my life, I done hep other people when dey wuz down, I ain never once in my life had to go to nobody and say I short. But dat month I had to go to my tittuh house.

Me: Tittuh, I hate to come to you like dis, but I short fa de light bill dis month, and maybe next one too.

Tittuh: Wha you talkin bout, hate to come? You my blood. E ain shame to be short, e shame to let your light get cut off and not say nutton.

She gimme wha I need, ain even blink, but I still felt e down in my chest like a stone. Cut-eye from nobody, mind you, she ain never gimme no cut-eye bout it, not once, but I wuz cuttin eye at myself. Dat de kind of pride you ain know you carryin till you hab to set it down.

Close to six month I had almost no work a-tall. E long stretch. Some week a day here, half day dere, cleanin fa buckra up de road, cuttin somebody yard, whatever come. I'd walk out day clean lookin fa sump'n, anyt'ing, an come back home wid near nutton in my pocket an de sun already high.

De day I member as de bottom, dat wuz a Wednesday, hot, and I done gone to three place lookin fa work and every single one tell me no, and I come home an de refrigerator wuz near empty an I had a chile lookin up at me hungry, and I ain had a good answer fa dat chile. I sit down on de step outside and I cry, right dere, cry like I ain cry since I wuz small myself. E ain shame to say dat neither. Dat de day I t'ink, I don't know how we gwine make it.

But den sump'n happen dat I still can't rightly explain, cept to say somebody wuz watchin over we. De deacon from de church, e come by dat same week, ain even know how bad t'ing wuz got, jes come by wid a box.

Deacon: We had extra from de harvest, thought bout you an your house. Y'all take dis, ain no trouble a-tall.

Rice, some greens, a mess of shrimp somebody done cetch, even a lil jar of benne seed fa parch. Small t'ing, I know, but e come right when e need to come, an e kept us from goin under dat week. I yeddy people say dat kind of timin ain no accident, and I believe dat now more dan I ever did before.

After dat, lil by lil, work start to come back. Fus jes a day a week cleanin fa a family down de road. Den somebody call me bout mendin net. Den de boat man himself call, say e could use me back part-time, e sorry bout how t'ing gone before. I ain proud bout it, I take every bit. E wuz small money at fus, real small, but e wuz steady, an steady mean sump'n different when you done been wid nutton.

De whole climb back took bout eight month, slow de whole way, one step forward an sometime a half step back. But e wuz movin, an dat wuz de difference. You could feel de ground come back under your foot lil by lil.

Wha I learn bout who you could count on, e ain always who you t'ink. Some people I bin good to fa year, dey ain call, dey ain come by, dey act like dey ain even know we strugglin. But my tittuh, de deacon, my workmate from de dock who ain hab much more dan me but still bring by a plate sometime, dem de one who show up. Blood show up sometime. Church show up sometime. But e de ones who actually put dey hand out, dem de one you learn to hold onto after.

An wha I learn bout myself. I learn I stronger dan I ever give myself credit fa. I always t'ink if I lost my work I'd fall apart complete. I ain fall apart. I bend way down, further dan I ever bin bent, but I ain broke. I learn I could ax fa help an still be a whole person after. I learn sweet-mout talk from people don't cost nutton and don't feed nobody either, e de action dat count, not de nice word.

E start to feel like sump'n I could survive, really feel like dat, bout de time de work got steady again, maybe seven, eight month in, when I could pay a bill on time again wid'out prayin over it fus. Dat de day I breathe different. Dat de day I know we gwine be aw right.

Now, I know you in your own hard year right now, my friend. I ain blind to it, I see e on you, I yeddy it in how you talkin. I ain gwine tell you e gwine be easy, cause e ain, and I ain gwine tell you e gwine be quick, cause e wa'n't quick fa me neither. But I gwine tell you dis, you ain by yourself in it, not while I'm yah. You need somebody to set wid you at day clean when you can't sleep, I'm coming. You need a plate brought by, I'm bringing it, don't even hab to ax. You need somebody to hold your hand through de hard part and don't say nutton foolish bout how e all gwine be fine, I can do dat too, cause I know e ain dat simple. My time, my hep, my two hand, dat yours fa as long as you need e. You gwine get through, cause I got through, and I ain no different from you, love. We gwine walk dis one together.
