Gal, leh me tell you bout dis card game we had Friday gone, cause you done miss the whole ting and I ain't hardly finish studying on it myself yet.

Four of we bin to the table dat night, me, my tittuh, the cousin from up the road, and the one who always deal cause e hand quick, quick like nobody else own. We set the stakes fore one card even touch the table, small money, quarter here, quarter there, nothing nobody gwine cry over come morning. We ain't studying no buckra bidness in dat game, just plain shrimp-money, someting fa laugh over and pass the night.

Early hand dem go by ordinary, ordinary, same as always. Nobody clear ahead, cards spreading round even-even, my tittuh win one small hand, the cousin win one, I win a little someting myself. We had benne candy on a plate somebody bin bring, sweet-mout the whole table, talking mess, laughing bout nothing in particular, the way oona do when the night still young.

Den someting change. The one dealing start to win. First hand, e win. Second hand, e da win again. Third hand, e still da win, and I say to myself, well, luck da run e way tonight, dat's all it is. But fourth hand running e still da win, and dis time e ain't just winning small, e winning clean, every single time, like the deck itself done pick a side.

The cousin say soft, half to eself, not even looking up good: "Da shuffle look mighty clean tonight, enty?"

Nobody pay it no mind the first time it come out e mouth. We laugh it off, somebody say luck come and luck go, dat's how card stay, ain't no shame in a good run.

But when the same one deal again, I bin watching e hand close dis time, close close, and I shum. I see how the card dem leaving off the bottom of the deck, quick quick, like cootuh bite, you don't even see it move till it done move already. And I ain't could hold my mouth no more, not after dat.

Me: "Oona see dat? E dealing off the bottom. I see e do it wid my own eye."

You could yeddy a pin drop right dey at the table.

The dealer: "I ain't do no such ting. Oona crazy fa say dat to me."

Flat, my friend, flat flat, no give in e voice at all, not one crack in it nowhere.

My tittuh side wid me right off, say e bin watching too and someting ain't set right bout how them card been leaving the deck all night. But the cousin side wid the dealer, say e ain't shum nothing wrong e own self, say we just vex cause we losing and looking fa somebody to blame it on.

So we start going back over the hand dem already play, one by one, arguing every last one. "Well dis hand here, look how e win dat one, e ain't had nothing special." "No, e win dat fair, e had the card fa true, I bin see it." We go back and forth long time like dat and ain't settle nothing, not one ting, the whole argument just chasing eself round the table. The dealer just sit dey wid e arm cross, cut-eye pon the whole table, ain't saying much more than "I done tell oona I ain't cheat, and I ain't saying it again."

Even so, we keep playing. Wasn't nothing fa do but keep on, cause ain't nobody want to be the one to say leh we stop, cause den it look like oona 'fraid, or like oona giving up the fight before it settle. But the night done change already, you hear me? The ease bin gone clean out it. We ain't laughing sweet-mout no more, we watching every hand close now, every card that come off dat deck, cut-eye pon the dealer's hand the whole time, even them of we who bin siding wid em.

We play on through like dat, hand after hand, everybody quiet mostly, just the card dem slapping down and somebody grunting low when e lose, till it near bout day clean and the sky already turning grey by the winder and we still ain't done. The last hand of the night, my tittuh took it, clean hand, fair fair, ain't nobody could say a word against dat one, e just had the card fa true and everybody see it fa dey own self, no argument left in nobody by dat point.

When we tally up at the end, we bin play eleven hand altogether dat whole night, start to finish. The dealer, the one we bin fuss over so hard, e come out ahead of everybody else by two hand. Not no landslide, just two, but two enough to keep the talk going long after the cards bin put away.

And dat's how it stay unsettled, girl, to dis day. Every time dat same four of we sit down to play card again, someway, somehow, somebody gwine bring up dat night before the first hand even deal. The cousin still say ain't nothing to it, just bad luck fa the rest of we and nothing more. My tittuh still say e shum what e shum wid e own eye, and e ain't changing e story fa nobody living. And the dealer just cut-eye and say the same ting e say dat night, word fa word: "I ain't do no such ting." Flat as ever, still no give in it.

We done argue it so much by now it ain't even bout the cards no more, it just a ting we do, part of how we sit down together. Somebody deal, somebody watch the deck close, somebody say "enty you remember dat night," and we all laugh bout it, but not all the way through, cause part of we still ain't sure, and part of we still sure dead certain, and ain't neither side gwine bend.

Dat's the whole of it, far as I could tell it to you. You had was to be dey yourself, my friend, cause I still don't know rightly what I believe bout dat night, even now.
