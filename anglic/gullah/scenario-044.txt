Day clean and de tide already pulling low, me and de new hand out on de dock, cast net folded over my shoulder heavy with de wet of it.

Me: Watch how I hold e. Line round de wrist first, den oona gather de lead line in dis hand, not grip em like you fixin to fight em, just let em lay.

I show it slow, throw it out, e open up round like a full moon and slap down flat on de water. Pull em back, shrimp jumping in de mesh already.

Me: Now oona try.

The new hand: Like this?

E grab de net wrong, ball em up in both hand, and when e throw, e just plop down in a heap, don't open at all.

Me: No, no. You da hold too much rope in de wrong hand. De lead line go in dis one, yeddy me?

The new hand: I thought the lead line was the one closest to me.

Me: Enty I just show you? Watch e again.

I throw it a second time, terser now, showing de wrist snap and not a word wasted on it.

Me: Wrist. Snap e wrist. Don't heave em like a rock.

E try again and de net ball up de same fool way, half in de water, half on de new hand shoe.

Me: Lord a mercy. How many time I got to show you dis? You ain't even watchin my hand, you lookin at de water!

The new hand: I am watching, I just...

Me: Just what? Just not doin what I show you, dat what.

The new hand: I'm sorry. I'm trying.

Me: Alright. Alright. Forget de rope for a minute, dis one hard fa learn if oona thinkin too much bout it. Just hold de net open like a sheet oona spreadin on de bed, and let de wrist do de throwin, not de shoulder.

E try one more time, slow, and de net bell out wide dis time, settling flat and clean on de water.

Me: Dere. Dat him right dere. I bin show you dat three time now and oona finally shum.
