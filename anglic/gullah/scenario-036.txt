Girl, let me tell you bout dis holiday I done host, cause you miss de whole ting and I ain gwine let you go round not knowing.

So it start back when de auntie say she too tired dis year, e say e knee ain wan stand up long by de stove no mo, and somebody haffa take de house. And I say, well, le me do um. I ain even pray on it good fore I say de word. My hand been shaking when I call de mama fa tell she. She say, "Enty you know how much work dat is?" I say I know, I know, but somebody haffa. Soon as I hang up de phone de nerves come down on me like weather.

I sit down dat same night and I write out a menu long like my arm. I done put down rice and okra soup, and de shrimp and grits, and a whole hog head cheese, and dressing wid oyster in um, and greens wid de neckbone, and red rice, and a cootuh stew cause de uncle been asking fa cootuh since summer, and benne wafer fa dessert cause dat always go fast, and a cake on top of all dat. When I read it back to my own self I had to laugh, cause dat ain no menu, dat a whole restaurant. But I ain scratch nothing off. I say I gwine do all of it.

Den de count start to grow on me. First it was just we own house, maybe eight, ten people. Den de tittuh call and say she bringing she husband people too, and de mama say de old deacon ain got nowhere to go dis year so e coming, and de neighbor hear we hosting and ask can e send de granchillun over cause dey mama working. By de time I done finish counting on my finger I done run out of finger and toe both. Near bout twenty-two people gwine sit down to dis table dat I first plan for ten.

De shopping trip like to kill me. I gone to three different store cause de first one ain had enough shrimp fresh, and de second one out de good rice. But de one thing I could not find nowhere in dis world was fresh benne seed. I gone to every store I know and dey all done sell out, look like everybody and dem mama done have de same idea dis year. I stand in dat last store aisle so long de one at de counter come ask me if I awright. I say I fine, I just lookin fa benne and it ain nowhere to be found. E laugh and say oona ain de first one ask um dat dis week.

De night before I ain gone to bed proper at all. I been up to past midnight cracking crab and washing greens and cutting up de dressing ting, and I still had de rice fa parch come morning. My back was talking to me but I ain listen to um. I lay down finally and my mind still da run through de whole list, worrying bout whether de oven big enough fa all what I plan.

I done write out a whole schedule on paper fore I gone to sleep. Six o'clock, put on de rice. Seven, de dressing in de oven. Eight, start de greens. Nine, de shrimp and grits close to done, everybody sit by ten. Look so neat on de paper, like a pretty picture. Enty I know better by now. Come day clean I wake up late cause I ain hear de alarm, and from dat first minute de whole schedule fall apart and never come back together. Everything wanted de oven at de same time, everything wanted my two hand at de same time, and I only got de one me.

Den de first disaster hit. I done put de dressing in and got so caught up wid de greens I forget all bout um til I smell someting burning and run in dey and de top done gone black and hard like a shoe sole. I stand dey wid de pan in my hand nearly cry. I say to myself, well, dat part of de dressing dead, we gwine have to work round um. I scrape off what burn, save what I could underneath, and stretch it out wid a next pan of cornbread I throw together quick, cause a table cyan sit dey wid no dressing on it.

Right bout dat time de doorbell ring and it was de cousin, come a whole hour early, before I even had my hair tied up good. I open de door still holding de burnt pan and e take one look at my face and ain even ask, e just walk straight in de kitchen and start tying on a apron. E say, "Where you want me?" and I could've cry from relief stead of from de dressing. E take over de greens so I could deal wid de stove, and e da season and stir like e own de place, which I ain mind one bit.

By den de stove and de oven both full up past what dey could hold. De rice pot on one eye, de shrimp and grits pot on de next, de red rice trying to squeeze in de oven side by side wid de cornbread, and de cootuh stew simmering on de back eye so slow it look like it ain never gwine finish. Every dish competing fa space and time same as if dey all people trying to get through one door. I done lost count how many time I move a pot from one eye to de next just to make room.

But den one ting come out better than I ever look for. Dat cootuh stew, de one I been most worried bout cause I ain make um in years, come out rich and deep, de meat fall right off and de gravy thick just right. I taste it off de spoon and had to stop and steady myself, cause I ain expect um to turn out so good after all de rush.

We finally sit down to eat near bout a hour and a half past when I first plan, sun already good and high, everybody hungry and fanning demself in de heat, but nobody complaining too hard by den, dey too busy smelling everything.

First bite go round and I watch face close. De uncle taste de cootuh stew and e eye roll back in e head, e ain say nothing, just nod slow like e in church.

Uncle: "Dis right here. Dis how e used to make um."

De old deacon reach fa de dressing, de part I done salvage, and e face do dat little pull, e chew slow and set e fork down easy.

Deacon: "It good, but enty it a little dry? I believe it sat in dat oven a hair too long."

I ain argue wid um, cause e ain wrong, and everybody da laugh a little, and I laugh too cause what else I gwine do.

But dat cootuh stew ain last no time at all. I look up not fifteen minute in and de whole pot done, scrape clean, and people still asking was it more. De tittuh lick e own finger and say straight out de bowl was too small.

Tittuh: "Girl, you better make dat every year now, you hear me. E gone fore I could even get a second helping."

By de time everybody done eat and set back full and sleepy on de porch, somebody bring up whether I gwine host again next year. I say I don't know, ask me again when my back stop hurting. De cousin, still in dat apron I never did get back from um, say oona already know de answer, e see it on my face soon as de cootuh stew come out right. I say maybe, just maybe, but next year somebody else buying de benne seed, cause I ain running round three store for dat again long as I live.
