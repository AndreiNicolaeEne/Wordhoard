Round about early afternoon I been out on the porch shelling peas when I see smoke way off cross the marsh, thin like a string, and I ain study it much. I tell my tittuh, "Look yonder, e smoke rising over by the pine," and e just cut e eye at it and go back to e work. Wasn't no wind fa carry it close den, so we just watch it, watch it, like it ain nothing but somebody burning brush.

But the wind shift. Wasn't no hour gone by fore that smoke thicken up and da roll closer, and my eye start to sting and water like I been cutting onion. I yeddy the fire 'fore I shum good, a kind of crackling far off getting louder and louder.

Tittuh: Enty you smell that? That ain no brush fire, that big fire.
Me: I yeddy um too. We got fa go now.

Wasn't no studying after that. I grab the Bible off the shelf, grab the benne cake my mother bin make that morning, grab a jug of water, and I holler for oona to get in the truck, don't tarry none. Twenty minute we gone, ain take nothing else but what matter.

The heat press up 'gainst my skin like a hot iron close to my face, and my throat gone raw, rough like sand, from all that smoke I done breathe. I look back one time 'fore the road bend, and the whole treeline glow orange like the sun done fall out the sky and land right down in them pine.

Two night we stay with kin off the island, not knowing if the house still there, not knowing bout the chickens nor the cootuh in the pond nor nothing. My mother wouldn't hardly eat, just sit there studying on it, and the youngest kept asking sweet-mout questions bout when we going home, like e trying to make it come true by asking it so much.

When day clean the third morning we drove back slow, quiet, everybody 'fraid to say what we thinking. And soon as I shum the house still standing from the road, ain touch, spared, my mother start to cry right there in the yard. All I could do was thank God and go set down on the step, my legs give out from under me.
