Dis de whole year I bin keep dat garden plot down by de church land, and I gwine tell oona bout um from de start to de end, de good and de bad, cause bofe come.

Come early spring I bin study on wa fa put een de ground. I done walk de rows in my head fore I ever tetch a hoe, tryin decide tween mo butter bean or mo okra, and how much benne I could spare room fa. In de end I settle on okra, butter bean, collard, tomato, a lil corn fa de back row, and benne long de edge cause dat always do good in de sandy dirt round yah. But I ain rush um in de ground. De old people always say wait til de last cold done gone fore you dare put anyting tender een de dirt, and dat cold can fool you, come back round when you least spec um. So I hold off good two, three week after de las frost fore I put in a single seed. Some a de young ones on de other plots dem put dey tomato in early, sweet-mout demself say dey gwine git a head start, and sho nuff a late cold snap bite half a dem tender plant right back down to nutting. I ain say nutting bout um, just kept my seed in de bag and wait.

Once I finally put um in de ground, de Lord bless us wid a good run a weather. Warm day, soft rain bout every third day like clockwork, sun ain too hard yet. Everyting come up strong and green, de bean vine climbin de pole fore I hardly turn round good, de okra shootin up tall, and I member standin dere one mornin, day clean, lookin out cross de rows and tinkin dis gwine be a fine year.

Fine year or no, de trouble ain wait long fa come find me. Bout a month in I go out one mornin and shum right dere in de butter bean row, big ol cootuh settin right in de middle a my vine like e own de place, done chew a whole section a young leaf down to nutting but stem. I run um off dat day but e bin come back, night after night, cause dem cootuh dem move slow but dey persistent, and dey love dat tender young bean leaf mo dan anyting else I got growin. I try put up a lil wire round de bottom a de row, chicken wire I bin buy from de buckra store up de road, dig um a few inch into de ground so e couldn root under. Dat hep some, cut de damage down considerable, but e ain stop um all de way. I still find a nibble here and dere all season, and every now and den I still yeddy sump'n movin slow and heavy through de leaf at night when I come check on tings.

Den come de dry stretch, and dat was de hard part a de whole year, mo hard dan any cootuh. Bout six week in de summer, rain just quit altogether. De ground crack open, leaf start to droop by midday no matter how early I water um. I bin haul water by hand every single day for near bout three week, bucket after bucket from de cistern down to de far rows, back and forth til my shoulder bin sore fa true. Da come out dere fore day clean so I could get some water in de ground fore de heat rise, den go back again in de evenin and do de same ting. E was a lot, but I wan lose wa I bin work so hard fa put in de ground, so I keep at um.

Now dat cistern, e serve two, tree plot on dat side, mine and de plot right next to mine, and dat de water pump we bofe share off. Cause I bin out dere so early and so late tryin to save my own crop, de one next to me start to feel like I bin hoggin de water and not leavin um enough turn. One evenin e come over while I still out dere fillin bucket, and e give me one good cut-eye fore e even say a word.

The neighbor: You out yah every day now, mornin and night bofe. Some a we need dat water too, enty?

Me: I ain mean to crowd oona out. I just tryin to keep my bean and my okra from dyin off in dis heat.

The neighbor: Well my tomato dem dyin off too. E ain right one person get de pump all de time and de rest a we wait.

I ain gwine lie, dat sting a lil, cause I felt like I bin doin wa anybody woulda do wid de ground so dry. But e had a point too, cause fair is fair on shared ground. So we sit down right dere by de cistern and work out a turn, me git de mornin hour befo de heat, e take de evenin hour, and Sunday we bofe skip and let de pump res. Once we had dat settle plain between us, de bad feelin ain last. E even bring me over a mess a e own tomato later dat week, no hard word bout um, just set um down and say e figure we square now. And we was.

Now out a everyting I put in dat ground, de okra was de clear winner, no question bout um. Dat okra just kept comin, pick um one day and by de next e already got mo ready. I bin fill basket after basket off dat lil row, gave away plenty, sold a bit too, and still had mo dan I know wa fa do wid. Ain never had a crop do dat good fa me befo.

But while de okra bin blessin me, de tomato bin givin me fits from another direction. After all dat dry spell den de rain finally come back heavy, and de tomato dem start rottin right on de bottom fore dey even git ripe good, dat soft brown spot spreadin up from de blossom end. E was de uneven water done um in, dry so long den soak all at once, and dey couldn take de shock. I lost a good third a dat tomato crop to um, no matter wa I try.

Right in de middle a all dat come de storm, blow through hard one afternoon, wind flat out layin whole rows down, corn stalk bent clean over, okra plant leanin sideways in de mud, benne knocked flat long de edge. I stood out dere after and just look at um a minute fore I could even start. Den I got to work, stakin de corn back up one by one, packin de dirt round de root a wa I could still save, tyin de okra to whatever I had fa hold um up. Took me most a two day, sore back and all, but I bin git mo a dat back on e feet dan I figure I would.

Come de end a de season I sit down and tally up wa de whole year bin give me. Basket on basket a okra, a good haul a butter bean once de cootuh done wid dey damage, enough benne fa parch and store fa myself, and wa survive a de tomato and de corn on top a dat. I trade a good chunk a my okra and some a de benne to another grower down de road fa a load a firewood, cause winter comin and I need dat mo dan I need to eat okra straight through December. Den I turn de ground under, pull de old vine and stalk, spread wa compost I had, and let de plot res fa de cold months.

Lookin back on de whole a um, dat was a year wid plenty trouble in um, de cootuh and de dry spell and de water spat and de storm all comin one after de other. But e was a good year too, maybe de best I ever had off dat lil plot, and I learn sump'n from every bit a de trouble same as I did from de good. I already studyin on wa I gwine put in come next spring.
