My friend, lemme tell you how the dinner table gone on Sunday, cause you ain gon believe it, and then again you gon believe it total, cause it always go the same way, every single time.

We done come back from church, whole yard full up with car, and the table set out long with everything on it, the rice and the gravy, the okra soup, the deviled crab, the cootuh stew simmer down real good, the shrimp e done fry up that mornin fore day clean even, and the benne wafer waitin on the side table for after. Everybody eatin good, everybody easy, laughin bout this and that, Gran tellin bout somebody from down the road, the uncle crackin on the cousin bout how e always come late to everything. Everybody guard down good by then, the kind of easy where people stop watchin what they sayin, cause the food good and the day good and don't nobody want fa think bout nothin else.

Now in this family it got one thing. One particular thing. Ain nobody ever write it down nowhere, ain nobody ever stand up and say "we ain gon talk bout that," but everybody done learn a long time ago, oona don't bring it up. Not at the table, not on no Sunday, not never if oona can help it a-tall. It just somethin everybody feel in they own body soon as it come round, like a door oona know not to open.

So we eatin good, and the eldest, she ain even thinkin, she just say somethin in passin bout a truck she shum back of the property that mornin, some buckra man drivin round out there slow, lookin at somethin, she don't even know what. Weren't even a real comment, just fillin up a quiet space at the table. But e ain even get the whole sentence out good fore the table gone still. Everybody feel it comin at once. You could yeddy the forks stop, right there, all together.

The uncle, e couldn't let it pass. E start easy though, testin the ground first, like e just askin a plain question.

Uncle: Now who tell oona bout a truck out there?

The auntie ain wait one second.

Auntie: E ain got to be nobody tell nobody nothin, e was right there in the daylight, everybody know what that mean.

Uncle: Oona don't know what oona talkin bout, oona ain never even understood the half of it from the start, and oona still don't.

Auntie: I don't understand? I done live through the whole thing more than oona ever did in oona life, don't oona dare tell me I don't understand.

And from there the voice dem da rise, one right on top the other, the way it always do, till ain nobody really listenin to nobody, just talkin loud so the other one got to yeddy it whether e want to or not.

The tittuh try fa save it. She reach for anything at all.

Tittuh: Oona taste this gravy? I put a little more file in it this time, e come out different, enty?

Ain work. Ain nobody even turn they head e way. The argument done pull everybody right back in, like the tittuh ain even say nothin.

Then the cousin, e bring up the other time, the big blowup from way back, e ain even mean to dig it up but e do it anyway, e just cain help e-self.

Cousin: This same thing bin happen before, oona remember, same argument, same everything, this ain new to nobody sittin at this table.

And that only make it worse, cause now it ain just this Sunday, it every Sunday it ever happen, all of it sittin right there on the table gettin cold same as the rice.

Gran, she the one finally say the thing everybody done agreed on without nobody ever writin it down nowhere.

Gran: We done say a long time ago we ain fa talk this at the table, enty? We done agreed on that.

But e ain matter one bit. The words just roll right on over what Gran say like e ain say nothin a-tall, the two of them still goin at it, still throwin the same charge back and forth, one sayin the other one don't understand, the other one flat denyin it straight back, no give, no ground, nothin move a inch.

Then the uncle push back from the table. E ain leave the room, e ain go nowhere really, just push the chair back a little piece, cross e arm, sit there lookin off toward the window like the yard got somethin in it e need to study real hard. Just puttin some air between e-self and the rest of em.

And after that it gone quiet. Real quiet. The kind where oona can yeddy every single fork touch every single plate, loud like somebody droppin somethin heavy. Ain nobody sayin nothin. Gran eatin slow. The auntie starin down at e own plate like e mad at it too now.

Little after that the daddy try. E just reach for whatever easy.

Daddy: Oona hear they done raise up a new preacher over to the other church?

And it work, a little bit. A few people pick the talk back up, a little laugh here, a little there, but e ain the same table it was before. Ain nobody go all the way back to how e was, easy and loose. Everybody just eatin now, polite, careful, like everybody watchin they own mouth close.

Time come for the benne wafer and the coffee, and I can tell you, my friend, wasn't nobody sayin sorry, wasn't nobody sayin nothin bout what just happen at all. Just clear the table, wash up, everybody hug goin out the door same as always, sweet-mout all round like nothin happen, "love oona," "see oona next Sunday," all that. But oona could shum it plain in how the auntie cut e eye one more good time at the uncle fore e get in e car.

Ain nobody say the thing out loud. Ain nobody gon say it. The family just done gone back to how e always do, quiet agree without one word bein spoke, never to bring it up again.

But you and me both know, my friend, and everybody who was settin at that table know it too, e gon come up again. Might not be next Sunday, might not even be next month. But e comin. E always come.
