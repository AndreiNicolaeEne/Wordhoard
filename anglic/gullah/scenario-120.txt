Tittuh, set down yah wid me a minute, cause I got someting fa tell you bout, and you ain't been dey fa none of it, not one day of it, so I got to carry you through de whole ting from de start to de end.

You 'member when I stand up dat Sunday after church and tell everybody at de table, oona hear me good now, I gwine run dat big race, de one dey do every year cross de bridge, twenty-six mile and some change. I ain't train one single day when I say it, not one. Enty dat foolish, to run oona mout 'fore oona foot even move one time? But I done say it in front de whole family, so dey ain't no quiet way fa slip out de back door on it later. My word done gone out de house 'fore my body even know what it bin sign up for.

De mother, she cut she eye at me straight cross de table dat Sunday.

The mother: "Chile, you done lose you mind? You ain't run further dan de mailbox and back in I don't know how many year."

Me: "I know it. But I done say it now, so I got to make good on it."

She just shake she head and pass de rice.

Well, dem first few week of training, e hard in a way I ain't never look for. I t'ink cause I done walk dese road all my life, run a little bit gwine be nutin, but my body ain't know dat message. Every session I come home more wreck dan de one 'fore it. Leg da burn, back hurtin, some mornin I can hardly bend down fa put on my own shoe. I lay down on de porch after and t'ink, if e dis hard right now, how I gwine do twenty-six mile of it come race day?

But I settle into a way of doing after dat first bad stretch. Every mornin 'fore day clean I up and out, rain or shine, mood good or mood low-down, don't matter none. Some mornin de wind off de water da blow so cold e cut through you like a knife, some mornin e hot 'fore de sun even good up, but I out dey doing what de plan say fa do dat day regardless. You got to keep at it or de whole ting fall apart, and I ain't have no room fa let it fall apart, not after I done tell everybody my business.

Den come de setback. I bin pushin too hard too soon, and one week my knee just give way under me, swole up big as a orange. Had fa go see de buckra doctor and everyting, and e tell me straight, no runnin at all, not one step, till dat ting settle down good. Tittuh, I bin scared. I lay up dey for near two week not knowin if I ever gwine get back to it, wonderin if I done gone and prove de mother right after all. But de knee come round slow, and when I finally get back out dey, I ain't run de way I bin runnin before. I cut de plan way back, ease into it soft, careful like I never bin careful bout nutin in my whole life.

Round dat time a tittuh from down de road start comin out wid me some mornin, and havin she dey side by side wid me make all de difference in de world, even on de day she wasn't dey. Just knowin somebody else out dey sufferin same as you, knowin somebody gwine yeddy you complain and complain right back at you, dat carry you through when de body want to quit on you.

But it was one day, one particular training day, dat like to end de whole ting for good. We bin out dey doin de longest run yet, and 'bout three quarter de way through, I feel my leg da shake, and den my body just quit on me all at once, leg and mind together, like somebody done pull de plug. I stop dead in de road and I couldn't hardly stand up straight.

The training partner: "You alright? Talk to me."

Me: "I don't know if I can finish dis one. I don't know if I can finish none of it, tell you de truth."

She ain't sweet-mout me none, ain't tell me no pretty lie fa make me feel good in de moment. She just say, "Well, we gon walk it den. Ain't no shame in dat." And we walk de rest de way home, slow as a cootuh, but we bin shum de finish of dat run all de same.

Right round de middle of de whole season, dat's when de doubt hit de hardest. De actual race day still so far off e ain't even feel real no more, just a date sittin out dey somewhere in de future, and I start to wonder if I bin foolin myself de whole time, tittuh. But den, not too long after dat, come a lil breakthrough, nutin big, nutin nobody but me and de tittuh woulda notice at all. Just one ordinary morning session dat go better dan any bin gone before it, leg feel light, breath come easy, and dat lil bit of ease put de fire right back in me when I bin bout ready fa let it go out.

Toward de end I tighten up everyting. Careful bout my rest, careful bout what I put in my mout, benne cake and rice and greens 'stead of whatever bin lyin round de kitchen. And dat last week, 'stead of pushin harder like every bone in me want to do, I ease way back, trust dat de month and month already put in bin enough on dey own. Dat kind of trust hard fa hold onto, tittuh, harder dan any run I ever run.

De night 'fore de race I couldn't hardly sleep one wink. Lay dey in de dark goin over every mile in my head long 'fore I bin need to, seein de whole course in my mind over and over till day clean come and I ain't even bin sleep at all.

But de race itself, tittuh, I got to tell you bout dat last stretch, cause dat's where e get hold of me good. Everyting in me, every last piece, bin beggin fa stop right round dat last few mile. Leg done gone numb, mind done gone off somewhere else entirely, and still I ain't stop. I don't know where dat kind of holdin on come from, but e come when I need it.

And den e done. Jus so, over. I cross dat line and it ain't feel like nutin I bin picture all dem month leadin up to it. I bin t'ink I gwine feel like flyin, like some kind of glory bust open in my chest right dey. Instead I just feel empty and quiet, like all de fight done leak right out my body at once, and I stand dey not even sure what to do wid myself no more.

Days after dat, my body ain't feel like my own body no more, tittuh. Took near a week 'fore I could walk down stair widout holdin de rail, 'fore my leg stop feelin like somebody else leg strap onto me in de night.

But I tell you dis, and dis de part I want you fa really yeddy: I done learn someting bout myself dat season dat ain't never leave me since. I done learn my body and my will can go on long after every part of me swear dey done. And once you know dat bout youself, you can't unknow it again. I ain't de same somebody who stand up in front dat table and run e mout dat Sunday. Dat somebody ain't know what e was signin up for. Dis one right here do.
