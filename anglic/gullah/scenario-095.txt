Since day clean de two of dem been down in Mama room, sortin through what e leave, box by box, and it don't take long fore it go wrong.

The elder: You slower dan a cootuh dis mornin, tittuh, we ain got all day fa dis.

The younger: I taking my time cause dis my mama things too, not just oona.

The elder: Well while oona take oona time, I gwine set dis benne tin wid my box. Mama gimme um wid e own hand fore e pass, say e for me cause I da de one always down here Sunday, cookin wid um.

The younger: E neva say no such a ting to you. Dat tin bin settin on dat shelf long as I remember, ain nobody special promise um to nobody.

The elder: Enty I shum wid me own eye, e give me dat tin and say de word out e own mouth? You wasn't even come round dat week, you was up to town.

The younger: Cut you eye all you want, dat don't make um true. You always did know how fa sweet-mout Mama when oona want something.

Neither one say nottin for a spell, only de fan turnin slow up top and de old dog scratchin at de screen.

The younger: Le we talk bout de money den, since we here. I know oona done gone to de buckra bank fore me and draw out more'n oona half.

The elder: I ain draw out nottin more'n what fair. Somebody had fa pay for de funeral fore oona even yeddy Mama gone. Somebody had fa see to um.

The younger: E ain even bout de tin, is it. E ain bout de money neither.

The elder: No. E ain.

The younger: E just... e gone, tittuh, and dis all we got left fa hold on to, and we tearin at um like buckra tearin land off a body.

The aunt: Oona two hush now, oona hear me? Oona sister, not stranger fighting over lan. Let de tin be, let de money be. Ain nothing gwine walk off tonight.

The elder: Yes'm.

The younger: Yes'm.

Both of dem set quiet after dat, lookin down at e own hand, knowin how e must a sound, two grown woman hollerin over what dey mama leave, fore e even cold in de ground good.
