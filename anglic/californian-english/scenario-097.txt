Me: Okay so I know you don't wanna hear "it gets better," everybody's already said that to you like ten times this week and it probably feels super empty every time. I'm not gonna say that. I just wanna tell you what my first year actually looked like, no filter, and you can take whatever's useful and leave the rest.

So the first few days, honestly, I was just numb. Like, completely checked out. There's this whole machine that kicks in, people bringing food, calling the funeral place, figuring out who's picking who up from the airport, and it's crowding out the actual thing that happened so hard that you don't even get a second to feel it directly. I remember standing in my kitchen holding a casserole dish somebody's coworker dropped off and just thinking, huh, okay, this is a thing that's happening now. That's it. That's all I felt. Not because I didn't care, just, there wasn't room yet.

And then everybody goes home. That's the part nobody warns you about. Everybody's there and there's noise and dishes and somebody's cousin asking if you want coffee, and then one day it's just done, they've all driven off, and you're standing in the house by yourself for real for the first time. Dude, that silence hit me like a truck. I actually remember sitting down on the floor in the hallway because I didn't trust my legs. Nothing left to distract from it anymore, it's just you and the quiet and the thing that happened, sitting right there with you.

My body did some weird stuff after that. I'd go like three, four days barely eating anything, not on purpose, food just didn't register as a thing I wanted or needed. And sleep got so strange, I wasn't sleeping through the night, I'd get these little bursts, like an hour here, forty minutes there, wide awake at 3am staring at the ceiling fan. Never anything whole. It took a long time before a full night's sleep felt like a normal thing again.

The first month was just bizarre because the world kept moving, like, visibly moving, way faster than I was anywhere near ready for. People at work asking about my weekend like nothing had happened. Traffic still backed up on the 405 same as always. It felt almost offensive at first, like, do you not know what happened, and then you realize, no, of course they don't, life just keeps going whether you're ready or not.

And then there was this one random Tuesday, nothing marked on the calendar, no anniversary, no nothing, just a totally ordinary day, and it undid me completely. No warning at all. I don't even remember what set it off that day. I just remember being on the floor of my car in a parking lot bawling before a errand that should've taken five minutes. You can't predict those. They just come.

Months later it was smells mostly, for me. Or some random little object, like I found an old grocery list in a jacket pocket once and that was it, I was gone for the rest of the day. There's no pattern to when it hits. You could go two weeks feeling like a person and then something totally dumb, a smell in a hallway, a song on the radio, and it's a wave out of nowhere.

There's this long middle stretch too, and I gotta be honest with you about it because nobody tells you about that part. It's not the sharp grief anymore, it's more like a fog. Just kind of dulls everything, even the stuff that's supposed to be good. Friend's birthday party, a good show, whatever, I'd be there and just sort of underwater the whole time. That part lasted way longer than I expected it to.

Somebody said something to me during that stretch that stung way more than it should have. Some coworker, meaning well I guess, told me I should "try to look on the bright side" because at least it wasn't sudden. I wanted to scream. And then like the same week, my neighbor, who I barely even talked to before, just left a plant on my porch with a note that said "no need to water this one, it's fake, one less thing." That one little thing did more for me than any of the official grief counselor stuff, any of it. Small dumb kindness just landed different than anything planned.

The anniversary was its own whole thing. I dreaded it for like a month beforehand, it was building up in my head as this huge looming date, and honestly the day itself was kind of a mess, I didn't do anything special, I think I just sat around and cried and ate cereal for dinner. But I got through it. However clumsy that looked, I got through it, and that was genuinely the whole point. Nobody needs a beautiful ceremonial day. You just need to still be standing at the end of it.

Appetite and sleep came back eventually but so slow, and never all at once. Like one week I'd sleep fine and eat like a person, next week I'm back to picking at crackers and up at 4am again. It's not a straight line, it kind of zigzags back on itself for a while before it settles.

There was a day, way later than I expected, where I caught myself actually laughing. Like real laughing, from the gut, not the fake kind you do to make other people comfortable. And right behind it came this guilt, totally uninvited, like how dare I laugh, like I was betraying something by feeling okay for ten seconds. That guilt is such a specific, dumb, awful feeling and I wasn't ready for it.

By the end of the year I was carrying it different. Lighter in some places, for sure, way lighter than those first months. But it never actually left, and I don't think it's supposed to. It just kind of folds itself into you differently over time.

So that's basically it. It does not stay this hard forever, I promise you that. But there's no set clock on it, there's no "by month six you should be fine" schedule anybody can hand you. So don't sit there measuring your year against mine, or against anybody else's. Yours is gonna look like yours, and whatever pace that ends up being, that's the right one.
