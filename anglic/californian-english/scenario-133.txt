So I got the plot back in like February, and honestly the hardest part was just deciding what to put in. I'm sitting there with the little paper packets spread out on the kitchen table going back and forth for like a week. Tomatoes for sure, that's not even a question, everybody does tomatoes. But then I'm like, okay, do I do the fancy heirloom ones that split if you look at them wrong, or do I just go with something that's gonna actually produce. I ended up doing a mix, a couple Early Girls for the sure thing and then like three heirlooms because I'm a sucker for the weird striped ones. Threw in some peppers, a row of bush beans, and squash because my neighbor two plots down swore up and down squash is basically foolproof out here.

Didn't put anything in the ground till the back half of March though, waited out that one last cold snap we had, the one where it dropped into the forties for like three nights and everybody on the group chat was freaking out about their citrus. Once that passed it was smooth sailing for a good six, seven weeks. We got this stretch of just perfect weather, sunny but not brutal yet, and everything took off. Like almost suspiciously fast. The bean sprouts were up in five days, the tomato starts doubled in size in like two weeks. I remember standing there one Saturday morning just kind of grinning at the plot like a dork because it actually looked like something.

Then of course the bugs show up. Aphids, first on the peppers, little green guys just clustered all over the new growth like they'd sent out invitations. I did the whole soapy water spray thing, mixed it up in a spray bottle, hit them every couple days. It worked, kind of. Knocked the population way down but they'd always creep back a little, especially after it warmed up again. Never fully got rid of them the whole season, just kept it manageable, which I guess is the best you can hope for with aphids honestly.

Then June hit and it just did not rain. At all. For weeks. And the plot doesn't have its own hose bib, you gotta haul water from the tank by the shed, so I'm out there every single morning before work with like two five gallon buckets, back and forth, back and forth, trying to get everything soaked before it got hot. That went on for almost a month straight. My arms were so sore. My partner kept asking why I even signed up for this and honestly some mornings I was asking myself the same thing.

And that's when the whole water thing with the guy next to my plot blew up. So the garden's got this schedule taped up by the shed, everybody's got their assigned days for the tank, and this dude apparently decided the schedule was more of a suggestion. I'd go out there on my day and the tank would already be like half drained because he'd hit it the night before too, off schedule, and then act confused when I brought it up.

Me: Hey, so I noticed the tank was pretty low again this morning, were you out here yesterday too?

The neighbor: Yeah, my stuff was looking rough, I just topped it off real quick.

Me: I get it, mine's rough too, everybody's rough right now, that's why there's a schedule.

The neighbor: I mean it's not that deep, it's just water.

Me: It's kind of that deep when I'm hauling buckets at six in the morning and there's nothing left.

It went back and forth like that for a couple weeks, honestly kind of annoying, nobody wanted to be the jerk about it but also nobody wanted their plants to die. Eventually one of the older guys who's been there forever, real chill, stepped in and just said look, we gotta actually enforce the sign up sheet, and put like a little padlock code thing on the tank valve that only worked on your assigned days. Sounds extreme but it totally fixed it. After that the neighbor and I actually got kind of friendly about it, joked about the whole thing, no hard feelings in the end.

Best thing I grew all year, no contest, was the tomatoes. Like it wasn't even close. Those Early Girls just did not stop. I was giving them away to literally everyone, coworkers, the people at the counter at my coffee spot, my building's group chat. My partner started hiding when I walked in with another bag of them.

Meanwhile the squash, the one thing everybody promised me was foolproof, got absolutely destroyed by rot. Turns out I was watering it a little too generous during that whole hand-hauling stretch, trying to overcompensate, and the base of the plant just went soft and mushy on me. Lost like half the squash plants to it before I figured out what was going on and backed off the watering right at the base.

Then in the middle of all that we got hit with a storm, one of those weird summer ones that just rolled in out of nowhere, hard wind and rain for like a day and a half. Flattened three whole rows, just laid the bean plants and half the peppers straight over sideways in the mud. I went out there the next morning and it looked like a disaster, honestly almost cried a little, not gonna lie. Spent the whole weekend staking everything back up, tying stuff to bamboo poles, propping the peppers with these little cages I had to run out and buy more of. Lost a few plants that just snapped, but most of it bounced back within like a week or two, which surprised me.

By the end of the season I actually sat down and tallied it up because I'm that person now apparently. Something like ninety pounds of tomatoes total, a decent haul of beans, way less squash than I planned on obviously, and the peppers did fine once they recovered from the storm. Traded a huge bag of tomatoes and some peppers to the guy at the far end who grows all these citrus trees, got a whole box of his lemons and a couple oranges back, which honestly felt like the better deal for me since I don't have room for a lemon tree where I live.

Put the plot to bed in November, pulled the last of the dead vines, turned the soil over, threw a cover crop of clover in to let it rest till spring. Looking back on the whole thing, yeah it was a lot more work than I thought going in, way more than just sticking seeds in dirt and waiting. But honestly it was a good year. Rough middle, for sure, between the bugs and the drought stretch and the whole water drama and then the storm on top of it, but it worked out. I'd do it again in a heartbeat.
