Ten o'clock only I am reaching, and already I am knowing something is off, because the attendance register is showing two names crossed, both on leave, one calling in sick and one not even calling, just not there. Two short before anything is even starting, and I am thinking, chalo, we will manage, what to do, this is not first time. The other operator is looking at me and saying, come na, let us do the round first, no use standing and worrying only.

Half past ten, round is fine. Machines running, second line humming like always, the chiller reading normal. I am having only three boys on the floor where usually five is there, and the packing table is already looking short-handed, but bindaas we are starting, no tension.

By eleven the first small thing happens. The gauge on the pasteuriser is jumping, up down up down, small small movements, nothing major, but I am noting it in the log because that is not normal behaviour, na. I am asking the boy near the panel, is this happening before also, and he is saying, sir it is doing like that only since one hour, I thought it will settle. I should have called then itself. That is my first mistake of the night, thinking it will settle on its own.

Then half past eleven, it does not settle, it breaks. The sealing unit on line one just jams, pouches half-sealed, milk spilling on the belt, whole batch getting spoiled, and the smell of burnt plastic coming from the heating element because it is stuck on for too long. I am shouting off the machine, off the machine, and running to the main switch myself because the boy is just standing there, senti, not moving, first time seeing something like this happening on his shift.

We are trying to patch it, jugaad style, because stopping the whole plant is not an option, deliveries are waiting from four in the morning onward for the vendors, bhaiya at the depot will be standing there for nothing if pouches are not reaching. So while three of us are trying small small fixes on line one, cleaning, resetting, checking the sensor, the other line I am keeping running with whoever is free, doubling up duties, one boy doing two jobs, running between stations.

I am calling the maintenance number at quarter to twelve. No answer. Twelve o'clock, calling again. No answer. Half past twelve, I am giving one missed call also thinking he will call back seeing it, nothing. What to do, yaar, this is the thing, at night everyone is switched off, phone is there but person is not there. I am telling the boy, try his other number, the personal one, and finally at one o'clock someone is picking up, and it is not even the mechanic, it is his roommate saying he is not well, sleeping, will not be coming.

So then I am calling the shift-in-charge above me, the senior, and he is picking up in that voice already, half annoyed before I am even finishing the sentence.

The senior: What is it now, at one in the night you are calling.

Me: Sir, line one sealing unit is down, I am trying but I am not getting it, mechanic is also not available.

The senior: Achha, I am coming, but this is really too much, every second week something is happening on your shift only.

He is arriving by half past one, not happy, grumbling the whole way from the gate, saying he was having some function at home, senti mood he was in, and now this. But once he is there he is working fast, I will give him that, hands moving, opening the panel, checking the wiring, and by two o'clock line one is limping again, not full speed but running.

That is when the second thing happens, right at two, worst possible time. The light goes. Not just flicker, full gone, whole area dark for maybe forty seconds before the generator is catching, and in that dark, one of the boys, carrying a crate near the chiller door, he is slipping on the wet floor near where the milk had spilled earlier, and going down hard, crate falling, his hand catching the edge of the rack on the way down. Not serious, thankfully, small cut, some swelling, but he is in pain, holding his wrist, and for two minutes everybody is around him instead of the machines, nobody caring about line two at that moment, only is he okay, is he okay.

We are sending him to sit, giving him water, someone wrapping a cloth on the cut, and the senior is looking at line two, which because of all the running around has started backing up badly, crates piling, and he is taking the call, stop it, no use pushing a half-broken thing with half the staff distracted and someone already hurt. Off the line, he is saying, we will lose this batch but better than losing a person properly. Bas, that decision at least is easy, even if it is costing us.

By three, there is this strange lull. Line one holding steady, line two off but at rest, the boy with the hurt hand sitting quietly with tea someone got him, cutting chai from the night stall outside, and for maybe forty minutes it is actually looking like the night is turning. I am even telling the senior, sir I think we are through the worst now, and he is saying, don't jinx it yaar, don't say like that.

And of course, like the film only, at half past three the third thing breaks. This time it is the delivery side. The van that is supposed to come at four for the first run to the depots, it is calling, driver saying tyre has gone flat on the highway, is not there for another hour at least, and by then our whole first dispatch window is finished, useless, because the shops opening at six are expecting stock by five.

The driver: Sir, wheel is punctured, I am trying to fix but stepney is also not in good condition, will take time.

Me: What to do now, everything is happening today itself, one after another.

So now we are having spoiled batch from line one, full batch lost from line two, a boy with an injured hand, and a van stuck on the highway, and the tally just keeps mounting through those hours, someone counting crates lost, someone counting pouches lost, the senior on the phone to the office trying to arrange a backup vehicle, and all of us just pushing through, half running on nothing but chai and jugaad and stubbornness.

Four o'clock, five o'clock, that stretch is a blur honestly, small small tasks one after another, resetting, cleaning, loading whatever could be salvaged, nobody talking much, just doing, eyes burning, legs aching, that kind of tired where you are moving but not really thinking anymore.

By six the sky outside the loading bay is starting to go that grey-pink colour, and the backup van has finally come and gone with whatever we could salvage, and the day shift boys are walking in fresh, asking what happened, and I am not even having the energy to explain properly, just saying, arre don't ask, whole night like that only.

Walking out at half past six, sun properly up now, and I am not even feeling relief, just this heavy nothing, too tired for relief itself, too tired for anything, just walking to the signal to get the first auto home, thinking I will sleep first, will feel something about all this later, once the body has caught up with what the night actually was.
