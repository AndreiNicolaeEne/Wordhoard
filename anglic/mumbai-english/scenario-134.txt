This year the mango crop back home, in our native place, was the best anybody in the family can remember, na. From January itself we were sensing something is there, trees full full, flowering like never before, and Uncle on the phone was saying, do one thing, come down early only this time, don't wait for summer holidays.

By March we were having almost double the usual crates, seriously. Usual season we get some fifteen hundred dozen, this time it crossed twenty-eight hundred, nearly double, exactly like Uncle said. And the hapoos, the alphonso itself, gave a count far past what it normally gives, ek number quality also, no faltu fruit at all.

Before this the best year was some eight years back, small small good seasons came in between but nothing close, and now this one has beaten even that, hands down.

Because of so much fruit ripening together, extra pickers had to be called from the neighbouring village itself, labour is there, but time is not there, fruit will turn on the tree only if we delay. Whole family plus four five extra hands, packing packing from morning to evening, current going off and on the whole night because the pump was running non-stop.

Once household need and all the relatives' quota was covered, the rest, some went for storage in the shed, some Uncle traded off to the wholesale bhaiya at the market. Paisa vasool that season, no doubt.

The eldest, my grandfather's own elder brother, ninety plus, seeing harvests since he was a small boy itself, said...

The eldest: In my whole life I have not seen crop like this one, arre. Rain came exact time it should come, then the whole season stayed mild, no big heat spell to spoil the flowering. Bas, God's grace, what to do, some years He gives like this, some years He doesn't.

Even now, whenever harvest talk comes up at home, somebody will say, arre that year na, that was the year, and every other season still gets measured against it only.
