Achha so that day, let me tell you properly, don't rush me.

We were going down to native place, me and the mother, because the father was not well there, some chest infection only but at that age everything becomes big thing na. Pass we had made, medical ground, all online itself, printout also I kept ready in the file, Aadhar, RC book, PUC, everything I stacked one on top of other like for an exam only. Because that time, you remember, district border pe naka was standing, this was not there before, they put it only because so much movement was happening and people were misusing the pass, going here going there for no reason, calling it emergency when it was just timepass visiting relatives. So government said no, checkpost will stand, papers will be seen properly, then only you cross.

By the time we reached the front the line had built up good, maybe fifty vehicles ahead easy, two-wheelers, one tempo, some auto also stuck in between not knowing why he even came this route. Half an hour we were standing since, bumper to bumper, engine off-on, off-on I was doing because petrol also is not cheap na. And I will be honest with you, my stomach was doing something the whole time. Not that we had done anything wrong, pass was genuine, father's report also was there in the bag, but still, when you see the barricade and the rifle standing there, something in you goes senti, starts imagining what all can go wrong, what if they say pass is not valid, what if they turn us back from here itself after four hours driving.

When our turn came the constable put his hand out, not even looking properly, just "papers." I gave everything together, the whole file. He took the pass first.

Constable: Where going?

Me: Native place, sir, Ratnagiri side. Father is not well there.

That one I answered straight, no tension, because it is simple only, true also. Then he looked up properly at me, and the mother sitting quiet in the back seat, and he asked the second one.

Constable: This medical ground pass, whose name it is on, and what is the relation exactly, because I am seeing two persons travelling and pass is showing one.

This I had to answer carefully, slowly, because one wrong word and he will start doubting whole thing. I said the pass is applied under my name as the son, mother is dependent travelling with me for same purpose, and I showed him the WhatsApp message from the doctor there also, on the phone, forwarded copy. He took the phone, held it close, read it two times.

Then he went quiet. Just flipping the papers, RC, then pass, then Aadhar, matching the address on Aadhar with address on pass, matching my face with the photo, that whole minute of nobody saying anything, only the sound of paper. I was standing there trying to keep my face like nothing, like this is routine only for me, but inside, honestly, heart was going fast fast.

Then he asked, what is the full address there, house number, because pass address was showing only village name, not full. And that I had actually forgotten the exact house number, we always just say "our house, near the temple" to anyone who asks locally, nobody uses number there. So right there, standing, I had to call the cousin on phone, quickly, "arre bhaiya house number kya hai apna, checkpost pe puch rahe hai," and he gave it, and I told the constable, and he wrote it down on his register with his own pen, slow, like he is not in any hurry even though whole line is waiting behind.

Ahead of us, few vehicles before, there was one fellow on a bike, alone, no family, and him they held much longer than us. His bag they opened, his phone also they checked, made him stand aside near the barricade while two of them talked between themselves looking at his papers again and again. Nobody in the line said one word about it, we were all just watching from the corner of the eye only, nobody commenting, because what to say, it is not our business, and also somewhere you are thinking, thank god it is not me standing there like that.

Whole time I kept my voice level, kept my hands still on the wheel even though they wanted to do something, tap tap, anything. Because you show even little bit shaking and these fellows notice, they are trained to notice only, and then more questions come, more time goes, and mother is sitting there tired from the road already.

Small thing he did though, the constable, after he wrote the address, he looked at the mother, and he said...

Constable: Aunty theek hai na, paani chahiye?

, just that, offering water, nothing big, but that one line, somehow that eased something in me. Small courtesy, no reason he had to ask that, pass business was already separate matter, but he asked.

Then he stacked all the papers together, tapped them once on his palm to straighten, and gave back in my hand.

Constable: Theek hai, chalo, jaldi jao.

That "chalo, jaldi jao" and a small wave of his hand towards the road ahead, that was it, that was the whole permission, and I put the car in gear and went.

I drove maybe twenty minutes down that road, mother not saying anything, me not saying anything, both of us just looking out, before I actually felt my shoulders come down from wherever they had gone up to. Only then I realised my hand was properly shaking on the gear now, after, not during, which is a funny thing if you think about it. And my throat was dry like I had been talking for one hour straight though I had said maybe ten sentences total to that man.

That is why I tell this one carefully to you, no filmy making, no adding extra drama to it like it was some big raid scene, because it was not that. Nobody shouted, nobody was rude, papers were checked, one detail I had to run and get on phone, one fellow ahead had it harder than us and we just watched quiet, and then we were let go with a "chalo." That is the whole size of it. But that half hour standing before, and that twenty minutes after where the fear was still sitting in the body even though the danger was already finished, that part was real also, and that I don't want to make smaller than it was either. So I tell it exactly like this, no bigger, no smaller, because that is exactly how it went.
