Arre listen, I have to tell you the whole thing from the start only, don't stop me in between, okay. That night the wind was something else na, whole building was creaking, the light had gone by ten itself and everybody's plants and buckets flying here and there on the terrace. I was thinking okay tomorrow morning some cleaning to do, small small mess, that's it. But when I went down, seriously my hand went to my mouth. Whole tree, that big gulmohar from next door, fallen clean across our fence. Not leaning, not resting, flattened it completely, half of it sitting in our side, branches on the water tank, everything.

First thing I did was go and check, whose tree is this actually, because you know these old plots, the line is not always so clear. But no, this one was easy to see, the trunk was growing right on their side, roots going into their soil, only the branches were coming over into our compound for years, giving shade and all, no problem with that. So the tree is theirs, hundred percent, no confusion there.

I went and knocked on their gate maybe eight, half eight morning. The uncle came out in his baniyan only, scratching his head, looking at the same scene I was looking at two minutes before.

Me: Uncle, see what has happened. Your tree has come down on our fence, whole thing is gone.

The neighbor: Yes yes I am seeing only na, whole night I heard some sound, I thought it's just wind banging the windows. What to do, it's an act of God, wind has done it, nobody's fault this one.

I didn't like that "nobody's fault" business from the very first line itself, I'm telling you. Because this tree was not some healthy tree that any strong wind would take down. It had been leaning, slowly slowly, since maybe two-three years. Everybody in the lane had noticed it, it was not a secret.

Me: Uncle, wind is there, that I agree, but this tree was leaning long time only. Since how many years I am seeing it tilting more and more towards our side. This is not one night's business.

The neighbor: Arre that's just how the tree grows, all trees have some lean, no? You are overthinking.

Then my workmate's mother, who lives two houses down, she came out with her tea only, and she said...

The mother down the lane: I remember uncle, the society meeting, two years back, the watchman himself had told to cut that branch, it was going too much on one side, he had said it will fall one day like this only. Everybody was there, no? Even you were nodding your head that day.

That one landed. Because now it was not me alone saying, somebody else was witness also. But did the uncle back down easy? No chance. That's when the real lafda started. Because now it comes to money, whose tree, fine, wind or not, fine, but who is going to pay for the fence, that's a different fight altogether.

The neighbor: Look, tree is mine, I am not denying, but wind is act of nature, insurance-walas also don't pay for that. Fence is a shared thing between us anyway, so fifty-fifty we should do.

Me: Uncle, how is fifty-fifty fair? If your tree was healthy and looked after, this would not have happened. You knew it was leaning, everybody knew, and still nothing was done. This is not some random accident, what to do type of thing.

Bas, from there it became a proper standoff only. Two-three days like that, not even greeting each other properly on the stairs, just small small nod, no more "chai peeloge" or nothing. His side was saying wind, my side was saying negligence, and neither one was ready to say okay fine I'll pay more. You know how it is na, once ego comes in, money becomes secondary only, it's about who is right.

Then this old uncle from the ground floor, retired railway fellow, he is there in every society matter, everybody respects him, he came into it, nobody called him even, he just came one evening with his stick, sat on the compound bench and called both of us.

The elder from downstairs: Do one thing, listen to me only for two minutes. Both of you are correct, both of you are also little bit wrong, that's how these things always are. Tree is his, that we cannot argue. But this fence, both families are using it since thirty years, sharing it, so some responsibility both sides carry for its upkeep also, isn't it. So instead of fifty-fifty as if nobody did anything wrong, or hundred percent as if it's some big crime, do sixty-forty. Owner of the tree takes more, other side takes little, both can walk away without feeling they have lost.

Nobody wanted to be the one saying yes first, you know, that "losing face" thing. So the elder just looked at the uncle and said...

The elder from downstairs: Uncle, you say yes first, na, you are senior, it will look good on you, not like you gave in, like you did the right thing.

And somehow that worked. The uncle said chalo theek hai, sixty-forty, and once he said it, my side also could say okay fine, done, without it looking like I won or he lost. Very clever move only, that old man, I'm telling you, he has seen life.

After that the real work started, and honestly that first day was the most awkward, worse than the money fight almost. His two sons came with a saw and some rope, our side sent the building's part-time mestri, and for maybe two hours everybody was just working, cutting the branches, dragging the logs, and nobody was talking properly. Just "idhar pakad", "usko udhar rakho", that much only. Stiff like anything, like two teams forced to play together.

Then around eleven his wife came out with a plate, cutting chai for everybody and some biscuits, didn't even ask if we wanted, just kept it on the wall and said...

The neighbor's wife: Arre sab log garam garam chai piyo pehle, kaam baad mein.

And somehow that small small gesture did what all the talking couldn't do. Everybody sat for ten minutes, drinking tea, and slowly the jokes started, somebody was telling how the tree gave such good shade for pigeons only, now where will they sit, that kind of timepass talk. Later when we were fixing the new posts, our side didn't have enough nails, and without even asking, the uncle's son just handed over his whole box, said keep it, no need to return small small things like that.

By evening the fence was up, new wood, proper cementing at the base this time, sturdier than the old one honestly, that old fence was some jugaad arrangement from years back, this one is proper.

Now, we are like before only, maybe even better. His wife sends tiffin sometimes when she makes something special, we send back on festival days. The tree business, nobody even mentions now, it's like it never happened, except the fence itself, which stands there solid, reminding. What to do, these things happen only, small small fights, but in the end everybody is neighbour only, has to live together, no?
