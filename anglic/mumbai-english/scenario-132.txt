Arre, eleven days, no less, one after another colder than we were expecting, and I am telling you, from day one itself it was beating what the forecast people were saying. Stove was on chalu the whole night, whole day, we were not even off-ing it for two minutes, coal in the sigdi in the society compound, small small children wrapped like parcels, everybody surviving on cutting chai, cup after cup only. We are having only one kerosene stove in the whole house, one uncle downstairs lent us his second one, otherwise what we would have done, God only knows.

By the fourth night the light had gone completely and was not coming back, off and on, off and on, till we gave up and were just sitting with the stove glow. Kerosene was not there in the shop, whole area had bought it up, so we did some jugaad with old newspaper and rag to keep the fire going.

Mother: Come na inside, you'll catch fever standing there in this wind.

Me: Two minutes only, just checking the tank.

The tap in the compound, the outside one, that thing was frozen since two days, ice sitting on top like a lid, nobody in this building had seen such a thing their whole life. Tank on the terrace, same story, we had to break it with a stick before anything was coming down.

The eldest: Do one thing, keep the sigdi close to the children's cot only, don't let it go out. Years back also we had a bad one, everybody still talking about it even now, but this, this has beaten that one, no doubt in my mind, what to do.

Worst night was the eighth, that one I am not forgetting ever. Wind was cutting through the shutters like nothing was there, and by morning two of our hens were gone, frozen stiff in the coop, we had kept them close to the wall itself and even that was not enough.

Then on the eleventh day, sun came out properly, real sun, not the weak one we were getting, and, yaar, whole lane came out just to stand in it. Ice on the tank was melting, dripping dripping, everybody laughing like children only, bas, that cold, finally gone.
