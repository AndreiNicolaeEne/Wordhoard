Arre sit, sit, no need to stand like that. You're asking what mornings are actually like in this house? Bas, once I start you'll be regretting the question only, but okay, since you're asking, let me tell you properly from the beginning.

First one up, always, before anybody, before even the alarm gets the chance to ring, that is the mother. Five-something, still dark outside, and she is up. She doesn't switch on the big light even, just the small one near the god's photos, and she is lighting the lamp there, very quiet, agarbatti smoke going up, five minutes of that before the house even knows it is morning. That is her thing, every single day, rain or shine, exam time or holiday time, doesn't matter. I used to think it was some big ritual, but no, it's small small, two minutes only, then straight to the kitchen for the first chai.

Then it is the father's turn, and he wakes to the exact same grumble every single day..."Wash-room is occupied again, is it," he is saying, half asleep, banging once on the door, and from inside the eldest is shouting back, "Two minutes only, na, why you people are always in such hurry." This has been going on for years and nobody has won this contest yet. Bas, everybody has just accepted it as part of the morning, like the wash-room war is a fixture, not a fight really, more like a small drama that repeats itself for no reason, we all know how it ends and yet every day it starts again fresh.

After that, the children are woken, and always in the same order, eldest first, then the middle one, then the youngest, and the mother's calling out is exactly the same words every time, like a recording only:

Mother: "Utho, utho, seven already, get up now, get up now."

She says it to the eldest's door first, then walks down and says the same to the middle one's door, then comes to the youngest last, sits on the bed itself and shakes the shoulder, because the youngest simply doesn't get up on voice alone, needs the hand also. Every morning, same order, same words, like there's a fixed queue that nobody wrote down anywhere but everybody in the house knows by heart.

And then the argument, almost every day, close to the same lines. Usually it's about the uniform:

Middle one: "Where is my other sock, na, I'm having only one."
Mother: "It's there only, look properly, don't just stand and shout."
Middle one: "I'm telling you it's not there, I searched already."
Mother: "You searched for two seconds and now you're saying not there. Do one thing, look under the bed."

And nine out of ten times it is exactly under the bed, and nobody learns anything from this, next day same fight, same sock story, different sock.

We are having a dog also, and he has his own fixed part in all this, the moment the newspaper hits the door with that thud, he is up and barking two-three times, not more, not less, just enough to announce, like he is on payroll for it. Then he goes and sits exactly at the shoe rack, waiting for whoever leaves first, because that person always gives one biscuit before going. He knows the whole routine better than some of us, honestly, jhakaas timing every day.

Breakfast is the same story every day also, poha in this house, mostly, sometimes upma, but the father, he never once varies his order, not once in I don't know how many years. Only two spoons of sev on top, chai after, not before, never during. If the sev comes before the poha even reaches the plate, he'll wait, won't touch, like some rule is there. The mother just knows this now, doesn't even ask, itself she keeps the sev aside till the plate is in front of him.

Something goes missing almost every morning too, and it's always hunted in the same two-three places, under the newspaper on the dining table, on top of the shoe rack, or on the kitchen slab next to the cooker. Mostly it's the house key or somebody's ID card. And the searching itself has become part of the noise, everyone shouting "check there, check there," while the actual person who lost it is standing in the same spot the whole time, not moving.

Then there is the sound that means the house is finally, properly awake, second whistle of the cooker, that sharp one, and after that whistle goes off, everything just speeds up at once, like someone pressed a button. Before the whistle, people are still moving slow, half in the wash-room queue, half searching for socks. After the whistle, it's all rush, all at once.

The youngest is always running late, same few minutes every single day, no matter how early the shaking-awake happens. So there is a proper scramble at the door near the end, tiffin getting shoved into the bag, shoes half on, the mother handing over the water bottle at the very last second, saying "wait, wait, you forgot this," except somehow it's never actually forgotten, she just says it out of habit now, every single day, like a checklist she's reciting more than actually checking.

Only one day this whole rhythm just stops. Sunday. No alarm voices, no wash-room shouting, no cooker whistle timing anything. House just goes quiet, everybody sleeping in properly, even the dog seems to understand, doesn't bark at the newspaper with the same energy. It's like the house takes one day off from its own performance.

And every single morning, whoever is leaving first, at the door, the same handful of words get said, no matter who is saying them or who is hearing them..."Jaake aana, eat something on the way, don't forget," and the person leaving just says "haan haan" without even really listening, already halfway down the stairs.

Honestly, telling you all this now, sitting here, I'm finding it quite funny, the whole thing, same wash-room fight, same sock story, same dog at the shoe rack, same whistle, same words at the door, day after day after day, like the house is running some fixed programme and none of us wrote the code for it, we're just performing it fresh every single morning like it's the first time. You've never seen a single one of these mornings and I've seen hundreds, thousands maybe, and still, some days, even I'm standing there thinking, what to do, this house and its routine, it never changes and somehow it never gets old also.
