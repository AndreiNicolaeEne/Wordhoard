Arre yaar, sit sit, I'll tell you the whole thing properly, start to end, since you were not there for any of it, no. You were in Dubai only that whole season, so for you this is just one photo I sent on the family group, finisher medal, big smile, done. But there was a whole season behind that photo, chalo, let me tell.

So what happened was, one Sunday, bindaas, sitting with the whole building group having cutting chai near the society gate, I announced. Just like that. Said I am doing the full marathon this year, the Mumbai one, forty-two kilometer, not the dream run, not the half, the full thing. Uncle from A-wing said very good very good, the watchman said all the best madam, my own mother was on video call at that time itself and she heard and she started, oh so now you'll break your knee is it, at this age, what is the need. And I had not run one single day before saying this, not even round the society compound one round. So after that there was no quiet way to take it back, no. Everybody was asking, prepone the training or what, how many km you running now, aunty from third floor kept asking every week like a checkpost. So training had to happen, whether I liked it or not.

First weeks, my friend, were something else. I used to think I'm fit only, I climb four floors daily no lift, I do all the running around for tiffin and office and what not. But this was different completely. First run itself I could not even do two km without stopping, gasping like a fish, legs like somebody else's legs only, not mine. Every session was leaving me more finished than the previous one. Whole body paining, calves like stone, and the next morning worse than the running day itself. I was like, what have I done, what to do now, cannot even back out.

Then slowly slowly I settled into one routine. Every day five a.m., rain or no rain, mood there or not there, alarm goes off and I go off. Monsoon was still going on and on some days the whole road outside was river only, but I used to go, raincoat, cap, whatever. My husband used to say,

Husband: Come back inside na, whole road is flooded, what marathon you'll run in this.

But I used to go still, because once you skip one day, next day skipping becomes easy, that is the danger. So most days, whatever the weather, whatever the mood, I kept at it.

Then came the setback, and this one nearly finished the whole plan before it properly started. I twisted my ankle one Saturday, coming down from the footbridge near the station, rushing for the fast local, and it went like a small pop only but by evening it had swollen like a small brinjal. Doctor said complete rest, no running for three weeks minimum, ice it, elevate it, do nothing. Three weeks felt like three months at that time. I was lying at home, foot up on two pillows, watching all these running reels on phone, so senti I became, crying one full evening also, thinking maybe this is not for me only, maybe I should just quietly tell everyone I've dropped out, what to do, some things are not meant to happen.

Mother: See I told you no, this running-unning is not for our body type, leave it now.

But something in me did not want to leave it. Once the ankle came right, I went back, but this time much more careful. Cut the plan down properly, no more of this jumping straight to long runs, built it back slow, one extra kilometer every week only, rest days properly marked on the calendar, no jugaad, no shortcuts this time.

Middle of the season a colleague from office, she also had entered for the same marathon, so we started running together on weekends. That made such a difference, yaar. The hard days, the days legs were not moving, having company there made it bearable. We used to meet near the signal, get down from our own buildings same time, run the whole sea route together, talking timepass nonsense to keep mind off the pain.

Training partner: Come na, five more minutes only, don't stop now, we've come so far already.

Those five more minutes she used to add up were the whole difference between finishing a run and not finishing it.

But one training day, I still remember, nearly ended the whole attempt outright. It was a thirty-two km long run, hottest Sunday of that month, and somewhere around the twenty-fifth km my body just gave up, no water left in my bottle, legs cramping both together, and mind also gave up at the same moment, I sat down on the footpath near a closed hotel, put my head in my hands and thought, bas, this is it, I cannot do this, this whole idea was faltu from day one. Had to call my husband to come pick me up in the car, could not even walk to catch an auto.

Right in the middle of the whole season the doubt was worst. Race day still two-three months away, felt like it will never actually come, like I'm doing all this training for something that doesn't even exist, so unreal it felt. I am having good days and bad days but mostly I am having this feeling only, what is even the point, twenty-six weeks of five a.m. wake-ups for one Sunday that may not even happen properly.

Then somewhere past that lowest point, nothing dramatic, just one ordinary Saturday morning run, twenty-two km, and it went better than any run before it. Legs felt light, breathing was even, I finished and was not even that tired, just pleasantly tired, mast feeling only. That one run did more for my head than anything else that whole season. Renewed everything.

Toward the end I tightened up properly. Food became very disciplined, no more random vada pav standing at the corner, no non-veg two days before any long run, proper rice and dal and curd, sleep timing fixed. And in that final week, instead of pushing harder like I wanted to, coach's advice, and the training partner's also, was ease off completely, trust the months already done, the body already knows now, don't ruin it in the last week trying to prove something extra.

Night before the race I barely slept, yaar, honestly. Lying there since maybe eleven, eyes open, going over the whole route in my head, checking the timing chip on the shoe again and again, checking alarm three times, mind going over things that don't even need going over. Some senti feeling also came, thinking of the whole season, the ankle, the crying, the sea route runs.

Race day, all fine fine until about the thirty-fifth kilometer, and then it hit like a wall, everything in me wanting to stop right there on the road and just sit down like that one Sunday. Legs were done, mind was done, but somehow I did not stop, kept the legs moving, small small steps, not proper running anymore, more like shuffling, but moving.

And then the finish line came, and I want to tell you honestly, it was nothing like what I had pictured. I had imagined big emotion, crying, arms up, all that filmy stuff you see in the reels. Instead I just felt numb completely, stood there for one full minute not even understanding I had finished, medal put round my neck by some volunteer and I just looked at it like it belongs to someone else. Only later, sitting on the ground near the barricade, it hit me properly and then I did cry, but quietly, not the big dramatic way I had imagined.

Days after, body was not my own at all. Could not go down the stairs properly for almost four days, was taking the lift for things I normally never take lift for, whole building watchman was laughing seeing me walk like that. Took a good five, six days before legs felt like my own legs again, not borrowed from somewhere.

But yaar, I'll tell you what changed. Before all this I used to think of myself as one ordinary person only, get up, go office, come home, small small life. Now I know, whatever I decide properly and stick to, whatever setback comes in between, I can actually finish it. Season taught me that. Paisa vasool, every bit of it, even the crying parts.
