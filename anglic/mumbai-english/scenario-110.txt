The mother: Come, sit, sit. Eat something first, then only we will talk. So, tell us, how is it there, Bangalore side?

Me: Arre what to tell, ma, it's a whole different world only. Big, yaar, so big you cannot even imagine. Our whole Mumbai you cross corner to corner faster than they cross one area there.

The eldest cousin: Means what, bigger than here itself? I'm not believing.

Me: Sach mein, bigger in spread, na. Here at least everything is on top of everything, walking distance only, this line to that line. There it is all spread out like anything, one place to other place, sometimes the better part of an hour just for crossing from this side to that side. And that also if traffic is behaving itself, otherwise forget it.

The uncle: Traffic worse than our Andheri signal? Not possible, I'm telling you.

Me: Uncle, worse in a different way only. Here it's noise noise noise, horns going non-stop, the local rattling, hawkers shouting, that whole symphony we are used to na. There it's more like... quiet-quiet, but then suddenly bumper to bumper for one full hour, nobody even honking, just sitting like that, everybody looking at phone. First few weeks I was feeling so senti, missing our noise itself. Now I don't know, ear has become used to quiet also.

The aunty from upstairs: Achha, and where all you are staying? Big flat or what?

Me: Aunty, small small only, one room-kitchen PG type arrangement, I'm sharing with two others. Compare to here it will look like a matchbox to you, but for there it's considered decent, spacious even.

The aunty from upstairs: Spacious you are calling that? Beta, our hall itself is bigger than that then.

Me: Exactly what I'm saying, no? What is normal there is different scale altogether. But rent, aunty, don't ask. Double, sometimes more than double what we pay here for society flat. Groceries also costlier, veg and non-veg both, though chicken I'm finding cheaper there actually, funny like that. Auto fare, forget it, they don't even go by meter half the time, straight bargaining only, like our old days.

The friend from the building: Bindaas life then, or it's tough? People friendly there or not?

Me: Yaar, that one is not a simple yes-no answer. Some are so helpful, like family only, the aunty next door in my building gave tiffin two-three times when I was down with fever, didn't even ask, just sent it up. But general public on the road, no, they are keeping to themselves only, minding own business, not like here where uncle-aunty will comment on your life free of cost, no charge. So friendly, haan, but different friendly. Not the loud friendly we have, more the quiet-helping-when-it-matters friendly.

The eldest cousin: And market wise? Where you are buying vegetable-vegetable and all?

Me: There is one market near my place, small small stalls, bhaiya types selling there also, same tomato-onion drama, same bargaining, that part is universal only, ma, don't worry. But for bigger shopping there is mall culture more, everything under one roof, AC blast pannu, convenient but costs more, no bhaav-tav possible there, price is price, take it or leave it.

The uncle: And work, how you got it, how it's going?

Me: Uncle, one senior from old office only had shifted there two years before, he did some jugaad, got my resume in front of the right people. Otherwise these companies want proper interview, three-four rounds, one whole day sitting and answering, giving exam type feeling only, worse than eighth standard finals, I'm telling you. Once you are in, work is there, work is there, no shortage, sometimes too much also, but at least the office itself is not stressful stressful, people are professional, off the drama, on the work only.

The eldest cousin: Achha and initially, you knew the area or you were like lost-lost?

Me: Arre don't even ask. First month I got lost three, four times minimum. One time I took wrong bus completely, ended up god knows where, had to call the roommate, "come na, pick me up, I don't even know which junction I'm standing at." Everything looking same-same to me in the beginning, all these tech park buildings, glass glass everywhere, no landmark like our nariyal wale uncle or that particular paan shop we all use as direction marker. Slowly slowly I learned, now I can go here-there blindfolded almost, but that first month, bas, don't remind me, so much timepass wasted just finding my own house.

The aunty from upstairs: And food, beta? You are eating properly or just outside hotel-hotel?

Me: Food is a mixed bag only, aunty. Some things better, I'm not lying, the fruits there are fresher, cheaper also, and this one place near office does a filter coffee that is ek number, better than any cutting chai I've had honestly, don't tell papa I said that. But proper Mumbai chow, forget it. Vada pav there is a crime, aunty, straight up crime, they don't understand what they are doing to that poor pav. And missing our bhel, our pani puri wala outside school, nothing is coming close. So better in some things, worse in others, what to do, that is the trade only you make when you shift.

The uncle: So overall, better or worse than here? Simple answer, come on.

Me: Uncle, uncle, that is not a simple question, no? Both are having their own paisa vasool, in their own way. Here I have all of you, this noise which is our noise only, that comfort of not having to think even one second before opening my mouth. There I have the work, the growth, the pace which is suiting my career right now. Comparing is like comparing... I don't know, comparing chai to coffee, both hot, both waking you up, but not really same thing, no?

The mother: Bas, chalo, leave all that. Simple thing I'm asking, will you come back for good or not? Settle here only?

Me: Ma... honestly, right now I cannot say fixed-fixed. Maybe few more years there, see how things go, project is there, opportunity is there, cannot waste that. But this place, this house, you all, this is always going to be calling me back only, that I know for sure. Whether it's next year or after five years, I'm not able to tell you today. Let's see how it goes, na. Chalo, is that answer okay or you want more?

The mother: Haan haan, okay, okay, eat now, getting cold otherwise. We'll ask again next time you're home.
