Arre yaar, sit, let me tell you properly what all happened that night, because you also love this fellow like I do, so you should know the full tamasha, start to finish.

See, it was supposed to be timepass only. Few drinks after office, at that hotel near the signal, nothing big planned. But by the end, forget it, gone case completely. Standing and swaying like the local itself was moving under his feet, eyes half shut, and every two minutes saying "I'm fine only, I'm totally fine" without anybody even asking him. That is always the first sign na, when the person starts saying he is fine before you say one word.

First real sign it had gone too far, this was right there at the table only. He tried to get up and the legs simply did not listen, one leg going this side, other leg going that side, and I had to catch his arm before his face went straight into the plates. Words also came out all jumbled, half English half Hindi, no full stop anywhere, just chalu going on and on, one long sentence with no meaning in the middle of it.

He kept insisting no no, he is okay, order one more only, what yaar, still early. Early, at that hour! Getting him to leave was a whole negotiation, not a small thing, you know how it is with him. First time I said come, let's go, he completely ignored. Second time, proper sulking, bas two minutes na, like a child being told the TV is going off. Do one thing, I told him, finish this glass and then we go, and only then something registered, and grumbling, cursing under his breath, achha thik hai fine, he stood up.

Then the walk. Arm around his shoulder the whole way, practically carrying half his weight, taking small small steps because he could not manage more, and every two minutes he is leaning his full weight onto me like I am some wall. No auto was there at that hour, otherwise would have just put him in one and finished the matter. People on the road giving all kind of looks. One uncle type, coming from the other side with his stick, stopped completely and said, loud enough for the whole lane, "kya ho gaya isko, itni raat ko yeh sab theek nahi hai", scolding us without even knowing us, some aunty also peeping down from her balcony. What to do, this is what happens only, everybody becomes judge for two minutes and then goes back inside.

Halfway, near the vada pav stall, shutter already down, we had to stop completely because his stomach could not hold anymore. Sat him down on the footpath, back against the shutter, and everything from the night came out there. Held his head, patted his back, kept saying only let it go, let it go, better now no, and it was better, little bit, after that, though the smell stayed with us the rest of the way, no escaping it.

In all this, took one wrong turn also, because it was dark and half my attention was on holding him up, half on the road, and we ended up one full lane extra, past some society where we don't even know anybody, before I realised arre this is wrong way, and turned back. Ten minutes wasted extra, and he not helping at all, just repeating where we going, where we going, every few steps like a stuck recording.

Water was the real fight. Found one paanwala still open, bought a bottle, tried to make him drink two-three sips. Complete refusal first, turning his face away like a stubborn kid, saying I don't want, I'm fine, leave me only. Had to practically hold the bottle to his mouth, saying come na, just two sips, it will help, trust me on this. Only after much convincing, small compromise, two sips down, and right after that came this senti moment, out of nowhere. He held my hand very tight, eyes all wet, and said, yaar, you know, you are the best, nobody else would even do this for me, I love you yaar, seriously, such filmy dialogue only, this kind of thing that only comes out when a person is this far gone, otherwise he is too shy usually, never says such things.

Somehow, eventually, we reached his society. And then the door, whole another lafda. Staircase light had gone that night also, some proper jugaad needed just to find the lock in the dark, and he is leaning his full weight against the door meanwhile, nearly toppling both of us, and my hand shaking from carrying so much weight so long, so the key would not go in straight. Finally after some struggle it opened, and I half-dragged half-walked him inside, straight to the couch, let him fall onto it. Took his shoes off at least, turned him on his side just in case, kept the water bottle right there on the side table.

Before finally going quiet, one more thing came out of him. Grabbed my hand again, very serious face suddenly, and said, I'm never drinking like this again, never, this is it, last time, promise. Said it with such conviction also, hand on chest and everything. But honestly, even while hearing it, in my mind I was only thinking, haan haan, we will see, this same promise I have heard before also no. These three-in-the-morning promises, they don't survive the morning sun usually.

Next morning, gave him one missed call first, then rang properly. Voice on the other side still half asleep, head clearly paining, and straight away, yaar what all happened, I don't remember anything properly. So I had to tell him the whole thing, bit by bit, the legs not holding at the table, the uncle scolding us on the road, the wrong turn, the water refused then finally taken, and of course the senti declaration of love and the big promise never to drink like that again. Two seconds silence on the phone, then complete embarrassment, I said what?! Some small pieces came back to him as I was telling, like oh yes, I do remember the paanwala, but most of it, genuinely blank, I had to fill it back for him, practically the whole night, piece by piece, and he kept saying no yaar seriously, I said that, after every second line. In the end we were just laughing about it, because what to do, this is the friend we have, this is not even the first time, and going by past record, definitely not going to be the last time either. Told him come over, I'll make one cutting chai, we'll talk properly. Ek number disaster of a night, but yaar, full paisa vasool of a story now, no?
