Two years I gave that company, from the day I joined as fresher only, sitting in that same chair near the window that never really opened, AC blowing directly on my neck the whole day. Two years is not small time, yaar. I saw three team leads come and go, I saw the office shift from Andheri to that new building where the lift used to hang for ten minutes minimum, everybody standing there doing timepass on phone, nobody saying anything, just standing. Even the guard downstairs, one uncle who used to salute everybody with just his eyes, he saw me go in fresh and come out tired, two years straight.

I am not one who decides fast. Whole one year I was only thinking, this is not it, this is not it, but what to do, rent is there, EMI is there, mother's medicine is there, so I kept adjusting, adjusting. Slowly slowly the adjusting itself became the job, you understand? The actual work I could do with left hand only, it was the adjusting that was taking everything out of me.

The final thing, if I am honest to myself, was so small anybody hearing it will say arre, for this only you left? Because it is true, for this only. One Friday evening, bag already on shoulder, about to leave, and the manager calls from his cabin, not even standing up, just voice projecting across the whole floor, "just five minute work is there, will you do." Five minute work became forty minute work, and my local I used to catch, the fast one from the station behind office, that one I missed. Next fast was not for twenty minute. Standing on that platform, sweating, seeing my train pull out empty-ish while I am standing there with the laptop bag digging into my shoulder, something in me just closed, like a shutter coming down. Small small thing. But it was sitting on top of two years of small small things, so it did not feel small that evening.

Whole weekend I was rehearsing what I will say. Not the real thing, obviously. The real thing was: you people don't value my time, you think because I don't say no, you can keep taking, taking, taking, and I am so senti about this whole job now that even thinking about Monday is giving me tension. That I could never say like that, na. So I made the polished version. I said it to the mirror, I said it to my flatmate over cutting chai in the evening, I said it in the auto also, quietly, lips moving, uncle driving must have thought this one has some lafda going on in the head.

Monday morning I went and asked the manager's PA, come na, just five minutes with sir, whenever he is free, no rush, very casual only I said it, like it is nothing, like maybe I am asking about tiffin timing only.

The PA: Come at eleven, sir is having client call before that, achha?

Eleven o'clock I went in. Closed the door behind me, which itself he noticed, eyebrow going up slightly. I sat down and I said my opening line, the one I had practiced so many times it did not even feel like my own voice anymore.

Me: Sir, I wanted to talk to you about my role here, going forward. It's not about any one thing in particular, I've just been having this feeling since some months now, thinking about a change.

That last part, "not about any one thing in particular", that was true and also completely not true, because it was about one thing, the Friday, the missed local, but I could not say that, because then it becomes about him personally, about that one evening, and I did not have the energy for that fight. Also, saying the real reason means admitting how small a thing it takes to break two years, and that felt like admitting weakness, right to his face. So I gave the bigger, vaguer thing instead, safer for both of us. That was my real reason for holding back, not politeness even, just not wanting to hand him that much of myself.

The manager was not very surprised, I could tell. He leaned back, did that thing with the pen, clicking it on and off, on and off.

The manager: Achha. Before you say more, let me ask, is this a money thing? Because if it is money, there is something we can work out, I was going to bring this up in next appraisal cycle only, but we can prepone it if needed.

Me: No sir, it's not really about money.

The manager: Because I can also talk to HR about your designation, maybe we change your title, give you more visibility on the client side, that side of things is missing for you, isn't it? That we can fix.

Me: I appreciate that sir, genuinely, but my mind is made up on this. It's just time for me to explore something else, that's all.

Polite reason, given very smoothly, like I had rehearsed it a hundred times, which I had. The true reason, that no title change, no extra five thousand, nothing was going to fix the feeling that this place had made a habit of taking my evenings, my Sundays sometimes also, and calling it dedication only, that I kept in my pocket, did not take out.

We settled on two weeks notice, standard only, nothing dramatic. He shook my hand, said all the best, said door is always open if you want to come back, we can always find something, chalo, no hard feelings.

That night, going home in the local, standing near the door where the wind comes, I got this doubt hitting me suddenly, like a slap. I am having this feeling now, what if I have done a mistake? Two years I built up there, everybody knew my work, new place I will be starting from zero again, proving myself again, and for what, for one bad Friday? My stomach was doing somersaults the whole ride home. I gave a missed call to my flatmate first because balance was low, she called back. I told her, yaar I think I have made a huge mistake, and she said, do one thing, sleep on it, in the morning it will look different. It did look a little different, but the doubt did not fully go, it just went quiet, sitting somewhere at the back.

Two weeks I worked like normal, handover documents, training the junior who was replacing me, all that. Last day came fast, faster than I expected. Small cake in the conference room, somebody had got vada pav also from the bhaiya's stall outside, everybody standing around eating, saying nice things. "We will miss you yaar." "Keep in touch, don't be a stranger." "Society mein bhi milte rehna", meaning stay in touch even outside work, which nobody actually does, everybody knows this, but everybody says it anyway, because what else to say.

Most of it was hollow, going through the motions, the way these things always go. But there was one moment, near the end, my workmate, the one who sat next to me for one full year, she pulled me aside near the water cooler and she just looked at me and said, "It was that Friday, no? The train thing." I did not say anything for one second, just looked at her, and she said, "I knew it, I was there when he called you back, I saw your face when you came back from the platform." I only said, "haan, bas, it was a lot of things," which was my way of saying yes without fully saying yes. That was the closest anybody came to the real story, and even that I did not confirm fully. Some things you keep for yourself only, na.

Now, months since I have left, I can say the relief is much bigger than any regret. First week I kept getting this urge to check office WhatsApp group at seven sharp, like a habit that would not off itself so easily. That has stopped now. I sleep better, my Sundays are actually mine, mother is happy I am home for dinner most days, not just tiffin left cold on the table. The new place has its own headaches, obviously, no job is fully bindaas, there is always some lafda somewhere, but at least here when the light has gone in the office and everybody is standing around doing timepass, it does not feel personal, it does not feel like they are taking something from me.

If you ask me now, would I do it again, same way. I would say yes to the leaving, hundred percent, but I would maybe say the real thing that day, not the polished version only. Because in the end he probably understood anyway, from my face, from the way I said it, everybody always understands more than what you actually say. What to do, that is how we are only, we dress it up nice and hope the other person reads between.
