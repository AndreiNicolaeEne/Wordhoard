Arre, listen na, let me tell you my idea of a perfect meal, only you will have to imagine, because you never had it, no?

It comes on a Sunday afternoon, that lazy time when the fan is going full and outside, down the lane, the chana wala bhaiya and the bhaji wala are shouting together. That is the mood for it, not any function, not any party, just an ordinary Sunday when nobody is rushing anywhere, and I am having this feeling since morning itself that today is going to be a good day.

The dish that stands out most is my mother's kombdi, chicken curry, done the ghar ka way, not hotel style. The gravy has to be that exact texture, thick-thick but still running, not dry like hotel gravy, and it has to reach you garam garam, straight off the stove, because two minutes later itself it is not the same thing at all.

Whatever is the best home food you have had there, this still beats that, no comparison, I am telling you straight, bas.

The smell reaches you first, before the plate even comes, the phodni, mustard and curry leaf hitting hot oil, that smell goes down the whole passage of our society, and the neighbours also come to know Sunday has arrived in our house.

We eat with hand only, everybody, rice mixed properly into the gravy, small small bites, no fork-work here. If achar is there, even better, though it is not needed really.

Normally it is shared among eight, nine people, whole family sitting on the floor around one big steel thali, one uncle from downstairs also joins sometimes, like that only.

The moment I always wait for is at the end, when the chicken is finished and only gravy is left, and my mother pours one more ladle just for the rice, and for two minutes everyone goes quiet, only sound is spoon on thali. That silence, that is the mast part, the paisa vasool part. What to do, I cannot explain it better than that.
