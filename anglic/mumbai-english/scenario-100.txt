Listen, what you are going through right now, I am understanding it fully, because I have also seen one such year, the worst year of my whole life only. Let me tell you properly, start to finish, so you know these things can be survived, na.

It started small small only, that is how these things start. My husband's company, the one where he was working since almost twelve years, they did some "restructuring," that is the fancy word they used, but what to do, in plain language it means they threw out half the staff, and he was one of them. One Monday morning he went for the meeting, by afternoon he was coming home with his files in a plastic bag, not saying anything, just sitting on the sofa, staring at the wall. I knew immediately, no need even to ask.

First the small comforts went, one by one. Cutting chai from the stall, we used to have two, three times a day, that became once, then only Sunday. Vada pav on the way from the station, gone completely. Non-veg we stopped, simple veg only, dal-chawal, that too small small quantity. No more hotel for dosa on Sunday mornings, that whole habit finished. Cable connection we cut off, the TV was just showing black screen after that, my mother-in-law was so upset, she is very filmy type, likes her serials, but what to do, money was not there. Auto we stopped taking altogether, only local, and even then we would walk that extra ten minutes from the station to save one more auto fare. My elder one was in eighth standard that year and I told her, this year skip the extra tuition, some help I will give at home itself, don't worry about your marks, just give the exam properly, that's all that matters.

Then it went to the bigger things. My gold bangles, the ones from my wedding, I took to the pawn shop myself, nobody else knew, I did not even tell my husband that day, I just came home and said I am not feeling like wearing today, that's all, cut the conversation short. Our LIC policy we had to break before maturity, lost money on that also, but money was not there, what to do. The landlord even preponed the rent date that month, said company policy changed, some faltu excuse only, and we had to somehow adjust.

The most difficult part for me was asking for help. I have never in my life borrowed from anybody, always been the one lending small small amounts to others. But that time, close to two months' maintenance was pending in the society, and I had to go to one uncle in our building, my husband's old college friend only, and ask him. Standing at his door, not even wanting to sit inside and have tea, just wanting to say the thing and go.

Me: Uncle, I am not liking to ask this at all, but we are stuck badly, is it possible...

Uncle: Arre, stop, no need to explain so much. Tell me how much you are needing, that's it.

That's all he said, didn't even let me finish. Gave without one more question. God bless that man, but even now when I think of that day I am feeling that same shame in my stomach, that helpless feeling of standing there with nothing to offer back.

Almost six months, maybe little more, there was hardly any work. My husband would go out every morning itself, dressed properly, files in hand, sit near the phone since morning some days waiting for some call that was never coming. Some days he would get one interview, come home hopeful, then nothing after, not even a missed call back. I started doing tailoring from home, blouses and falls, whatever ladies in the building would give, and I also started making extra tiffin for one bai's family in the next building, small small jobs, hardly any money in any of it, but at least something was coming in.

The worst day, I will remember it my whole life, was when the light had gone in our flat because the bill was pending since two months, and same evening my younger one had high fever, and I was standing in the dark with the child in my arms, and I did not even have enough for the chemist downstairs. That night, after the children slept, I sat on the steps outside our door and cried properly, first time in the whole business I let myself get that senti.

But some luck was there also, small small. One neighbour, didi from the third floor actually, she came to know somehow, came down with some old clothes for the kids and quietly also gave me an envelope.

The neighbour: Keep it. Don't say anything to anybody, not even to your husband if you don't want. Just for the child's medicine.

She went off before I could even refuse properly. That kept us going a few more weeks, that one envelope.

Then, bit by bit, work started coming. My husband's old colleague, one contact from before only, told him about a small trading office needing accounts help, part time first, three days a week. Small salary, but something is better than nothing, na. That call felt like ek number news that day, I am telling you, after so many months of silence. Slowly, over almost eight months, it built up, three days became full time, then a proper position again, my tailoring work also picked up, one lady recommending me to another lady, word spreading bit by bit. It was not fast, every month felt long, but the black cloud was lifting.

What I learned in all this, the people you expect to help, close relatives, own blood even, they will find some excuse, will say we are also going through tough time only, and disappear. One distant relative even came with only advice, no money, just gyaan, very pakau that visit was. But people you never expected, that neighbour, my husband's old friend, even the milk bhaiya who let us run on credit for two months without one word of complaint, these are the ones who actually stood. I don't take anybody at face value now, I watch who shows up when there is real trouble, that is the only test that matters.

And about myself, I learned I am stronger than I ever thought. That I can do things I never imagined doing, standing in queue for hours, doing work I used to think was beneath me, asking for help without dying of shame each time. It changes you permanently, that kind of year, in ways that don't go away even after the money comes back.

The day it started feeling survivable, I remember it exactly, it was when I could go to the chai stall again and order two cutting chai without doing calculation in my head first. Small small thing only, but that day felt paisa vasool for everything we had been through. I told my husband, I think we are coming out of it now, and he just nodded, didn't say much, but I could see something loosen in his shoulders that had been tight for close to a year.

Now, coming to you. I know what you are going through right now, this year of yours, I am not blind to it, I can see it in your face every time we talk, even when you are saying everything is fine only, chalo bas, don't worry about me. I am not going to tell you it will pass quickly, because it will not, and I am not going to tell you not to worry, because what to do, worry will come anyway. But I am telling you this, whatever you need, small or big, you come to me, no hesitation, no shame, that udhaar business I understand better than anybody now. Do one thing, whenever it gets too much, you just call me, don't sit alone with it, don't even think twice. My time is there for you, my ear is there for you, any hour, you give me one missed call and I will call you back immediately. Come na, sit with us for dinner some evening, don't keep saying no, no. You are not alone in this one, that much I am promising you.
