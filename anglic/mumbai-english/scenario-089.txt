Arre yaar, you missed the whole thing na, so let me tell you properly from the start, no shortcuts, because otherwise you will just say I am exaggerating like always.

So this cycle business. Everybody in the society, from the smallest one in fourth standard to the watchman's boy, is riding since years, only I never learned, and I don't know why, just kept postponing postponing, one thing or other always came in between. Then last year I thought, bas, enough, this year I am doing it, whatever happens. My workmate said are you mad, at this age, people will laugh, and I said let them laugh, what to do, better late than never only.

First lesson itself was hopeless yaar, total disaster. The instructor is this old fellow near the maidan, does this timepass job in the evenings for the society kids, teaches for years and years. He put me on the cycle, held the back, and the moment he left his hand I was going straight into the gutter side. My legs were doing something, my hands were doing something else completely, nothing was talking to each other. He is very patient man, but blunt also, no sugar-coating type. He told me straight, "at your age it will take longer, your body has forgotten how to learn new-new things, small children pick up like that only, for you it will be slow." I said okay fine, I am ready, but inside I was feeling very senti about it, not going to lie.

And then within days itself, small small children, half my age, some even less, were zooming past me on the same ground where I was still wobbling like a fool. One boy, maybe eighth standard only, came and asked me, uncle why are you holding the handle so tight, and I wanted to say beta go do your homework, but I just smiled and said learning learning.

That whole first week my body was aching in places I forgot I even had. Every small muscle was making its presence known, every time I sat down, stood up, even while sleeping I would turn and something would shout. My mother was seeing me limping around the house and just laughing, saying serves you right, at your age doing all this. I said ha ha very funny, but the legs were really not in a joking mood.

Then came the fall, the big one, the one I still remember clear clear. I was getting slightly overconfident, thought arre I am getting the hang of it now, and the front wheel caught in a small stone or something, and next thing I know I am flat on the ground, cycle on top of me, breath completely knocked out. For one whole minute I could not even make sound, just lying there looking at the sky, thinking this is it, I am done with this whole nonsense, why did I even start. Some small crowd had gathered by then, some kids, some society uncles doing their evening walk, everyone rushing.

Instructor: are you there, are you there, say something na
Me: (still no breath, just making some pathetic sound)
An uncle from the society: uff, at this age, why are you doing all this drama, better sit at home only

I told the instructor right there, bas, I am not coming back, this is not for me, too much for one middle-aged fellow's dignity. He didn't argue much that day, just said okay okay, rest first. But my workmate, when I told her the whole story next day at office, over cutting chai, she just said one thing.

My workmate: come na, one more time, just once, then decide whatever you want

I don't know why but that "come na" worked on me, softened the whole thing, so I went back one more time. And then somehow I kept going back, day after day.

The progress after that, honestly, was so slow it was almost funny. Weeks would go by for one small thing to get right, like just balancing without the instructor's hand on the seat, and meanwhile the same small crowd would be sitting on the compound bench, watching, some clapping when I did something right, some clearly enjoying my misery, especially the kids, who would giggle every time I wobbled. One aunty from the third floor used to come specially at that time, sit with her tiffin, watching me like it was some evening serial, very filmy the whole scene was.

What kept me going, I realised later, was I stopped comparing myself to those fast fast learners, the kids who picked it up in one afternoon, ek number types. I started comparing only to myself, one month back. Last month I could not even sit straight on the seat, this month I am pedaling ten feet without falling, that kind of thing only. That comparison is the only one that ever helped, the other one was just faltu, made me feel chindi about the whole effort.

Then, somewhere around the fourth month, something clicked. I cannot explain in words properly what happened, it was not in my head, it was in the body itself, like the body suddenly understood something the mind was trying to tell it for months. One evening I was riding and I realised, arre, I am not thinking about it anymore, my hands are not gripping so tight, my legs are just doing it, on their own. That day I did almost two rounds of the maidan without getting down even once.

Instructor: see, I told you no, it takes time only, but it comes
Me: mast feeling yaar, I don't know how to explain

I never did catch up to those small kids, they were still faster, still doing all sorts of tricks, no hands and all, showing off shamelessly. But by the end I made my peace with that completely. I was not there to become the fastest, I was there to just do the thing I never could, and now I could do it without even thinking, the same thing that felt impossible on day one.

Looking back now, whole season sits very fondly in my memory, not sourly at all, even the fall, even the aching legs, even the aunty watching my misery with her tiffin like a movie, even the boy calling me uncle in that innocent-cruel way children have. All of it folded into something I am actually glad I did, proud of it now, tell people about it like some big achievement, though it was just learning what four year olds learn like that only. But for me it was one whole season of humiliation turned into something jhakaas by the end, and honestly, paisa vasool, every bit of it, the falls included.
