The younger one: Aunty, that story you always tell na, give properly today, start to finish, no cutting short halfway.

Me: Okay okay, sit, sit, don't be in that hurry. This is not one you rush, understand. Every time it comes up like this only, some evening when the current has gone and everybody is sitting out on the steps with cutting chai for timepass, and one new person, maybe just shifted into the society, is asking why the older ones always say don't go by that bend near the nullah bridge after dark. Then somebody nudges somebody, and it starts, chalo, like that.

First thing you must understand, nobody has ever seen it properly, not one person in this whole area, in all these years. It comes as a feeling first, hair standing on the arm, and only then, after that, it comes as a voice. Never a shape first, not even a shadow. Voice first, and shape maybe never at all. That is how it has always been told, from my grandmother's time itself.

And what it does, that is the worst part only. It calls you in a voice exactly like somebody you love. Your mother, your husband, your own child, whoever will pull you fastest. Not to frighten you there and finish, no, that is not the purpose. It is calling to walk you off the path, little by little, away from the road, away from anybody who could come running if you screamed. Taking you where no help is there, that is the whole game of it.

And it only comes after midnight, never one minute before, and only on amavasya night, no moon at all, black sky, not even one star helping you find the ground under your own feet. Any other night you can walk that bend all you want, nothing will call you, haan, I have walked it myself plenty times on ordinary nights. Only that one dark night in the month, after twelve, that is when.

The spot itself, don't be thinking some big jungle story, it is nothing grand. Just that one bend past the nullah bridge, before our society gate, where the road curves and the streetlight has been fused since I don't know how long, no light is there at all and nobody comes to fix it. That is the whole place. Not the whole area, not the whole suburb, just that one bend. People from other lanes have their own such spot too, I have heard, everybody's got one near them, but ours is ours only, and nobody here wants any lafda with it, so nobody goes past there alone after that hour, simple as that.

I heard it first from my own grandmother, when I was small small, maybe eighth standard only, sitting on her lap on an evening just like this one, light gone, current not there. She told it plain, like she was telling me the milk price had gone up, no drama in her voice at all. But one thing, when she reached the end she would laugh, ha ha, quick like that, and her mouth would laugh but the eyes, they never came along with it. Even as a child I noticed, the eyes were somewhere else entirely.

First proof she always gave, and I have seen it with my own eyes so I am not just repeating like that, is the patch of ground right at that bend, small small patch, nothing has ever grown there. Grass tried, weeds tried, even the corporation planted one gulmohar sapling there some years back, thinking to fix the whole dark corner with a tree and a new light together. Sapling dried up in one month flat, and the light, that fused within the week itself, and nobody has bothered since.

Second proof is the kind you always get with these things, somebody heard from somebody who heard from somebody else, na. Some watchman two lanes down, bindaas type fellow, not scared of anything usually, was cutting through there one night on work, and heard his own name called clear as anything from the mangrove side, and he ran so fast he lost one chappal and took an auto home without even going back for it. That story, everybody has some version of, cousin of a cousin, always one person removed from you.

But the one they tell in full, every single time, no skipping, is about my own aunt, my mother's younger sister. This is maybe thirty, thirty five years back now. She was coming back from a wedding in some hotel two stations down, had missed the last local and come the rest of the way walking with one neighbour who turned off before the bend, so she was alone by the time she reached it. And she heard her name, called out from off the path, in her own mother's voice, my grandmother's voice, calling her to come this side. And she went, she actually went, three, four steps off the road toward the dark, before something, she cannot even say what, made her stop and turn back to the road.

She tells it the same way every single time, not one word changing, all these years: it called me in my mother's own voice, saying come, come this side, and my legs were already walking before my mind even understood what was happening.

The detail that makes even the ones who don't believe go quiet for one second is this, my grandmother was at home the whole time that evening, everybody in the house can say so, she never once left that night. So whose voice was my aunt hearing then, if not my grandmother's, sitting safe at home the whole time.

But the detail that gives the doubters their opening, and bas, they take it every time without fail, is that it was a wedding, and there was drink there, and my aunt is the first to admit she had two three pegs that evening, nothing more, but enough for people to say, arre, what to do, in that state only who will believe what she heard. Some faltu excuse or other they always find. My own nephew, the one doing his engineering, calls the whole thing too filmy to be true, laughs before I even finish telling it.

I am not going to pretend the story stays the same in every mouth. From some it comes soft, almost like a lullaby, from others it comes hard, like a warning with a slap behind it. My own telling changes little bit depending on who is sitting in front of me, softer for someone your age, harder if it's some smart type laughing before the story is even done. I have never minded that difference. Story is like that only, it bends to the mouth carrying it, and underneath it is still the same story.

But this one thing has stayed same in our family, no matter who is telling it. If somebody calls your name from somewhere dark, from off any path, you do not answer, not one word, not even to ask who is it, until you have looked with your own two eyes and seen who is actually calling you. Only then you open your mouth. That much, everybody here still does, without even thinking about it twice anymore.

And you can ask me straight, I will tell you straight, I don't fully believe it, not in that complete way, not like I believe the sun will come up tomorrow. But in all my years, not one single time, not once, have I answered a call from the dark before I have seen the face that comes with it. Not one time.
