That evening the light had gone just after dusk itself, no warning, no notice from the society committee, nothing. Father was on call with the relative that time, had to cut the call fast, battery already low, saying will give a missed call once current is back. One second the fan is going nicely, next second bas, whole flat dark.

Father: Whole lane is dark only, some transformer problem they are saying, not our fuse, so no use checking anything from our side.

What to do, whole house went into scramble mode, everybody feeling here and there for the matchbox.

Mother: It was there in the kitchen drawer only na, who has moved it, achha wait, found it.

Inside, hardly two-three matchsticks left, and one candle stub, half burnt from last power cut. New candle is not there in the house, torch cell is there but no bulb fitted in it since months, useless. So that one chindi-looking stub had to give paisa vasool service for the whole household that night.

The eldest, eighth standard, banged into the same stool near the fridge, not once, two-three times, each time going arre and everybody laughing even with the tension in the air.

The eldest: How to see anything in this, what to do, I am giving exam tomorrow itself and syllabus not even finished.

We put the candle on a saucer in the middle and everybody gathered close, like some small puja only. One uncle from downstairs did some jugaad with his phone torch and came up to check on us.

Uncle: Do one thing, on the mobile torch, save the candle for later, no?

Grandmother: Come na, sit close, don't waste it standing there.

Mother tried the switch two-three times out of habit, on-off, on-off, knowing well it is useless. After some time everybody gave up fixing anything that night, chalo, will see in the morning only. So we sat, small small talk, filmy gossip, a little senti talk about old power cuts from before, waiting.

Around eleven, still no light, so haan, one by one everybody went off to sleep in the dark itself, fan not even moving, just like that. Morning it was back on its own, nobody ever found what the actual lafda was.
