Arre what a storm that was, yaar, even now when I am telling it my hands are getting cold only. That evening the sky had gone that funny colour, na, not black-black, more like greenish yellow, and everything outside had gone quiet-quiet, no bird sound, no dog barking, nothing. My mother came to the balcony and she is saying,

The mother: Something is not right today. Look at the sky, how still it is.

And I am like, achha, monsoon time only, will pass, don't tension. But within one hour the wind is picking up so fast that I am rushing down to bring in the clothes from the line, the flower pots, the plastic chairs on the society terrace, whatever is there outside I am running to get inside. My husband is holding the grill gate because it was banging banging on its own, and I am shouting to the eldest,

Me: Come na, help me quick, tie that rope on the gate before it flies off only!

The eldest: Coming, coming, wait!

By the time we finished tying and closing everything the wind was really rising, whoosh whoosh sound like something angry, and I called everybody inside, the mother, the husband, the children, even the domestic help who was staying that day because she could not go home in that weather, I told her, stay here itself only, no need to go now. We all sat together in the passage, away from the windows, small small torch and candles ready because I had the feeling the light is going to go, and true only, within ten minutes current gone. Full darkness, and the wind sound so loud that we literally stopped talking, nobody was saying anything, we were just sitting there holding hands, the younger one pressed against me tight tight.

Then we heard this massive crack sound, like something splitting, and then a huge thud, so close that the whole building shook a little bit. My heart just stopped for two seconds, I am telling you. My husband is shouting,

The husband: That's a tree only, must have fallen, don't go near the window!

Then something is banging on the roof, really banging, like someone is pulling the sheets with force, and next thing we know there is this ripping sound and half the tin shed portion over the back room is just gone, flown off like paper, rain coming straight in, wetting everything, the mother's puja things, everything. We are all shifting to the front room in the dark, tripping over each other, and just then one of the windows in that room, we heard crack, glass has cracked corner to corner, but by God's grace it held, it did not burst fully, otherwise God knows what would have happened with glass flying everywhere with small children there.

That was the worst part honestly, when the window cracked. I was holding the younger one so tight, and inside my mind I was thinking this is it, roof is gone, window is going, what next, but outside I did not say one word of that, I was only saying, don't worry, don't worry, it will pass, everything will be fine. Only after, days after, I told my workmate the truth.

Me: I am telling you, that time I was so scared inside, I thought the whole roof is coming down on us only. But what to do, in front of children I cannot show all that, na.

Her workmate: Arre baba, that is so senti even hearing it now, my hands are getting goosebumps.

So we sat like that, I don't know, maybe one and half hour, two hours, wind howling, rain like someone throwing buckets, that ripped roof flapping somewhere making horrible sound whole time, tree branches hitting against the building side, and us just sitting close together in the dark saying prayers, achha bhagwan, bas ab, enough now. And then, all of a sudden, just like that, the wind dropped. Like someone switched it off. From that mad noise to complete silence in maybe fifteen minutes. That silence was almost more scary than the noise, you know, that kind of ringing quiet after.

We waited some more time, nobody was moving, then my husband took the torch and went to check, and slowly slowly we all came out to the balcony. What a sight, yaar. Right at the gate of our society, one big gulmohar tree, fully down, blocking the entire path, nobody could go out or come in until that was cleared, two three days it took the BMC people to send someone with the cutting machine, meantime everybody was climbing over branches only to go to the main road for milk, vegetables, whatever.

Our roof, that back portion, completely gone, tin sheets nowhere to be found, must have flown God knows where, and the room underneath, everything soaked, mother's cupboard, old photos, all wet. That needed real work after, proper mestri, proper waterproofing, not some jugaad patch job, we knew that from day one itself. The cracked window we just taped up for the time being till the glass wallah could come and fit new one.

And then I am looking at the neighbour's side, their fence, that nice compound fence they had put up just some months back, completely flat, gone, lying on the ground like it was never there. I felt bad for them of course, but seeing that, honestly, I am thinking, at least it is only a fence for them, look at us, whole roof gone, tree blocking our gate, window cracked, what to compare even. That is when it really hit me how much worse ours was.

Next morning, no light, no water pump running because no current, mobile network also gone, everybody in the society standing outside comparing notes, this uncle from second floor lost his whole grill covering, that aunty's plants all destroyed, one bhaiya's two-wheeler got a branch fall right on it, fully damaged. For two three days it was like that only, no light has gone and not coming, we were doing everything by candle, cooking on the small gas stove carefully, tiffin arrangements gone haywire, kids missed school because how to send them with tree blocking and no transport, the local trains were not running either that side, such a mess only.

Cleanup took almost one week properly, mestri people coming, roof people coming, everybody in the building pooling money for the society gate repair also since the boundary wall had some damage too. Bit by bit things came back, light came back on the third day, water next day after, and slowly slowly normal life resumed, back to running for the local, back to cutting chai in the morning, back to same same routine.

But even now when I tell this story, I cannot tell it calm calm, I don't know why, every time I reach that part about the tree sound, the window cracking, my voice just goes up, arre baap re, that sound, I am telling you! And my mother, whenever this story comes up at some gathering, she will say, don't even remind me of that night, I still get shivers only thinking of it. Filmy type experience honestly, but living through it was no timepass at all, I tell you, it was the real thing.
