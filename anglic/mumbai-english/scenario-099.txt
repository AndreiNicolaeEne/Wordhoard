Me: Arre, is it you only?! I was just getting down from the auto and I thought no, cannot be, but it is you only!

The classmate: Yaar, I was thinking the same thing! You have not changed at all, same face, only some weight has come on. What are you doing here, this side is not your area no?

Me: Came for some society work, our building's redevelopment papers, some uncle is handling it, don't ask, full lafda going on since two months. And you, still in the same office?

The classmate: Haan, same company only, just branch has changed. Come, let's have one cutting chai there, that stall is still standing, can you believe. Since school time it is there only.

Me: Ha, true true. Chalo, five minutes I am having, then I have to go, meeting is there.

The classmate: Bas, one chai, what to do, we are meeting after so many years, at least five minutes give me.

Me: Okay okay, come na, don't make it senti now itself, we just met.

The classmate: (laughing) Achha achha. So tell me, you still go to that same school side ever? I passed from there last month, by chance only, the auto took that route.

Me: No yaar, not since ages. But you said, and now I am remembering everything. Tenth standard, that building, third floor, corner class.

The classmate: Don't remind me of tenth standard, my hands are becoming cold just now. That maths teacher, remember?

Me: Oh god. How can I forget. Whole school was scared of that one, not just our class.

The classmate: The rule was so simple only, that is the funny part. No talking once the sir has started writing on the board. That's it. One word also, and you are finished.

Me: One word! Even if you are asking your bench partner for a pencil, doesn't matter, sir will turn around like radar is fitted in the back of the head.

The classmate: And the punishment, don't even say. Same line, hundred times, in the fair notebook, not rough, fair notebook only, otherwise it doesn't count.

Me: "I will not talk in class when the teacher is writing on the board." Hundred times. I am telling you I can still write that line in my sleep, it is in my hand now itself, my hand remembers it more than my brain.

The classmate: Exactly! And checking also very strictly, if even one line is looking hurried or the handwriting is different from other ninety-nine, redo the whole thing, no mercy.

Me: No mercy at all. Remember what he used to say, that same dialogue, every single time, word for word? "Whatever is the reason, the reason is not acceptable in my class."

The classmate: (laughing loudly) Yes yes yes! That exact line! Whatever is the reason, the reason is not acceptable! Even if the building was on fire I think he would say the same thing.

Me: Full building on fire, and he will say, reason is not acceptable, take out your notebook.

The classmate: Ha ha, stop it, my stomach is paining now. But you remember what happened to that boy, back bench, that day, when he forgot his homework diary at home?

Me: Which incident, the diary one?

The classmate: The diary one only. Whole class was watching, dead silence, and sir made him stand on the bench, in front of everyone, for the entire period. One full period, standing there, not saying one word, just standing.

Me: I remember, yaar. Whole class was looking down at the notebook, nobody even breathing loudly, everybody thinking, please don't call my name next.

The classmate: I am telling you honestly, even now, this many years later, if someone suddenly says "stand up," my legs go weak for one second. That fear has entered somewhere deep, it is still there.

Me: Same here, I am not joking. Sometimes in office also, if boss suddenly says "come here for a minute," something in my stomach does that same drop, like tenth standard is happening again.

The classmate: (laughing) See, this is what that teacher has done to us permanently. But tell me, you remember that one incident, the girl who talked back to him?

Me: Which one, don't tell me there was a girl who actually talked back to that man and survived.

The classmate: I am telling you, I saw it with my own eyes, otherwise I also would not believe. Sir was scolding her for something, some small thing, homework was one day late only, and she just said, straight to his face, "Sir, you also sometimes come late to class, then what?"

Me: No way. No way, yaar. What happened then, tell fast.

The classmate: Whole class stopped breathing. I am telling you, pin drop silence, like even the fan stopped. And sir just looked at her for maybe ten seconds, full ten seconds, and then he just said, "Sit down," and continued the lesson. That's it. Nothing else.

Me: Nothing else?! No hundred lines, no standing on bench, nothing?

The classmate: Nothing! To this day I don't understand how she got away with that. We used to talk about it for weeks after, like it was some legend only, some story from another world.

Me: True hero that girl was, honestly. We were all too scared even to ask him for extra time for the exam, and she is telling him straight up he comes late.

The classmate: (still laughing) Bindaas she was, full bindaas. But you remember his habit, that thing he used to do with the chalk, that tic, tapping tapping on the table before starting any topic?

Me: Oh my god, yes! Tap tap tap tap, exactly four times, every single time, before he opens his mouth. We used to count it in the back bench, betting on it, will it be four today or will he break the pattern.

The classmate: It was never five, never three, always four. We used to imitate him after school, tap tap tap tap, then "Now. Open your books." Full class doing his voice, his walk also, that stiff walk.

Me: Behind his back only, in front we were like statues, not even blinking.

The classmate: But one thing I still remember, one time he was actually kind, and it shocked all of us more than the strictness even. That boy, the one whose father had passed, remember, he came to school two days after the funeral itself.

Me: Oh, I am remembering now, faintly.

The classmate: Sir didn't say one word to him about homework, about anything, just came, put a hand on his shoulder for two seconds, said "Take your time," and moved on. Never mentioned it again, not even once, whole year.

Me: I did not know that. See, this is what makes it complicated no. If he was fully bad only, it would be simple. But that one moment.

The classmate: Exactly. That is why, looking back now, whatever fear it was, it has turned into something else only. More like respect I think, honestly.

Me: I feel the same. At the time we all hated him, secretly of course, but now when I think of that man, I don't feel hate, I feel like, he made us disciplined, whatever the method was.

The classmate: True, true. By the way, do you know what happened to him after, any news? Is he still there or what.

Me: I heard he retired, some years back, and went to his native place, somewhere Konkan side I think. But this is all hearsay only, nobody knows for certain.

The classmate: Somebody told me he is still around, seen at some temple near the old school, doing some puja every morning, very old now. But again, who knows, could be some other retired teacher only, people mix up these things.

Me: Whatever it is, if I ever see him, I don't think I will be scared anymore, I think I will just want to say thank you, seriously.

The classmate: Ha, same here. Though I am telling you, even now if someone says his name in front of me, something small still moves inside, like old habit only.

Me: (laughing) Same, exactly same. Full grown man now, and still one flicker of that old fear is there.

The classmate: Chalo, leave all that, tell me, what is happening otherwise, life is treating you how? Married, kids, all that?

Me: Married, one kid, second standard now itself, can you believe, time flies like anything. And you, still bachelor life or settled down?

The classmate: Settled, two years now. Shifted to a new society, far side, but rent is manageable, what to do, this city is like that only, everything is costly now.

Me: True, true, everything has become costly. Even this chai, remember how much it was in our time, now see the rate.

The classmate: Don't even start on rates yaar, whole conversation will become senti. Better let's exchange numbers properly this time, don't just say "will call" and vanish for ten years again.

Me: Ha ha, okay okay, give me your number, I will give you missed call right now itself, save it.

The classmate: Done. And listen, next time, proper meeting only, not just five minutes at chai stall, deal?

Me: Deal, pakka. Chalo, my meeting time is coming, I have to run now, but this was so good, seriously, made my whole day.

The classmate: Mine also. Take care yaar, and don't disappear again for ten years, okay?

Me: Not this time, promise. See you soon.
