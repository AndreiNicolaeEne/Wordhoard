Every month the mother keeps some cash aside in the steel almirah, in the small tin behind the god photos, for milk and tiffin and all these small small expenses. Today she opens it and it is looking light only. She counts once, twice, even holds it up to the window like the notes will multiply if she stares long enough. Nothing. She checks the other place also, inside the old Complan tin in the kitchen shelf, just in case someone has shifted it, but it is not there either.

She starts asking, mild-mild first.

Mother: Nobody has touched the tin na? Money is short only, maybe two three hundred.

Father: I have not even opened it, yaar, since when I would touch your tin.

The elder one: Achha bas, don't look at me, I was at college whole day only.

The younger one: I also don't know, Mummy, what to do, I was not even at home.

Mother: Wait wait. You were not at home? Then who was alone in the house that day, tell me na, think properly.

The younger one goes quiet for one second, then flat, no hesitation.

The younger one: I said no I don't know. Why you are asking me only, again and again?

Mother: Because you were the one at home that day! Bunk the lecture and sitting at home, and money also goes missing same day, is it coincidence?

The younger one: So whatever happens in this house, it is me only, is it! I have not touched your paisa, how many times I say!

Mother: Then where has it gone, tell me, it does not walk on its own legs!

The younger one: I don't know na, ask the almirah, ask the tin, why you are shouting on me for something I have not even seen!

Mother: Because nobody else was there, that is why! Straight face and lying to my face also!

The younger one: I am not lying! Believe whatever you want, bas, I am going.

Nobody backs down. The tin sits there, still light.
