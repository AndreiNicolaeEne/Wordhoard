Arre, don't even ask, ya. Whenever this topic comes, I only start laughing, but that day, I promise, nobody was laughing, nobody. You want to hear the whole thing properly, start to end? Okay, sit, sit, I'll tell.

So this was for my cousin's wedding, native place side, and we had planned it so well, so well, months in advance only. Tickets booked, ST bus confirmed, my friend and I had even preponed our leave from office by two days extra just to be relaxed and not rushing rushing at the last moment. I am telling you, on paper it was one fully sorted trip. Everything mast, everything jhakaas, this uncle that uncle also saying enjoy enjoy, safe trip.

First hint should have come right there at the depot only. Bus was supposed to leave sharp six, we reach five-thirty itself, bags ready, tiffin packed, cutting chai in hand, feeling very number one about our planning. And then, nothing. No bus. We are standing, standing, asking this conductor that conductor, "bhaiya, bus kahan hai," and everybody giving different answer. One fellow says driver is not there, one says some paper work, arre what paperwork at six in the morning, ya. Bus came finally at quarter to eight. Two hours gone before we even left Mumbai, and I am telling my friend, this is a sign only, let's see how the day goes, come na, don't be so senti about it, we'll manage.

Manage what, I ask you. Barely three hours into the ghat, engine starts making one sound, like it is coughing, coughing, and then, dead. Bus just stopped, side of the road, everybody looking out the window like something is going to happen from looking only. Driver gets down, opens the bonnet, stares at the engine for full twenty minutes like he is reading a newspaper inside there. By then people are getting down also, na, what else to do, sitting inside is worse. Slowly slowly a whole crowd built up on that road side, our bus people, some other vehicle's passengers who also stopped to see the tamasha, chai wallah appeared from nowhere with his kettle, that man made more business in one hour than whole week I think.

We were sitting there since three hours, easy, maybe more. Nobody, and I mean nobody, could tell us when it will move. Conductor is saying "bas madam, mechanic is coming," this driver is saying "spare part ka jugaad kar rahe hain," some passenger is on phone shouting at the depot people, and depot people probably also don't know. What to do, everybody just standing standing, small small groups forming, some sitting on their bags, one aunty distributing extra puri bhaji to nearby children because otherwise it will spoil only.

And then, as if this was not enough, sky turned. Just like that, dark clouds came from nowhere and started pouring. No shed, no shelter, proper ghat road, and we are running with our bags to stand under one tree which itself is not doing much good, water coming through the leaves also. That is the moment my friend says, "yaar, I'm going to see if that dhaba has some cover," and goes off in that rain and crowd, and within minutes I lost sight completely. I am calling, calling, no answer, phone network also gone in that ghat area, and for a solid twenty minutes I did not know where my own travel companion is, in that crowd, in that rain, on that road. My heart was properly in my mouth, I tell you, all sorts of filmy thoughts coming to mind for no reason.

Then this one uncle, complete stranger, sees me standing alone looking lost, and comes and says, "beta, don't worry so much, dhaba is just there past the bend, everyone is going that side only, your friend will also be there." He even walked me halfway, holding his umbrella over both of us, not even his problem, no obligation, nothing, just helped like that. I am telling you, small small thing, but I still remember that uncle's face till today, whoever he is, wherever he is. That kindness I have not forgotten.

Found my friend at the dhaba, safe, drenched, but safe, and we both just stood there hugging like we won a match or something, then started laughing at ourselves for being so dramatic. But money problem also started from there, dhaba wallah is charging double for shelter and chai in that rain, "supply and demand" he is calling it with a straight face, and by the time we settled everything, extra fifty gone just like that, which was never in any plan, not even in the loosest planning.

Bus was declared "not fixable today itself," so now the real lafda started, what to do next. This is where my friend and I had one proper argument, loud one, in front of that whole stranded crowd.

Me: We should just wait na, some other bus will definitely come from this route, why to take tension.

My friend: Wait for what, it's already evening, wedding function starts tomorrow morning, you want to reach at the mandap in this same wet clothes or what.

Me: So what's your genius plan, arre, just walk into the jungle?

My friend: There's a jeep stand little ahead, shared jeep people go that side only, at least let's try, better than standing here like statues.

Me: Fine, fine, chalo, but if this goes wrong also, don't blame me later.

My friend: Bas, come na, stop the drama, let's just go.

So we went, found a shared jeep, driver swearing he knows a shortcut that will save time. Shortcut, my foot. That "shortcut" cost us two extra hours, ya, because this driver took one wrong turn somewhere in the dark, kept saying "bas do minute, bas do minute," and we ended up on some completely different road, asking farmers with torch light where we are. By the time we got back on the main route, everybody in that jeep was quiet, tired, just staring out at nothing. That was the lowest point for me, I won't lie, sitting there at midnight, soaked, hungry, legs cramping, not even the energy to be angry anymore, just this heavy heavy tiredness sitting on the chest.

Then, small stroke of luck, finally. Near the last junction, another ST bus, half empty, going the exact direction we needed, stopped for fuel right where our jeep also stopped. Conductor, decent fellow, saw our state and just waved us in, no extra fuss, no extra fare even, "baitho baitho, adjust ho jayega."

We reached native place little past four in the morning. Journey that was supposed to finish by seven previous evening. Nine hours late, easy nine hours, maybe more if you count from the very first depot delay. Both of us looked like we had walked through a war, hair everywhere, clothes still damp, eyes red red from no sleep, and the first aunty who opened the door just took one look and said "arre bhagwan, what happened to you two," and honestly what to say, where to even start.

But you know, sitting here now, all I can do is laugh, na. That whole thing, mad as it was, has become our number one story at every family function since. Everybody wants to hear it again and again, and every time I tell it, something new small small detail comes out. Worst journey of my life, hundred percent, but somehow, telling it like this, laughing laughing, it feels almost paisa vasool.
