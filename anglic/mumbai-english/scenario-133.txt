So this year I took the garden plot behind our society, na, the one near the water tank, and let me tell you the whole thing start to finish because it was a proper saga only, not some timepass hobby I did for two weeks and left.

Early spring itself I was confusing what to plant, tomato or bhindi or what, my husband was saying do tomato only, low maintenance, but the watchman's wife who has been doing this plot business since years, she said, "Do one thing, mix it, don't put all in one basket, na." So I did small small beds, tomato, bhindi, one methi patch, and coriander just for timepass because coriander anyway grows itself. I put everything in maybe three weeks after the last cold went, my mother was saying too early too early, cold is still there, but I said bas now, enough waiting, and thank god the weather was very good that time, sun-sun daily, no rain nuisance, everything came up jhakaas, seedlings standing straight like they are also excited only.

Then this thinning worm or whatever came on my bhindi, small green fellow, eating the leaves like nobody's business. I asked the fellow at the nursery, he gave some neem spray, said spray every three days it will go. It only partly worked, yaar, some leaves saved, some gone, chindi damage but still there was damage. I was so senti about it in the beginning, like this is my first serious crop and already lafda started, but then I told myself, arre, this is normal only, every grower gets this, what to do.

Then came the dry patch. No rain for weeks, tank level also dropping, and I was going morning-evening both times with buckets, hauling water by hand daily, my hands were paining, my back was paining, but plants need water, no? Cannot just watch them dying na. This went on maybe three-four weeks like this, proper grind, and this is where the whole water tap lafda started with the neighbouring plot fellow.

He is having the plot right next to mine, and there is only one tap point for two-three plots, so turns are there, everybody knows this, but this uncle was taking the tap for one hour, two hours, filling his drums also for outside use, not even for his plants only. One evening I went and I said,

Me: Uncle, it's my turn now only, you have used it since one hour already.

The neighbour: Wait wait, five minutes, water is not there in my drum still.

Me: Every day you are saying five minutes, five minutes. My tomato is drying there, no?

The neighbour: Arre, why you are getting so worked up for water, chalo take it take it.

But this happened maybe three-four times, same drama, and finally it became a proper building lafda, other plot holders also complaining about him, so the society secretary called a small meeting only, nothing formal formal, just standing near the tank in the evening.

The secretary: Everybody listen, do one thing, we will fix timing. Morning six to seven this side, seven to eight that side, and weekends we will rotate. No arguments after this, okay?

The neighbour: Fine fine, no issue from my side also.

After that it settled, thank god, otherwise it was becoming daily tension for nothing, water lafda in a garden plot, can you imagine, and both of us anyway staying in same society only, seeing each other in the lift also, so what is the point of fighting like this.

Now coming to the good news portion. My tomato, yaar, it was ek number this year. Like properly mast, red red, heavy bunches, neighbours were coming and asking, "What did you put, some special khaad or what?" I was just doing normal cow dung manure, nothing fancy, but the weather cooperated and maybe the water fight also made me more sincere about watering timing, ha ha. Whatever it is, tomato gave paisa vasool result, full basket every alternate day almost.

But then the methi, that one got hit by some rot in the roots, don't know from where, maybe too much water standing there in that low corner, the leaves were turning yellow and falling, and half the patch I had to just pull out and throw. Very sad thing, because methi I had planted thinking of my mother-in-law only, she loves methi thepla, but what to do, some crop always troubles you, this year it was methi's turn.

Then, this I will never forget, one storm came suddenly, not even monsoon season properly, just one evening dark clouds came and such wind started, and by next morning half my bhindi rows were flat on the ground, stems bent, some completely uprooted. I went in the morning and just stood there for two minutes like this, seeing the damage, feeling very senti again. But then I called my husband, called the watchman also, and we spent the whole Sunday, three of us, tying up the bent stems with small sticks, propping them back, packing soil around the roots. Some plants recovered, some didn't, but at least the rows were standing again by evening, looking decent, not like a war zone.

So this is roughly the whole year, na, ups and downs like that only. At the end when I tallied everything, tomato gave the most, bhindi gave medium after the storm and the worm both, methi gave very little because of rot, coriander was just bonus timepass crop that kept giving small small handfuls whenever I needed.

Whatever extra tomato I had, I gave to the neighbour only, the same tap fellow, because he was growing chillies and I needed chillies for my pickle, so we did some jugaad barter, my tomato for his chillies, no money changed hands, just neighbour adjustment, and honestly that felt more satisfying than selling it also.

Now the plot is put to bed for winter, I cleared the dead stems, turned the soil, covered the empty patches with dry leaves so it rests properly till next season.

Looking back, I will say it was a full paisa vasool year. Tiring, yes, water fight also happened, some crops failed also, but overall bindaas experience, and next year I am already planning what all to prepone and what to change. Gardening is like that only, no? Every season teaching you something new, what to do, this is the game.
