Six in the morning only I saw it, water just outside the door, ankle deep, and I thought, monsoon is monsoon only, it will go, no? Told the mother not to worry. By seven I checked again and arre, already it had come up past the ankle, and now the fear started small small. Started lifting things off the floor, the gas cylinder, the sack of rice, the tin trunk, the tiffin box, whatever was down, up on the bed, on the shelf, on top of the fridge, wherever highest, no time even to think properly, we were having so much stuff to move and only two hands each.

By ten, ten thirty the water was at the knee, and on the road outside it was like a river only, people wading with pants folded to here, one uncle from the next building bindaas as ever, pushing his scooter through it like nothing had happened. Not one auto was running, not the fast local, not the slow local, everything shut. Tried calling the office to tell, cut the call three times, no network in this weather. The didi from upstairs called down.

The didi: Do one thing, come na, bring whatever is left and come up, ground floor is not safe anymore.

We put everything, everything, on top of the cupboard, on the loft, tried jugaad even, bricks under the fridge legs, but what to do, no higher place was there. One aunty from the ground floor flat lost her whole almirah of clothes, just like that.

It kept climbing, kept climbing, by one, one thirty it had crossed even that, waist height in the corner, water is not there to stop for anybody's jugaad. Achha, the watchman had off the mains already, so at least no danger of shock, that much lafda we were saved. We sat in the didi's house, waiting, waiting since four hours now, just that sound, water hitting the wall, dhak dhak, nobody filmy about it, everyone senti, everyone just quiet.

The mother: Haan, chalo, it will go down only, has to.

Around five, six it started going down, slowly slowly, we watched the society compound wall like it was a clock, yaar, counting the bricks it uncovered.

Next day morning we went down. The whole wall, that line, dark, above my head. Bas. I sat on the step and could not say one word.
