Ah bas, sit, sit, let me tell you what all happened today, whole lane was upside down only, I am still smelling of leaves and diesel, see my hands even.

So there is that big gulmohar tree, na, the one outside the society gate, must be there since forty-fifty years, older than all of us put together. Last night's storm, whole thing came down, roots and all, right across the lane, blocking the gate, blocking the gutter side also, nobody's car nobody's scooter could go, not even one auto could come inside, the milk van bhaiya was standing outside honking honking for twenty minutes, what to do, tree is not moving for anybody's horn.

Morning time the secretary uncle from A-wing came down still in his lungi only, no shame no nothing, and he was shouting up at every balcony, come out, come out, everybody, tree has fallen, gate side is completely blocked, we cannot be waiting for BMC people, they will take one week minimum, some jugaad we have to do ourselves only. Word went up and down the lane so fast, yaar, faster than any WhatsApp group message, one door to another door, aunty telling aunty, kids running to tell their friends, in twenty minutes only whole building was out.

We were thinking five-six people will come, chalo, maybe eight if lucky. Arre, more than twenty people turned out, I am not even exaggerating. From B-wing, from the chawl side also, the ironing bhaiya from the corner, even two-three from the next lane who just heard the noise and came to see what lafda is going on, and stayed to help only. Everybody got some small small job, some people were cutting the smaller branches with whatever hacksaw was there in the house, some were dragging the cut pieces to one side, women were sweeping up all the leaves into one big pile, somebody was standing at the corner directing the two-three vehicles that were trying to squeeze past.

The heaviest part was the main trunk, thick like this, no rope was lifting that easily. The gym-going boy from second floor, the one who is always doing his running up and down the stairs for exercise, he took that whole load on himself practically, shoulder and all, working with the watchman and two labour boys from the site nearby who just came walking by and joined in, bindaas, no one even asked them, they just started pulling.

Then this small disaster happened, na. Rope was tied wrong side only, somebody's jugaad went wrong, and one big branch swung back and cracked clean into the front glass of the milk van that was still standing there, whole windscreen, crack crack crack. Everybody just froze for two seconds, then started laughing-shouting together, what to do now, milk van bhaiya was going senti, seeing his windscreen like that, saying who is going to pay for this, is this some cheap tamasha or what.

That is when the argument started also, over how the next branch should be cut, from which side.

The mechanic uncle: Cut from top side only, na, otherwise whole thing will swing back again like that one.

The electrician from C-wing: Arre what top side, you cut from top the base will still fall this side only, first cut the base, na, do one thing, let the labour boys hold the rope tight this time.

The mechanic uncle: You are not even holding the saw, how you are telling me cutting technique, faltu talking only.

Two more uncles jumped in, one on each side, voices going up, and for one minute it was looking like proper lafda. Then the oldest one there, the grandfather from ground floor, banged his stick on the ground once and said, bas, enough, both of you, this is not some ego competition, we are here since morning only to finish one job, come na, let the boys with the rope decide, they are the ones holding it. Everybody went quiet, somebody made a joke, tension passed off, chalo, back to work.

By then two-three households had already brought out chai and biscuits, cutting chai in small glasses going around, one aunty brought a whole tiffin of vada pav for everybody, another one brought some extra parathas she had made for lunch already, saying eat na, eat, no one is going home for lunch today it seems. The kids were given the job of collecting all the small small twigs and leaves into the gutter side pile, and carrying the water bottles up and down for whoever was thirsty, they were feeling very important doing that, like it is their contribution only.

The grandfather I told you, he was not sitting quiet also, he is eighty-something but he was standing there the whole time, insisting he will hold one end of the rope for the smaller branches, saying I am not some showpiece to just sit on the bench and watch, give me some role also. Nobody could argue with him, what to do, he is like that only, since young age itself very active man.

One funny thing happened around afternoon, everybody was covered in mud and leaves, working hard, and suddenly some uncle's mobile started ringing full volume, some old filmy song, right there in his pocket while his hands were completely full of branches, he could not even reach it, it kept playing loud loud for almost one full minute while he was shouting somebody take it out from my pocket na, and everybody just stopped working and started laughing, could not control, even the labour boys were laughing, that mobile ringtone became the joke of the whole day after that.

We thought this whole thing will finish by lunchtime, two-three hours max. It went on till evening only, almost three hours more than anybody expected, light had almost gone by the time last branch was cleared off the road. But by end of it, whole trunk cut into pieces and pushed to one side, gutter cleared out completely, road open again for at least one vehicle width, and all the mess swept into proper piles for the BMC van to pick up tomorrow. When the last big piece was finally rolled off the road, somebody just started clapping, and then everybody was clapping and cheering together, proper cheer, like something big had happened, ek number feeling, I am telling you.

Evening time, whole lane was sitting out on the steps, tired, dirty, but nobody wanted to go inside straight away. People who don't even normally talk to each other were sitting together drinking tea, telling the same stories again and again. Only for that one evening, but it felt different, whole street felt like one house only, not fifty different doors. Mast feeling, yaar. Paisa vasool day, even without any paisa involved.
