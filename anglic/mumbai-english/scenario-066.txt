The new neighbour: Arre, since morning I am hearing people saying something about some wild animal in this lane. What happened, yaar, everybody is talking like it is some big filmy story.

Me: Ho, that one. Sit, sit, I'll tell you the whole thing, no shortcut version, otherwise you will ask ten more questions after. So basically it started long back, maybe eight-nine months only. First time nobody even believed, that's the funny part. It was the milk bhaiya's boy, coming for early delivery, he said he saw something big and yellow-brown jump over the compound wall of the corner building and go into the nullah side. Everybody just laughed only. My mother also said, this fellow watches too much Discovery Channel, itself he is imagining things, don't take tension. Some uncle from the ground floor said, arre it was some stray dog only, at four a.m. eyes play tricks, what to do, everybody sees ghost-ghost in the dark.

So that first one just got waved off, nobody official even wrote it in the society register. Then maybe two weeks after, second sighting came, and this time it was not some random boy, it was the watchman. Now the watchman everybody trusts, thirty years he is doing night duty in this lane, he doesn't get scared for timepass, he doesn't do drama for fun. He came running in the morning, hands shaking a little, saying he saw it sitting calm-calm on top of the garbage shed near the third building, just watching him, not even running off immediately. That changed everything. Once the watchman said it, people stopped saying imagination-imagination.

After that sightings kept coming, small small, here and there. Somebody's tenant said it came near the parking, one aunty said her plants in the balcony garden got all disturbed, soil thrown everywhere, like something was digging for something. The bins also, every second day the corner one would be found toppled, lid off, whole area smelling, crows and all having a feast on top of it. Everybody started saying it is coming for food only, poking in the bins, going through the garden patches behind the last building, that side is more quiet, more dark, less streetlight.

Then the lane split, seriously, into two camps, like election time. One side, mostly the older ones, saying leave it be, don't disturb, it is also some poor creature, its jungle only got cut somewhere, where else will it go, it hasn't hurt anybody, why to make big lafda for nothing. Other side, mostly the ones with small children, saying no no, this is not some cute case, wild animal near children playing in the compound is not something to take lightly, something has to be done, today itself somebody should call somebody.

Then this thing happened which made the second camp very loud. One household, the one in B-wing ground floor, their small dog went missing one night. Gate was open a little, dog gone, and by morning everybody straight away said, it's that animal only, must have taken it. No proof, nobody saw anything, but you know how it goes, one person says, then next person adds masala, by evening the whole lane was saying it ate the dog, full drama, like some crime scene discussion happening in front of the paan shop.

That same week there was a proper society meeting, ground floor hall, chairs all arranged, and it became a real shouting match. One uncle stood up and said, bas, enough of this leave-it-be nonsense, we are not living in some jungle safari, somebody call the forest people now itself. Another aunty said, don't be so senti about a street dog that anyway used to roam loose, and don't blame the poor animal without seeing with your own eyes, itself you are making a story. Somebody from the back shouted, do one thing, why everybody is fighting, let's just decide something and move, what to do, sitting and arguing whole night will not solve. Chairman was trying to control, saying come na, one at a time, one at a time, but nobody was listening, everybody talking over everybody, typical.

Around the same time, one family, without telling anybody, tried to handle it on their own. They put out some meat with a rope trap kind of jugaad arrangement near their gate, thinking they will catch it themselves and hand it over to someone, big hero plan. Whole night they sat with a torch, and of course nothing happened, the thing didn't even come near, and by morning some other stray dogs had finished off the bait and the family looked quite foolish in front of the whole lane. Everybody was pulling their leg for weeks after that, pure timepass topic for the building WhatsApp group.

But the real turning point, the thing that finally brought everything to a head, was a night encounter. One of the flat owners, coming back late from night shift, parking his scooter, suddenly saw it sitting just there near the transformer, maybe ten feet only, and it looked straight at him before slowly walking off into the dark side lane. That man was properly shaken, he was saying his hands were trembling on the handle, he couldn't even find his keys for two minutes. After that even the leave-it-be camp went a bit quiet, because seeing it far off in a garden is one thing, seeing it that close at night is another thing completely. But still there was disagreement, some saying it is dangerous, has to go, and others saying no, it was probably more scared than him, it is just unlucky that its usual route brought it through our lane, poor thing is not looking for trouble, only looking for food and a quiet path back.

So after all this back and forth, finally the society committee did the sensible thing and called an actual forest department fellow, experienced type, who does this only for a living, goes around these fringe areas near the hills checking such cases. He came, walked the whole lane, checked the nullah side, checked where the bins are kept, checked the garden patches, and told everybody straight, this is its normal route only, it is not hunting people, it is passing through for easier food, secure your garbage properly and it will not have reason to come close to the buildings. He gave clear instructions, no drama, no big operation, just practical steps.

So that's what the lane settled on. Bins got proper lids now, some buildings even put small locks on the shed door. Watchman keeps half an eye toward that dark side lane during his rounds, nothing tense about it, just habit now. Sightings still happen, maybe once in two-three months somebody sees something far off near the nullah, but nobody panics, no meeting, no drama, it has become routine only. Now when people talk about it at the paan shop or during evening walk, it is not with fear, it is more like a story, everybody has their own version, everybody adds their own little twist, the family with the failed trap still gets teased, the watchman is treated like some hero for that first real sighting. Bindaas topic now, honestly, whole thing has become the lane's own filmy story, better than any TV serial, and it didn't even cost us anything except some new dustbin lids.
