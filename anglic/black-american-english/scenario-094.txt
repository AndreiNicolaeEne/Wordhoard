The mother: Y'all come sit with me a minute, I need to talk to you.

They ain't say nothing, just climbed up next to her, and she took both they hands before she started.

The mother: I got to tell y'all something and I need you to hear me good. Grandma passed last night. She gone.

The eldest thought on it a second before asking.

The eldest: What that mean though, gone? Like gone gone, or gone like when she stay over her sister house for a week?

The mother: No baby. Gone like her body stopped working and ain't nothing gone start it back up again. Her heart stopped beating, and can't nothing bring that back, not doctors, not nothing. That's what death is. It's done happened now, I can't undo it, and I ain't tryna make this no easier than it is, cause you deserve the truth.

The youngest: Where she at right now? Still at the hospital?

The mother: They finna move her from the hospital directly, then she going to the funeral home to get ready.

The eldest went quiet against her shoulder and started to shake a little, so she pulled them both in tighter.

The mother: Come here, both of y'all. I got you. Come here, sugar.

The youngest spoke muffled against her.

The youngest: We gone see her again? Ever?

The mother: Not like you used to, no. Not her calling the house or cooking Sunday dinner no more, but you gone carry her the rest of your life, in how you laugh, in how you treat people, in things you got from her you don't even know yet. That part don't never leave. I ain't gone lie to you and say different.

She held on a minute more before she let them go.

The mother: Now listen here. Next few days it's finna be a lot going on round this house. Mama'n'em coming through, phone ringing off the hook, people bringing food don't nobody even know who all sent. Friday we going to the funeral home to see her, Saturday the service.
