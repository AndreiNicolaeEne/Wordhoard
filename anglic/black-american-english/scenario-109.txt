The door ain't even all the way open before the mother eyes go wide like she seeing a ghost.

The mother: Wait. Wait wait wait, is that, oh my GOD, oh my GOD it's you, it's really you...

Me: It's me, Ma.

The mother don't say nothing else after that, she just grab a hold and don't let go. Arms locked all around the neck, face pressed in, and she stay right there like time ain't moving, like the clock done stopped for her on purpose. A whole minute pass and she still ain't turned loose.

The mother: Look at you. Look. At. You. You done got so big I ain't even, boy you was skinny when you left, what happened to you, who is you now?

The eldest: Let me see, let me SEE: (pulling at the shoulder, turning him this way and that like she checking merchandise) you done filled all the way out. You was a little bitty thing.

Me: I ain't little no more, that's for sure.

The eldest: Naw you ain't, you a whole grown man standing in my mama house talmbout you ain't little. EIGHT YEARS. You know that's eight years?

Me: I know how long it's been.

By then it ain't just the two of them no more, it's the whole front room done emptied out into the hallway, everybody coming from every direction at once, and don't nobody wait they turn.

An auntie: Is you eating? You look like you eating. Who all is cooking for you out there?

A cousin: What all you been doing, for real, cause mama'n'em ain't told me nothing, they just said you was coming and to be here...

The eldest:, and you couldn't call nobody? Eight years and it's a phone in your pocket the whole time...

An auntie: Let the man breathe, y'all finna talk him back out the door...

The auntie try to say it light but her voice already cracking on the end of it, and she turn her face off to the side quick, wiping under her eye with the back of her wrist like ain't nobody gon notice. Somebody notice. Don't nobody say nothing about it though, they just let her have that.

The auntie: I'm alright, I'm alright, I just: (breath catching) it's been a long minute, that's all. I'm alright.

Back by the kitchen doorway a little cousin stand half hid behind the mother leg, peeking out one eye at a time, ain't said a word yet.

The mother: Baby, come here, come say hey. You remember, naw you was too little, you wasn't even walking good last time.

The little cousin: (barely above a whisper) Hey.

Me: Hey yourself. Look at you, you got so tall I ain't even recognize you back there.

The little cousin duck back behind the leg soon as he say it, shy of all the noise, and don't nobody push it no further, they just let the child ease into it on they own time.

Me: (sniffing at the air) Hold up. Is that, that smell like Ma cornbread. That EXACT smell. I ain't smelled that in eight years and I walk in the door and it's right there waiting.

The mother: I made it for you. Knew you was coming, I wasn't finna let you walk in this house to nothing on the stove.

The bags still sitting right there by the door where he set them down, ain't nobody thought about them one time since, everybody too busy talking over everybody to remember they there.

An auntie: You know who all done changed since you been gone. You know your cousin got married?

Me: Which one?

An auntie: The one that stay over east, the one used to be so hardheaded, married, TWO babies now, if you can believe that.

The eldest: And the one you used to run around with, he done moved clean up to the north side, got him a whole house and everything...

A cousin: And ion even know if you heard but grandmama sold the old place, that's they house now, them people that bought it, ain't the same no more...

Me: Wait, grandmama SOLD it? The house? Nobody told me that.

The mother: We wasn't finna call you up with all that while you was way out there by yourself. Wasn't nothing you coulda done about it noway.

Me: Still. Man, a lot done happened.

The eldest: A lot done happened is right. It's a lot of people done came and went through here since you left, more than you even know.

Me: (looking around, slow) It's smaller in here than I remember. Everything look smaller. But it smell the same, it feel the same when you walk in, that ain't changed at all. Same couch, even. Y'all still got this same couch?

The mother: Don't start on my couch.

Me: I'm just saying. Eight years and the couch still here holding on.

The eldest: Some things don't move. Some things do.

A cousin: So how long you here for this time? For real, don't be finna tell me a week and then you gone again...

Me: Ion know exactly yet. Longer than last time though. I ain't rushing back out.

The mother: Good. Cause I'm already planning what we cooking Sunday, whole family coming through, I done already called half these people...

An auntie: She been on the phone since she found out you was coming, calling mama'n'em, calling everybody...

The mother: Somebody got to feed this family right, can't just let him walk in and walk right back out without a proper sit-down.

The eldest: (grinning all of a sudden) You still Big Head though. You know that. Eight years, whole grown man, and you STILL Big Head to me.

Me: (laughing) I knew that was coming. I knew it.

The eldest: Ain't nobody forget that. Some things follow you your whole life.

A cousin: (confused) Wait, what's Big Head from?

The eldest: You wasn't there for that. Don't worry about it.

For one second, right after that, don't nobody say nothing at all. The whole room just go quiet all at once, like everybody run out of words the same moment, and it's nothing left to do but stand there looking at each other, the mother still holding on to his arm like she scared to let go all the way, the auntie still dabbing her eye, the little cousin peeking out a little braver now, everybody just taking him in, like they making sure he real.

Then the mother let out a breath and pat him on the chest, soft.

The mother: Come on in the house for real now. Set them bags down somewhere they ain't in the way, and come eat something. We got all night to talk.

And the noise start back up again, but easier this time, everybody settling in around the table like the first hour of shouting done cleared a space for something calmer to move into, and the bags finally get carried on in from the door.
