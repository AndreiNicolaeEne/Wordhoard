I'ma just start this letter before I talk myself out of it again, cause it's been months now I been meaning to write you, real months, not the kind you say and mean two weeks. Every time I sit myself down for it something come up and pull me right back to the kitchen or the phone or somewhere, and then a whole season done gone by and I ain't wrote a word. So here go, finally, the long one I owe you.

House first, since that's what you ask about before you even ask about a person. The water heater finally got fixed, for real this time, not the tape-and-hope fix your daddy did that one summer that only held till August. Man came out, found the pilot line ain't never been right since it went in the ground, replaced the whole line front to back, and now the water be hot in under a minute, first thing in the morning too, no more standing there shivering waiting on it. That thing been broke, I mean been broke, since before you even left, so don't ask me why it took this long.

Course soon as one thing get right another thing go and act up. The back door don't close all the way now, it stick on the frame, you got to lift it a little and push with your hip to get it to catch. Mama been on about it since spring, say it's finna warp worse before winter get here and we need to deal with it before it's a real problem, and she right, I just been slow getting to it, same as everything else on that list.

We planted them collards back where your garden used to be, they coming in good, and a fig tree off the side of the porch, the little one, it's already taller than the fence and mama swear it's growing faster than anything she ever put in the ground. Mama'n'em put up a swing on the porch too, the hanging kind with the chain, and it's a lot of people done sat in that swing one time or another, whole block practically.

Now the joke, I ain't explaining it, you know: the raccoon still ain't paid rent.

Big thing since you left is the workmate, the one who used to run the counter with your friend at the shop, her whole job moved. Company shut the local office down, told everybody it's remote now or find something else, so she home most days working off her kitchen table, and she picked up a little side thing doing books for the church to fill in the gap. She say the extra money nice but she stay saying she miss seeing people face to face, miss the walk to work even, miss having somewhere to be in the morning. It's a strange kind of change, the kind where nothing look different from outside but everything is.

Neighbors done shifted some too. The family two doors down, the ones with all the kids, they moved out to the county back in spring, wanted more yard I guess, more room for all them children to run. New family come in behind them, quiet folks, keep to theyself mostly, and they got a baby now too, stroller be out front near every evening when it cool enough to walk. And the one across the street, her family grew different, her daughter had the baby back in the winter, so now it's a whole lot more noise coming from over there than it used to be, in the good way, the kind of noise you don't mind hearing through a window.

Two people ask after you every single time I see them, without fail. First is the auntie from down at the church, she stop me in the parking lot near about every Sunday, same conversation, like clockwork:

The auntie: Where your brother at, he still ain't call this house.
Me: He busy, he working, you know how it is out there.
The auntie: Everybody working. Don't nobody call home no more. Tell him I said hey, and tell him I'm praying for him.
Me: I'll tell him.

Second one is the one at the counter, your old friend from the shop, she catch me every time I come through the door:

The one at the counter: What all he doing out there? He good?
Me: He good. He just be quiet like that, always was.
The one at the counter: Tell him don't be a stranger. Tell him I said that talmbout he too busy for us now.
Me: I'll tell him that too, word for word.

I got a confession I wouldn't say to your face but I'll put it down in ink cause a letter can hold what my mouth won't. Some nights it's real quiet in that house now, quieter than it should be for how many of us still living in it, and I be missing you in a way I don't say out loud to nobody, not even mama, not even to myself most days. Ion know why it's easier writing it than saying it straight to a person, but there it is, on the page where I can't take it back.

Season was something else this year. Summer stayed hot longer than usual, didn't nobody get no real break from it till near October, everybody dragging by August, fans running in every room. Then it dropped cold fast, like it skipped a whole month and went straight from summer clothes to coats. The fig tree handled it fine though, tougher than it look, tougher than half the grown folks around here about the heat if I'm honest.

Let me ask you some things now, cause you don't tell me nothing unless I ask you direct, you always was like that. What all your days look like out there, morning to night, start to finish? Is the work treating you right, or is it wearing on you the way it used to wear on you here, cause I can hear it in your voice sometimes even when you say you fine? And who you got out there for you, I don't mean just people you work with, I mean somebody you'd call at eleven at night if something happened, cause a place ain't a home till you got that, and I worry on it more than I let you know.

I'ma send them pictures I keep saying I'ma send, the porch and the fig tree and the swing and all, I promise you this time, for real this time, not like the last four times I said it. Write me back when you get a minute, and don't wait a year like I did, cause a year is too long between hearing your own voice on the other end, even just in ink.
