Me: Alright, come on in out the cold, my luvver, no sense stood there on the step. So you'm wanting the whole tale about next door, are you. Somebody's been chatting already, I 'spect, and got it half wrong and all, most like. Well I'll give you my own side of it and you can chew it over yourself after, cos there idn't nobody else round here gonna give it you straight, babber.

Right, so. Back along, when we first come to this street, gert years ago now, we was thick as anything, the two houses. In and out of each other's kitchens all hours, borrowing sugar, borrowing the mower, his kids scrumping off our tree in the back and nobody minding one bit about it. Sunday dinners took it in turns between the two houses, near enough every fortnight. His wife used to bring a drop of zider down of a summer evening and we'd sit out the back till it went proper dimpsy, dark enough you couldn't see your own hand. Proper close, we was. I mean that. Tis hard to credit now, looking at it, how close.

Then he goes and puts up a shed. Right up hard against the line, near enough touching our fence, and her leaking rainwater straight onto our path every time it come down, which round here is most days as you'll soon enough learn. I never said nothing for months, cos you don't, do you, not over a shed. But it kept teasing away at me every time I went out the back and seen it sat there, gert and ugly, tipping water where it shouldn't.

So I asked him, nice as you like. Just mentioned it over the fence, said could he shift it back a foot or put a gutter on it, no rush, whenever he got round to it. Nothing. Weeks go by. I asked him again, same as before, still polite, still no edge on it at all. Nothing again, like the first asking never happened. Third time I asked, I'll admit there was a bit of an edge on it by then, cos three times is three times, idn't it, and a person's entitled to go a bit teasy by the third go. I said to him straight, where's all that water suppose to go to, if not off your roof and onto my path.

Then it come back to me through her that lives the other side, who was always carrying tales both ways between the two houses, she do love an audience, mind, that I'd apparently gone round "having a right go," shouting the odds, when all I done was ask him plain would he sort it. By the time it got to me it had turned into a whole different conversation to the one I actually had. That's how it goes on this street, see, everything gets a bit bigger by the time it's been through three mouths.

And when I finally said something to his face about the shed being ignored, he stood there bold as brass and told me he'd never once been asked, not the first time nor any time after, and that I was making the whole thing up out of nothing. Flat denied it, straight to my face. That's what got me worse than the shed ever did, if I'm honest, being told I never asked when I know full well I asked him three separate times over the best part of two months.

So I did what anyone round here would do. I never said another word about it to him. I just stopped going out in me daps of a morning to pull his bins round for the lorry, which I'd always done for years without him even having to ask, on account of his hip being bad. Small thing. Petty, I know it, but there it is.

And then the comment come back to me, same one carrying tales, that he'd said up the road that we was "the sort that smiles to your face and holds a grudge like a dog with a bone." That's the one that stung, out of everything said on either side. Cos I never once smiled at that man and meant different. I said what I meant to him every time, plain as anything.

I did try and mend it, mind, about a year in. Took a cake round, proper job it was too, near enough his wife's own recipe, knocked the door myself. He took it off me but never asked me in, said ta very flat, and shut the door before I'd even got back down the step. That was the last time either of us knocked the other's door.

Whole street took sides after that, roughly down the middle, which is a mazed way for grown people to carry on if you ask me, choosing a house over a shed and a cake nobody even got thanked for proper. Some of them still speak to the both of us same as ever, God love them for it, and some picked a house early on and stuck to it, and that's how it's stood since.

The argument that finished it proper, the one everybody on the block heard whether they wanted to or no, that was out the front, broad daylight, gone half six of an evening, the two of us going at it over the fence loud enough the whole street had their windows open pretending not to listen. Everything come out then, the shed, the water, the cake, years of it, all at once, right there in front of God and everybody.

That's when the accusation come in about the fence panel and all, that I'd took it down myself out of spite and slung it over into his garden. I told him flat, no such thing, it was the gale had it down same as it took two others on this road that same winter, and he weren't having none of it, called me a liar to my face in front of half the street who was watching from their windows.

That's going on eight years of silence now between the two houses. Eight years and we've not said a proper word to each other, not so much as a nod some days, though we're near enough close enough to touch fences still. Funny how it shows itself in the small things day to day, mind. He won't put his bins out till the very last minute now, so's I can't do it for him even by accident. I still walk the long way round the block of a morning sooner than pass his gate.

And I'll tell you the honest truth of it, my luvver, cos there idn't no sense lying to you when you've asked me straight and sat there this long listening without one word. Some days, this far on, I couldn't tell you for certain who started it. The shed, I s'pose, if you want a first cause put to it. But I've turned it over that many times now I idn't wisht sure any more whether it was the shed, or the water off that roof, or that first time I never said nothing at all and let it fester quiet. I know my own side of it proper well, see. I just idn't so sure any more it's the whole of it.
