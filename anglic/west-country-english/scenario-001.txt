Her started with it just gone teatime that evening, little twinges her'd had plenty of backalong, false starts and all, so I never thought no different of it.

Me: Don't fret, my luvver, tis probably nothing, sit yourself down.

Her did have them twinges most weeks by then anyway. But come nine o'clock her went proper quiet on me, and then:

Her: This in't like they others. This is it, I reckon. This one's here to stay.

Soon as her said it I knowed her was right, some feeling comes over a room like that, you can't mistake it for nothing else, see.

Then it was hours of it. Up and down the landing in me daps, her leaning on the windowsill, me rubbing her back till my arm went numb, the pair of us watching the dimpsy come down outside and then the dark go on and on and on. Gone two in the morning it turned proper hard, her crying out something wisht, teasy with the pain of it, half mazed, saying her'd no more left to give.

Midwife came in about four, when we'd near given up managing on us own.

Midwife: Right, my babber, we'm nearly there now. You're doing proper job of this.

Last hour her pushed till her'd nothing left at all, and when it come to it there was this gap, only a few seconds, but it went on forever, no sound in that room at all, and I thought summat had gone wrong, I really did. Then the cry come, gert loud cry, fit to wake the street, and inside the minute I had her in my arms, second one, same as the first only smaller, and I don't mind telling you I went proper daft with it, tears and all.

The eldest was up along with her nan that whole night, never knew a thing of it till morning come. Just us three in that room when it happened, mind. You don't forget a night like that, not ever.
