Right then. I'm sat down to this at last, and don't be giving me grief over how long it's took, because I know, I know, tis been near on the whole year and I've had the pad out on the table a dozen times and found summat else to do instead. But here I am, kettle on, and I idn't getting up again till this is done, so you'll have the lot of it, house and all.

Start with the house, since I know that's what you'll want first. The roof over the back kitchen finally got done. Took the roofer three goes to even work out where the leak was to, on account of the weather, but he was up there Tuesday fortnight and had it proper sealed by teatime, no more of that drip-drip into the bucket we've had going since before you left, mind. Dad stood under it after, looking up, and all he said was "proper job," which from him you know is high praise indeed. Course, no sooner is one thing mended than another goes. The shed roof's started letting in now, just the one corner, but enough that the mower's sat under a tarp till someone gets round to it. Boiler's another matter, dad do reckon her's on her way out, been saying that two winters running mind, so I idn't holding me breath. And the fence panels down the side went over backalong, in that big blow we had, you'd have laughed to see me out there in me coat trying to hold the thing up on me own like it was going to make any difference. New ones are up now, and dad's put a row of leeks and the runner beans along the wall where the old ones used to lean, so that bit of garden's looking better than it has in years. Mum's hung a couple of baskets either side the front door too. Proper cheerful, coming up the path of an evening.

Big change since you went is my work moved. The whole place shifted out to Avonmouth back in the spring, the old building went for flats, same as everything does round here now, so I idn't walking in of a morning no more, it's the bus or nothing, and that bus is a law unto itself, see. Means I'm up gert early these days, out the door before it's hardly light, and not back till it's near gone dimpsy again some evenings. Takes some getting used to. But the pay's a bit better for it, so I won't complain too loud.

Neighbours you'll want to know about an' all. The old boy two doors down's gone to live with his daughter out Kingswood way, his knees wouldn't do the stairs no more, and there's a young couple in the house now, seem alright, quiet, got a car that beeps something chronic when he parks it. And the family on the corner have had their third, a little one, screaming fit to wake the street some nights, but mum's round there with a casserole every other week like it's her own grandchild, so I don't think she minds one bit.

Two of them ask after you regular, mind. Mum, obviously, every single time I'm round there, first thing near enough, before I've even got me coat off. And the woman at the corner shop, bumped into her Tuesday, stood there counting out me change:

The shopkeeper: Any word from your sister then?
Me: Writing to her tonight, soon as I get in, I said.
The shopkeeper: Tell her we still keep her sort in, babber, for when she's back, mind.

So there's that, whenever you fancy your name coming up over a tin of beans.

Now here's the thing I'll write down because I can't say it to your face without going soft. Some mornings I still put two mugs out before I remember there's only me wants one. Been doing it the best part of a year now and I still catch meself at it two, three times a month. Don't go making a fuss of it when you write back, I only put it down here because it's easier with a pen in me hand than it would ever be down the phone.

Season's been a funny old one. Winter was a wisht business, rain near enough sideways from October clean through to March, the vield down the allotments gone to a lake for the best part of a fortnight and dad going down there in his wellies just to stand and look at it like looking would drain it any faster. But summer's turned proper lovely since, warmest start we've had in years, sat out the back of a varm evening most nights now with the radio on low. Took the youngest down Weston for the day a few weeks back and it was gert crowded already with grockles even though the season's barely started, you'd think they'd wait their turn. Your godmother rang from down Cornwall way and says it's worse there, emmets everywhere, and she'll sort out that thing she's been promising you dreckly, which we both know means whenever the mood takes her and not a day sooner.

Still no word from the badger, in case you were wondering.

Right, questions, since you never do answer half of what I ask you down the phone. How's the new place treating you, is it warm enough now the weather's turned, or is that a daft question to be asking somewhere so far off? Have you found anywhere decent to get a pint yet, somewhere that'll do for when I finally get up to see you? And is the job still going the way you wanted it to, or has it settled into just being a job like they all do in the end? Write it all down proper this time, don't just put "fine" and leave it there like you did on the postcard.

Youngest is after new daps again, grown out the last pair in about five minutes flat same as always, and got caught scrumping off next door's tree again an' all, same tree we used to go over the wall for, some things don't change round here whatever else does, you'll see. He sends his love, or would if he knew I was writing, so I'm sending it on his behalf.

I'll get a few bottles of the zider sent up to you before the frost gets to what's left of it this year, promise, same as I always say and never quite manage till you chase me for it. Mind you write back sooner than I did. Look after yourself up there, my luvver, and don't be a stranger. All my love, and mum's, and dad's, and the youngest's, and the shopkeeper's an' all seemingly, to hear her tell it.
