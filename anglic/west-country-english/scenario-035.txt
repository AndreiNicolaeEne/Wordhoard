Me: Now you can say what you like, but the falafel stall up the top's the best in the whole market, and there idn't no arguing it.

My mate: Give over, babber. Best falafel my arse, tis dry as an old boot half the time. You want proper food you go the noodle stall down the end, that's proper job, that is. Chicken in that broth so good you'd lick the bowl clean.

Me: I went down there once, mind, and the one on the counter, her'd run out the good broth and give me the leftover watery stuff off yesterday, cold an all. Proper teasy it made me, queuing that long for nothing.

My mate: Never.

Me: Straight up, ask anyone. That queue's full of grockles who don't know no better, that's why it never gets no shorter. Locals do go the falafel stall, we know what's good, see.

My mate: You're mazed if you reckon falafel beats a bowl of noodles on a cold day. That stall's been there since I was a nipper, backalong, when we'd come off scrumping down the lane after and want summat hot in us.

Me: Gert long queue don't mean gert good food, it just means folk are wisht and desperate for anything hot.

My mate: Says the one whose daps still stink of chilli oil from where you dropped your lunch down 'em last week.

Me: That's beside the point, mind.

My mate: Anyway, what about the churro stall, where's that to now, up by the steps? Nobody can say a bad word against that one.

Me: True enough, that one's proper job and all, gert sugar on 'em, lovely with a cold zider.

My mate: Right then. Only one way to settle it proper. We go back and try both again, no messing, once this dimpsy's over and we've had our tea.

Me: Fair enough, my luvver. See you up there.
