The mother sat the two of 'em down close on the sofa, proper close, knees near touching, before she said one word of it.

The mother: Right you two, listen to me a minute. I got summat to tell you and I want you hearing it proper.

Then she said it plain, no dressing it up none.

The mother: Your nan's died, my babbers. Passed in the night, in her sleep, no pain to her.

The eldest went quiet a second then asked it straight, wanting it real.

The eldest: But what does that mean though, dead? Like where's she gone to?

The mother: It means her body's stopped, love, stopped for good, and her idn't coming back to it. Nobody knows for certain where the rest of her's gone to, if anywhere, but her's not hurting and her's not frightened, not any more. That's the honest truth of it, far as I know it.

The youngest tugged at her sleeve, face all screwed up.

The youngest: Will they bury her in the ground?

The mother: They will, babber, in a nice quiet spot, and we'll go and see it when it's ready, mind.

She pulled the both of 'em in gert tight then, arms right round.

The mother: Come here to me, the pair of you. Come on now, my luvver.

A small voice came from somewhere in against her jumper.

The eldest: Will we ever see her again though?

The mother: Not in this life, my love, no. I idn't going to lie to you about that. But we'll keep her close all the same, in what we remember, see.

She held on a minute more before she let 'em go.

The mother: Now listen here, over the next few days there's going to be people coming round the house, and Nanny and Grandad'll be that busy sorting things, and then there's going to be a funeral, a proper goodbye for everyone what loved her. Don't you go worrying when it comes, you'll know what it's for. Off you go now, mind, back to your game.
