Mother: Right, who's gone and broke that zider flagon off the windowsill, then? Tis smashed to bits all over my clean floor, look, glass everywhere.

The eldest: Idn't me. I never even bin in the kitchen since dinner, ask anyone.

The middle one: Idn't me neither. I reckon twas the wind done it, the back door were open a crack and it do blow gert strong through there of an evening, could've knocked it clean off the sill.

Mother: Funny sort of wind that shuts a cupboard door behind itself and all. And where's the dog to, then, if it were the wind - he's bin shut up in the yard since dimpsy, so don't you go putting it on him neither. And that flagon's bin wobbly on that sill backalong, I said so twice, so summat had to give it a proper knock.

The middle one: Well, might've bin the eldest's daps left right there on the step, I nearly went flying over 'em meself.

The eldest: I never left my daps there! Her's the one always leaving stuff about, mazed as a brush, that one.

The middle one: I only said the wind done it, I never -

Mother: Just now twas the wind, now you're saying you tripped over daps and near knocked it yourself going past. Can't be both, my luvver, so which is it.

The middle one: ...might've caught it with my elbow. Weren't my fault though, not proper.

The youngest: I don't know nothing about it, ask me dreckly, I'm watching telly.

Mother: Nobody's watching nothing tonight, mind. Made a right proper job of it between the lot of you, and not one of you'll own it, so nobody's going scrumping up the allotment Saturday, and nobody's playing out till that floor's swept and the money's found for a new flagon. All of you. See.
