Me: You'm quiet this marnin, my luvver. What's amiss with you? You've had that tea sat there gone cold and you ent touched it.

The mother: Sorry, love, I am, I know I am. I can't shake this dream off me. Been sat here going over it and over it since I got up and I don't know why it's got me so mazed, it's only a dream in't it, but it won't leave off.

Me: Well go on then, tell us what it was. Might shift it out your head if you say it out loud.

The mother: Alright. So I'm walking home, right, same as any other night, ordinary as you like, nothing wisht about it to start with at all. Same old road I do walk every day of my life, past the shop, past the school, all of it just as it should be. Only the road kept on going. And going. Past where the turning should have come up, past where the house should have been sat, and I never even thought it strange at the time, in the dream like, I just kept on walking same as normal, like it was nothing.

Me: That's how they do go though, dreams, in't it, you don't question it till after.

The mother: That's just it. So eventual I do get there, and the house is there, same as ever from the outside, same gate, same step, everything proper as it should be. But her'd got a room in her I never seen before. A whole room, love, never existed, not in this life, not in that house, not ever.

Me: What sort of room was it then? Go on.

The mother: Small one. Low ceiling, lower than any room we've got, and a little window looking out onto nothing at all, just grey, no garden, no street, nothing. There was an old dresser against the wall, the sort my mother used to have backalong, and a chair set by the window. And sat in that chair, calm as anything, calm as if no time had passed at all, was my mother.

Me: Never!

The mother: I'm telling you. Sat there just as ordinary as you or me sat here now, doing something with her hands, sewing or the like, and her looks up at me and starts talking away, easy as anything, like her'd only seen me the day before.

Me: What, talking to you? What was she saying then, what did she say?

The mother: That's the thing, I can't get it back. I've tried and tried since I woke. I know she said summat, I know it was said proper and clear, and I know I understood it at the time, whatever it was, I felt like I'd been told something, something that mattered. But the actual words, gone. Vanished clean away.

Me: Try again, go on, close your eyes and have a think.

The mother: I am trying. It was... no. It's no good, love, it's not there. I get right up to the edge of it and then it's gone again, like reaching for summat in the dark and your hand just closing on nothing.

Me: That's proper strange that is.

The mother: I know. And then it shifted, the dream did, same as they do, and I was looking for something. Searching, proper frantic, going through drawers, under things, behind things, and it weren't turning up nowhere it should have been. I kept thinking, it was here, it was here only a minute ago, and it just wasn't.

Me: What was you looking for though?

The mother: That's it, I couldn't tell you. Not even in the dream I couldn't say what it was I was after. I just knew I had to find it and I hadn't got it and the not having it was getting worse the longer it went on.

Me: That's a proper teasy sort of dream, that is.

The mother: Teasy idn't the word for it. It got that bad, the panic, I could feel my heart going before I'd even woke up, if that makes sense, like it started in the dream and just carried on through into waking. And then I did wake, sudden like, heart going nineteen to the dozen, and I lay there a good minute not rightly sure which one was real, the dream or the room I'd woke up in. Had to look round proper careful at the wardrobe and the curtains before I was sure it was our room and not that other one.

Me: That'd put the wind up anyone, that would.

The mother: It has an' all. I'm sat here still half in it if I'm honest with you.

Me: Mind, they do say when someone gone turns up like that, calm as anything, it's meant to mean summat good. Peaceful, like. That's what they say round here anyway, in't it.

The mother: That's what my mother used to say herself, funny enough, about her own mother. Said if you dream of the dead sat there quiet and content, not fretting, not saying nothing awful, it's a good sign. Means they're alright where they've got to.

Me: There you go then. Nothing to be mazed about. She was sat there calm as owt, weren't she, doing her sewing, easy as Sunday. That's a good dream that is, however you look at it.

The mother: I s'pose. Though what she was telling me I'd give summat to know. Feels like I've had a message and dropped it before I got it home, if that makes any sense at all.

Me: Maybe her'll tell you again another night. Maybe it idn't finished with you yet.

The mother: Don't say that, I don't know as I want it again, not with that searching part at the end, that fair did me in.

Me: Could be the two bits idn't even joined up proper. Dreams do that, chuck two things together that's got nothing to do with one another.

The mother: Could be. Or could be I'm looking for whatever it was she told me and can't get back. That'd be about right, wouldn't it, mazed old me, losing the one thing that mattered and searching the house for it after.

Me: Proper daft, the pair of us, sat here reading tea leaves out of a bad night's sleep.

The mother: Proper daft is right. Drink your tea, go on, before that's stone cold an' all, and let's say no more about it. Though I don't mind telling you, my luvver, I'll be thinking on that room the whole day through.

Me: You will an' all. Mind you tell me if she comes back to you though. I want to know what her was on about.

The mother: I'll tell you first thing, don't you fret. See if I don't.
