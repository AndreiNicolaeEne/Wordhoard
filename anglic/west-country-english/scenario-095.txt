The eldest: Right, we got to start somewhere, and I'm having the clock off the mantel. Her told me tis mine, backalong now, months before her went.

The younger: Her never said no such thing to me and I was round here just as much as you was.

The eldest: I idn't making it up. Her sat right where you're stood and said, my babber, that clock, tis yours when I'm gone, and I never forgot it.

The younger: Convenient, that. Where's it to now anyway, still sat on the mantel where anyone could've took it.

The eldest: Don't you start on me about taking things. What about the money then, cos there weren't but a few thousand left all told and you was down the bank sorting it before I even got the phone call.

The younger: That idn't fair and you know it idn't. I paid for they funeral flowers out my own pocket, done it proper job too, and never said one word about it.

The eldest: I never said you done nothing. I'm saying don't tell me you idn't took more than your share, cos I do know what I do know.

It goes quiet a minute, the younger not looking up from the table.

The younger: I don't care about the clock. I don't care about the money neither, none of it. I keep thinking of us scrumping down the lane behind her house when we was kids, coming home of a dimpsy evening with our pockets full and her pretending she never noticed. I just want her back a minute, that's all this is.

The aunt comes through from the kitchen with the tea towel still in her hand, drawn in by the raised voices, and stands a moment in the doorway before she says her piece.

The aunt: Now that's enough, my luvvers, the both of you, that's enough. Her's not a week gone and here's you two gone proper teasy over a clock and a few pound. Her'd not want this, see.

Neither of them says anything for a while. The eldest notices the bottle of zider still standing in the pantry, never opened, and puts the clock down without meaning to decide anything by it.

The eldest: We'll sort it dreckly.

The younger: Dreckly, aye.

They catch each other's eye a second, both of them gone gert wisht and ashamed of it, the pair of them squabbling like children over what's left of the woman that raised them, mind.
