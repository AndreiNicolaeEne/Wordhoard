Right then, babber, sit down a minute and I'll tell it you proper, cos you wasn't there for none of it and I'm that glad it's over I could burst.

Six o'clock this morning, still mazed from me bed, and straight onto shifting the blocks off the wagon before I'd even got me tea down me. No easing in at all, that's how 'twas. First job was stacking they slabs round the back and that set the pace for the whole day, cos once you're in that rhythm you can't let up.

Backalong I'd have managed easier, I reckon, but I idn't as young as I was. Me back started up within the hour, this gert ache right across here, and me hands gone raw off the rope. The workmate, her's been on it years longer than me, her showed me how to hold it proper so it didn't tear me palms worse.

We had us a break about ten, quarter of an hour, and that idn't near enough when you've been at it since six. Me daps were soaked through by then and all.

Come three, half three, that's when it went proper hard. I was that teasy and wisht I could've walked off it and no one the wiser, dimpsy near come on early it felt like, hours still left in me. But you don't, do you.

One thing come easy though, the fence panels, reckoned that'd take till dark and we had it done by dinner, proper job that was, a nice bit of a lift in an otherwise grinding old day.

Forty quid owed me for that job alone. Tomorrow's another day the same, or they might give us the one off to rest the body, don't know which yet.

Mind how you go now. Fancy a zider, my luvver, see you dreckly.
