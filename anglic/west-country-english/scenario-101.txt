Me: Alright my luvver, you never guess who was there.

My mate: Go on then, tell us.

Me: The one used to sit up back in class, right titchy little thing ,  I'm telling you, I never knowed her at first, gert big woman now, hair right down her back, had to ask who her was.

My mate: Get away, never.

Me: Straight up. But then the one what ran round with we scrumping, backalong, exact same, idn't changed one bit, still got that same daft laugh, still calling everyone babber soon as her opened her mouth.

My mate: Proper job, some things do stay how they be.

Me: Do you mind how thin he was though, always last picked for footy, couldn't run for toffee ,  and now he's built like the side of a house, on the docks I reckon.

My mate: Weren't the other one meant to come, did the milk round with we?

Me: Her never showed. Her mother's took bad again and her had to stop home and see to her.

My mate: Ah bless her, shame that. Would've been proper good to see her.

Me: But listen, you'll never believe it, the one what was that mazed at maths, couldn't add two and two to save his life ,  he's some sort of professor now, up London, teaching it and all.

My mate: Never in this world.

Me: Straight up. Proper job for him, idn't it.

My mate: Makes you wonder what they was all thinking, looking at we two.

Me: Go on then, we idn't changed that much, have we.

My mate: Bit more grey about it. Bit slower up the stairs. Bit of a belly on we and all. But same old underneath, same old.

Me: Same old. Zider still went down the same, mind.

My mate: We ought to do it again dreckly, and all, not leave it years and years next time.

Me: Ar. Mind we do, don't leave it. See you there, whenever it be.
