Of a dimpsy sort of evening, three of them sat out the back with a drop of zider going round, and the talk turned, like it do, to what's wrong with everyone.

The eldest: My knees, babber, that's my trouble. Been at it years now, this stiffness. Her's proper bad first thing, the left one 'specially, like her's rusted overnight. I do have to sit on the edge of the bed a good five minutes before her'll bend at all.

The next: Years, you say. That idn't nothing, my luvver. I do go three, four nights a month not sleeping a wink for this shoulder. Lay on it, it's screaming. Lay off it, it's screaming anyway. Some mornings I'm that mazed with tiredness I don't know my own daps from my slippers.

The third: Well I'll tell 'ee summat for nothing, the pair of you can hush now. I've had this back of mine going on since backalong, before either of you was living round yer. Tis older than the friendship, mind, and that's saying summat, idn't it.

The eldest: Go on then, who's got it worst? I reckon tis me.

The next: Not likely. Nobody's losing sleep same as I do.

The third: Neither of you's had it as long as I have, see, and that's the proper measure of it.

The eldest: Proper job, calling yourself the worst off when you can still get up them stairs quicker than me.

The next: Someone's got to carry the crown, love.

The third: Won't be handing it over, I'll tell 'ee that for nothing.

And not one of them give an inch, same as always, till they was all three laughing too hard to argue it further, the zider going warm in the glasses and nobody a bit closer to deciding who'd got it worst.
