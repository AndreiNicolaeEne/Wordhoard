Me: Go on then, give us the zider cake, before it goes forever. Where's it even written down to?

The aunt: Idn't written down nowhere, my luvver, never has been. Tis all up here, my nan showed me backalong, standing right by that same stove.

Me: Well go on then.

The aunt: Right. Flour first, pound of it, near enough... no, hold on, bit over a pound it was, she used the big cup, a good heaped one, gert scoopful anyway, don't be too fussy on the amount, mind. Then you do want your butter and sugar creamed up proper, and, oh hang on, I nearly left out the egg, that's gotta go in before the flour, not after, silly me. Two eggs, beaten in good.

Me: And the spice?

The aunt: Ess, spice next, whatever you got, nutmeg's nice. Fold that gentle into the flour, don't go rushing that bit now, my nan'd have your guts for garters if you rushed the folding, her always said you knock all the air clean out and her's flat as a foot after. Then the zider goes in last, good glug of it, apples she scrumped herself half the time, proper job when it's homemade.

Me: How much zider though?

The aunt: Never measured that in my life, babber, just till the batter looks right, judge it by eye same as her did. In the oven then, and you know her's done when her's stopped singing, you'll hear it, that little hiss goes quiet, and her springs back when you press her middle. No need for no clock.

Me: Could I do it with shop cider, not the scrumpy stuff?

The aunt: Ess, you could, at a pinch, tidn't be quite the same, mind, but it'll do. See.
