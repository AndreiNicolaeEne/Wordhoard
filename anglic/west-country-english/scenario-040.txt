Me: Right, sit down a minute, cos I got to tell you the whole of it, start to finish.

Mother: Go on then, love, how'd it go?

Me: Well I were up before the sparrows, couldn't hardly eat me breakfast I were that nervy, just walking round the kitchen like a mazed thing. Got there twenty minute early anyway, stood outside not knowing where the door was even to, felt proper daft.

Father: Who'd you see first, then?

Me: Bloke in charge come out, shook me hand, dead friendly actually, showed me round quick before anything got going. Then he hands me this first job and honest, it were less than I thought, just sorting stock, I were braced for summat gert complicated and it weren't. Made a right pig's ear of it mind, stacked a whole shelf wrong way round in front of everyone, felt me face go red as anything, but nobody said nothing much, one of the girls just laughed and said don't fret, first day's first day.

The eldest: Did you have a break at least?

Me: Had one about eleven, sat with the others sizing 'em all up, quiet lot mostly, one or two proper babbers though, having a laugh. There's this one older workmate, her's been there donkey's years, her do know everything back to front, come over without me even asking and showed me the trick of it, proper helpful, saved me twice over.

Father: Pace alright, was it?

Me: Faster than I thought it'd be, tell you the truth, never stopped hardly, nine hour on your feet and you feel it in your daps by the end, legs like jelly.

Mother: So how'd it leave you feeling, then?

Me: Walked home in the dimpsy just going over it all in me head, every little bit, the mistake and all. But I'll say this straight, it went proper job. Proper job it were.
