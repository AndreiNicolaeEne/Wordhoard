The child: Mum, can I have they new daps? Proper ones, not off the market.

The mother: You idn having new daps this month, my luvver, and that's the end of it.

The child: But why not though.

The mother: 'Cause we in't got the money spare, babber. Rent's gone up and the leccy's due an' all. I do graft hard enough as it is without buying gert forty-quid trainers every time you fancies a new pair. Tis not me being tight. Tis just how it is right now.

The child: I could do the bins every week for you. And hoover through of a Sunday.

The mother: You could do they things anyway, love, they idn worth forty quid a week to me.

The child: What if it's for my birthday then, could I have 'em after that?

The mother: If your nan sends summat and there's enough left when your school shoes are paid for, we'll see. I in't promising, mind, only saying we'll see.

The child: But her up the road already got a pair. Her mum got her some Saturday gone.

The mother: Her mum's business idn ours, babber. What her family do spend don't put nothing in my purse, do it. Every house got its own way of managing, and ours idn hers.

The child: Tis so unfair though.

The mother: Don't you go on so, you'll have me mazed. Tell you what, they old ones of yours, where's they to? I'll clean 'em up proper and put new laces in, and if you'm good we'll get a bag of chips Friday an' all. That do you for now?

The child: ...S'pose. But I'm still wanting 'em.

The mother: I know you are, love. Mind you get they bins out first though. See.
