Mother: Right then, sit yourself down and give us the truth of it, cos I've had the eldest on at me all week asking what it's actually like up there and I never know what to tell her.

The eldest: Well go on then. What's it like.

Me: Big. Proper big, like you can't get your head round it till you're standing in the middle of it. You know how you can get from ours right into town and back in twenty minutes if the traffic's kind? Up there that's just crossing from one side to the other, and some days it's the better part of an hour and you en't hardly gone nowhere, you're still on the same old road when you look up.

Our nan: The better part of an hour! Her's never done, walking that.

Me: Not walking, Nan, on the tube mostly, but aye, an hour some days, easy. And the noise of it never stops neither. Down here of a dimpsy it goes proper quiet, don't it, birds and that, the odd car going home. Up there there's always summat going, all hours, sirens and that lot, you get so you don't hear it after a while, but I do notice it something chronic when I'm back down here, how quiet it goes of a night.

The eldest: So where you living then. Proper place or one of they boxes everyone's on about?

Me: One of they boxes, babber, if I'm honest with you. Smaller than what you're all picturing, I know that much, cos our kid asked me on the phone if I'd got a garden and I near laughed myself sick. It's about the size of the kitchen and front room put together and that's your lot, and you pay through the nose for it an' all. Rent alone's more than what Mother pays on this whole house in a month, easy double, maybe more.

Mother: Never.

Me: Straight up, tis. And the shopping's dear too, milk and bread and that, not so different, but do anything else, go out for a bit of tea or stand a round down the pub, and it's gert money for what you get back. I near choked first time I saw what one round cost me.

Our nan: Wisht. I couldn't be doing with that, not on my pension.

The eldest: Are the people alright though? Friendly and that, or not?

Me: That idn't a straight yes or no one, tell the truth. Some are lovely, proper lovely, get talking to you on the bus like they've known you years and want to hear your whole life story. Others you could stand next to for ten minutes and they wouldn't look up off their phone. It idn't unfriendly exactly, mind, it's just everyone's gone somewhere, in't they, everyone's in that much of a rush, so you learn quick who's got time for you and who en't. My workmate's proper good as gold, always looking out for me. The fella in the flat upstairs I've said about three words to in a whole year.

Mother: That's a funny old way to live, that.

Me: You do get used to it, see. There's a market near me, mind, does help a good bit, bit like ours only ten times the size of it, and once you know your stall, the one you always go to, they do know your face after a while and that's nice, that's proper. Half the veg off there's cheaper than the shop anyway, if you know where to look.

The eldest: And work, how's all that work then, cos our kid's on about maybe going up there herself.

Me: Depends what you're after. If you got a trade or you're in an office it idn't so bad, plenty going, but you got to be sharp about it, everyone's after the same jobs and there's ten of you queuing up for every one going spare. I do temp about a bit still, bar work and that, while the proper thing was sorting itself out. It idn't like here where you know a fella who knows a fella and it's all sorted over a pint before you've hardly asked.

Our nan: We do look after our own down here, always have.

Me: You do. I'll give you that one, gladly.

Mother: Did you get lost much, first off? I do think of you wandering about not knowing where's what, out there on your own.

Me: Lost proper, aye, three or four times at least, first couple of months I was there. I'd come up out the tube thinking I knew which way was which and be walking the wrong way entirely, ask some fella where's that to and he'd just point and carry on walking, not stop and take you there like they would down here. Took me a good while to get the layout of it sorted in my head, which lines go where, which way's which without having to think on it. Backalong I couldn't have told you left from right up there if you'd paid me. Now I do know it like the back of my hand, mostly, touch wood.

The eldest: And the food. Go on then, cos I bet you do miss a proper Sunday dinner something wisht.

Me: Oh, the food's a mixed bag if ever there was one. You can get anything up there, any hour, any sort, food from everywhere in the world practically on your doorstep, and some of it's proper lovely, better than what you'd get down here even, I'll say that honest. But a decent bit of cheese, or a proper loaf, or a good drop of zider like Nan's dad used to make out the back, you don't get that so easy, everything's either dear or it just idn't right somehow, not the same. And nobody up there's ever heard of scrumping. Tried explaining it to a fella at work once, sneaking over a wall for someone's apples, and he looked at me like I was proper mazed in the head.

Our nan: Course he did. Gert city fella, wouldn't know one end of an orchard from the other, would he.

Mother: So go on then, straight answer this time, no dodging round it. Is it better or worse than here?

Me: You're not getting me on that one, don't you try it.

Mother: Go on, just say!

Me: It's different, in't it, that's all I'll give you. Some things it's better, some things here's better by a mile, and I en't choosing between the pair of you, so don't ask me that again, not tonight nor any other night.

The eldest: You always do that, every time.

Me: Cos there en't a straight answer to it, is there, and if I said one you'd hold it against me forever, whichever way I went.

Our nan: So will you come back then, proper, for good like? Cos I do worry on it, you know, all that way off from us, and me not getting any younger neither.

Me: Honest answer, Nan? I don't rightly know. Some days I think I will, soon as I've got a bit put by, come back and get somewhere proper down here and not think twice on it again. Other days I think I've gone and got too settled up there now to shift it all back. I en't ruling it out, put it that way, not by a long chalk. Ask me again next year and see.

Mother: Mind you do, then.

Me: I will, mind.
