It was coming on dimpsy when we met by the shop, the pair of us leaning on the wall with a can of zider apiece, and I'd been putting this off longer than I ought.

Me: Look, I idn't trying to make a thing of it, babber, but that forty quid you had off me, that's five month gone now and more.

My mate: Oh, here we go.

Me: I'm not going on about it. I just want it sorted, mind. You know I do.

My mate: I know, I know. Work's been proper mental lately, I've not had two bob spare.

Me: You said that back in June an' all. You said you'd have it to me by the end of that month sure as anything, and June's backalong now, idn't it.

He went a bit teasy at that, kicking his daps against the wall and not looking at me.

My mate: I never said sure as anything. I said I'd try.

Me: You did say sure as anything, I remember it proper clear, and I do keep asking myself where that money's got to.

My mate: Well I don't remember it that way, and I don't much like you keeping score on me like I'm some kind of scrounger. We're mates, aren't we?

Me: Course we're mates. That's why I've waited this gert long time and not said nothing before now.

He went quiet a minute then, proper wisht-looking, like I'd caught him out and knew it well as he did.

My mate: I've got twenty on me now, I can give you that, and I'll have the rest to you by the end of the month.

Me: Twenty's twenty, and that's proper job as far as it goes. But mind you don't go mazed again and let it slide, cause I've heard end of the month before, see.
