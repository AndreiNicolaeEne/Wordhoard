The stall's up by half seven, kettle already going, bacon on the griddle afore anyone's even asked.

The stallholder: Alright my luvver, usual is it? Bacon roll, brown sauce, tea two sugars?

The regular: Go on then. Only do it in a bap today, none of they baguettes, my teeth idn't up to it this week.

The stallholder: Proper job, I got you, won't be a minute.

Bacon spitting away, and they get on same as always while it cooks.

The regular: Been a mazed old week, I tell ee. Her at work's had me covering two shifts, I'm proper wisht by Friday.

The stallholder: You do come up here what, three times a week now? Reckon you'll be sleeping standing up dreckly.

The regular: Four if her's paying me the overtime, babber.

The stallholder: Business been steady, mind, can't grumble. Weekends is gert busy, weekdays it's just you lot keeping me honest. Idn't like backalong when you'd queue right down past the daps shop.

The regular: Aw don't, you'll have me weeping in me tea.

The stallholder: Zider's sold out again an' all, second week running. Emmets come through and buy the lot, don't half know what they're doing with it neither.

The regular: Grockles, more like, up Clifton way.

The stallholder: Same difference, my ansum.

They both laugh, and the roll's wrapped and passed over the counter.

The stallholder: There you go. One bacon bap, none of yer fancy stuff. Two eighty, please.

The regular: There's three, keep it.

The stallholder: Ta, my luvver. Mind how you go now.

The regular: See you Thursday then, same as ever.

The stallholder: Aye, tis a date. I'll have the griddle going before you're even round the corner.
