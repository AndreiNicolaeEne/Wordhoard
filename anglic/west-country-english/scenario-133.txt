Right, so this is the whole of it, start to finish, the way the year on the plot went.

Come the back end of winter I was sat there of an evening trying to work out what to put in, going round and round it same as every year. In the end I kept it simple, mind: broad beans, a row of spuds, courgettes, a few tomato plants under the bit of polytunnel the fellow before me left up, strawberries along the front bed, and a row of sweet peas just for the look of it. Nothing gert fancy, just the usual, but done proper.

Didn't go putting nothing in till a good few weeks after the last cold snap, same as my nan always told I - you put it in too early round here and one sharp frost'll have the lot of it, and then where you to. So I held off, held off, and when I finally did put it all in the ground the weather turned proper kind on us. Week after week of it, warm and soft, and everything come up strong. I do go up the plot near every evening in a normal year just to check on things, but that spring I was up there twice a day and all, in me daps first thing before work and again after, couldn't hardly believe how well it was all going. The old boy on the plot next to I do reckon he hadn't seen a start like it in years, and he'd know, he's had that plot backalong since before I was born near enough.

First trouble come on the broad beans, blackfly, thick along the tops of near every stem. Tried the soft soap and water in the spray bottle, same as everyone tells you to, and it knocked it back a fair bit but never proper saw it off, not really. I was out there most mornings squeezing the little blighters off with my fingers and all, and you'd get it down to nothing and a week later it'd be back up the tops again. Beans still cropped decent in the end, so I idn't complaining too hard, but it never did go away entire, that blackfly, not the whole season.

Then come the dry stretch, right through the middle of the summer, weeks of it with hardly a drop out the sky. I was down there every single day with the cans, hauling water up from the tap by the gate, back and forth, back and forth, till my arms was fair done in. The heat had I proper mazed some days, forgetting what I'd already done and starting the row again from the top. You'd water of an evening, dimpsy coming on, and by dinnertime next day the ground was baked hard again like you never touched it. Teasy work, that, day after day the same.

And that's when the bother started with the water. There's a tap for four of us plots to share and it's meant to go in turns, everyone knows that, but the fellow on the plot below mine took to filling his big butt right up whenever he fancied, morning, noon, didn't matter, and her'd be running near onto the path with the overflow while I'd come down of an evening and find nothing left for my beans and courgettes, all of it gasping. Went on like that the best part of a fortnight before I said summat.

Me: You been on that tap again this morning, en't you.

The neighbour: Alright babber. Only filling the butt, same as always.

Me: Same as always is the trouble, see. Some of us got stuff in the ground actual dying of it while you're topping up a tank you idn't even using half of.

The neighbour: Didn't realise it was that bad down your end.

Me: Tis bad, proper bad. I've had to carry water up from the standpipe by the shed three days running.

The neighbour: Fair enough, my luvver. Tell you what, mornings mine, evenings yours, and we leave it at that.

Me: That'll do I fine.

And that's how it's stood ever since, mornings his, evenings mine, and no more bother over it, which I was glad of because nobody wants to be falling out with the bloke two plots up over a tap.

Best thing I grew all year, no question, was the courgettes. Put in three plants and near drowned in the things, giving them away to anyone who'd stand still long enough, marrows the size of your arm if you left one too long under a leaf without looking. Third one out the three, her never did stop cropping right through till the first frost took her. Proper job, that lot, best crop I've had off that plot since I took it on.

Strawberries was another matter. Netted them too late is what it was, and the birds had the best of the early ones before I got round to it, whole rows pecked to nothing overnight. Got the net over sharpish after that and saved what was left, but I lost a fair few to the blackbirds before I learned my lesson there, mind. Half expected to catch some kid scrumping off the plum tree on the corner plot while I was at it, same as I would have done meself at that age, but never did.

Middle of August we had that storm come through of a night, proper wind and rain both, and I come down the next morning to find near half the bean rows flat on the ground and the sweet peas pulled clean off their canes. Wisht sight, that was, first thing, standing there looking at it. Spent the best part of a weekend staking it all back up, tying it in again, propping the worst of the bean rows with bits of old fence post I had lying about. Lost a few plants outright but most of it come back up alright once it was tied in proper.

Come the back end of the year I sat down and tallied the lot of it up, and it weren't a bad haul at all when you put it all together, spuds and beans and a fair few sacks of courgettes and enough strawberries in the end to make jam twice over. Traded a good bagful of spuds and some of the last courgettes to the woman on the far plot for a few bottles of her zider, proper stuff, made off her own apples, which I was glad of because I never got round to putting in nothing of my own for that. Dug the last of it over end of October, put the manure on, covered the beds down for winter and left it to rest.

All told it were a proper good year on the plot. Hard work, see, more of it than you'd think sat here talking about it, what with the water and the birds and the storm and all, but there idn't much beats standing there of a dimpsy evening looking at a row you grew yourself, and I'll be back up there putting the beans in again come spring, same as always.
