The foreman: Before you lot bugger off, we do want tomorrow sorted else it'll get forgot over the zider.

The eldest: Go on then, what time we starting?

The young one: Half six, get ahead of it before it's baking up there.

The eldest: Half six, my babber? I idn't stood on a ladder at half six for nobody.

The driver: Seven's proper enough for anyone. I do have to drop the nipper off first anyway.

The foreman: Seven it is, and no arguing after.

The eldest: Proper.

The foreman: Right. It's the roof job over the top end, felt and battens, mind we get the ridge sorted before the weather turns, cos by dimpsy tonight that sky was looking proper wisht.

The young one: We got enough felt though? I heard we was running gert low on it.

The driver: There's a roll and a half left in the yard, and the tower's still up there and all, her's not been brought back down yet.

The foreman: I'll bring the cutter and the nail gun. Where's the long ladder to, you still got it?

The eldest: Got it. And who's doing the hip end then? That's two men's work and I idn't doing it on me tod.

The foreman: You are, my luvver, tis you or nobody, you're the only one small enough up there without the whole lot coming down. And wear proper boots, mind, not daps.

The eldest: Cheers for that. Proper job, that is.

The foreman: Right, headcount. Four of us, seven sharp, that's me, you two, and...

The driver: Not me tomorrow, I idn't able. Dentist first thing, been put off twice backalong already.

The foreman: Well that's buggered it then, idn't it.

The young one: You owe us one now, mind. Proper owe us.

The driver: I'll get the zider in tonight, call it square.

The foreman: Proper job. Seven then, and mind you're not late, else you're up the hip end and all.

The eldest: See you all at seven.
