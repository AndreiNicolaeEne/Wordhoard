Weren't but dimpsy when her went out the door, half seven she said, back for her tea, and now the clock's gone eleven and there idn't a sound on that step.

Half hour over, I says to myself, then hour, then two, walking it out loud round the front room same as if talking'd bring her quicker. "Tis prob'ly nothing," I says, "prob'ly gone in the offie with they girls from work for a can of zider and lost track of the time, that's all tis." Half believing it and all, because I do want to.

Up to the window, back to the chair, up to the door, listen, nothing, back to the chair. Keeps looking to they empty daps by the door like looking'll make 'em fill up on their own. Mazed I am, proper mazed, gone round they same four steps a dozen time now.

"Summat's happened," I says, and wish straight off I hadn't, because once tis said out loud it can't be unsaid, and my head's off down the wisht end of the road before I can catch it back.

I mind the time backalong when her granfer were three hour over from the yard and turned up with his arm in a sling and some tale about a forklift, and we did laugh about that after, proper laughed, but that were after, and this idn't after yet.

Half thought to get my coat on and go up the road and look for her myself, and then thought no, stay put, she'll come home to an empty house and no one in, that's worse. So I sat back down, teasy as anything.

Gone past four hour now. A car door outside, my heart up in my mouth before I've even stood, then footsteps going on past. Just the neighbour. Nothing.

Then the gate. Proper the gate this time, and the key scratching at it.

Me: "That you, my luvver?"

The eldest: "Alright. Bus never come, had to walk it, my phone's gone dead an' all."

Me: "Well, there's my babber, in you come, I've been sat here going proper teasy. Where was you even to?"

The eldest: "Told you, the bus."

Me: "Mind you charge that thing tonight. See you get some tea down you first."
