It start on the old man's verandah, down near the wharf side, one Friday when the boat done come in and everybody stap loose, no work tomorrow. We sitting round the table under the light, deck of cards going, Five Hundred, same as always. Me and my cousin one side, the old man and his wantok other side. Nothing serious, just talktalk and kaikai, cordial for the pikinini running round the yard, beer for the big mob.

Halfway through the second hand somebody joke around, "eh if you lose this one you gotta do something for us." Just joke, my friend, nothing behind it. But you know how that mob is, they hear blood in the water and they come.

The eldest: "Do something like what."
My cousin: "Twenty dollar, make it twenty dollar, that's fair."
The old man: "Twenty? Make it more, this one here he too flash with his cards."

I laugh it off, I say alright, twenty dollar then, small money, nobody gonna cry over that, and everybody settle on it before one more card even get thrown down. Twenty dollar bilong the bet, locked in, everybody witness. Me I was feeling good that night, I save play this game long time, so I go in confident, tokim them straight, "bai I take your money easy."

The old man's side they not so sure, but they still talking big, egging it on, more mob coming round from the other yard when they hear cards slapping and laughing loud. Ol man's grandkids stap watching from the step, olketa pikinini quiet for once cos they know something on.

Game itself, my friend, it went up and down long time. First few hand mipela winning, trump falling right, the old man he shake his head, "you two too lucky tonight." Then it turn round, his wantok start calling trump proper, taking trick after trick, and the room start getting loud, everybody calling out, "go on, go on!" Backwards and forwards like that, nobody clear ahead, right up till the hand that finish it.

That last hand, my cousin misjudge the bid, call too high, and I'm sitting there with cards that can't save it no matter what. I see it coming two card before it happen, that sinking feeling, and true enough, we go down, and we go down hard. Whole table erupt.

I groan out loud, my friend, no point hiding it. I put my cards down and I say, "yeah, yeah, you got us, that's it, we lus pinis." No use crying, game done finish, everybody see it clear.

Now here's where the story turn strange, and this the part you gotta listen good. Nobody wanted that twenty dollar no more. The old man wave his hand like the money nothing to him now. He look round at the mob standing there, all of them grinning, and he say the payment gonna be a performance, right there in front of everybody, not some quiet handshake thing behind the house.

The eldest: "Twenty dollar too easy for you now. You gonna sing."
The old man: "Sing that island love song, the one everybody's grandmother like, and you gotta dance it too, proper hip movement, not just standing there like a stick."
My cousin: "And you gotta wear something for it. Can't just do it in your work shirt."

They name everything right there, nothing left loose, so nobody could argue after. I try one last time to talk them down, I say, "come on, just take the twenty, I'll pay now, cash, we finish," and the whole yard just laugh at me, laugh tumas, like I say the funniest thing all night.

The old man: "No way, my friend. Twenty dollar bai come and go, but this one bai they still talking about at Christmas."

So that's how it stand. No bargain, no way out. They set the day, next Saturday, at the hall when the big family gathering on for the old lady's birthday, whole community mob gonna be there, from the little pikinini right up to the old ones sitting front row with their walking stick.

Saturday come round quick. Hall full up, tables all set, kaikai everywhere, and me I'm standing out back sweating, wearing this grass skirt thing my aunty dig up from somewhere, coconut shell bra top she make quick quick that morning laughing the whole time she sewing it. Someone call out my turn, whole hall go quiet, then somebody hit play on the music, that old island love song everybody know since pikinini days, and out I go.
