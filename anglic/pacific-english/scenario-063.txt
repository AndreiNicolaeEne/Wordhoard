I gotta tell you this one. Ol' story now, must be two year back, but I still get little bit wild in me head thinking about it. You member that big blow we had, whole island losim power, coconut tree down by the church snap clean in half? Same night, that one. Wind coming solid from about nine o'clock, rain sideways, and me I just stap inside listening the roof lifting little bit every gust come through. Bin pray the roof holdim tight, cos them old iron house, they shake but they no fly away, that's the main thing you hoping for.

Morning time I go out early, still dark-dark, just to check the yard, and straight away I see em. Fence, my fence, my side, all pushim down flat, post split up, wire all tangle up like net after big fish bin caught in there. And laying right across the top, whole big almond tree, whole crown of em down in my yard, takim half my banana with it too.

First thing I do, I walkim the line, working out whose tree that one, cos round here fence line and tree line don't always sit together proper. And I see em, that tree bin growing right on the boundary self, little bit lean already even before the wind, roots half my side half their side, but the trunk, the main trunk, that stap their side, right by their steps. So that one, that's their tree, no argument. Everybody who look can see em.

So I go round, knock the door, tell the neighbour, look, your tree bin come down, crushim my fence proper bad. And straight up, first thing out of the neighbour's mouth...

The neighbour: That's just the wind, ay. Nobody's fault that one. Wind do what wind do.

Me: Wind never plant that tree right on the line though. And that tree bin leaning long time already, everybody could see em leaning.

The neighbour: Leaning don't mean falling. Tree always lean little bit round here, salt air do that to em.

And I'm standing there thinking, this one gonna be a long one now. Cos I remember plain, the aunty from up the road, she bin tell that neighbour, must be a year before, "That tree gotta come down or cut back before em fall on somebody." I was there self when she said it, standing right by the gate. So I bring that up too.

Me: Aunty told you already, long time. She said cut em back before em fall.

The neighbour: Aunty talk to everybody about everything. Don't mean I gotta listen every word she say.

So that's how it start, and that's how it stop, for a good while too. Nobody backing down. Me I'm saying, your tree, your job fix my fence. Neighbour saying, act of god that one, wind's doing, not paying for nothing. We go round and round like that for near a week, fence still down the whole time, dog getting through both yards, the kids' ball rolling straight over and back, everybody watching us two not talk proper over the hedge no more.

Then the old man from two doors up, the one who fix everybody outboard motor, he come round one evening, nobody bin call him, he just come self. Sit down on my step, have cup of tea, don't even bring up the fence straight off, just toktok this and that, weather, fishing been bad lately. Then after little while he says, quiet like...

The old man: You know, that fence there, that one old already before the wind even touch em. I member your old man put that one up, must be fifteen year now. Wire all rust through the middle part anyway.

Me: True, that part true.

The old man: And that tree, that one grow up natural right on the line self, nobody plant that one to spite nobody. Tree don't know whose side it's on.

So what he doing, see, he giving both of us mob a way to come down that don't make neither one look like they lost. He telling the neighbour, without saying em straight out, that fence was old and due to go anyway, that this not just about your tree, everybody due fix that fence regardless. And he telling me, quiet, that this neighbour not the type to run from a job, just proud, needing a way to come round without looking like they cave in first.

Next day the neighbour come round self, no old man there this time, and just says...

The neighbour: Split the wire and post, new ones, half half. Tree clearing I do myself, my tree my mess.

And that was em. Nobody had to say sorry, nobody had to say who was right proper. Just half half, and get on with the work.

First morning we work together, oh, stiff. Real stiff that one. Both of us out there with chainsaw and bushknife, cutting up that big almond, not talking much, just "pass that one," "watch the branch there," like that. You could feel em still, that little bit of pride still sitting between us. Hour, two hour, just workim, sweat, no proper toktok at all.

Then round about smoko time, the neighbour's wife bin bring out cup of tea for both of us, and little bit of damper too, still warm from the fire, and she just put em down on the sawhorse, no fuss, no big talk, only "here, you fellas eat something," and walk back inside. That's the one done it I reckon. Small thing, but you could feel the whole yard go soft after that, like the wind gone right out of it finally, the argument wind I mean, not the real one.

After that we work good together, real good. Neighbour handim me the post-hole digger before I even ask for em, I passim back the good hammer when their one broke down. Two family working like one mob by the second day, kids running round both yards again, dog going through the gap that wasn't even fixed yet and nobody minding at all.

Took us about four day all up, working evening time and weekend, to get the whole thing back up proper. New post, new wire, tight this time, and we cut that tree right back too, this time for good, not just trim, cos nobody want to go through that one again.

And that fence now, that one stronger than the old one ever was, pinis. Straight line, wire tight, post set deep down. Sometime I think that wind done us a favour, long way round, even though nobody like to say that out loud.

These days, me and that neighbour, real good now. Better than before I reckon, cos before we was just neighbour, say g'day over the fence, that's all. Now we sitting down together sometime, cup of tea, proper toktok. The kids from both yards, they just one mob now, in and out both house like it's one place altogether. Fence still stap there strong, but nobody really thinking about the fence no more. That's how you know em right, that whole thing, when you stop looking at the thing that started it all in the first place.
