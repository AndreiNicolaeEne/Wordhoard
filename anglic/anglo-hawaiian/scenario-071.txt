Eh brah, you wen ask me how was, that whole ting with the tree. I tell you, was one day.

Was the big monkeypod, been standing back of the camp houses since before any of us wen move in, trunk fat like one car. One big wind night wen come through, knock the whole ting down flat right across the ditch by the end of the street. Roots up and everything. And when he fall he wen take out one whole section of the drain wall too, so now the ditch stay all choke up with mud and branch. Had already plenty rain that month, everybody was scared what more rain gonna do to that street if the ditch no can run. Garans, if nobody clear that thing, the whole camp gonna flood. That kind job, no way one house can do by themself. Too big fo one family, too heavy fo two guys with one borrow chainsaw.

Was the uncle from the corner house, the one always up early doing his garden, he wen go out first thing, see the tree down, and he no even go back inside change his slippers, he just start walking up the street banging on doors. By the time he reach the far end, maybe twenty minutes, everybody already know. Word go up and down that street faster than any phone call, cause everybody's windows was open anyway on account of the heat.

Me, I wen think maybe seven, eight guys gonna show, the usual ones. But brah, by seven-thirty I count, was like twenty-two, twenty-three people standing out there already. I nevah see that many people turn out for one thing, not since I was small. Old timers, guys still in they work clothes before they go job, ladies with the kids still in slippers, even the haole family from the end of the street wen come out help, and some people from the next street over wen hear and just walk over too.

So then was figuring who do what. The uncle who wen call everybody, he say...

The uncle: We gotta cut um up first before we can move um, no can just drag the whole tree. I go call the guy with the flatbed, ask him come help with the big pieces.

The man across the street, he get the chainsaw, so he stay the one doing most of the cutting. But the real heavy lifting, was the big guy from down the street who do landscaping for his job, he wen take on rolling the biggest sections down to the truck near enough by himself. Nobody else can even budge them kind logs. He just stay out there the whole day, sweat soaking through his shirt, not even complaining. Everybody wen tell him slow down, drink water, but he no like stop, he just say...

The one doing the heavy lifting: I almost pau this section, let me finish um first.

So we get some guys cutting, some guys clearing branch, ladies organizing water and towels, and then the ditch itself, that was its own job, digging out all the mud that wen pile up from the busted wall.

Was going good maybe two hours, then the small disaster. Couple guys wen try roll one big log up onto the truck bed using two planks like a ramp, and the plank wen snap, log roll back down fast, near take out the tailgate, almost catch somebody's foot too. Everybody wen jump back, one titah scream, but was okay, nobody wen get hurt, just scared everybody good. After that they wen slow down, get more guys hold the planks steady.

That's when the argument wen start too, about how fo clear the ditch, some guys say scoop the mud first before moving more logs, other guys say clear the logs first so got room fo work.

The neighbor: No, you gotta clear the ditch first, if not the water no can even run when the rain come.

The other neighbor: But how you gonna clear ditch when you still get logs sitting on top of um, that's lolo, do the log first.

Was going back and forth, little bit hot, till the auntie down the end wen just walk over and say...

The auntie: Eh, you guys, just split up. Half go log, half go ditch, same time. What, only one ditch in the whole world?

That wen settle um just like that, everybody wen laugh little bit and get back to work.

By that time the food wen start coming out too. The mother from two houses down wen bring out one big pot rice and some kalbi she had marinating since morning, and one titah from three houses down wen bring musubi and cold drinks, whatever was in the icebox. People just eating standing up, plate in one hand, working with the other. Was choke food by the afternoon, more than anybody could finish, grindz just sitting out on the table under the carport for whoever get hungry.

The kids, they wen get they own job, picking up all the small stick and leaf and putting um in bags, running back and forth like was one game, arguing who get more bags fill up.

And the eldest on the street, the one everybody know stay past ninety, he wen come out with his cane and say he not just gonna sit on the porch and watch. Everybody wen try tell him rest, but he no like, so they wen give him a job holding the hose steady, hosing down the mud so wasn't so heavy fo shovel. He wen take that job serious too, like was the most important one there.

The one moment everybody wen laugh together at the same time, was when the chainsaw guy wen cut through one section and one whole nest of mynah birds wen fall out with um, birds flying everywhere squawking, and the guy wen jump back so fast he wen fall right on his okole in the mud. Everybody just wen bust up, even him. Broke da mout laughing, some guys had to sit down they was laughing so hard, tears and everything.

We wen figure the whole thing gonna be pau by early afternoon, maybe one, two o'clock. But was more like five, five-thirty before everything really finish, tree all cut up and hauled, ditch all clear, drain wall stack back with da kine block somebody had leftover from another job. Three hours later than anybody thought, easy.

But when it wen finally pau, somebody wen give one shout and everybody just wen cheer right there in the street, all tired and dirty and happy. I get chicken skin just thinking about um now. We wen get the whole tree cleared, the ditch dug all the way clean, drain wall built back up good, plus we wen fix that pothole that was there forever too since everybody was already out there with tools anyway.

That evening the whole street wen feel different, I telling you. People sitting out on they porches longer than usual, just talk story, kids still running around even after dark. Nobody wanted to go inside right away. Was only that one evening, maybe, but for that one evening, felt like the whole street was one house.

Was one long day, yeah?
