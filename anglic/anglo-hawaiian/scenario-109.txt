The screen door stay stuck little bit like always, and I gotta shoulder um open, and for one second nobody even look up from what they doing. Then the mother turn from the stove with the spoon still in her hand and her whole face just wen break, like she seeing something she cannot believe stay real.

The mother: Oh my God. Oh my GOD. Is dat you? Ho, look who stay home, look who STAY HOME--

She drop the spoon right in the pot, no even care um clattering on the floor, and she coming at me fast, both arms already up before she even reach.

The mother: Ay, get ova here, come, come come come, lemme look at you--

She grab me and she not letting go. I try for pull back little bit just fo breathe and she squeeze tighter every time, like if she let go I go disappear again.

The mother: I not letting you go. No. You stay right here little bit.

The eldest come round from the kitchen with the dish towel still hanging off one shoulder and just stop, staring.

The eldest: Ho. HO. Look at this guy. You wen get so big, brah, what you was eating ova there, whole cow?

Me: Shaddup, I stay same size I always was.

The eldest: No you not, look at your shoulders, look at your face even, you look like one whole different person, garans.

And then everybody stay talking at once, all the questions coming from every side same time, nobody even waiting fo the answer before the next one already coming--

The auntie: Eh, when you wen land? Who wen pick you up from the airport? You get bags, where your bags--

The father, still in his chair, not even getting up yet, just calling out: You wen eat already or what, you like eat?

The eldest: You still get dat job or you wen quit?

The auntie: You seeing anybody ova there? You no like tell us nothing yeah?

Me: Wait, wait, one at a time, I no can answer everybody same time--

The auntie stay wiping her eyes fast with the back of her hand, trying fo laugh um off like nothing.

The auntie: I not crying. My eyes just itchy, get dust in here or something--

Nobody fooled, her voice all thick already, and she turn her head little bit fo wipe again before she come grab me in one hug too, tight, quick, like she embarrassed of it.

By the auntie's leg the littlest one stay watching everything, not coming close. Too little fo really remember, and shy of all the noise and the fuss everybody making.

The mother: Ay, come say hi. You remember, ah -- was so small last time--

The littlest one just shake the head and duck further behind the leg.

The mother: Leave um, leave um, he shy. He go come around.

Me, sniffing the air: Ho, get kalua pig cooking or what? Smell just like -- ho, I stay getting chicken skin just from the smell of um.

The mother: I wen put um in early this morning already. I knew you was coming, I wen tell everybody, he like his grindz first thing when he walk in that door, so I wen make plenty.

The bags stay right by the door still where I wen drop them, forgotten completely, nobody even looking at them.

The eldest: Oh, you no even know yet -- titah wen get married last year, ah. Small kine wedding but was real nice.

Me: Get out, fo real?

The eldest: Fo real. And da kine, the cousin, he wen grow like one whole foot since you left, he stay taller than me now, lolo kid.

The auntie: And the family from the other side of camp, they wen move Mainland side already, two years now, the house stay empty.

Me: Everything just wen keep going without me, ah.

The mother: Of course um wen keep going. Life no stop just cuz you stay gone.

I look around the room slow.

Me: But dis, the house, stay exactly the same. Same couch, same picture frame on the wall, same crack right there by the door I always used to bang my head on.

The father: We wen paint um one time.

Me: No you never.

The father: Okay. We wen think about paint um.

Everybody laugh at that, even the littlest one peeking out little bit now.

Me: But outside, ho, everything else stay so different. The mill stay gone completely already, get one whole new building where the old store used to be.

The mother: Yeah, plenty stay different now. But not us.

The father: So how long you can stay this time?

Me: I only get two weeks.

The mother: Two weeks? That's it?

The father: Well, we go make um count then. I stay pau hana already anyway, not going back till Monday, so.

The eldest: I already thinking, we should do one big potluck, get everybody come, make choke grindz--

The mother: Yeah, I go call titah, I go call everybody, we go do um this Sunday, get plenty time fo cook proper.

The auntie: I go bring the poke.

Me: Ho, was thinking about your poke the whole time I stay gone, no joke.

The auntie: I know, dat's why I making um first thing, before anything else.

Me: Was rough at first ova there, no joke. All the haole guys at the job stay so stiff, no small talk or nothing, everybody just quiet, keeping to themself.

The mother: Had one time we thought you nevah coming back at all, you know. You wen go so quiet on the phone.

Me: I know. I sorry.

The eldest, grinning wide already: Eh, remember what we used to call you? Buggah.

Me: No. Ho. Not dat again.

The eldest: Buggah! Buggah, come home already--

Me: I nevah miss dat one, garans.

The mother, laughing: He was always doing something lolo, dat's why we wen call him dat in the first place.

For one moment though, all dat just stop. Everybody stay looking at me, quiet, all at once, like they checking I was really there, really standing in that kitchen and not just one dream somebody wen have too many times already.

Nobody saying nothing. Just looking.

Then the mother wipe her hands down her apron, one more time, slow.

The mother: Okay. Okay. Come, sit down, eat something first, you can talk story the rest of the night, we get time now.

And everybody move slower after that, like the whole room finally letting out one breath it wen been holding since the door first wen open. We go sit down together, and it stay calm finally, just us, home.
