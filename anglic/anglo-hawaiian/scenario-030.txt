Me: Auntie, I stay tryna make your chicken hekka an' I nevah wen write um down when you wen show me. Can tell me again, from da top?

The auntie: Ho, again? Okay okay, no rush me. First ting, you get da shoyu, maybe one cup, little bit more if you like um more salty. Put um one bowl first, mix um wit da sugar.

Me: How much sugar you put?

The auntie: Eh... maybe half cup, little less even. You taste um as you go, if not sweet enough you add more, yeah?

Me: Okay. What else go in.

The auntie: Ginger, oh, I almost forget da ginger! Cannot forget dat one, small piece, smash um good first. Grandma always put dat in, if you no put um da whole ting stay flat, no taste right at all.

Me: So shoyu, sugar, ginger. Den what.

The auntie: You brown da chicken first, little bit oil in da pan. Den da onion go in, let um go soft. Den you pour da shoyu mix ova top, den da long rice, den da watercress last, cuz dat one wilt fast, no like put um too early.

Me: And how long you cook um for?

The auntie: Dat's da part, no can rush dat one, garans. Grandma always tell, "no rush da hekka," gotta let um simmer low, slow, or da chicken come tough an' dry. She used to say if you stay rushing um, might as well throw da whole pot out.

Me: How you know when pau?

The auntie: No need one clock for dat. When da kitchen stay all smell sweet an' shoyu-kine, an' da noodles come clear, dat's when. An' da sugar part, I gotta be honest, I nevah really measure dat one, I just go by eye, till da color come dark brown, dat's how Grandma wen do um too, she nevah measure nothing.

Me: Can I use pork instead da chicken?

The auntie: Mmm... can, yeah, can do dat, but no really da same ting. Still ono, just different, yeah?
