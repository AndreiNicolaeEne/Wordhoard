Ho, dis one hard for tell but I go tell um anyway, start to finish, cause everybody wen ask me already like three four times.

Was one afternoon, stay hot an sticky, regular kine day, nothing special. Then round two o'clock the sky wen go still. Not just calm, but still, like everything stay holding they breath. Get dat green-grey color up in the clouds, and no more one bird singing, no more nothing. My mother wen come out the kitchen wiping her hands on her apron, stay look up, and she tell...

Mother: "Ho, no like da kine. Something coming."

And garans, was coming. Within the hour the wind wen start pick up, first just rustling the mango tree out back, then more, then more, till was whipping the clothes off the line before we even wen think fo bring um in. I wen run outside try grab watever I could, the plastic chairs, the hose, my slippers by the door, all da kine junk we get lying around, cause you no like nothing flying round when da wind really come up. The old man was up on one ladder trying tie down the tarp on the carport, and my mother stay yelling at him fo come down already, come down, no be lolo up there.

Old man: "Wait, wait, one more tie, then I stay done."

Mother: "You no can wait, get inside now, the wind stay too strong already!"

By the time he wen climb down, the wind was really rising, howling through the camp like something alive. Choke branches already coming down all over, we could hear um cracking somewhere down the road, and somebody said after had one whole carport gone few houses down before ours even wen touch. We wen gather everybody in the front room, all of us, the grandmother in her chair with her rosary, the little one on my lap, the eldest holding the flashlight even though was still daylight outside, just in case. Get that feeling in your stomach, yeah, when everybody stay quiet and just listening.

Then the power wen go out. Just like dat, click, and the fridge stopped humming and the whole house wen go dark except what light was coming through the shutters we nevah wen close yet.

The eldest: "Ho, dat wasn't even the worst part yet and already no more lights."

We wen laugh little bit, nervous kine laugh, but nobody really laughing inside.

The wind got so loud, brah, so loud you couldn't even talk story no more. You had fo lean close, yell in somebody's ear just fo say pass the water. The whole house was shaking, the windows rattling in they frames, and then we wen hear this crack, one huge crack, like the whole sky stay breaking, and one tree wen come down. Not on top the house, thank god, but close, close enough we wen feel um through the floor, that thud, and my mother wen grab the little one so fast, hold um so tight.

Little one: "Was that our house?"

Mother: "No, no, stay okay, stay okay, shh."

But her voice was shaking too, I heard um.

Then part of the roof, the corner over the back room, wen just strip off. We wen hear it go, this ripping sound, metal against metal, and rain wen start coming in sideways, pooling on the floor by the back door. The old man wen try put one bucket there but the wind was pushing the rain in so hard the bucket wasn't doing nothing. And then the window in the kitchen, the one dat always stick when you try open um, wen crack, one long crack right across, but it wen hold. It wen hold, and I remember just staring at dat crack thinking please, please no break all the way.

I no going lie, I was scared. Real scared, chicken skin all up my arms, and I nevah say nothing about it dat night, nobody did, we just wen sit there close together, the grandmother praying quiet in the corner, the eldest holding the little one's hand, my mother's hand on my back the whole time like she stay checking I still there. Only after, days after, when we stay talk story about um, everybody wen admit it.

Titah: "Ho, I was so scared I thought we all going die in there, garans."

Me: "Same, same, I nevah tell nobody dat night but I was praying too."

And then, all sudden, the wind wen just... stop. Not slow down, just stop, like somebody wen turn off one switch. The quiet after was almost worse than the noise, cause you no know what stay waiting outside. We all wen sit there little while longer, nobody talking, just listening fo make sure was really pau.

Old man: "Stay pau, I think. Let's go check."

We wen open the door slow and step outside into dis grey, dripping world. Leaves and branches everywhere, roofing metal twisted up in the yard like paper, and right there blocking our driveway, the whole way out, was dat same tree dat wen come down, one huge old monkeypod, roots up in the air and everything. No way we could move um ourself, had fo wait fo the neighbor guy with the chainsaw come clear um few days later.

The roof, ho, the roof was bad. Whole corner section gone, you could see clear into the back room from outside, insulation hanging down all wet and sad looking. Dat wasn't going be one quick fix, dat was real repair, contractor kine repair.

But den we wen look next door, at the neighbor's place, dat haole guy stay renting the corner house, and his fence, the whole fence, was flat. Completely flat, laid down like one wave wen just roll over um and press um into the grass. And I remember thinking, eh, compared to what we wen lose, dat's nothing, dat's just fence, he can put um back up in one weekend. Us, we had one roof needing real work, one tree blocking the driveway, one cracked window still had fo replace.

The next few days was all cleanup, all of us out there, the whole camp really, everybody out there together, sharing chainsaws, sharing tarps, somebody's auntie bringing grindz fo everybody working, rice and teri chicken in big foil pans, broke da mout kine even though was just simple food, cause you cannot clean up all day on one empty stomach. The old man up on the roof again, dis time with help, my mother sweeping water out the back room over and over, the little one trying help carry small branches and getting in everybody's way, but nobody wen tell um stop cause was good, was good he wanted help.

And now when I tell the story, when people ask me about dat storm, I still catch myself doing um, still get to the part about the tree coming down and I gotta say ho, or the roof stripping off and I gotta say ho again, like I stay right back there hearing um. My friend always tease me about it.

Friend: "You tell dis story like five times already and you still going 'ho' every time, yeah?"

Me: "No can help um, brah. Some kine things you nevah stop feeling when you tell um."

And dats true. Every time. Garans.
