Eh, you wen ask me what mornings stay like in dis house, so sit down, cause once I start talk story bout dis I no can stop, get too much stuff happen every single day, same time, same order, like clockwork but nobody wen wind da clock.

First one up, always, before anybody, before even da first alarm start beeping, dat's the old one. Rain or shine, she up when still dark outside, when even da roosters no stay talking yet. She nevah need nobody fo tell her get up, her body just wen learn da time long time ago and nevah forget um. She make her way out to da lanai first thing, and she check da plants. Just check um. Touch da soil, see if need water, pick off one dead leaf here, one dead leaf dere, talk to um little bit under her breath. Five minutes, maybe, but she no skip um, not once, not even da morning she wen have da flu bad. I ask her one time why she gotta do um so early when everybody else still sleeping, and she just look at me like I stay lolo fo even ask.

The old one: "Cannot let um wait. Everything need checking befo everything else need checking."

Bout twenty minutes after dat, you start hear da grumble. Same grumble, every single morning, coming from da back room, low and rumbly like one engine no like turn ovah. That's da husband. He nevah did like mornings, still no like um, and probably nevah gon like um. Every day he make da same sound getting up, complaining bout something, da pillow too flat, da floor too cold, da blanket wen slide off, don't matter what, he just complain fo complain, get da grumble out his system befo he can face da day proper.

And right on top of dat starts da wash-room war. Every single morning, him and da eldest, both of dem wanting go first, and nobody evah win cause nobody evah give up either.

The husband: "I get work, I go first."
The eldest: "You always say dat. Everybody get work."

Dey go back and forth like dat two three times, den one of dem just barge in and lock da door, and da other one stay outside banging, but nothing evah get settled. Garans tomorrow same thing happen again, word for word almost.

Once dat war stay pau, at least for da moment, den come da kids, and dey wake up in da same order every time, no matter what. First da eldest, cause she stay oldest and suppose to set example. Den da middle one, dragging feet da whole way. Den, last, always last, da little one, who can sleep through one earthquake if you let um, garans.

And da calling out, same order, same words, every single morning, like one chant I no can help but say even when I stay half asleep myself:

Me: "Get up!"
Me: "Brush your teeth!"
Me: "Where your shoes?"

Always dose three, always dat order, and if I switch um up even one day I feel like something gon go wrong.

And every morning, close to da same time, close to da same words, get one argument, always bout da same thing, who wen use up all da hot water.

The middle one: "Somebody wen use um all up again!"
The eldest: "Not me, I only shower two minutes!"

Nevah once dat argument get settled, but nevah once anybody stay really mad about um either, it just part of da noise, like background music you no even notice no more.

Get one dog too, and she get her own job in all dis, though nobody wen teach her um, she just wen figure um out herself long time ago. Minute she hear da rice cooker click, she stay planted right by da kitchen door, tail going choke fast, waiting fo whoever gon drop one piece of Spam, and garans somebody always do, on purpose or not.

Breakfast stay da same, every day, rice and eggs and whatevah else stay in da fridge, sometimes Spam, sometimes hot dog, sometimes just shoyu on top, but always rice, always eggs, dat nevah change. And da old one, she nevah once vary her order in her whole life. Plain rice, one umeboshi, one cup hot tea, dat's um, every single morning, rain or shine, she no like nothing else, and if you offer her something different she just wave her hand at you like you stay talking nonsense.

Every morning too, without fail, something go missing. Most times um da car key, sometimes um just one slippah, one, nevah both, and everybody go hunting da same three places, top da rice cooker, inside da couch cushions, and right by da door where um suppose to be in da first place but somehow nevah is. Ten minutes lost every morning to dat same hunt, and still nobody wen learn put um back where um belong.

But da sound dat tell you everybody stay finally, properly awake, dat's da radio. Minute dat radio click on in da kitchen, dat's um, dat's how you know da whole house stay up and moving fo real, not just half-up like befo, still stumbling round in da dark bumping into furniture.

Da middle one always running late, same few minutes every single morning, no matter how early anybody wake her up first. She could get up one hour early and still she gon be dat same few minutes behind everybody else. Nobody even bother chase her no more, we just build da time into da whole morning now.

Den come da rush right at da door, bags grabbed, lunch handed over, one slippah found just in time cause somebody wen check da couch cushions one more time, and nothing evah get left behind only cause everybody check um three times befo anybody step outside. Choke noise, choke movement, all in da space of maybe two minutes, and den, just like dat, quiet again.

Sunday though, da whole thing change. Whole house go quiet, nobody rush, nobody grumble da same way. Da old one still up first, but she no check da plants da same way that morning, she just sit wit her tea longer, let da light come up slow around her. Feel different. Good different. Like da house stay taking one breath.

But every other morning, right befo everybody walk out da door, same words get said, every time, no matter who saying um to who...

Me: "Drive safe, yeah?"

And honestly, telling you all dis now, sitting here wit you, I still find um funny, da whole thing. Same performance, same order, same fights ovah da same hot water, same hunt fo da same missing slippah, every single day, like we stay rehearsing one play nobody evah gon perform for real, and everybody already know all dea lines by heart. You nevah seen one morning of it yet, but now you know how um go, and I garans you, tomorrow um go exactly da same.
