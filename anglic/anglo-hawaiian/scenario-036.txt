Eh so you nevah come, yeah, so let me talk story about da whole ting from da start cuz was one whole saga, garans.

So get this, every year was my auntie hosting, every single year, and this year she wen tell me, "eh, your turn already, I stay tired." And I wen laugh first cuz I thought she was joke, but she wasn't joke, she serious. So I wen say okay, I go do um. And right after I wen say yes I get one bad feeling in my stomach already, like, what I wen just sign up for. My wife stay looking at me like, "you sure or what," and I stay like, "yeah yeah, no worry, I got um." I nevah got um. Not even close.

So first ting, da menu. I wen sit down with one paper and one pen like I stay planning one wedding or someting. Kalua pig, but not from da imu, from da oven cuz no more room fo dig one pit in da yard. Lau lau, three kine, pork, chicken, and one fish fo da titah who no eat meat. Poke, two kine, cuz get people who like da spicy and get people who no like. Chicken long rice. Lomi salmon. Haupia fo dessert, and den my wife wen add mochi rice too cuz her mother stay expecting um. By da time I stay done writing, get like fourteen dishes on one paper fo what was supposed to be one simple potluck-kine ting. My neighbor wen look at da list and go,

The neighbor: "Eh, you feeding one army or what."

Me: "No, just da family."

The neighbor: "Same ting, brah."

And das before da guest count even wen blow up. First I stay thinking maybe twelve, fourteen people, just close family. Den my auntie wen invite her whole church group cuz "dey always come, dey no can not come." Den my wife's side wen hear get one gathering and dey wen invite deyself too, which, fine, but nobody wen tell me till three days before. By da end we get like thirty-two people confirmed and probably five more who go just show up cuz dat's how um go. Thirty-two people. Fo one first time hosting. Lolo, I know.

So da shopping trip. Choke stuff fo buy, and I stay running from da regular store to da Filipino store to da one Japanese market fo get certain kine ingredients, and everyting stay fine until I stay looking fo fresh ti leaves fo wrap da lau lau. No more. Not at da first store, not at da second store, not even at da farmer's stand where my cousin usually get um. I stay driving around town like one crazy person asking everybody,

Me: "Eh you know where get ti leaf?"

The one at the counter: "Try da flower shop by da highway."

And das where I wen finally find um, at one flower shop, of all places, sitting next to da orchids like dey stay embarrassed to be there. I wen buy da whole bunch dey had, no joke.

Night before, I stay up till like two in da morning. Prepping da meat, marinating, chopping onion till my eyes stay burning, wrapping lau lau with my fingers all cramped up. My wife wen pass out on da couch at midnight, tapped out, and I stay there alone in da kitchen thinking, "why I wen say yes to dis." I had one whole schedule wrote out on paper, six a.m. start da imu-style oven thing, seven put in da pig, eight prep da poke, nine steam da rice, and so on, real organized, real proud of myself.

Dat schedule nevah survive contact with da actual morning. Not even close. I wen oversleep by forty minutes cuz I stay so tired from da night before, and everyting wen shift back. Den my sister wen call asking if she can bring her mother-in-law too, and I stay on da phone instead of starting da oven. By eight a.m. I stay already one hour behind and stressing.

And den, da disaster. Da chicken long rice, I had um on da stove on low, or I thought was low, but somebody. I not gon say who but it was my wife's uncle who wen "help" by turning up da stove cuz he thought da rice was cooking too slow, da bottom wen burn black, whole pot, ruined. Da smell wen fill up da whole house, dat burnt-rice smell dat no can hide no matter how many windows you open. I stay standing there looking at dis pot like somebody wen die.

The uncle: "Eh, I nevah touch nothing, I swear."

Me: "Da knob stay on high, uncle."

The uncle: "...oh."

So now I gotta make one whole new pot from scratch, and I get like two hours left before people stay coming. I stay scrambling, sending my nephew to go buy more chicken broth cuz I nevah account fo doubling da batch, stirring like mad, praying.

Right in da middle of dis chaos, my wife's workmate wen show up early, like way early, forty-five minutes early, and instead of stay sitting around awkward, she wen just roll up her sleeves and jump in.

The workmate: "Eh, what you need me fo do."

Me: "Cut dis, stir dat, watch da oven no burn."

The workmate: "Got um."

And honestly she wen save my life dat morning, chopping cabbage and watching da rice while I stay dealing wit da oven situation, cuz by now da oven get da pig going, da lau lau steaming on top of da stove, da haupia trying fo set in da fridge but da fridge stay so packed nothing can chill properly, and every burner stay taken so da poke stay sitting out longer than I like. Kitchen stay like one battlefield, pots everywhere, counter no more space, my wife stay yelling from da other room asking where da folding tables at.

But get one bright spot, da poke. I nevah expect much cuz I wen throw um together kinda rushed, but somehow da shoyu-ahi one wen come out perfect, da onion and da limu balanced just right, and when my titah wen taste um she wen make one face like she wen see God.

The eldest: "Who wen make dis poke."

Me: "Me."

The eldest: "You for real? Dis one broke da mout, brah."

Chicken skin, honest, hearing dat from her.

So finally, after all dat, da food wen come out maybe hour and a half late from when I first told people. People stay hungry, stay talking story in da yard, kids stay running around, and finally we wen call everybody fo come eat. First bite, get dat quiet moment where everybody just stay chewing and nodding, and den da comments start coming.

The mother: "Ooh, da kalua pig stay moist, good job."

The neighbor: "Lau lau stay ono, who wen wrap dis."

And den, of course, someone gotta give da blunt verdict on da one dish dat nevah work, da long rice, even after da redo, wen come out little bit watery cuz I wen rush um.

The uncle: "Eh, no offense, but dis long rice stay kinda soupy, yeah?"

Me: "Yeah, I know, had one incident."

The uncle: "Garans. I can tell."

But da poke, ho, da poke wen disappear first, gone in like twenty minutes, people going back fo seconds, thirds, my titah telling everybody fo try um before um all pau.

The workmate: "Eh who make dis poke again, dis stay da best one I wen eat in long time."

By da end of da night, everyting stay pau, dishes stay pau, people stay full and happy and talking story on da porch till late, and my wife wen lean over and go,

My wife: "So, you go host again next year?"

Me: "No can. Never again. ...Ask me again next month doe."

My wife: "Yeah, dats what I thought."

And everybody wen laugh cuz dey know, dey already know I go do um again. But garans, next time I stay hiding da stove knobs.
