Me:

Get one thing dat always come up, yeah, when da young ones stay hanging around late, after grindz, when everybody sitting on da steps and da mosquito stay biting and nobody like go home yet. Somebody new to town, or somebody who wen move here from town side, dey ask, "Eh, how come nobody like walk da ditch road after dark?" And dat's when da story come out, same as it always come out, because somebody always gotta ask, and once dey ask, get no stopping um.

I no like scare nobody, but I gotta tell um straight, da way I wen hear um. Da thing, whatevah you like call um, nobody evah really seen um. Dat's da first thing you gotta understand. You no see um first. You hear um first. Get one voice, and da voice come before anything else, before any shape, before any shadow move where shadow no should move. Some folks say get one shape after, someting tall, someting standing wea nothing should be standing, but by da time you seeing shape, you already wen make one mistake.

What um do, is um call. Um call in one voice you know. Not one stranger voice, dat wouldn't fool nobody. Um call in da voice of somebody you love. Yo mother, yo faddah, yo own kid, whoevah stay closest to yo heart, dat's da voice um going use, calling yo name from off da path, from down in da gulch, from someplace dark where no light stay reaching. And da whole point, dey say, is fo pull you off da path, away from da road, away from anybody who could help you if someting wen go wrong.

Um only come after midnight. Nevah before. And only when get no moon, when da sky stay black black, no stars even, sometimes. Full moon night, new moon night wit clouds still glowing behind um, nah, um stay quiet. Only da real dark nights, da kine dark you no can see yo own hand.

Da spot everybody talk about, right hea by us, is da bend by da old ditch, wea da flume used to run, back when da mill was still going. You know da spot, road make one sharp turn right dea, and da water stay running undaneath, and get one big mango tree leaning ova da bank, roots all tangle up like rope. Nobody say um stay all ova da island, no. Um stay right hea, dat one bend, dat one stretch. Dat's it. I no like hear nobody go telling dis story like um belong to da whole island, cuz um don't. Um belong to us.

I wen hear um first from my tutu, when I was one small kid, maybe seven, eight years old. We was sitting outside, pau hana already, everybody tired, and somebody wen ask da same kine question dis young one wen ask me tonight. My tutu wen tell um straight, no laughing, no joking, face all serious, like she was telling you da bus schedule. But get one part, when she wen get to da part about da voice calling yo name, her eyes wen go someplace else, someplace far, and she wen laugh, one little laugh, but um nevah reach her eyes. Not even close. Dat kine laugh dat's really saying, I no like talk about dis no more, but I gotta finish da story cuz dat's how um go.

First proof dey always bring up, is da ground right dea by da bend. Get one patch, small kine, maybe as big as one car, wea nutting evah grow. Not grass, not weed, not even da stubborn kine grass dat grow anywhere else on dis island. People wen try plant someting dea, back in da day, flowers, ti leaf, one time somebody wen try banana even. Nevah take. Just stay bare, red dirt, packed down hard, like someting stay walking dere ova and ova, keeping um bare.

Second proof, dis one everybody heard from somebody who heard um from somebody else, you know how dat go. Get one guy, friend of one auntie's boy, was driving home late from one night shift, and he wen see, in his headlight, someone standing right at da bend, just standing, and when he wen slow down to see who dat was, cuz he wen tink maybe somebody need one ride, da headlight wen catch nothing. No one dea. Road stay empty. He nevah stop his car near dat bend after dat, garans.

But da one story dat always get told all da way through, every single time, is what wen happen to my uncle, years back, when he was still one young man. He was walking home late from town, cutting through by da ditch road, and he wen hear his own name, clear as anything, coming from down in da gulch, off da path. And da voice, he say, was his own faddah's voice, and his faddah was already passed, so dat wen mess him up plenty. He wen stop, and den he wen start walking toward um, three, maybe four steps off da path, before someting wen stop him. He nevah could explain what dat someting was, just say someting inside him wen go cold, chicken skin all up his arm, and he wen turn right back around and walk, den run, all da way home, nevah look back one time.

To dis day, he tell um da same way every time. He tell um:

The uncle: "I heard my faddah, clear as you standing dea right now. And I wen answer him in my head before I even wen think about um. Dat's what scare me da most, not da voice, but how fast I wen start walking toward um. Like my feet nevah even ask my head first."

Dat part, about answering before even thinking, dat's da part dat make even da doubters go quiet, just for one second. Because everybody knows dat feeling, dat pulling, when somebody call yo name and yo body move before yo mind catch up. Dat part hits true fo everybody, believer or not.

But get one other part dat give da doubters dea opening too, and dat's da faddah part. Cuz plenty people wen point out, eh, how convenient, da voice always sound like whoevah just passed, or whoevah da person miss da most. Doubters say dat's just yo own mind, yo own grief, playing tricks in da dark, and dat's a fair ting to say. I no argue um.

Da story change, depending who stay telling um. My tutu's sister used to tell um softer, more like one warning, gentle kine, more like one lesson than one scare story. Get one uncle on da other side who tell um harder, more graphic, add mo details every time, until you no even sure which part was real anymore and which part he wen make up fresh dat night. I nevah minded da difference. Dat's just how story stay, yeah? Every mouth give um one different shape, but da bones stay da same.

Da one small ting we still do, even now, even da young ones who say dey no believe, is nobody hea evah answer if dey hear dea name called from someplace dark, not until dey wen check wit dea own eyes who stay calling. You hear yo name from da dark, you no answer. You look first. You make sure. Only after you see um wit yo own eyes, den you can talk.

I gotta be honest wit you, cuz dat's da only way I know how tell story. I no fully believe um myself. I like tink I'm one practical person, I seen too much in dis life to believe every kine story my tutu wen tell me. But I garans you dis, in all my years, I nevah once answered when I wen hear my name called from da dark. Not one time. Maybe dat make me lolo. But I stay hea still, yeah? So maybe lolo works out just fine.
