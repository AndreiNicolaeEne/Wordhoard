By da middle of da night was down to jus us, five of us stay left after everybody else wen go home already. Da mother stay sitting by da end of da table where he always used to sit, like she nevah even notice she wen take his spot. Da eldest was folding one paper plate into little squares, not really folding um, just doing something wit her hands. Da auntie had da rice cooker still going even though nobody stay hungry no more, and da uncle stay leaning back in his chair wit one beer he wen forget for drink, gone flat awready. I was da one still up, moving plates around, not really cleaning, just fo have something fo do.

Get one plate of grindz still out on top da counter, choke leftover kalua pig and da mac salad nobody wen touch since dis afternoon, broke da mout kine food, da kind he always used to make too much of on purpose so get plenty left over for take home. Nobody stay talking loud. Was quiet kine talking, da kine where everybody know they going stay up little more, no need say um out loud.

The auntie: Eh, you guys remember how he used to park da truck? Every single time, same way, nose out toward da road. I used to tell um, uncle, you no need do dat every day, but he wen do um anyway.

The eldest: Dat wasn't so he could leave fast, Auntie. He wen tell me one time was from his old job days, back when da haole luna up at da mill used to make everybody park facing out, ready fo go quick. He jus nevah broke da habit after.

The auntie: (laughing) Oh, see, I always get dat wrong. I stay telling people dat story for years and I nevah had um right.

Somebody let out one laugh too loud for how quiet da room was, was da uncle, catching himself mid laugh like he wen surprise his own self. But nobody wen mind. Was good for hear, actually, first real laugh all night.

The mother: You know what he used to say, every single time he wen pau doing anything. No matter what. Fold one napkin, pau hana. Wash one dish, pau hana.

Me: (saying um exact, drawing out da word like he used to) Pau haaana.

The mother: (quiet little laugh) Dass um. Exactly like dat. I stay hearing um in my head still.

Had one small silence after, not one bad kine silence, just everybody sitting wit da sound of um still in da room.

The uncle: You guys know he used to leave money for da family next door, back when they was struggling? Envelope under da door, no name on um, nothing. I only found out cause I wen catch um doing um one night, and he made me garans promise I no can tell nobody.

The auntie: Wat?! He nevah told me dat. All dis time and he nevah say notting.

Room wen go quiet again after dat, everybody just sitting wit um for one moment, like they was all thinking da same thing but nobody had da words yet.

The eldest: (getting up, breaking um) Eh, who like more rice. Get plenty still. Pass your plate.

She wen go around da table again, dishing out more even though nobody wen ask, just fo have something fo do wit her hands, fo move little bit.

The auntie: (to da eldest, little bit of edge in um) You know, you still owe me for dat one time you wen borrow my punch bowl and nevah bring um back.

The eldest: Auntie, dat was fifteen years ago.

The auntie: I know when it was.

Nobody wen push um further. Was one old thing between them, had nothing to do wit tonight, and both of them seemed to know dat, so they wen jus let um sit there.

The uncle: You guys remember da rice cooker thing? Da old one, wen broke maybe ten years already, lid nevah even close right, had for prop um wit one spoon. Everybody wen tell um, jus buy one new one, brah, they cheap. He nevah listen. Not one time.

The mother: He said dat rice cooker knew how he like his rice. Like da machine had feelings.

The uncle: I wen buy him one new one for his birthday one year. Still in da box in da garage, nevah even open um.

Everybody wen laugh little bit at dat, real laugh dis time.

The eldest: He was so lolo about dat kine small thing. Small kine stuff, he no give up, garans. But anything dat really mattered, he nevah even put up one fight.

Nobody wen say um outright, but was like everybody wen agree on da same thing right then, jus by how they was all nodding, jus by how da room wen settle quiet and easy, dat he was da kine guy who give and give and nevah tell nobody, and dat was going stay da thing everybody remember him for, more than da truck or da rice cooker or any of dat kine story.

The mother wen start crying then, quiet kine, almost no sound, jus da tears coming down while she was still holding her cup. Nobody wen stop talking. Da eldest wen reach over and hold her hand for one second, still talking to da uncle same time, and da mother wen let da tears come while everybody else wen keep da talk going soft around her, like they was giving her room fo feel um without making her feel like everybody stay watching.

The uncle: (gentle, still talking, not stopping for da mother) We still gotta go down tomorrow, take care da paperwork. I go handle dat side, no worry.

The eldest: I stay taking care da flowers already, and I go call da rest da family in da morning, let them know when everything stay set.

The auntie: I go bring food for after. Don't worry about dat side, I got um.

Was said plain, no big ceremony to um, jus who doing what, da kine talk you gotta have even when you no like have um yet.

By den was jus me still awake, everybody else nodding off little bit in they chairs, da mother wit her eyes closed but still holding her cup, da auntie's rice cooker gone quiet finally, like even da machine wen pau for da night. I wen stand up, grab da last of da beer, pour little bit in whatever cup was close by.

Me: Eh. To him, yeah? For everything he nevah tell us he was doing, and for making us talk story like dis one whole night.

Nobody wen say notting back, jus everybody wen lift they cup little bit, tired kine, and dat was um. Room wen go quiet after dat, but was one good quiet, chicken skin kine quiet, and outside da crickets was still going like nothing wen change.
