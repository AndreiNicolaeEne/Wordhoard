So this year I wen take one plot down by the old camp lots, where get all the rows fo rent from the association, and I stay telling everybody the whole story now cause was one whole year, brah, up down up down.

Early spring I was still deciding what for put in. My neighbor stay planting already, get her whole row marked off with string, and I like know what she doing so I go ask.

Me: Eh, what you put in yours?

The neighbor: I put long bean and da kine Manoa lettuce, and one corner tomato. You still thinking?

Me: Still thinking. No like waste the seed if one more cold night coming.

So I wait. Everybody said the last cold snap not done yet, get one more wen coming end of the month, so I hold off. Couple weeks after that pass I finally put um in, long bean, one bed of corn, some tomato starts my uncle wen give me, and squash on the side by the fence where get more sun. And ho, the weather afterwards was choke good, sun almost every day, little bit rain at night just right, everything wen jump up so fast I couldn't believe. The bean was climbing the pole inside two weeks. Felt like I could do no wrong that season, garans.

Then came the first pest. The beetle wen find my bean leaves and was eating um up lace-like, little holes everywhere. I wen try one soap water spray my titah told me about, mix da kine dish soap little bit in the bucket and spray um every morning. Helped, but only half. The beetle still wen come back after couple days, so I had for pick um off by hand too, drop um in one can of water. Never fully solved um but the bean wen survive, so I take that as a win.

Then the dry stretch hit. No rain for what, three four weeks straight, and the ground was cracking already. I had for haul water by hand every single day just for keep everything alive, five gallon bucket back and forth from the spigot, up the row, down the row, every morning before work and again when I come home. My back was screaming but no can let the plants die after all that work already put in.

That's when the water trouble wen start with the plot next to mine. Get one shared spigot between about four rows, and everybody supposed to take turns, but with everything so dry, people stay hogging um. One morning I go for fill my bucket and the hose already running full blast into the next plot, been running long time by the look of the mud everywhere.

Me: Eh, you been on this long time already, yeah? I still get my whole row for water.

The plot-holder: I only put um on ten minutes ago.

Me: More like forty, look the mud. Everything stay dry over here and dying while you flooding yours.

The plot-holder: No need get like that, I no even know it was your turn.

We wen go back and forth couple days like that, little bit tense, nobody really talking to each other unless was about the water. Finally the old-timer who kind of runs the association, she come by and make us talk story about um proper.

The old-timer: You two need one schedule, that's all. Odd days you, even days her. Write um on the board by the gate so nobody can say they never know.

We wen do that and honestly, after that, no more problem. She even started leaving me couple extra minutes when she seen my tomato looking stress, and I do the same when her lettuce was bolting from the heat. Funny how one little schedule can fix what felt like one whole war.

My best crop by far was the long bean. That thing just kept coming and coming, every three four days I get one whole bag full, giving um away to everybody at work, my auntie, the neighbor, even people I barely know at the parking lot. Broke da mout too, stir fry with little bit garlic and shoyu, ono every time. Clear win, no argument.

But while the bean was going off like that, the corn wen get hit different kind of trouble, the birds. Every morning I come check and get more ears pecked open, kernels missing, husk all torn back like somebody wen unwrap um already. I wen try hang up old CDs on string, flash in the wind for scare um, and that worked little bit for a week, then the birds ain't even care anymore, they just sitting right on top the fence looking at me like, so what. Lost maybe third of the corn to that, no real fix I could find.

Middle of summer we wen get hit with one storm, wind and rain sideways for two days straight. I go down after and half my corn stay flat on the ground, couple tomato cages wen tip right over, squash vine all tangled up in the mud. Chicken skin little bit, seeing all that work just laid flat like that in one night. Took me the whole weekend propping everything back up, restaking the tomato, tying the corn stalks together in little bundles so they could stand again, digging the squash vine out the mud and giving um fresh dirt around the roots. Some of um never came back, but plenty did, more than I expected, actually.

By the time fall came and I stay tallying everything up, was one solid year despite all that. Choke long bean, good corn even with the birds taking their cut, tomato that never stopped till the vine finally wen pau on its own, and enough squash I never even know what for do with um all. I wen trade one big box of squash and extra tomato to another grower down the row for some eggs, cause his chicken stay laying more than he can eat and I stay needing eggs more than more squash at that point. Fair trade, nobody complaining.

End of the season I wen pull everything out, turn the soil over good, throw in some compost and put the cover crop seed down so the ground can rest till spring. Plot stay put to bed now, quiet, waiting.

Looking back the whole year, I go say was worth every bit of it, the bug, the dry stretch hauling water like one lolo every single day, the little water war with the neighbor that wen turn into something better than before, the storm that flattened half my rows. No was one easy year but no garden year ever is, yeah? Get always something coming for test you. But the bean alone was worth the whole thing, and the trading, and the talking story with everybody up and down the rows, that's the part I go remember more than any of the trouble. Already thinking what I go put in next spring.
