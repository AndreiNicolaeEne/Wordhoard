Now then, our kid. Sit thissen down a minute, I want to tell thee proper what's gone on wi' me and t'husband this week, because it's been reet grim. He's not a bad lad, tha knows, just daft wi' money sometimes, and this week it all came to a head.

It started ower a settee, nine hundred quid off t'internet, on t'card, never said a word to me till it turned up Tuesday morning. I saw t'statement Wednesday night and that were that.

Me: What's this then, nine hundred quid to some firm I've never heard on.
The husband: Calm down, ours were knackered, I thought I'd sort it.
Me: Tha never asked me. Tha never even mentioned it, not once.

And then, out of nowhere, he drags up our nephew's motorbike money, four hundred quid I lent him three year back and never saw again, nowt to do wi' any of this.

The husband: Tha's one to talk. Tha give our nephew four hundred quid for nowt and never said a word about that.

Sharpest thing said, that were him, and it went right through me.

The husband: At least I spend money on this house, not chuck it down t'drain for folk who can't be bothered to say ta.

Silence after that, proper silence, best part of two days. Him in front of t'telly of a night, me side t'table doing owt to keep missen busy, fettling cupboards that didn't want fettling.

He backed down first, love. Made us a brew Friday, sat down, said he were sorry, said he should've asked before he spent owt that size.

My verdict, our kid. He were wrong to buy it wi'out a word, right gormless if tha asks me, but I were wrong an' all, dragging up summat from three year back that had nowt to do wi' any of it. It'll be reet though. It always is wi' us two.
