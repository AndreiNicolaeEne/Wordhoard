Word got round dead fast, tha knows. By early morning t' house were already rammed. Folk just kept coming and coming, and every one of them fetching food an' all, more than anybody had asked for, tha knows how it goes, plates of ham and pork pie and breadcakes and cake after cake till there weren't a surface left bare, not t' sideboard, not t' windowsill, not even t' arm of t' settee had escaped a plate on it. Our mam were trying to side t' table so there were somewhere to put mugs down and it were a losing battle from t' start. Everybody in dark stuff, plain, nothing fancy, nowt needs saying about that, tha knows what it is.

A few of us carried her out together, me and t' eldest and two of t' cousins, and I'll tell thee, love, I don't know how my legs held, I were shaking summat rotten but I got through it, we all did.

Partway through, one of them just went, all of a sudden, no warning at all, just broke right down, roaring proper, and I'll be honest nobody moved to stop it, nobody shushed them or pulled them out, we just let it happen, because what else is there to do. Somebody stood up and spoke, and one thing they said has gone round everybody since, we've all been saying it to each other this last day or two like it were a spell against summat. Words said there were plain, short, no dressing up, no going on. Just what needed saying and no more. Then a handful of earth were let go and that were that, done, finished, no more to it than that, and somehow that were t' hardest bit, that little handful, more than owt else that day.
