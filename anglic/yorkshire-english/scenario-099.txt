Me: Ey up! Now then, is that thee? I've not seen thee in donkeys'.

The old mate: Bloody hell, ow do, love, ow do! I thought that were thee but I weren't sure, tha's gone grey on us.

Me: Cheek. Look at thee an' all, tha's hardly a spring chicken thissen. What's tha doing round here then, tha still living out that way?

The old mate: Nay, moved a couple of year back, over t'other side now. Bit more room for t'bairns to laik about in t'garden. It's champion actually, quiet like.

Me: Ah, grand. And how's work going on, tha still at same place?

The old mate: Aye, still there, God help us. Nowt much changes, tell thee truth. Freezing in winter, mafted come summer, and t'boss still hasn't fettled that heating in five year. What about thee, tha keeping busy?

Me: Same old same old. Weather's been siling it down all week though, hasn't it, I'm sick of it.

The old mate: Nithered this morning I were, waiting for t'bus, honest to God. Anyroad, go on then, what's tha been up to, owt exciting?

Me: Not really, tha knows how it is. Actually, funny tha should ask, I saw someone t'other day tha'll remember. Passed us right by t'market, nearly didn't clock him at first.

The old mate: Go on then, who?

Me: Old teacher. Tha knows, the one from top juniors, ruled that classroom like nowt else on this earth.

The old mate: Oh my God. Not him. I've not thought about him in years and now I'll not sleep tonight.

Me: I know, I know. Terrifying, weren't he. Do tha remember the one rule though? Say nowt unless he asked thee direct. That were it. Tha never, ever chelped, tha never called owt across t'room, tha never even whispered to your neighbour or that were curtains.

The old mate: Oh aye, and if tha did...

Me: If tha did, tha were writing lines. Same line, hundred times over, every single time, no matter what tha'd said or done. Didn't matter if tha'd just breathed wrong, hundred lines it were.

The old mate: "I must not talk out of turn." I can still see it in me own writing, hundred times down that page, hand aching summat rotten by the end of it.

Me: And do tha remember what he used to say to the whole class? Stood there, hands behind his back, and he'd go, "I've forgotten more than the lot of you will ever learn, so shut your mouths and listen." Word for word, that.

The old mate: Oh my days, yes! Sends a shiver down us even now, hearing thee say it.

Me: Dost tha remember when he did that to, well, tha knows who I mean, the one who kept talking during silent reading. Made him stand up front of the whole class and write his lines out there and then, in front of everybody, while we all just sat and watched, not daring to breathe.

The old mate: I do, I do. We were all just sat there thinking, thank God it's not me, thank God it's not me.

Me: I'll tell thee summat and all, I still remember freezing up when he called on us. Proper froze, like me tongue had gone to sleep. Knew the answer an' all, and nowt would come out.

The old mate: Everyone did that, don't feel bad. I froze that many times I thought he'd give us a nickname for it.

Me: But dost tha remember the one time someone talked back to him? And got away with it?

The old mate: Oh aye! I still can't believe that happened, honest, even now. I think about it and I'm still gobsmacked.

Me: Go on, tell it again, I want to hear it proper.

The old mate: Right, so he's stood there going on about summat, can't even remember what now, and this one just pipes up, bold as owt, and says summat back, cheeky as you like, right to his face, and we all just froze solid, thinking, that's it, he's finished, we'll never see him again. And old teacher just stood there a second, looked at him proper hard, and then, nowt. Just carried on with t'lesson like it never happened. No lines, no shouting, nowt.

Me: I know! I've never worked it out. Maybe he respected it, tha knows, someone with a bit of backbone.

The old mate: Must've done. Because God knows nobody else got away with owt like that, not before, not since.

Me: Do tha remember his little habit though, that we all used to mock behind his back? The way he'd tap his ring on t'desk three times before he said owt important. Tap tap tap, and then off he'd go.

The old mate: Oh my God, yes, we used to do it back to each other in t'playground, didn't we, tap tap tap, dead serious faces on, taking him off somehow.

Me: We were gormless, honestly, thinking back on it. But do tha remember the one time he were kind though? Proper caught us off guard, that did.

The old mate: I do actually. Was it when someone were roaring in class, upset about summat from home, and he just, stopped everything, sat down next to them, dead gentle like, and let them be. Never told them off for crying, never made a show of it.

Me: That's the one. Nobody expected that off him, not in a million year.

The old mate: What's tha heard about him since then anyroad? Is he still about?

Me: I heard he retired years back, and then summat about him moving off somewhere, but I don't know for sure. Might've heard wrong.

The old mate: I heard summat similar and all, but nobody really knows, do they. Someone said he'd passed on, someone else said they'd seen him down the coast somewhere, hale and hearty. Tha never gets a straight answer.

Me: No, tha's right, it's all hearsay. Funny though, in't it, looking back, we were that scared of him at the time, proper terrified, and now...

The old mate: Now it's more like respect really, in't it. He were hard on us, but tha knew where tha stood with him, and half of what he taught us has stuck more than owt from any other teacher.

Me: Exactly that. It's turned into summat else altogether, hasn't it, all these years on.

The old mate: (laughing) Honestly though, I'll tell thee summat for nowt, if I saw him walk round that corner now, this minute...

Me: (laughing) I know exactly what tha's going to say...

The old mate: I'd still stand up straight, I swear to God, summat in us would just click straight back to being ten year old.

Me: I would an' all, don't lie to yourself. Little flicker of it never really leaves thee, does it.

The old mate: Never does. Right, anyroad, that's enough of him, he'll be having us both worrying about our homework next. How's your mam keeping, is she still over that way?

Me: She's grand thanks, fettling round the house same as ever, drives us mad but there we are. How's yours?

The old mate: Champion, tha knows how she is, still on at us to visit more. Our lass has started nursery now an' all, proper little madam she's turning into.

Me: Ahh, bless her. Mine's growing up an' all, taller than us near enough. Costs a fortune in shoes, I'll tell thee that for nowt.

The old mate: Tell us about it. Right, listen, I'd best get on actually, got to nip to t'shop before it shuts, but it's been reet good seeing thee, honest.

Me: And thee, love. We'll have to sort summat proper, not just leave it to chance in t'market.

The old mate: Aye, we will, I mean it this time. Tara then, look after thissen.

Me: Tara, love. Mind how tha goes.
