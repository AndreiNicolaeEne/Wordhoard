Water were at t'door first thing, no more than ankle deep, just creeping in under like it were having a look at us. Stood there nithered in me socks, thought nowt much of it, put t'wood in t'oil, made a brew, told missen it'd shift by dinner. Went back an hour on and it were up past me ankle proper, so I got shifting - sided t'table double quick, telly off t'floor, spice tin and owt that'd spoil carried up onto t'stairs while there were still time.

By midmorning it were at me knee, siling in through t'door like there were no stopping it, and me and next door were ferrying boxes up two at a time.

Neighbour: Sithee, get that up higher, it's not stopping yet.
Me: I know, tha knows I'm trying, give us a hand wi' this breadcake tin an' all.

Everything as could go up went up, chairs on t'table, books on t'chairs, and still it kept climbing, higher than owt we'd raised, right past where we'd put things safe not an hour since. Peaked early afternoon, proper high, and there were nowt to do then but stand on t'stairs and listen to it slapping and pushing at t'walls, hour after hour.

The eldest: Is it stopping yet? I don't like it.
Me: Not yet, love, sit thissen down, it'll be reet, I promise thee.

She were near roaring by then, and I'll not say I blame her. Said we'd not shift again while it dropped, and we just sat, mafted with worry more than owt, listening to that water working away at brick. Felt right gormless stood there able to do nowt about it. Must've been four, five hour past the peak before it started to go down, so slow you'd near not credit it were moving at all.

Went back next day and there it were, right round every wall, a proper tide-line, water been and gone and left its mark like it wanted telling. Champion mess. Nowt for it now but fettle it and start again.
