Me: Ey up love, come in, put t'wood in t'oil, tha's letting all t'cold in. Sit down, I'll tell thee about t'reunion, it were a right do.

My mate: Go on then, tell her properly, don't miss owt out.

Me: Well tha knows the lass that were skinny as owt at school, allus laiking about at dinner time, never still a minute? I walked reet past her twice and never knew her. Put weight on, gone grey an' all. I says sithee, is that thee, and she says aye, it's me alright, few years'll do that.

My mate: And then there's t'other one, exact same, not a day changed, same gormless grin on him, still can't hold his ale. Tha'd swear no time had passed at all.

Me: That's what gets me though, compare them two. Back then she were the loud one, full of chelp, up on t'tables carrying on, and now you'd not credit it were her, dead quiet. Him, he never said two words at school and now he'll not shut up.

My mate: Word was the one that used to sit at t'front didn't show, summat about her mother being poorly, couldn't leave her on her own.

Me: Aye, shame that. But tha knows who surprised me, the one everyone reckoned were gormless, never amount to owt? He's a doctor now, over in Canada, married wi' three bairns. Nobody saw that coming, not in a month of Sundays.

My mate: Hall were mafted an' all, heating full on, and table were fettled up nice, proper spice out and breadcakes and everything.

Me: What about us two then, changed much dost tha reckon?

My mate: Nay, not really, much the same, bit more nithered in winter happen, bit slower up t'stairs.

Me: It'll be reet though. Let's not leave it that long again.

My mate: Champion idea. Sithee before Christmas, eh, while it's siling down and nobody wants out anyroad.
