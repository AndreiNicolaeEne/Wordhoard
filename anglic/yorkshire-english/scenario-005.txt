Friend: Ey up. Now then, how's tha getting on, love? Only popped us head round door to see how t' three of thee are faring.

Mother: Oh, champion, ta for asking. We're getting there, tha knows, one day at a time.

Friend: Aye? Baby settling in all right, is she?

Mother: Well... since it's thee asking, and not just being polite, nay, if I'm honest, it's been rotten. She were up four, five times last neet. I've had three hour's sleep in a stretch if that, and that were broken half through by her roaring again.

Father: And I'll tell thee summat else, my back's given up altogether wi' all the lifting and pacing t' landing at three in't morning. Can't hardly straighten up this morning.

Friend: Oh, you poor beggars, that sounds grim, that does. Nobody tells thee it'll be like this, do they. All "it'll be reet", and then this.

Father: Nay, they don't. And I did summat proper gormless an' all, put t' kettle in't fridge this morning and milk on t' side by t' hob. Stood there ages wondering where t' kettle had gone, tha knows, head's gone to mush.

Friend: Ha, bless thee. Right, that settles it, I'm not stood here mithering thee wi' questions, sithee, I'll have her for us this afternoon, give thee pair a couple of hours to sleep proper, and I'll bring a breadcake and summat hot round an' all, save thee cooking.

Mother: Eh, tha needn't, we'll manage...

Friend: Give over, love, tha won't. Let us do it.

Mother: ...Go on then. Ta ever so much. We'd not say no, honest.

Father: Aye, champion. Ta, love.
