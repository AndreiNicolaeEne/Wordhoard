I kept the garden log inside my head all year, so sithee, here's how it went, start to finish.

Back end of February I were stood at t'plot with me flask, working out what were going in where. Same as every year, half of it were guesswork and half were spite against what went wrong last time. Settled on beans up t'top end, spuds down t'bottom where it's heavier ground, and a row of leeks along t'side for after everything else were done. Didn't put nowt in t'ground while back end of March, mind, because tha doesn't trust an early frost round here, not one bit, and me dad allus said if in doubt wait while the blackthorn's out. So I waited, and then a fortnight after last cold snap I got it all in, and by, didn't it come on canny. We had a right good run of weather through April into May, warm enough of a morning, not too mafted of an afternoon, and everything fair shot up. Beans were up t'canes before I'd hardly got t'canes in straight.

About early June I found blackfly all ovver top of t'broad beans, thick as owt, whole tips gone black wi' them. Tried a squirt of washing up liquid in water, soft soap spray, like me neighbour telled me, pinch t'tops out an' all, tha knows, that's where they go for. It knocked some back but not all, they kept coming on every warm spell, so I were forever out there of a morning before work pinching and spraying, and it never fully shifted, just about kept it in check enough that we still got a crop off it, no more than that.

Then July went siling wi' no rain at all for weeks, ground went hard as t'road, and I were down there every single day wi' two cans, back and forth from t'butt, watering by hand because there were no getting a hose on it, site rules and the reservoir low an' all. Weeks of that, morning before work and again teatime, arms fair dropping off, and you'd stand there of an evening looking up hoping for a cloud and nowt came.

That's where the barney with next plot started, over whose turn it were on the one good standpipe, because half t'lotties share it and there's a rota pinned up in t'shed but nobody reads it while there's a row on. He'd be down there of a morning filling six cans and I'd turn up wanting mine and it'd be:

The neighbouring plot-holder: Now then. I were here first, tha knows.

Me: Aye, and tha were here first yesterday an' all, and t'day before that. It's my slot, sithee, it's wrote down.

The neighbouring plot-holder: Don't chelp at me, lass, I've watered every day this fortnight same as thee.

Me: I'm not chelping, I'm telling thee what's on t'sheet.

Went on a bit like that, not nasty, just tired and dry and short wi' each other, both of us nithered wi' worry ovver us plants really, though it were roasting weather to be arguing in. In t'end the site steward sorted it, put a proper timed rota up laminated so it couldn't get lost or argued wi', mornings even plot numbers, odd t'other, swap at teatime, and that were that, no more bother. We were civil after, even shared a brew off his stove one evening once it were settled, and he telled us his own water-worry stories off other years and we had a laugh ovver it.

Best crop by a mile were them same broad beans that had had t'blackfly, once they got past it, champion crop, sacks of them, gave loads away, folk on t'site sick of t'sight of me coming ovver wi' another bag. Not more beans, they'd say, and I'd say ey up, you'll be glad of them in December. Grand year for beans altogether.

Meanwhile summat were badly wrong wi' t'strawberries. Birds had every one that turned red a day before I got to it, blackbirds mostly, cheeky as owt, sat right there on t'netting waiting for it. Netted it proper eventually but by then they'd had best of it, so that were a right disappointment set against them beans doing so well, felt like one hand giving and t'other taking away.

Then partway through August we had a storm blow up out of nowhere of an evening, proper wind and siling rain together, and next morning half me runner beans were flat on t'ground, canes down, three whole rows laid ovver like summat had sat on it. Spent best part of a Saturday on me knees in t'mud staking it back up, tying it in again, salvaging what I could. Lost a fair bit that just wouldn't stand back up, nowt could be done wi' that, but most of it came reet again wi' a bit of graft.

By t'back end, when it come to totting it all up, it were a fair old haul: onions, spuds, leeks, them beans and all sorts besides. Traded a good sack of spuds and some onions off to a fella two plots down for a load of his rhubarb, because I'd nowt worth eating off mine, mine were all woody and gone to seed, daft mistake left it too long, and he'd more rhubarb than he knew what to do wi', so that worked out champion for both of us.

Come November I got it all put to bed proper, forked ovver, muck barrowed on, canes and stakes cleaned off and stood in t'shed corner, water butts covered so they don't freeze and crack. Stood there of a Sunday, nithered through to me bones, having a last look ovver it, and thought, well. It's been a right up and down do, this year, good start, bad bug, dry spell had me hauling water while me arms give out, a row wi' next door ovver it an' all, storm flattening half me beans, but it'll be reet, it allus is, and when it comes down to it we had more good off it than bad. Champion year, all told. Roll on next spring.
