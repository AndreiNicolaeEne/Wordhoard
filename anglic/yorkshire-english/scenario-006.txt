The eldest: Now then, tha's laiking tig wi' us today so listen up, sithee, I'm only telling thee once afore t'others get here.

The younger one: Ey up. 'Ow many do we need to start?

The eldest: Four at least. Three's nowt but running round in a circle shouting, doesn't work proper. Once we've six or seven it's champion.

The younger one: Where's t'boundary then?

The eldest: T'wall by them bins is one side, t' big tree down yonder's t'other, lamppost ont' corner's t'top and back to t'gate's t'bottom. Owt past that and tha's out of bounds, dun't matter who's chasing thee.

The younger one: What if I go over t'wall while I'm being chased?

The eldest: Then tha's out for that go, simple as. Walk thissen back in, stand where tha crossed, carry on from there. No chelp about it, that's just 'ow it is.

The younger one: 'Ow do we pick who's It first?

The eldest: Bunce up, fists in, one two three, odd one out's chasing. Or seeing as tha's new we'll just say tha's It first off, get thissen used to it.

The younger one: What counts as tagged, though?

The eldest: Sithee, flat o' me hand, on thy back or thy arm, like that. Not a shove, not a bray, just a tap and tha's copped.

The younger one: Then what?

The eldest: Then tha's frozen where tha stands, can't move thy feet, while somebody else still running comes and taps thee loose again. Round's done when every last one of us is frozen at once, and whoever got copped last is chasing next go.

The younger one: What if I get it wrong?

The eldest: Doesn't count against thee, love, first go's just for learning t'rules. It'll be reet. Now then, tha ready, or does tha want it again?
