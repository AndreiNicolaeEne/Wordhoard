Me: Ey up, park thissen there, love, and I'll tell thee, though tha's not to blame us for how daft it sounds said out loud to somebody who's never lived through one.

Me: It starts wi' the fella, every day, wet or fine, up before the alarm's even had a thought about going off. He'll not be shifted off it. First thing he does, before he's said word one to a living soul, is stand at the counter and put kettle on, then just stand there while it boils, staring at nowt out the window into the dark. Same five minutes, same spot, every morning God sends. He says it's the only bit of the day that's properly his, and I believe him, cos I've seen the state of him on the rare morning summat gets in the way of it.

Me: I'm up second, and I wake to the same grumble every day of my life. Doesn't matter what season it is, there's always summat.

The fella: It's siling it down again out there. I'm nithered stood at this door.

Me: Come August it'll be the opposite and he's stood there mafted instead, moaning about the heat same as he moaned about the cold, and either road it's the first thing I hear before my eyes are proper open.

Me: Then there's the wash-room, and that's a contest that's never once been settled in this house and never will be, cos it's the eldest against the fella every single morning, and neither of 'em's ever given an inch.

The eldest: I were in there first!

The fella: Tha were in there twenty minutes, love, that's not first, that's hogging it. Get thissen out or I'll bray on this door while me arm drops off.

The eldest: She'll not come out while she's good and ready, and not a minute sooner.

Me: Same words, near enough, every day. I don't referee it any more, I just step round it like it's a puddle left in the yard.

Me: Bairns come up in the exact same order every morning and all, eldest first, cos she'll not have it any other road, then the middle one, then the little one last, cos the little one would sleep through owt short of the roof coming off. And the calling's the same order and all, up the stairs, same three words, round and round.

Me: Up. Teeth. Bag. Up. Teeth. Bag. And if I miss one out they'll tell us quick as owt, tha knows..."you've not said teeth yet", like I've broke some ancient law.

Me: There's an argument most mornings and all, close enough to the same words you'd swear they'd learned it off by heart. It's the eldest and the middle one, over a hoodie.

The middle one: That's mine, tha's had it on all week.

The eldest: It were on my chair, so it's mine now.

The middle one: You never asked!

Me: Same three lines, practically, every morning, and one of 'em roaring about it summat rotten before it's even ate breakfast, and the other giving it some right chelp back. I've stopped trying to work out whose it actually is. I don't think either of 'em knows any more, if truth be told, it's just the shape of the morning now.

Me: Dog's got its own fixed part in it and all. Minute the toaster's down it's sat under t'table, not moving, not making a sound, just sat there like a little judge waiting on crusts. Same spot every day, and heaven help thee if you sit in its spot by accident, it'll stand there giving you a look till you cave.

Me: Breakfast's the same every day and all, but the little one never varies, not once, not in years, no spice while there's a proper breakfast ate, and the proper breakfast is always the same two breadcakes, jam only, cut corner to corner, never toasted, never so much as butter under the jam. Tha could offer owt else on God's earth going.

The little one: No. Same as always.

Me: Same as always, every day, like the sun coming up, stood there half gormless still in her nightclothes till the plate's put down.

Me: There's always summat missing an' all, one shoe, near enough every morning, and it's the same three places we look for it. Under the settee, behind the door, and, nine times out of ten, in the dog's basket, cos the daft thing likes the smell of feet, tha knows.

Me: Then there's the sound that tells the whole house it's properly awake at last, and it's not the alarm, it's not the radio, it's the pipes banging when the immersion clicks on for the shower. Great clattering racket, like summat trapped in the wall, and the second that goes off you'll hear feet moving twice as fast in every room.

Me: Middle one's always running a few minutes behind and all, every single day, doesn't matter what time we start, doesn't matter how early I call up. So it's rush at the door then, someone shouting to side t'table before we're all stood there, coats grabbed off the hooks half wrong, bags shoved into hands, and put wood i' th'oil, love, it's nithering, and nothing's ever forgotten, not cos anyone's careful, just cos we've done it that many times over it does itself now.

Me: Sunday's the one day it all stops. No shouting up the stairs, no wash-room war, no pipes banging like the house is under attack. Just quiet, and it feels that strange I near enough don't know what to do with missen while it lasts.

Me: But every other morning, whoever's last out gets the same handful of words off us at the door, same as it's said every day of the year.

Me: Got thi bag. Got thi key. Sithee later, love, mind how tha goes.

Me: Word for word, near enough, every day, and then the door goes and it's quiet of a sudden, like nowt just happened, when the whole house has been in uproar the best part of an hour.

Me: And that's it, love, that's a morning in this house, same shape every day, same grumbles, same fights, same shoe missing off somewhere daft, same everything, and I stand there some mornings once they've all gone and I have to laugh at us, cos it's champion really, when you think on it, the whole daft performance done fresh every day like it's never happened before, and tha's never once seen it, not properly, not from the inside. So there tha has it, love. That's what tha's been sleeping through.
