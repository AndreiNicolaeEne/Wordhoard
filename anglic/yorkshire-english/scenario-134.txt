Right, I'll tell thee about t'year everybody still talks about, because tha knows, we don't get one like it twice in a lifetime.

We knew early doors this one were going to be summat different. By June it were plain as owt, plants were away before we'd hardly got t'seed in, and by t'time we were counting sacks we were looking at near enough double what we'd normally get off that ground. Doubled, and no messing.

It were t'spuds that did it, mind. Every other year they're steady, nowt to write home about, but this year they came up in that field like there were no tomorrow, and when we weighed in we were miles past owt we'd ever had before, past even that big year years back that folk used to go on about. That were beat, and beat well.

Had to get extra hands in an' all, because tha can't laik about when it's ready to lift, it'll turn on thee if tha leaves it, and there were nobbut so many of us to get round it before it went over. So we had lads and lasses coming in just for t'lifting, and even then we were siling it down half t'week and racing t'weather to get shut of it in time.

Once we'd covered what t'family and t'neighbours needed there were loads over, so some went in store for t'winter and rest got sold on to them as wanted it, no sense letting it go to waste when there's them as'll take it off thee.

The oldest grower: I've been at this since I were nowt but a lad missen, and I've never seen a year to touch it. Rain came right when it were wanted, then we had that mild stretch right through, and that's what done it, not owt else.

It's still what we measure every year against now. Any middling harvest and somebody'll say it's nowt like that year, and everybody knows exactly which one they mean.
