Eight year I'd been gone and the first thing I clocked coming up the path were the state o' the gate, still hanging off same hinge it allus did, nobody ever got round to fettling it. I'd me bag on me shoulder and me hand were half up to knock when the door went and there she were.

Mother: Oh my God. Oh my God, it's, oh my GOD, come here, come here, get in here now...

She'd her hand over her mouth first, like she couldn't credit what were stood on her step, then she just grabbed us, arms right round, and she weren't letting go, not for owt. I could hear her breathing gone all funny against me shoulder.

Mother: Look at thee. Just look at thee. I can't. I can't believe it's thee stood there.

Dad had come through from t'kitchen by then, wiping his hands on a tea towel he'd no reason to be holding, and he stood in't doorway a second just looking, and then he were on us and all, arm round both of us at once so we were near knocked into the door frame.

Dad: Now then. Now then, lad. Eeh, look at the state of thee.

Mother: He's changed, in't he. Look how much he's changed. Tha's gone all, tha's filled out, look at thee, tha's a proper...

Dad: He looks knackered is what he looks.

Mother: He looks grand, don't listen to him, tha looks champion, tha does, I don't care what he says.

There were a right commotion in that hallway then, the pair of them still hanging off us, and the sister come thundering down't stairs two at a time shouting summat I couldn't even make out over the mother still going on, and she got to the bottom and just stopped dead a second, hand over her mouth same as Mother had done, and then she were crying before she'd even got her arms round us.

Sister: I'm not. I'm not even crying, don't look at us, I'm fine...

Me: Tha's roaring, look at thee.

Sister: I'm NOT, shurrup, get here...

Mother: Bags, love, leave thi bags, just leave 'em...

And me bags did just get left, right there by the door where they landed, nobody so much as looked at 'em again for a good hour, they were just summat to step round.

From behind the sister's legs there were a little one peering out, four or five, hanging back against the banister like she were trying to disappear into it, and I realised it must be the sister's lass, the one I'd only ever seen in photos sent on, and she were looking at us like I were a stranger stood in her house, which I suppose to her I were.

Sister: Go on, say hello, don't be shy, this is...

The young one: (nowt, just staring, one thumb near her mouth)

Mother: Ey, don't push her, she'll come round, won't tha, flower. She's not seen him since she were nobbut a babby, she don't remember him at all.

Dad: Give her time. She'll be climbing all over thee by teatime, tha won't be able to shift her.

There were questions coming at us from every side by then, over top of each other, so as I couldn't rightly answer one before another were fired.

Mother: How long can tha stop? Tell us tha's stopping a good while this time...

Sister: Has tha eaten? Tha looks like tha's not eaten in a month...

Dad: What's the flight like, were it siling it down when tha landed, it's been siling here all week...

Mother: Did tha get me letters, all of 'em, I sent that many...

Sister: Tha knows our cousin's wed now, and expecting, and she's moved out to the other side, and her sister's lad's got that tall tha wouldn't credit it, and...

Dad: Let him get his coat off, woman, give him a minute...

Mother: I'm only asking...

Me: I can't, one at a time, I can't hear misen think...

And there were a laugh went round at that, first proper laugh, summat easing off a bit after all the shouting.

I stood there a second just taking it in, coat still on, and I said what were in me head without really meaning to.

Me: It smells exact same in here. That's, is that still the same soap in't bathroom, or...

Mother: It is an' all, tha's got a nose on thee.

Me: And the settee's moved.

Dad: Aye, your mother had a fit o' rearranging two year back, took us a week to find owt after.

Me: House looks smaller than I remember. Everything else looks just same. Chippy sign's still crooked.

Dad: It'll never get fixed, that. Been crooked longer than tha's been alive near enough.

Mother: How long, though, love, tha's not said, how long we got thee for...

Me: Fortnight. Bit less, maybe.

Mother: A fortnight. Right. Right, well, we'll have to make the most of it then, won't we. I'm doing a proper dinner Sunday, whole lot of us, I'll get your gran over and everyone...

Dad: She's already planning it, look at her face, she's off already...

Mother: Well somebody's got to mark it, he's been gone eight year, we're not just having us tea and getting on with it like nowt's happened...

Sister: I'll bring a pudding. I'll do that one tha used to like, if I can remember how.

Dad: She can't cook to save her life, don't let her near an oven, we'll be having it out of a tin instead...

Sister: Shurrup, I'm not that bad...

Mother: You're both as bad as each other, honestly, and him just walked in the door and you're already at it...

Dad turned to us then, gave us a nudge with his elbow, the old look on his face.

Dad: Still put t'wood in t'oil, lad, if tha's coming in, there's a draught fit to knock thee over.

Mother: Eeh, he's not heard that in eight year and you come out with it first thing...

Me: I'd forgot that one. Put t'wood in t'oil.

Dad: Some things don't change.

And that got a laugh an' all, proper one this time, the young one even cracking a bit of a smile from behind her mum's leg though she'd no idea what were funny.

For a second, right in the middle of it, everyone just went quiet altogether, like it hit 'em all at once that I were actually stood there and not just a voice down a phone line, and they all just looked, Mother with her hand still half up near her mouth, Dad with the tea towel forgotten over his shoulder, sister wiping under her eyes with the back of her hand, the young one peering round the banister still working out who I were. Nobody said owt for a good few seconds. Just looking.

Then Mother clapped her hands together like she were waking herself up out of it.

Mother: Right. Right, come on, get thi coat off proper, get sat down, kettle's not long boiled, I'll mash a fresh pot. You're letting all't heat out standing in that doorway.

Dad: Get his bags in and all, they've been sat there gormless this whole time.

Mother: I'll do him a bit o' toast while the tea's mashing, tha must be clemmed...

Sister: There's spice in the tin still, gran's sort, tha used to nick it when tha thought no one were looking...

Me: I always thought I got away with that.

Sister: Tha never did. Not once.

And with that the whole thing settled a bit, everyone drifting through to the kitchen, the young one finally letting go the banister and trailing after at a safe distance, still watching us sideways like she hadn't quite decided about us yet, and the kettle going on, and it were like the first hour had all come out of us in one go and now there were just the ordinary business of being home again, however long that lasted.
