Reet, so it were coming up forty-odd and I'd gone and decided, out of nowhere as far as our lass were concerned, that I were going to learn to skate. Ice skating, tha knows, proper blades and everything, not the roller sort. Every other bugger I knew had learnt it as a bairn, six or seven year old, fell over a few times and forgot about it. Me, I'd never so much as stood on t'ice in my life. Don't ask me what set it off. I think I were just fed up of watching and never doing, fed up of being the one stood at t'barrier holding coats while everyone else laiked about like it were nowt at all.

First lesson were hopeless, love, and I mean hopeless. My head knew exactly what it wanted my legs to do and my legs said now then, we'll not be doing that, ta very much. The instructor were a patient sort, I'll give him that, but blunt as owt with it.

The instructor: Tha's going to find this harder than most, at thy age. Body doesn't learn the same after forty. Don't take it personal, it's just how it is.

I didn't take it personal. Well, I did, a bit, but I nodded like I hadn't. Tha spends thy whole life being reasonably good at most things and then tha stands there gormless as a coat stand while a seven-year-old skates rings round thee, laughing while she does it an' all, not even nasty about it, just because it's easy for her.

Muscles down the sides of my legs, in my back, under my arms even, all making themselves known every time I so much as sat down or stood up. I'd be sat of an evening and our lass would say summat to me and I'd groan just getting up to answer t'door. She thought it were hilarious.

Her: Tha's walking like an old ewe, love, what's tha done to thissen.

Me: Don't laugh. I hurt in places I forgot I owned.

And then there were the fall. There's always one fall that sticks, isn't there, out of all of them, and this were mine. Went down hard, feet just gone from under me altogether, no warning, and I landed flat on my back on t'ice and it knocked every bit of breath clean out of me. I remember thinking quite calm about it, like it were somebody else's decision I were agreeing with. I weren't roaring, but I were close, I'll not lie to thee.

I said as much after, once I could talk again and got myself off t'ice with about as much dignity as a sack of spuds.

Me: I'm done. I mean it this time. I'm too old for this carry-on, I'll bray missen doing it again and that's without owt else falling on me.

But my friend, one of t'other learners, wouldn't have it.

My friend: Tha's not packing it in over one fall, come on, one more go, just one, and if tha still wants to quit after that then fair enough, I'll not say another word about it.

So I went one more time. And then, tha knows how it is, one more time turns into just going again the week after, and before I knew it I hadn't actually quit at all, I'd just had a bad night.

After that it were slow, love, ever so slow, and humiliating with it more often than not. Weeks of getting one small thing wrong over and over, some tiny bit of it, a turn, a stop, whatever it were that week, and a little crowd of regulars always stood watching an' all, some of them dead encouraging, calling out well done and that sort of thing, and some of them just plain amused, not unkind exactly, but tha could see it in their faces. I got used to being watched. Had to, really, there were no getting round it at a public rink.

What kept me going, if I'm honest, were never comparing missen to them fast little kiddies, because there were no sense in it, none at all. What I did instead were look back a month and think, well, a month back I couldn't even stand up off t'ice on my own, and now I can, so summat's shifted even if it doesn't feel like much. That were the only comparison worth owt. Against missen, never against them that'd picked it up in an afternoon like it were nothing.

One evening I pushed off and my legs just knew what they were doing without me asking them to think about it, and the thing that had been flat impossible in week one were just difficult now, ordinary difficult, the sort of difficult you can live with.

I never did catch up to them fast learners, mind, and by the end I'd made my peace with that proper. There's always going to be somebody better, quicker, tha knows, that's just how owt is. What I had instead were things I could do without even thinking about them by t'end, that I couldn't do a stroke of when I started. That's not nowt. So that's thy answer, love, since tha asked. Started too late, fell flat on my back and near enough gave it up there and then, got laughed at by more seven-year-olds than I care to count, and ended up grand at summat I couldn't do a lick of at t'start. Can't ask for much more than that, can tha.
