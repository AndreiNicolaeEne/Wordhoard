Newcomer: Ey, somebody said there's a fox been living on t' street. Is that reet? What's the story wi' it then?

Me: Ow do, love. Aye it's reet enough, sit thissen down a minute and I'll tell thee the whole of it start to finish, because everybody's got their own version and half of it's chelp if tha asks me, so tha might as well have it proper.

Right, it started back end o' last year. First anybody said owt about it, it were the lass two doors up. She were putting summat out and reckoned she'd seen a shape moving down the entry behind t' garages, low and big wi' a tail on it, and she said fox straight off. Nobody believed her, tha knows how it goes, they just laughed it off. Her own fella were the worst, said "give ower, love, there's nowt down there but t' bins and thy imagination," and that were that for a bit. Everybody thought no more on it.

Then not long after, a fortnight maybe, the one across way saw it and all, and that's when folk started taking notice, because she's not one for making things up or laiking about wi' stories. She's lived on this street donkey's years, everybody trusts her. She comes out one morning to side her bin and there it is, bold as owt, sat right on top of her wall staring at her. She said it never moved a muscle, just watched her a good minute, then it were off over t' fence like nowt had happened. After that it were fox this and fox that all up and down t' street, because if she's seen it then it's been seen, no messing.

After that it were about regular. Folk started spotting it of a night, poking round t' bins, tipping lids off, having a rootle in gardens, digging up bits here and there. One or two soft-hearted types started leaving food out for it, which didn't help matters one bit, because then it thought it were welcome.

Street split reet down t' middle over it. One lot said leave it be, it's not doing no harm, it's only trying to get on same as owt else, there's always been foxes about and it'll be reet if folk just fettle their bins proper. Other lot wanted summat done, sithee, wanted somebody called out, said it'd have a cat one of these nights or get in through an open door.

Then the mother's washing went missing off the line, whole load of it gone in one night, and that were when it turned nasty. Half t' street reckoned the fox had had it, dragged off for bedding or nowt, and the other half said don't be gormless, foxes don't take sheets, it were more like kids messing about or just wind, but there were no proof either road and it got argued summat rotten over garden fences for a week.

It all come to a head proper at the do they had on t' green. Started civil enough, then it turned into a right slanging match, one lot shouting it were cruel to want it caught, other lot shouting they weren't having their bins gone through every night for the sake of somebody's soft spot for wildlife. Voices going up and up till somebody's dad were near roaring wi' frustration and had to be walked off home to calm down.

One household tried to sort it thissen, wi'out asking nobody. Bought a cage trap off t' internet, baited it up wi' a breadcake and some scraps, set it out siling it down one night thinking that'd keep it hungry and desperate. Caught nowt but rainwater and a very confused cat from three doors down, which weren't a good look for anybody involved.

What really brought it to a head were the night the eldest come back from a late shift and it crossed right in front of her, close enough she could've touched it, and it gave her that much of a fright she were shaking when she got in, proper roaring about it after. That's when the argument really flared, because half the street said see, told thee it were dangerous, and the other half said nay, it's not dangerous, it's just unlucky where it's wandered, it's more scared of thee than thee is of it, it's only doing what a fox does. Went back and forth for days, nobody budging.

In the end somebody had the sense to get an outside voice in, a fella who deals wi' this sort proper, knows foxes inside out. Come and had a look round, walked the length o' t' street, had a look at bins and gardens and where it were getting in. Said it were nowt to fret over, no sign of owt sick or aggressive about it, just a young one that had found easy pickings and moved in because folk had made it too easy. He were champion, dead straightforward about it, no messing, told everybody straight what wanted doing and what didn't.

Street settled on it after that. Bins to be fettled proper, lids weighted down, nowt left out no matter how sorry folk felt for it. Nobody feeding it, not even the ones leaving out spice for it thinking it'd fancy a humbug same as owd folk do. And somebody to keep half an eye of a night, just to know it's still about and behaving itself, not because owt were owed to it.

Since then it's all gone quiet, tha knows. Bins are secure, nowt's gone missing that anybody can pin on it, and it's still about, tha sees it now and again crossing at t' bottom of a night, but it's routine now, nobody's fussed. Alarm's died right down. If tha mentions it now folk just laugh and say ey, remember when it had thy washing, and somebody'll chelp back that it never did, that were kids, and it's more of a tale folk tell now than owt to be bothered about. It's grand as it is. Leave it be and put wood in t' oil and it leaves thee be and all.
