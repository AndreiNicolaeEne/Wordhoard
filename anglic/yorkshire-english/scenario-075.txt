Me: Ey up... hang on, is that thee?

The old friend: Bloody hell, now then! I don't believe it, what's t'chances of that.

Me: I know, I'm just stood here thinking nay, it can't be.

The old friend: How long's it been then, eh? Must be ten year at least.

Me: More than that I reckon. Last time I saw thee were before I even moved, so that's got to be twelve, thirteen year now.

The old friend: Bloody hell. Right, go on then, what's tha been doing wi' thissen since?

Me: Ah, you know, same old really. I'm doing joinery now, got two bairns, eldest's just started school. Missus works down at t'hospital.

The old friend: Champion, that's champion that. I'm still at t'same place, only I'm manager now. Married, no kids yet, we're just fettling t'house at minute, doing it up room by room.

Me: Good on thee. Ey, tha knows what I still think on? That time we got chelped off by t'caretaker for laiking about on t'field wall, and he said he'd bray us if he caught us at it again.

The old friend: Ha! I'd forgot all about that, you daft sod. We were roaring laughing after, soon as he'd gone.

Me: We were an' all. Ey, owt ever come of that lad we used to knock about with, the one who lived down by t'shop?

The old friend: Nay, no idea, love. Nowt from him in years. Went offcumden somewhere south I heard, but that's all I know.

Me: Shame that, in't it. Right, sithee, give us thy number this time, and I mean it, I'll actually text.

The old friend: Aye go on then, and don't be a stranger this time or I'll come find thee missen.

Me: I won't, I promise. It'll be reet, we'll sort summat out for a pint.
