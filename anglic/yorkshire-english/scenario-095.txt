The elder: Right, side t'table so we can spread this lot out, we'll be here while Christmas else.

The younger: It's nithered in here an' all. Weather's siling it down out there and no heating on.

The elder: Never mind heating, look, that clock's mine. She said I could have it off t'mantelpiece years back, said it to my face.

The younger: Did she heck as like. First I've heard of it, and I were round here more than thee.

The elder: I'm not lying, tha knows I don't chelp for nowt. She stood right where you're stood and said it.

The younger: Convenient, that. Champion, real champion, fighting ovver a clock off Woolworths.

The elder: Don't start on me.

The younger: I'm not starting owt. While we're on it, what's happened to t'money? There were supposed to be a few thousand left and it's nowt like what's there now.

The elder: Are you saying I've had it off her?

The younger: I'm saying it's funny how it dwindled while thee were sorting her bills and not answering t'phone to me.

The elder: I paid for her funeral out my own pocket, tha knows, while thee were three hundred mile off. Say that again and I'll bray thee.

The younger: Don't be gormless.

The elder: She's gone and we're stood here fratching over a clock and a bit o' brass like it means owt. I don't care about t'clock. I want her back, that's all.

The younger: I know. I don't either, not really.

The aunt: Now then, you two, whole street can hear you. Put wood in't oil behind you and sit down, I'll wet the tea, get a breadcake down you both.

The elder: Sorry. Got carried away.

The aunt: She's only just gone, loves. You're bound to be raw.

The younger: It'll be reet, I s'pose.

The elder: Aye. It'll be reet.

Both go quiet a minute, a bit gormless-looking, hearing themselves proper for the first time, roaring at one another over what's left of her, not a fortnight since they buried her.
