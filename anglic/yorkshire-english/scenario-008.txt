The child: Go on, can I not have it? I'm only askin' again.

The mother: Now then, love, shut door, I'm nithered wi' it siling down out there. And nay, tha can't, not this week. Truth is we've nowt spare for it. Your dad's shifts got cut back and it's gone on t'gas and on shoes for thee. That's the size of it, I'll not mess thee about.

The child: I could do t'washing up every night. And side t'table an' all, wi'out being telled twice.

The mother: That's grand of thee to offer, flower, but it's not chores that's stopping us, it's brass. Doing t'pots won't put owt back in me purse, will it.

The child: What about me birthday then? Could I have it if I've saved missen up and been good till then?

The mother: If things pick up by then, aye, we'll see. I'll not promise thee nowt, mind, but I'll not say nay forever neither. Depends what's coming in. Tha'll have to wait while then like the rest of us wait while summat.

The child: But she's got one already, and her mum's not even working!

The mother: Sithee, doesn't matter what's over t'road. They're offcumdens, come up wi' money from down south, tha knows. Every house manages its own way, love, and their way in't ours to measure missen against.

The child: (roaring) It's not fair though.

The mother: None o' thy chelp now. Here, I'll get t'old bike out t'shed Saturday and we'll fettle it up between us, new tyres on, mebbe a bell, and I'll fetch thee a bag o' spice an' all. It'll not be new, but it'll go, and it'll be thine.

The child: ...s'pose that'll do. For now.

The mother: That's a good lass. It'll be reet, tha knows. Now get thissen a breadcake, tea's not long off.
