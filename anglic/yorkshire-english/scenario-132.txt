Ey up, I'll tell thee about that cold, because tha knows we still talk on it round here. Eleven days it went on, straight through, not one of them giving an inch. First morning we thought nowt of it, just frost on t'windows same as any November, but by dinnertime t'yard tap were froze solid and we knew it were a different sort of cold altogether, colder than owt forecast had said.

After that we kept t'stove going round t'clock, coal in, wood in, "put t'wood in t'oil" shouted every time somebody came through, because tha daren't let a draught near it. Missen and the lad took it in turns of a night to feed it, three hours on and swap, because if it went out even an hour t'whole house went to nowt and back to nithered in no time.

Pipe under t'kitchen went on day four, thawed a touch in t'afternoon then froze again solid and split clean through, water everywhere and not a plumber to be had for love nor money, everyone in same boat, tha knows.

The eldest: I've seen nowt like it since the big freeze, and even that one didn't hold on this long. This one's beat it, lass.

Worst night were the ninth, cold enough to bray thee stood still on t'step, and that's when we lost one of t'hens, found her stiff as owt in t'coop come morning. Roaring, the youngest were, right roaring over that hen.

Then eleventh day the sun come up proper for first time, dripping off t'eaves by dinner, and honest, it felt champion, like tha could breathe again. It'll be reet, we kept saying all through it. And it were, in t'end.
