The winner: Now then. Ey up, sit thissen down, kettle's just gone on. Put t'wood in t'oil behind thee, it's siling it down out there.

The loser: Ow do. Aye, I'm fair nithered, walked up from t'bus stop and never brought a coat. Owt to eat wi' that brew?

The winner: There's spice in t'tin, and a breadcake if tha wants it toasted. Side t'table a bit first, I've got t'scoresheet out again.

The loser: Nay, not this again. We've laiked this game over that many times in us heads I could tell thee it in me sleep. Forty-one moves and it's done, lad.

The winner: I'm not lettin' it lie though. Thy opening were wrong from t'off, tha knows it were. A different line there and we're not even sat here havin' this row.

The loser: I don't know owt of t'sort. I'd played that opening twice against thee before and had thee both times. It were sound on t'day, and tha can't tell me different just cos it went how it went in t'end.

The winner: Champion for thee twice before, means nowt on t'third go. Tha should've reckoned I'd have summat ready for it by then, tha's not gormless, tha knew I'd been studyin' it.

The loser: Should've, should've, that's all after t'fact talk, that is. At t'time it were t'right move and I'll stand by it now same as I stood by it then.

The winner: Aye well. First dozen or so, nowt in it either road, I'll give thee that much, we were both feelin' each other out, playin' careful. That bit I'll not argue over.

The loser: Champion, summat we agree on then, put that in t'book and all, it might be t'only line in it.

The winner: But then we get to round about move twenty and tha knows exactly what I mean. Tha moved thy knight off and left t'whole side of t'board open for nowt. Plain mistake, that were. Nowt clever hidden in it, nowt deep, just a mistake.

The loser: It were not a mistake, it were t'right move given what I could see at t'time, and tha's judgin' it now knowin' how it turned out after. I'd no way of tellin' thy bishop were lined up like that, it looked sound to me then and it looks sound to me now and all.

The winner: Tha's stood by that move that many times tha's worn t'carpet through sayin' it, love. It were a blunder plain and simple and it's what cost thee t'game.

The loser: If owt cost me it were that exchange further on, round move twenty-eight. Tha traded rooks there and left thissen wide open and all, don't come at me like tha's t'only one made a move that day.

The winner: I traded rooks cos tha forced it, tha'd have taken me either road, I'd nowt else on.

The loser: Tha forced nowt. Tha could've held that pawn back a move and none of it happens.

The winner: Chelp, all of that, tha's tryin' to lay thy own fallin' apart at my door.

The loser: And tha's not doin' t'same to me now? Sithee, by move thirty-four tha's out there tellin' folk down club t'game were as good as lost for me. It were not lost, I still had chances in it.

The winner: It were lost, and tha knew it and all, that's why tha went mad wi' that comeback of thine round move thirty-six.

The loser: That comeback nearly had thee, don't tell me tha weren't sweatin' it, I saw thy face.

The winner: It come too late though, didn't it. Three moves too late. Should've started it when I took t'rook, not after.

The loser: If I'd played bishop to d4 instead of what I played on thirty-eight, that saves it outright. I've gone over it missen that many times sat up at neet. Bishop to d4 and tha's in bother, proper bother.

The winner: Tha's had a year and more to find that move sat at home wi' no clock runnin' on thee. On t'day, in t'moment, tha didn't see it, and that's t'whole difference between us, love, right there.

The loser: Aye, well. Move forty-one though, we're not arguin' over what it were. Tha took my queen, mate followed two after, that's that, plain as owt.

The winner: We agree on that much then. One of t'only things we do.

The loser: I'll give thee t'move. I'll not give thee it meant owt beyond that one minute of t'game. I had t'stronger position whole way through if tha's honest wi' thissen, tha just found one line right at t'end of it.

The winner: Tha's talkin' gormless if tha reckons that. I were ahead from move twenty on, I just told thee that not two minutes back.

The loser: At t'time tha said nowt of t'sort though. We shook hands, tha said "good game" and that were it, nowt about who'd had t'stronger side all along.

The winner: Cos it were siling it down out there and I wanted off home before I got soaked through, that's all that were. Doesn't mean I weren't thinkin' it.

The loser: Convenient, that. Very convenient.

The winner: It's like t'last one and all, when we played down club two winters back. Same road exact. Tha gets ahead early doors, I claw it back, and folk stood watchin' say it were close, but it were me had t'stronger position both times if owt were counted proper.

The loser: Don't bring that one into it, it's nowt to do wi' this game, two different boards, two different nights. Tha can't make a pattern out of it just cos it suits thee to say so.

The winner: It's a pattern right enough, and tha knows it, else tha'd not be gettin' that road about it now.

The loser: I'm not gettin' any road about owt, I'm sat here havin' a brew wi' thee and tha's still tellin' me I lost twice when it were once, on one board, in one match.

The winner: Once on t'board, aye. Tha can keep that one, champion for thee.

The loser: I will keep it. And I'll keep sayin' thy opening were reckless and tha got lucky it held long enough.

The winner: Lucky. Right. Well, we'll not agree on that while t'end of time, will we.

The loser: It'll be reet. We'll say no more on it.

The winner: We'll say no more till next time tha wants goin' through it again, and then we'll be sat here wi' t'scoresheet out same as tonight and nowt settled, same as ever.

The loser: Aye. Same as ever. Pass us that breadcake while it's warm, I'm not roarin' about it, I'm just sayin' me piece.

The winner: Tha's chelpin' same as tha did over t'board, that's all tha's doin'.

The loser: Bray me then, if tha's that bothered.

The winner: I might and all, one day. Now then, drink thy tea, it's gone cold arguin' wi' thee.
