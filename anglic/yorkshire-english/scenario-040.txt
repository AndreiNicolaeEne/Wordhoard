Ey up, sit thissen down, our kettle's on, I'll tell thee t'whole of it.

Were up at half five, couldn't eat nowt for nerves, put t'wood in t'oil behind us and walked down to t'stop shakin like a leaf, nithered an' all, proper raw first thing.

Got there early doors, stood about like a spare part while t'shutters were still down. Then a big fella come and let us in, "Ey up, now then, ow do," all in one go, and set us straight to work.

The supervisor: reet, lad, tha's t'new one? Follow me and I'll show thi t'ropes.

First job he give us were sortin and fettling stock i' t'back, said have it done while dinner. I thought that'd tek us all mornin but he wanted it in t'hour, less time than I'd reckoned on, work were faster than I'd braced missen for an' all. Back o' t'stockroom were mafted, no air in there, mind, after t'cold outside.

Dropped a whole tray on it clatterin down front of everyone, could've roared, felt right gormless, but nobody chelped owt, just got on wi it.

Break time I sat wi mi breadcake sizin up t'others, quiet like. One o' t'older ones had a bag o' spice out, offered us some. There were an offcumden started same day as me, from over Bradford way, nowt wrong wi that mind.

The one who helped: tha don't want to do it like that, flower, tha wants to do it like this, save thissen some bother.

Never asked, just showed us. Nine hour on us feet, siling it down outside all afternoon, tha knows, but it went quicker than I'd thought once tha got stuck in, no time for laikin about.

Walked home after, replayin it all in mi head, every daft bit, thinkin on that tray.

Sat down at us tea, mam had sided t'table already.

The mother: ow'd it go then, love?

Me: it'll be reet. Champion, actually, once I got stuck in. Grand bunch, mostly, and he said if I were late again he'd bray us, laughin like, so I know where I stand.

The dad: sithee, said tha'd manage.

Me: aye, tha knows, summat to build on.
