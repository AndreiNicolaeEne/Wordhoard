Me: Ey up.

Mate: Now then. What's up wi' thee, tha's a face like a wet week on.

Me: Sit thissen down a minute, I want a word wi' thee. That roundabout Tuesday, that's what's up.

Mate: Oh give over, not this again.

Me: Not this again? I were sat right behind thee in t' car and watched that wagon miss thee by nowt. Nowt at all. My hands were shaking on t' wheel after, proper shaking, couldn't hold mi tea when I got in, heart going like a drum in mi chest and it weren't even me on t' bike.

Mate: Tha's mekkin' a meal of it. Bike were never ganna touch it, I had it sorted.

Me: Tha calls that sorted. Sithee, remember two year back, same daft overtake near enough, and tha come off and cracked tha collarbone? That were a warning, that were, and tha's stood there telling me it's harmless.

Mate: That were different, road were wet -

Me: It's always summat wi' thee, in't it. Road were wet, wagon were early, sun were in tha eyes. One day none o' that'll matter. It won't be a broken bone next time, tha knows that. It'll be under a wagon, and that'll be that, done, and tha won't get another chance while tha's learnt owt.

Mate: ...

Me: And who's left, eh? Tha mam. Tha little lass. Me, stood at t' front wondering why I never said owt when I had t' chance. I'll be the one roaring at that funeral, and I'll not forgive missen for keeping quiet about it now.

Mate: Alright. Alright, tha's made thi point, stop looking at me like that. I'll not say I'll pack bike in altogether, but I'll ease off them roundabouts and slow through town, that's a promise.

Me: It'll have to do. Just be careful, love, that's all I'm asking.

Mate: I will, tha knows. It'll be reet.

Me: It'd better be.
