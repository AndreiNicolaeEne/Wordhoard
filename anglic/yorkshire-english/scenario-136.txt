She'd gone up to t'tin on top o't wardrobe where she kept a couple hundred by for t'month's stuff, and it were leet in her hand soon as she lifted it down. Counted it twice on t'kitchen table, and all, just to be sure, then had a look in t'biscuit tin an' all, where it sometimes got shifted when the leccy man were due round. Nowt there either.

She started asking round, mild like, at first.

Mother: Has anyone been in that tin at all? Only it's leet, and I know how much should be in it.

The eldest: Not me. I've not been near it, I swear.

The youngest: Don't look at me, I've been at work all week, haven't I.

Mother: Well summat's shifted it, it don't walk off on its own, does it.

Then she thought on it. Only one of them had been in t'house all that day, off poorly, home on thissen while t'rest were out.

Mother: Right. Was tha here Tuesday, on thi own, all day?

The middle one: Aye, I were, but I've not touched thi money, sithee.

Mother: Tha knows I'm not daft, love. There's nob'dy else could've had it.

The middle one: I've told thee once, I've not had a penny of it. Pack it in now.

Mother: Don't tha lie to me, I can see it on thi face!

The middle one: There's nowt on my face but sick to death of being blamed for owt that goes wrong in this house!

Mother: Somebody's had it, and it weren't nob'dy else, were it, only thee, home on thi own all day!

The middle one: I'm not saying it again. I've not had it, and tha can shout thissen hoarse, it won't change nowt!

Nob'dy shifted an inch after that, and nob'dy fettled t'tea neither, they just stood there, both on 'em, sure as owt they were right.
