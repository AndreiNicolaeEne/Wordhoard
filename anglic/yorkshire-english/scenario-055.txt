Now then, sithee, tha's missed a right day and I'm not being funny, I'll tell thee it all from t'start because nowt of it went how we'd planned.

We were meant to be up and off for eight, van booked for half eight sharp, everything laid out t'night before ready to go. Only our youngest were roaring half the night with her teeth playing up, none of us got a wink, so I didn't stir while gone nine, and straight off we're running late before we've even had us tea let alone opened t'curtains.

The husband: I thought we said eight.

Me: Aye well tha can go and tell her teeth that.

Then first thing goes wrong before we're even out the door. Key wouldn't turn in t'front lock, jammed solid, been dodgy for weeks and I kept saying summat wants doing about it and nowt got done, typical. There's me stood on t'step in me coat wrestling wi' it like a gormless article, van chap already rung to say he's five minutes off.

The husband: What's tha doing out there?

Me: It's stuck, it won't budge, come and put some welly in it.

Took the pair of us and a drop of oil before it'd give. Nearly didn't get out of us own house.

And then who turns up, not asked, not rung, just turns up: the neighbour, stood on t'step in her pinny with a flask of tea under her arm.

The neighbour: Ey up. I heard t'van pull up and thought tha could do wi' another pair of hands, tha's not shifting a house on thi own.

And honest, she were worth three of us that morning, in and out wi' boxes while the rest of us were stood about like spare parts working out where to start.

It were going along all right for a bit and then t'shout went up. Me nan's mirror, the big one off t'landing wall, been in t'family donkey's years, and somebody, never did get to bottom of who, everyone's blaming everyone else to this day, set it down wrong on t'stairs and it went straight through the bottom step and shattered, glass everywhere. I heard the crash and the scream at same time and me heart went right through t'floor. Stood there looking at seven years bad luck in bits on t'carpet and nobody said a word for about ten seconds, then:

The husband: Oh for God's sake.

And our eldest started roaring proper, tears and everything, because she thought she'd get t'blame for it.

Me: Nay love, it weren't thee, don't take on.

We never did find one of the boxes an' all. Kitchen stuff, marked up plain as day, seen it go on t'van meself, and it never turned up at t'other end. Rung the van firm, rung round, nowt. Gone. Whoever's got it, I hope they're enjoying us good pans, because we're not.

Then there were t'argument over t'wardrobe. Great big heavy thing, solid wood, weighs a ton.

The husband: We'll carry it flat, it'll go easier.

Me: Tha'll have the bottom out of it going flat, we want it on its end.

The husband: It'll not go through t'door on its end, tha daft beggar.

Back and forth we went, stood there in t'hallway, while the poor van chap's just stood watching us waiting for us to sort us selves out. In t'end we did it his way, flat, and I were right an' all, it near enough had the bottom out of it, though I've not let him forget it since.

Which brings me to t'stairwell, because that were a whole other saga. That wardrobe would not go through for love nor money. Turned it this way, turned it that way, up on end, flat, sideways, nowt worked, it just would not clear t'bend at top of t'stairs.

The neighbour: Tip it towards thissen a bit more, go on.

The husband: I am tipping it, it's stuck fast.

Twenty minutes we were at it, the neighbour holding one end, me holding t'other, the husband down below shouting instructions nobody could follow, and in t'end we had to have t'door off its hinges before it'd budge. I were fair mafted by then, sweating summat rotten and it were only just gone dinnertime.

And that's when t'weather turned an' all, because up to then it had been all right, bit grey but nowt doing, and then it started siling it down, proper hammering, and we're running boxes out to t'van getting soaked through, cardboard going to mush in us hands.

The husband: Get a shift on, it's coming through t'boxes!

Went from dry to drowned in about the time it takes to say it.

By that point we were all starving and nowt had been ate all day, so we stopped, stood up, right there among t'boxes in t'kitchen that weren't ours to keep no more, and had us dinner off paper plates balanced on a tea chest. Breadcakes, ham, a flask of tea gone lukewarm, and honest, it were the best meal I've had in years, don't ask me why. Saved t'afternoon, that did. Nobody said much, everybody just stood there eating, and for about twenty minutes it were the only bit of peace we got all day.

The neighbour: There's nowt like a breadcake for putting heart back in thee.

After that it were just load, and load, and load again, and it's them last few loads that finish thee off, tha knows. Legs gone, arms gone, everybody talking in half sentences, the neighbour still going strong bless her, and me just thinking get it in t'van, get it in t'van, one more, one more. Me back were talking to me summat proper by t'end of it.

Last thing, before we locked up, I walked through them empty rooms on us own. Funny how a house looks once it's stripped bare, every room bigger and smaller both at once, marks on t'walls where t'pictures used to hang, dust in squares where t'furniture stood. Stood in what were us bedroom for God knows how many years and it were dead quiet, just me and t'rain on t'window, and I'll not lie, I had a right lump in me throat. Said tara to it in me head, daft as that sounds, then locked t'door for t'last time and put key through t'letterbox for t'new lot.

The husband: Tha coming, or is tha stopping wi' t'house?

We didn't get to t'new place while gone dark, proper dark, van headlights the only light on t'street, everybody dead on their feet, and when we counted up what we'd got off t'van it were less than what we'd started t'day with, what wi' t'mirror and t'missing box and all sorts left behind in t'rush. But we were there. Kettle went on before owt else, curtains not even up yet, and we sat on boxes drinking us tea.

Me: It'll be reet.

The husband: Aye. It'll be reet.

Champion do, that were. In't it.
