Me: Right, I'll say it plain, t' best stall on that market is t' one on t' corner, no argument, done.

My mate: Nay, tha's gone soft in t' head. T' one by t' fish counter knocks spots off it, everyone knows that.

Me: Knocks nowt off owt. I've had their pie, it were dry as owt, sat there like a brick while I were choking it down wi' half a cup o' tea just to shift it.

My mate: One bad pie and tha's writing 'em off? Champion cheek that is. Their breadcake wi' t' hot beef is proper grand, gravy right through, none o' this dry muck tha's on about.

Me: Aye well I went there Tuesday, queued ten minute in t' siling rain, and t' lass behind t' counter were that gormless she gave us t' wrong order twice. Nithered to death I were, standing there for nowt.

My mate: That's t' lass's fault, not t' stall's. Anyroad my corner stall's had you over an' all, tha knows, remember when they charged thee double and didn't even say sorry?

Me: That were a fluke.

My mate: Fluke my foot. Face it love, mine's t' best in t' whole city, best in t' county, best stall this side o' t' Pennines, nowt else comes close.

Me: Give over, tha's chelping now, that's a right daft claim that is.

My mate: Well it might be.

Me: Sithee, what about t' one round back, does breadcakes and all sorts, nobody's ever had owt bad to say about that one.

My mate: Aye, that's true that, can't fault it, not once.

Me: Right then, settle it proper. We'll do mine Friday, thine Saturday, and that one and all so it's fair, and we'll see who's chelping then.

My mate: Reet, tha's on. It'll be reet, one way or another.
