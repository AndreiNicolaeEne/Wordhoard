I've said it every year since we were courting and every year summat's eaten t'money, so this year it's happening and that's that.

The lass: Ow do, all right, calm thissen down, I'm not arguing wi' thee. How much we got though, that's the question, because dreaming's free and flights in't.

The lass: Right. Champion. Get pad and pen out then, side t'table, we'll do it proper, number by number, none o' this "it'll sort itself out" business, because it never does, it just leaves us skint in't second week wondering where it's all gone.

The lad: Biggest lump first then, getting us there. I were looking last neet whiles it were siling it down outside, four hundred quid return, and that's not even a daft time to fly, that's decent.

The lass: Four hundred out of a thousand's a lot to put on nowt but sitting on a plane wi' thi knees round thi ears.

The lad: Aye but there's no trip wi'out it, is there, no use having six hundred quid and no way of getting there. So that's four hundred gone, straight off, and we've six hundred left for everything else.

The lass: Right, six hundred. How many days we saying then, because that changes everything under it.

The lad: I were thinking a week.

The lass: A week's nowt, we'll have just got us bearings and be flying home again. We've waited this long, I want more than a week.

The lad: We can't be daft about it either, love, every day's another day of spending. Ten?

The lass: Ten I can live wi'. Ten proper days, not counting the flying days as full days, ten where we're actually there doing summat.

The lad: Ten it is then. Now sithee, there's one thing I'm not budging on whatever it costs, and that's top of t'Empire State. I don't care if it's a tenner or fifty, we are not going all that way and stopping on t'pavement looking up at it.

The lass: I know, I know, I weren't going to argue thee out of that one. We'll just have to fettle summat else to make room for it then. We can drop down a grade on where we're stopping, share a room wi' bunks instead of us own beds, save us a fair bit there and no harm done, we're not going to be laid in bed all day anyroad.

The lass: Seventy on top o' four hundred, that's four hundred and seventy. Stopping over, ten nights, cheaper room, that's about a hundred and sixty all told.

The lad: Four hundred and seventy and a hundred and sixty, put together that's six hundred and thirty.

The lass: Aye, and mind, if tha can get them flights down any, even fifty quid off, that's another day we could have. Eleven instead of ten. So keep thi eyes on it, don't just book t'first thing tha sees.

The lad: I will, tha knows I will, I'm not that gormless. Right, what's left then.

The lass: Six hundred and thirty spent out of a thousand, that leaves three hundred and seventy for food, gadding about, and owt that goes wrong, because summat always goes wrong.

The lad: Food. Twenty a day, the two of us, that do it?

The lass: It'll have to. Breadcake and a coffee of a morning, summat proper at neet, twenty a day between us is tight but it's doable if we're not eating out posh every meal.

The lad: Twenty a day, ten days, that's two hundred.

The lass: Two hundred on food, six hundred and thirty before that, that's eight hundred and thirty.

The lad: And I want a hundred put by and never touched unless summat proper goes wrong. Missed connection, lost bag, owt like that. A hundred sat there doing nowt, just in case.

The lass: A hundred aside, that's nine hundred and thirty. Seventy left in't whole pot.

The lad: Right, now, this is where I want us fifty for one big neet out. One posh do, proper meal, maybe a show, summat to remember, not just breadcakes and t'subway the whole trip.

The lass: Fifty's fifty though, love, we could just as easy put that fifty away and not spend it at all, keep it back for owt else, or bring it home wi' us.

The lad: What's t'point of a dream trip if we come home wi' fifty quid still in us pocket and nowt to show for it. We'll have earned one good neet out.

The lass: I'm not saying no to it exactly, I'm saying we've to be careful, that's all. We're tight as it is.

The lad: I know, but sithee, we said we'd do this proper for once, not scrimp on t'one thing that'd actually make it feel special. I say it stays in.

The lass: All right. All right, it stays in. Fifty for one neet out, we've earned that much at least. Chelp me if I change mi mind on it later.

The lass: Four hundred flights, seventy Empire State, hundred and sixty stopping, two hundred food, hundred put by, fifty for t'neet out. That's... hold on... that's a thousand and thirty.

The lad: Thirty over. We said not a penny past a thousand and we're thirty short of that mark.

The lass: We are. Summat's got to come off then, we can't be roaring about it, it's only thirty, it's not the end o' t'world.

The lad: That'd do it and all. Walking's free, and it'll be reet, we're not that soft.

The lad: Nine hundred and eighty out of a thousand. Twenty left spare.

The lass: Twenty spare for owt we've missed, a bit o' spice for t'plane home or summat daft like that.

The lad: Champion. That's it fettled then, proper, number by number, same as tha said.
