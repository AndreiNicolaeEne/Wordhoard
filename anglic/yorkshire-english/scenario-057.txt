The tenant: Now then.

The landlord: Ey up, love, come in out of it, tha's fair nithered, is it siling it down again out there?

The tenant: Aye, proper chucking it down. Put t' wood in t' oil behind thee, there's a right draught off that door.

The landlord: There tha goes. Sit thissen down, I'll not keep thee stood. Cup o' tea?

The tenant: Go on then, champion, ta.

The landlord: How's things, then, tha's kept busy?

The tenant: Can't grumble. Nithered most mornings this week, mind, and mafted by dinnertime, tha knows how it gets.

The landlord: Aye, it's been a rum sort of week for weather right enough.

The tenant: Anyroad, I'll not take up all thy afternoon, I wanted a word about t' rent.

The landlord: Oh aye?

The tenant: Aye. I've paid thee on t' first of every month, four year running now, never missed one, never a day late, tha knows that as well as I do.

The landlord: Tha has an' all, I'll not say different.

The tenant: And that back window's never shut right since I moved in, I've told thee before, draught comes under it summat rotten, I've had a towel down there all winter just to fettle it a bit.

The landlord: Aye well, I did say I'd get a joiner to look at it.

The tenant: Tha did say that, aye. So what I'm asking, could tha see thy way to knocking a bit off. A fifth off, that's what I were thinking, seeing as how things stand.

The landlord: A fifth? Nay, lass, I can't be doing that, not with what's going out on this place missen.

The tenant: I'm not after owt daft, just summat fair for a tenant as pays on time and puts up wi' a draughty window and nowt done about it.

The landlord: I hear thee, I do. I could manage a tenth, happen, no more than that.

The tenant: A tenth's not a deal between us, is it. Could we not meet somewhere in t' middle?

The landlord: Let me think on it over t' week, love, I'll not say no straight off.

The tenant: Champion, I'll leave it wi' thee then.

The landlord: Aye. Ta for coming round. Mind how tha goes, it's still siling it down out there.
