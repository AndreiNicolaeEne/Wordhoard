Me: Ey up. Are you in? I've come about that parkin, the one me gran used to make.

The aunt: Now then, get thissen in, tha's letting all t'heat out, put wood in t'oil.

Me: Sorry, it's siling it down out there, I'm fair nithered.

The aunt: I heard it on t'roof all morning. Sit thissen down then, I'll try and get it out me head, she never wrote nowt down did she, your gran, it were all in her hands, not on paper.

Me: That's why I've come, before it's lost altogether.

The aunt: Right well, sithee. Start wi' t'oatmeal, medium oatmeal mind, not fine, about a pound of it, give or take, tha can't be too fussed wi' t'scales on that one. Then t'flour, self raising, happen half again as much... oh, and I nearly forgot, mixed spice, a good teaspoon, can't leave that out or it's nowt but a plain cake and no spice at all.

Me: How much treacle does it want?

The aunt: Treacle and syrup together, melt em in a pan wi' t'butter, on a low light, and that's t'bit she allus warned us about, tha knows, never rush that bit, never let it catch or boil, she'd have thee off t'gas altogether if she caught thee turning it up.

Me: What order's it all go in then?

The aunt: Dry stuff in a bowl first, mix that up, then pour t'melted in while it's still warm, not hot mind, then egg and a drop of milk to loosen it, however much it wants, that bit's never measured, tha just knows by eye when it looks right, sloppy like a thick batter and no more.

Me: And how do you tell it's done, without watching t'clock?

The aunt: When it springs back under thy finger and t'kitchen smells right, that treacly smell coming through the ginger, then it's done. No clock wanted, gran never owned one that kept time anyroad.

Me: Could I use golden syrup instead of treacle, if I've none in?

The aunt: Aye, tha could, it'll still be reet, only it won't be as dark nor as sticky, still champion mind, just not quite same as hers.
