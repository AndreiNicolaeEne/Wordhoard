The wife: Now then, tha's summat wrong wi' thee this mornin'. Tha's sat there wi' thi tea gone cold and tha's not said two words since tha got up.

The husband: Sorry love. I'm reet, honest, just... me head's still on it, tha knows.

The wife: On what?

The husband: That dream I had. Can't shift it. Been turnin' it ovver since I opened me eyes and it's not give me a minute's peace since.

The wife: Go on then, what were it about? Sithee, drink thi tea while it's warm, and tell us.

The husband: Right, well. It started off nowt strange at all. I were just walkin' home, same road I allus tek, past t'shop and up by t'school, ordinary as owt. Nothin' odd about it, not at first.

The wife: Right.

The husband: But it kept goin' on. Tha knows how long that road is normally, ten minute if that. Well in this it just kept on and on, past where us house should've been, and I kept thinkin', it's just round next corner, it's just past that lamppost, and it never were.

The wife: That's not like thee, gettin' lost on thi own road.

The husband: I know, and I weren't even bothered about it while I were in it, that's the funny thing. Just kept walkin'. Anyroad, I got there in t'end, house looked right enough from outside, and I went in, and there were a room in it I've never seen in me life. Not in this house, not in any house I've ever lived in. Just there, like it'd allus been there.

The wife: What sort of room, though? Go on, what were in it?

The husband: Well that's the thing. Me dad were sat in it.

The wife: Never.

The husband: Aye. Calm as owt, love, sat there like he'd only popped in for five minutes, like no time had gone by at all. Not a day of it. And he were talkin' to us.

The wife: What were he sayin'?

The husband: That's just it, I can't tell thee. I've been sat here since I woke tryin' to get it back and it won't come. I know he said summat, I know it meant summat, I can feel it were important, like I'd been told a thing I needed, but the actual words is just gone. It's like tryin' to hold water in thi hand.

The wife: Eh, that's give me right shivers, that has. Try again, go on, close thi eyes if tha has to.

The husband: I have tried, love, three or four times now sat here. It's on t'tip of it and then it's not, it just slides off. I don't think it's comin' back. I'll just have to leave it.

The wife: That's a rum do, that is.

The husband: It is. And then it shifted again, like they do, and I were lookin' for summat. Proper franticlike, tha knows, openin' drawers, lookin' under things, and it weren't where it should've been, and I kept lookin' and lookin' and it just weren't turnin' up nowhere.

The wife: What were tha lookin' for, though?

The husband: I can't tell thee that either, love, and I'm not messin' thee about, I mean it, I couldn't say. Even while I were in it, lookin', I didn't rightly know what it were I were after. Just knew I had to find it and it weren't there.

The wife: That's summat, that, lookin' for a thing tha can't even name.

The husband: I know. And it got worse, the longer it went on, the more panicked I got, heart goin' like owt, and I woke up wi' it still bangin' away, lyin' there in t'dark not rightly knowin' for a minute which were real, dream or t'room I'd woke up in. Had to lie still a good while just gettin' me breath back, workin' out where I actually were.

The wife: No wonder tha's sat there like a wet weekend. That'd shake anyone up, that would.

The husband: It did. I'm champion now, don't get me wrong, it's just stuck wi' us, tha knows how they do sometimes.

The wife: Well I don't know what it's supposed to mean, someone gone turnin' up like that, calm as a Sunday. Me nan allus said if they come back peaceful in a dream it means they're reet where they are.

The husband: Me mam used to say summat similar an' all, that it's them lettin' thee know they're fine and not to fret. I don't know if I believe that or if it's just what folk say to make it easier.

The wife: Could be nowt more than thi own head sortin' things out, mind. That's what our lass reckons, she read it somewhere.

The husband: Aye, could be that an' all. Though it's a rum way of sortin' things out, if it is, given us in a state like this ovver it.

The wife: Well whatever it were, he looked well, didn't he.

The husband: He did, love. Looked grand actually. Better than he did towards t'end, if I'm honest.

The wife: There tha go then. Take that from it if nowt else.

The husband: Aye. Maybe that's all it were tryin' to tell us and I've just gone and forgot the easy bit of it.

The wife: Could be. Or maybe it weren't owt to do wi' him at all and it were all about whatever tha were lookin' for and lost. Might be that's the bit tha wants to be thinkin' on.

The husband: Eh, don't, tha'll have us up all night on it now.

The wife: I'm only sayin'. Drink thi tea, it'll be stone cold.

The husband: It already is.

The wife: Well side thi cup ovver here then and I'll warm it up. Least it'll be reet by time tha's off out.

The husband: Ta, love. I'll not lie, I'm glad I've said it out loud. Sat here on us own wi' it goin' round and round in me head it were doin' me in a bit.

The wife: That's what I'm here for, love, tha knows that. Owt else tha needs to get off thi chest while we're at it?

The husband: Nay, that's the lot. Though if I see that room again I'm havin' a proper look round it next time, see if there's owt else in there.

The wife: Get away wi' thee. Drink thi tea and let's be gettin' on, we'll be late else.

The husband: Aye, aye. Give us two minutes.
