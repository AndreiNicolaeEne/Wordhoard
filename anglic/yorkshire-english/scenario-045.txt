Ey up, I've come for me suit and it's stood ont' hanger looking nowt like what I ordered.

Me: Now then. This isn't right, this.

The tailor: Ow do, love, aye, that's thine, finished it missen last night an' all.

Me: It's not, though. I asked thee for three buttons and a straight hem, and this one's got two buttons on it and the hem's gone all angled at t' front.

The tailor: Nay, tha's mistook, that's exactly what were agreed when tha came in, I don't make daft mistakes like that.

Me: I'm telling thee straight, I stood right where I'm stood now and said three button, straight across, plain as owt. Sithee, I'm not gormless, I know what I asked for.

The tailor: Hang on then, hold thi horses, no need for chelp, I'll go and check t' book.

The tailor goes through to t' back, and I stand there mafted in me coat, waiting.

The tailor: Right, I've had a look at t' original measurements, and tha knows what, it says two button here in me own hand, but there's a note pinned under from when tha called back about t' hem, and that's near enough thi own writing, straight, underlined twice.

Me: So it's t' buttons tha's got wrong then, not me.

The tailor: Aye, looks like it. Our lass must've copied it down wrong off first note when she were doing t' order, two instead o' three. I'll not lie to thee, it's our mess up.

Me: Well it wants sorting, I'm not walking about in a coat that won't sit right for owt.

The tailor: It'll be reet, love. Leave it wi' me, I'll have three button back on and that hem put straight, give us two days and it'll be champion, better than it were first time.

Me: Two days then. I'll not chelp on about it again if it's sorted proper.

The tailor: It will be, sithee. Put t' wood in t' oil on thi way out, it's siling it down and I don't want it blowing through.

Me: Go on then. Ta, love.
