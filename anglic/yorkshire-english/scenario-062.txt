Me: Ey up, love, sit thissen down a minute. Tha's an offcumden thissen, only landed on this street a few week back, so I don't suppose anybody's telled thee owt about it proper. I'll tell thee my side, and that's all I'll tell thee, because there's nowt more to it than my side any road, whatever gets said different.

Now then. Years back, and I mean years, this street were nowt like it is now. Us and them next door, we were thick as owt. In and out of one another's kitchens like there weren't a wall between the two houses at all. Her mother, God rest her, used to send our lot round of a Sunday wi' a bag o' spice for the kids, and my mother'd send it back with a breadcake still warm off the top, and on it went, back and forward, year in year out. Kids laiking out in the street together, running wild up and down like the whole road belonged to nob'dy but them. If it were nithered out in January, whichever house had coal in'd have both families sat round it. If it were mafted come August, other house had shade and a fan going and you were in there wi'out asking. If washing were out and it started siling it down of a sudden, whoever were nearest got both lines in before owt got soaked through. That were how it were. Tha'd not credit it now, looking at us, but that's God's truth, that is.

Then, oh, must be twenty year since now, it started wi' nowt at all. Not a row. Not a falling out. They put a shed up. Right against the line, practically leaning on my fence, and it cut the afternoon light off our kitchen window summat rotten. I didn't say a word about it for months, mind. Thought, it'll be reet, they'll shift it come spring, plant summat nicer there instead. Only they never did.

So I asked. Polite as owt, over the fence of an evening while he were out having a fag. Said, would he mind shifting shed back a few inches, only it were cutting the light off proper. He said aye, no bother, he'd see to it. Weeks went by. Nowt happened. I asked again, same as before, no edge on it whatsoever, just a "any road, you still thinking on shifting that shed?" Same answer. Aye, aye, he'd get round to it. Third time I asked, I'll not lie to thee, there were a bit of an edge on it by then. Anybody'd have one by then, tha knows. And I got fetched all sorts of nowt for my trouble that time an' all.

Then it started coming back to me sideways, through her two doors up, who's forever carrying word between the two houses like she's paid a wage for it. She telled me they were saying I'd been having a right go at him over that shed, stood there shouting at him over the fence like a fishwife. Shouting! I never raised my voice once in all that, and I said so plain, and she just shrugged, like carrying it were nowt to do with her, only the messenger. By the time it landed on my doorstep it were a different tale altogether from owt that actually happened, I'll swear to that on owt tha likes.

So I went round myself to sort it, straight, and she stood there bold as brass and said I'd never asked about that shed at all, not once, that she'd have had it shifted gladly if I had. Flat denial, to my face, like I were making it up out of thin air. Right gormless I must have looked, standing there, because I'd nowt to say back to that sort of brass neck. That were the first time I thought, right, summat's shifted here for good and it isn't shifting back.

So I'll own it, and I'm not proud, I stopped putting their bin out when collection day moved and they were away visiting. Small thing. Nowt to write home about. But I knew full well what I were doing when I did it.

Then the comment came back to me, same one carrying word again, and this were the one that stuck a knife in proper. She'd said to somebody, might as well have said it to my face for all the good it did keeping it from me, that our lot were all mouth and no manners, allus had been, and her mother were same before her. Her mother. Gone eight year by then, and never did a thing to anybody in her life but bring bread cakes round of a Sunday. That were the one comment. Not shed, not bin, none of the rest of it. That were the one I've never forgiven, not properly, not to this day.

I did try and mend it, mind, I'll give missen that much credit. Baked a cake, one of them fruit ones her mother used to fettle every Christmas, took it round myself, said let's leave all this daftness, we've known each other too long for a shed and a bin to come between us. Handed it straight back to me on the step, unopened, said they didn't want owt off us, thank you very much. That were that. Nowt else to be done after that.

Rest of the street's split down the middle since, near enough. Half of 'em side wi' us, half side wi' them, and tha can tell which is which by who nods at who over the wheelie bins of a morning and who looks straight through thee instead. There were one almighty row out in the street an' all, everybody's curtains twitching, both households out there giving it large where every last soul on this block could hear every word of it. Somebody said summat about braying somebody, I won't say who, and that were about the size of the whole street's business that day.

Then somebody claimed we'd damaged a fence panel of theirs, or nicked a bit of trellis off the side of their yard, I forget which now and it hardly matters, and I denied it flat, because I never touched a thing of theirs, whatever it were meant to be.

Fifteen year that silence has run now, love. Fifteen year, and it shows itself daily in the smallest of ways. We don't so much as look at one another over that fence any more. Bins go out same morning, side by side, not a word passed between us. If washing's out and it starts siling it down sudden, nob'dy's rescuing owt off next door's line these days, tha can bet thy life on that, whatever it were like before. And if I'm honest wi' thee, and I don't say this to many that ask, some days I'm not right sure any more who actually started it. Whether it were shed, or summat before the shed I've gone and forgot all about, or whether two households that close together were bound to fall out over summat sooner or later, whatever shape it took. I couldn't tell thee straight, not now, not really. But that's my side of it, for whatever it's worth to thee.
