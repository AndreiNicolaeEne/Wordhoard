Quarter past eleven now, and he said eight. That's not "a bit late," that's three hour and rising, three and a half by the time I've done owt about it. I'll give him while midnight, I says to missen, knowing full well midnight'll come and go same as everything else has tonight.

First off I thought, he'll have missed the last bus and walked it, that's all, that's what's happened, tha knows how it goes of a Friday, buses packed to bursting. Told missen that for a good hour, half believing it, half not.

Then I'm up again, side t'table wiped for the third time though there were nowt on it to wipe, and over to the window, and back to the door, and put t'wood in t'oil again though it were never open in the first place. Sat down. Up again.

"He'll be reet," I says out loud, to no one, to the dog stretched out not caring one way or other. "He'll be reet, he'll be reet." Then the other thought comes in anyway, the one I'm not letting in, about roads and lorries and hospitals at this hour, and I have to stand up sharp to shift it off us.

Minds me of the time his dad were three hour late off the rig and I sat same as this, same chair, same clock ticking, and it were nowt in the end, only the phone were dead. That's the comfort of it and the dread of it both together, tha knows, because it were nowt that time.

Half four in the morning now. I could go out looking, walk down as far as the lights and back, only what if he comes while I'm gone and finds the house empty and thinks summat's up with me. So I sit.

A car outside, engine, doors, up I go, heart going like billy-o, and it's gone past, off up the road, nowt to do with us.

Then the gate.

Me: Is that you.

The eldest: Ey up. Aye, it's me, sorry, phone were dead, we...

Me: Never mind your phone. Get in here out t'cold, tha's nithered looking, and don't you dare tell me it's nowt, three hour I've sat, love, three hour...
