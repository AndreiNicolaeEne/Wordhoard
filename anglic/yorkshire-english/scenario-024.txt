Him: Right, we need to get this list sorted, we're thirty over what t'room'll hold, they told us straight.

Her: I know love, don't go on at me, I've had a day of it.

Him: I'm not going on, I'm saying we sit down now and fettle it before it's too late to fettle owt.

Her: Go on then, where do we start.

Him: Start with them neither of us hardly speaks to. Thy cousins from over Wakefield way, my second cousins, folk off work we only see at t'Christmas do. That's happen fifteen off, straight away, no bother.

Her: Fifteen. That'd get us near enough half of what we need.

Him: Champion. Right, next.

Her: Not touching her though.

Him: Who.

Her: Tha knows who. She's stopping on that list whatever else comes off it.

Him: Ey up, here we go again.

Her: I mean it. She were there for us when nobody else were, she's not going anywhere.

Him: Well if she's stopping, someone off my side has to go instead. Only fair, that.

Her: Here it comes -- your side's had more seats than mine at every do since we got engaged, don't tell me it's fair.

Him: That's not true.

Her: It is though. Your mum's lot fill half the room and mine get shoved on the edge table like offcumdens.

Him: Don't start that tonight, I'm mafted enough as it is.

Her: I'm not starting, I'm saying what's true.

Him: What if we did a little do after, for them that don't fit, sandwiches and that, nowt fancy.

Her: No. Absolutely not, someone always finds out and then it's worse than if we'd never asked.

Him: Just a thought, no need to bray me for it.

Her: A daft one, that's all.

Him: Right, count 'em again.

Her: Still a dozen over, near enough.

Him: Bloody hell.

Her: I'll shift two off mine. Two, and no more. And she's stopping, I'll not budge on that.

Him: Fine. Two. We'll sithee tomorrow and sort t'rest. It'll be reet in't end.

Her: It'd best be. Put kettle on, I'm parched.
