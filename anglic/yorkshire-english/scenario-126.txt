Me: Ey up. Tha'll never believe what happened to us last neet.

My mate: Go on then, let's hear it. Bet tha were half asleep and dreaming summat.

Me: I were wide awake, tha knows. Thinking clear as owt, not a bit muddled.

My mate: Aye, well, go on then.

Me: I'd got up for a wee, gone three, and I looked out t'landing window down at t'garden, and there were someone stood right at back fence. Dead still. Not a move on 'em, just stood there looking up at t'house.

My mate: It'll have been shadow off t'streetlight, flower, or washing tha'd left flapping about.

Me: There were no washing out. It'd been siling it down since dinnertime, I fetched it all in missen afore tea. And a shadow doesn't stand there three minute while I'm watching it, then just isn't there when I look again.

My mate: Happen it were t'neighbour's lad out with dog, or some offcumden cutting through to t'ginnel.

Me: Gate were bolted on inside, sithee, I checked it missen come morning. There were a frost an' all, and nowt, not one footprint, leading up to where it stood nor away from it neither. Summat stood there a good while and left no mark on owt.

My mate: Tha's having us on now.

Me: I swear it on me nan's grave, every word's true, I'll say it while I'm blue in face. I know what I saw and I weren't dreaming it, I weren't nithered nor half-asleep nor owt else.

My mate: I still say tha were dozing and thy mind were playing tricks on thee. I'll not believe it, not if tha swears on owt going.

Me: Champion. Don't believe us then, see if I care. But I know what stood in that garden watching t'house, and I'll not forget it while I'm ninety.
