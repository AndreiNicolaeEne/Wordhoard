Ey up. Now then. Sit thissen down a minute, I've to tell thee this one proper because tha weren't there and it wants telling right, start to finish, none of it left out.

It started dead soft, like these things always do. We were down t'local of a Tuesday, quiet night, nowt on telly worth stopping in for, and there's a frame of pool laiking on between me and this mate of mine, and somebody chelps up from the side, half laughing, not thinking owt of it either.

Someone from the crowd: Go on then, put your money where your gob is.

Me: Aye, go on then, why not.

That were it. That were the whole start of it, one daft line lobbed across a pool table wi' nowt riding on it at all, and by t'time anybody'd chalked a cue again it had grown a life of its own, tha knows how it does.

Now here's the thing, nobody'd actually said what were being lost. Money got mentioned first because that's what you say out of habit, but it were only a shape of a bet, no bones in it yet. My mate were dead confident, stood there swaggering like he'd won it already, and I'll admit I weren't half as sure as I were letting on, but tha never lets on, do you.

And the lot standing round, they smelled it, and they were straight in chelping, topping each other before a single ball had even been potted.

One from the crowd: Twenty on it.

Another: Nay, make it worth summat.

A third: Twenty's plenty, nob'dy wants owt daft.

And it settled dead sensible in the end, twenty quid, small enough it wouldn't sting whoever lost it, and we shook on it right there before either of us had so much as broke off.

Pub were mafted that night, packed in, telly off, everybody crowded round that one table like it were t'World Championship, and it were a right ding-dong of a game, back and forth, back and forth, neither of us properly ahead, missing easy ones, sinking daft long ones off the cush that shouldn't have gone in at all. I thought for a good while it could go either road. But then it turned. It turned hard. He left us a shot that were nowt, an open black fair begging for it, and I knew, and everybody round that table knew, before I'd even chalked up, that it were done. Bet were lost right there in everybody's head, minutes while the game itself were even finished.

And when it were finished, properly finished, he just stood there, cue in hand, and groaned this great groan like he'd been shot.

Mate: Aye, that's me, I've lost it. No getting round it, no point chelping on about it now.

Fair do's to him for that much, at least, no excuses, no messing.

Now here's where it goes a bit strange, because that twenty were never handed over, not a penny of it. Because while he's stood there groaning, somebody in the crowd, I still don't rightly know who started it, pipes up.

Someone in the crowd: Nay, forget the money. He's doing a turn for it.

And that were it, decided, on the spot, no vote needed, and they weren't vague about it neither, they laid it all out there and then, dead specific: he had to get up in front of the whole lot of them at the next do, dressed up daft, and sing a full song, start to finish, wi' actions, no bottling out halfway through it.

He tried it on, mind, tried to buy his way out of it.

Mate: Go on, I'll just give thee t'twenty and we'll call it quits.

Whoever it was: Nay, too late for that now, love. You're doing t'turn.

And that were final, no bargaining, no way out of it now, laughing at him while they said it and all, and you could see under his moaning that he knew it too, missen included, we all knew he weren't getting out of it.

So a night got set, same local, few weeks on, a proper do, place packed out, and it were siling down outside all evening, which somehow made it feel more like an occasion, everybody piling in out the wet with their coats steaming on the radiators. And when it came round he actually did it proper, didn't try and slope off or do it half-hearted, gormless as he can be about most things but never about a bet once it's set.

Got himself a wig off somewhere, God knows where he'd dug that out from, and a shirt done up wrong wi' the buttons all skew, and stood himself up on the little bit of stage they've got in the corner and gave it the full song, start to finish, actions and all, everybody stood round roaring already before he'd even got past the first verse.

But the bit that still gets brought up, the bit that had the whole pub gone that night, were when he went for this big high note right near t'end, proper reaching for it, eyes shut tight, giving it everything he had, and the wig went. Just went, slid clean off the side of his head and landed straight in some woman's pint at the front table, and he never even stopped singing, just carried on bald as owt in that daft skew shirt, dead straight-faced, like nowt had happened at all. Place went up. Grown men roaring, one lad had to sit down he were laughing that hard, somebody else nearly choked on their pint. He finished the whole song like that, wig gone, took his bow dead serious, and it were champion, honestly, better than if it had gone smooth.

That's the thing of it, that story's outlasted the twenty quid by a mile. Nobody remembers the twenty now. Half of them can't even tell thee proper how the bet got started in the first place. But get that lot together now, any do, any Christmas, any funeral even, and somebody'll bring up the wig in the pint, and it grows a bit bigger every time, tha knows how it does. It's two pints it lands in now, last I heard, and he did a full lap of the bar bald before anybody could stop him, none of which happened, but nob'dy's stopping it either. He just sits there and lets it happen, groans the same groan he did the first time, and says it'll be reet, it's always been reet, and puts t'wood in t'oil on his way out same as ever, story trailing after him bigger than when it went in.
