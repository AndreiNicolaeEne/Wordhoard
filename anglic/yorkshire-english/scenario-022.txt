The teller: Ey up! Now then, I've been dying to catch thee, I have.

The listener: Ow do, love. Tha's got a face on thee like tha's swallowing a secret and it won't go down.

The teller: I am an' all. Tha knows who I mean when I say she were stood off by t'burger vans wi' him, at t'festival, Sunday teatime?

The listener: Who? Go on, don't mess about, who.

The teller: Her from up t'road, and him. Standing right off from everyone, away from t'crowd, laughing their heads off at summat, and I mean close, love, closer than owt two mates'd stand.

The listener: Never! Get away, that's never nowt, is it. Tha's not just reading it in cos they were having a laugh?

The teller: That's what I thought an' all, till I heard t'other bit. My mate from work seen 'em again later, walking back towards t'car park, just t'two on 'em, everyone else long gone by then.

The listener: Bloody hell. Who else knows by now, does tha reckon? Half of Leeds'll have heard by dinnertime, way these things travel.

The teller: Aye, well, it'll be all round before we've even had us tea, I'll bet thee that.

The listener: Champion bit of gossip that, whatever it turns out to be. But sithee, it could still be nowt, tha knows. Folk stand close at festivals, everyone's had a few, doesn't mean owt.

The teller: True enough, love. Only road to know reet is to ask one on 'em straight out, isn't it.

The listener: Aye. It is that. Reckon I'll be doing that missen, first chance I get.
