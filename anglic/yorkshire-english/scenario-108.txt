Now then, our kid. Ey up, and all, while I'm at it, because you always did say I never open owt properly.

I'll put me hands up straight off, this has took me months to sit down and do proper, and you've every right to have a go at us for it when this finally lands on your mat. I've started it about three times on the back of them old envelopes and it's ended up under the fruit bowl each time, half a page and nowt more. So tonight I've made a full pot of tea, shut door on the racket downstairs, and I'm not shifting while it's done. Here it is, whole, the way it should have come months since.

House first, because I know you'll want that before owt else. T'back step's finally been done, would you believe it, after your dad going on about it since before you left. It were cracked right through and rocking every time anybody stood on it, and he's only gone and got a fella from two streets over to relay it proper, level and everything, no more catching your foot of a morning. Mind, no sooner is that fettled than t'guttering's started letting go over kitchen window, so it's one thing out, one thing in, same as it's always been with this house. And your mam's had a rhubarb patch put in down t'side where the old coal bunker were. She reckons it'll be grand by back end, though it looks like nowt but sticks at minute.

The big news, and I should have told you this while it were happening rather than let you hear it secondhand off her down the phone, is she's packed in at t'factory. Twenty-odd year on that line and she's finally had enough, what with them cutting shifts about and half the floor gone since back end. She's took mornings at t'school canteen instead, dishing dinners out to bairns who chelp at her something rotten, she says, half of them mythering her for spice before they've even had their dinners, and she reckons if one more of them chelps she'll bray it, though we both know she'd do no such thing. Comes home happier than I've seen her in donkey's years, mind, so I'll not begrudge her the pay cut. Your dad reckons she'll be back within the twelvemonth, bored stiff, but I don't see it missen.

Sithee, don't ask our mam about the tin of paint. She'll know exactly what I mean and she won't laugh, not properly, but you and me both know it's still funny.

Far as neighbours go, you'll remember the lot at the end, the ones with the caravan always half up the pavement. Gone, moved out towards the coast back in spring, and there's a young couple in now, offcumdens by the sound of them, keep themselves to theirsen mostly. Stood there looking right gormless the day the removal van couldn't get round the bend, if I'm honest, though the fella waved once after, which is more than the last lot ever did. And her two doors down, you'll want to know, she's a grandbairn now, her eldest's lass had it just gone, back in spring, a right bonny little thing by all accounts, though I've only seen it the once, wrapped up like a parcel on the step, laiking with nowt but its own fingers.

I saw her at t'gate the other week, matter of fact, and it went summat like this.

Her two doors down: Now then, love, how's your sister getting on then, is she still over there?

Me: Aye, still there, seems to be doing all right for herself.

Her two doors down: Well you tell her sithee from me, and tell her I've still got that dish of hers she never came back for.

Me: I will an' all, though I'll not promise she'll remember it.

Your old gaffer from the warehouse asks after you every time I'm past there, wants to know are you still doing the same line of work and is it paying better than what he had you on, which I always say aye to whether it's true or not, because it shuts him up quicker. He's not the only one either. The woman at the corner shop still asks, every single week, like clockwork, ever since you used to get your paper off her.

Here's a thing I'll write down that I'd never say to your face down the phone, because it'd come out wrong and you'd fuss over us for no reason. Some mornings I still get two mugs down before I remember there's only me having me tea at that table now. It's nowt, I'm not stood there roaring over it or owt daft like that, it just catches us a second, and then I put the spare mug away and get on. Don't go saying owt back about it when you write, just let us have said it once.

Back end were mild enough, nothing to write home about, but this winter just gone were a proper nithering one, sithee, the kind where you're glad of the fire and cross at the electric bill in the same breath, and I'll admit I still say put t'wood in t'oil to an empty doorway some nights when there's nobody left to hear us say it or laugh at us for it. Then soon as spring turned it went the other road entire and we were fair mafted by May, sat with all the windows open and still no air moving. It's been siling it down on and off this last fortnight, mind, so I'll not complain too loud about the heat while it lasts.

Now then, questions, because I want proper answers and not just "aye it's grand" fobbed off down the phone like you do. Is that flat of yours warm enough of a night, because I worry, and don't just say aye to shut us up. Have you made any pals out there yet that you'd actually want round for your tea, or is it still just folk from work? Are you eating proper, or living off owt out of a packet because you can't be bothered? And how's the job itself treating you, are they working you like a mule same as they were before you left, or has it eased off any? I want it all, tha knows, not the three-line version.

Your dad's stuck to his guns and all, still won't call it owt but a breadcake, never a roll, not once in thirty year, and I don't reckon he'll shift on it now just because there's nobody left in the house to argue it with him but me and your mam, who's given up trying.

Right, I'm nearly done and it's gone eleven, and I did say to missen I'd not go to bed while this were finished. I know I say it every single time and never do it, but I mean it now, I'm getting them old photos of the pair of us sorted into a box and sent on to you, the whole lot, promise. It'll be reet, sithee, this time it's going in t'post before the week's out.

Take care of thissen, love, and side t'table for me in that head of yours while you're reading this, just for old times. Write back when you've a minute, and don't leave it as long as I did.
