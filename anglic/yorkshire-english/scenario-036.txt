Everyone else in t'family's had a go over t'years and it always looked dead easy when it were someone else doing t'graft. I sat down one night with a cup of tea gone stone cold and wrote t'menu out proper. Started sensible enough, then I thought, well, we'll have this an' all, and before I knew it I'd got about four more dishes than any table needs. Tha knows how it goes, you get carried away thinking it'll all be grand, and it looks reet impressive on paper, which is where it should've stopped, if I'm honest.

And then t'numbers kept creeping up an' all. First it were just us, me mam and me sister. Then me sister says can she bring her fella, then it were her workmate who'd nowt on that day, then it were the neighbour who'd been on her own since her husband passed, and I weren't going to say no to that, were I. Ended up with near enough double what I'd first planned for, and I'm sat there thinking, well, that's t'oven sorted then, isn't it, no room left for owt else at all.

Went shopping two days before and it were bedlam. Queuing outside t'shop before it even opened, nithered right through waiting for the doors, siling it down the whole time an' all. And the one thing, the ONE thing I could not find anywhere in that shop nor the next two I tried, were fresh sage. Everybody and their dog had bought it up. I were stood there like a right gormless article, up and down the herb aisle three times like it might magic itself onto the shelf. Ended up ringing round half t'family asking had anyone got sage growing, and me nan, bless her, had some in a pot on her windowsill, so that were sorted, but it cost me an afternoon I hadn't got spare.

Night before, I were up while gone one doing prep, peeling and chopping and getting stuff marinating, radio on, absolutely knackered, thinking I'd got it all timed out lovely. I actually wrote a proper schedule on paper, hour by hour, what goes in when, what comes out when, colour coded an' all if tha can credit that.

That schedule lasted about twenty minutes into the actual morning before it fell to bits completely. Half the stuff wanted t'oven at the same time, the veg took longer than I'd reckoned, and I were stood there at half seven already behind and it hadn't even properly started.

Then came t'first proper disaster. I'd got summat in there I were dead proud of, and I got that busy sorting summat else that I clean forgot about it while it were sat cooking, and by the time I remembered it were bone dry and black on top, ruined outright, no saving it. I stood there for a good minute just looking at it, could've roared right there in t'kitchen.

The neighbour: Ow do, I'm not too early, am I?

Course, not everything landed. One dish just didn't work, texture were all wrong, and me sister's fella, who's an offcumden, not from round here at all but moved up a good few years back, says exactly what he thinks and no more of it. He gave his verdict straight out, no dressing it up.

Her fella: Nay, this one's not reet, this. What's gone off in it then?

But the dish that come out well, the one I'd been that nervous about, disappeared first of the lot, gone in about ten minutes flat, and I had three separate people after the recipe and one saying it were champion, best thing on t'table, which after the morning I'd had felt grand beyond owt.

Someone asked would I do it all again next year and I said, half joking, half not, ask us again in about a fortnight when I've got the will to live back, flower, because right now I don't know if I ever want to see a roasting tin again as long as I live.
