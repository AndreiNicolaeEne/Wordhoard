Sit thissen down a minute, love, I'll side t'table and put kettle on, there's a breadcake in't tin if tha wants one, it's siling it down out there summat rotten. I want to tell thee about t'crossing on t'way back, and I'll tell it as it were, no bigger and no daft either, just how it went.

Tha knows that stretch after t'docks, where road forks and one side goes on up and t'other cuts through to where all t'lorries have to go through checking, on account of it being where two lots of paperwork meet, one country's rules ending and t'other's starting, and everything and everyone has to stop there whatever they're carrying or where they're headed. It's always been there, long as I've been doing this run, and it'll always be there while somebody higher up decides different.

There were a queue back near half a mile by time I got to it. Wagons and cars both, nose to tail, engines ticking over, nowt to do but sit and wait. Best part of half an hour before I got up to t'front of it, and I'll not pretend I sat there calm as owt, because I didn't. Tha gets that feeling in thi stomach, even when tha's done nowt wrong and carried nowt tha shouldn't, just from knowing somebody's about to go through everything tha's got and ask thee questions tha'd best answer straight. I were gripping t'wheel more than I needed to, I know that much.

When it come to me, I wound t'window down and handed over papers, passport and t'delivery note both, and t'officer took 'em without much of owt on his face either way.

The officer: Where you travelling from.

Me: Leeds. Come off t'ferry this morning, running down through.

That were easy enough, said it plain, no messing, because it were true and there were nowt to dress up about it. But then he asked me summat else, and this one I had to be more careful with, because it weren't a straight yes or no, it were about how long I'd be over and what I'd be doing while I were there, and tha has to get that reet because if it don't match what's on t'paper they've got in front of 'em, that's when questions turn into more questions. So I took a breath, thought it through proper before I opened my mouth, and told him same as it said on t'note, no more, no less.

Then he went quiet. Stood there thumbing through it, checking summat against summat else, and that bit of silence stretched out longer than it needed to, or felt like it did. I've never known a man read two bits of paper so slow. I just sat there, hands on t'wheel, trying to look like a fella with nowt on his mind.

And then he asked for t'insurance card, and for a second my head went blank, I couldn't place where I'd put it, and I had to lean right across and start going through t'door pocket and t'glovebox both while he stood there watching, and I found it stuffed down wi' some old receipts, and I felt a right fool handing it over creased up like that, but he took it same as owt else.

There were a lad two up from me in t'queue, driving for one of them firms out east, and they had him stood outside his cab a good while, going through everything in't back, unloading a fair bit of it onto pallets right there on t'tarmac. Nobody said owt about it, not him, not me, not anyone else stood waiting, we just watched out t'corner of our eyes and kept us mouths shut, because that's not summat you comment on, not there, not then. Tha just looks straight ahead and lets it be.

All that time I were trying to keep my face steady and my voice from doing owt daft if he spoke to me again. It's harder than it sounds, keeping thissen looking like there's nowt going on inside when there's plenty. I've heard fellas say they get used to it after enough runs, and maybe they do, but I never have, not properly, not the way I'd like to.

Then he handed t'insurance card back and said, not unkind about it, "There's no bother, lass, forms get creased, that's all they're for," which were a small enough thing but it eased summat in my chest I hadn't known were sat there so tight. Little bit of kindness off a man whose job it is to be suspicious of thee, that goes further than it should, tha knows.

He squared t'papers up, passed 'em back through t'window, gave t'side of t'door a tap wi' his hand and just said, "On you go then," and nodded me on past t'barrier.

I drove on same as normal, didn't want to look like I were in a rush about owt, but I'll tell thee, it were a good twenty minutes down that road, past t'services and round two bends, before owt in me let go. I pulled into a lay-by in t'end, just to sit a minute, and my hands were shaking on t'wheel worse than they had been back at t'checkpoint itself, which seems backward when tha thinks on it, but that's how it took me. Champion relief, once it come, but it come late and it come all at once.

I tell it like this, careful, not making more of it than there were, because there weren't owt happened to me, not really, not next to what some folk go through at places like that. I got asked some questions, I answered 'em, I got waved on. That's all it were. But I'll not pretend it were nowt either, because it weren't nowt while I were sat in it, and I'd be lying to thee if I told it any other road. So there it is, straight as I can give it thee, no bigger, no smaller. It'll be reet, telling it plain like that, same as it were reet in't end, standing there in that queue.
