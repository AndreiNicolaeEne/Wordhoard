Right so back before all this kicked off we was five of us crammed into a place built for three, easy. Me, the wife, the eldest, the middle one and the youngest, all in one poky little house, and then the wife's mum come to live with us an all after her fall, cause there weren't no way she was managing them stairs on her own no more. So that's six of us in a two up two down, bunk beds in one room, the wife's mum on a sofa bed in the front room every night, packing it away every morning like she's in the army. Couldn't carry on like that, could it. Something had to give, innit.

So we sat down, me and the wife, proper sat down with a pad and pen like we knew what we was doing, and worked out what we reckoned it'd take. Loft done out proper, two rooms up there for the kids, and a bit built out the back for a downstairs bathroom so the wife's mum ain't got to do them stairs for a wee in the night. Reckoned four of us on it, doing it round work and that, reckoned six months tops. Six months, bruv. Swear down we actually believed that when we said it.

First fortnight in, man's cousin, who'd done a bit of building before, he's up there marking out where the joists was gonna sit, and turns out he's took his measurement off the wrong wall, the chimney breast weren't where he thought it was on the old plan. So every single joist we'd already got in was out, the lot of it. Had to pull it all back down and start again from nothing.

Me: "You sure about this, cuz?"
The cousin: "Yeah yeah, trust me, trust me."

Turns out no, don't trust him, he was telling porkies to himself more than anyone. Two weeks of graft gone, just like that. Allow it though, weren't really his fault, the plans was old and the house had been extended once before, decades back, and nobody told us properly.

Then there was the Saturday man's mate's brother turns up with his mate from the ends, didn't even ask, just rocks up with a van full of tools and a look on his face like say less. Linked up right there on the pavement without a word said the night before.

Me: "Oi, what you doing here, g?"
Mate's brother: "Heard you was struggling, innit, couldn't leave you to it, fam."

Gassed don't even cover it, honestly. Man dem was up on that roof all weekend, the whole crew of us, and didn't want a penny for it either, just wanted feeding and a few cans after. Best Saturday of the whole job that was.

Then the weather turned right when the roof was open, no covering up there proper yet, and it rained for what felt like the whole world for the better part of a week. Nothing you could do, roof open to the sky, tarps ripping in the wind every night we put fresh ones on. Long, that week. Proper long. Sat in doing nothing, watching money go nowhere, watching the calendar go nowhere, everyone getting on each other's nerves back at the yard.

Round about month five we run out of blocks for the back wall, supplier let us down, said two weeks till the next delivery come. Felt like being mugged off, that did, paid up front an all. Couldn't wait two weeks with the wall half up and more rain forecast, so we made do, used what was on hand, mixed old reclaimed brick in with what blocks was left over, packed out the bit that wanted doing quick with timber we had spare in the shed. Weren't pretty but it held, and you'd never know it now under the render.

Bad moment come when the cousin's up on the scaffold doing the fascia and his foot goes through where a board weren't sat right, and he's down, proper down, off the platform and onto the pile of sand we had there for the mix, thank God for that sand. Everyone's heart in their mouth for them two seconds.

Me: "You alright, you alright?!"

He's just laying there a second, then he starts laughing, going "Yeah, yeah, I'm calm, I'm calm," but you could see his hands shaking after, and none of us was laughing with him properly for a good hour. Nothing broke, nothing worse than the fright of it, wallahi, but man aged about ten years standing there watching it happen in front of him.

Big argument come over the loft hatch and the stairs up to it, how it should be done. The cousin wanted it one way, straight up against the chimney breast to save space, and the wife's mum's geezer, who does a bit of carpentry on the side, wanted it turned the other way round for the headroom.

The cousin: "You put it that way you're gonna be ducking every time you come down, that's mad."
The geezer: "And you put it your way there's no room to swing a cat up there, what's even the point of it then."
The cousin: "You're being a whole wasteman about a hatch, it's not that deep."
The geezer: "Says the melt that had us pulling down two weeks of joists."

Went on like that a good hour, back and forth, taking the mick out each other more than actually deciding anything. Till the wife come up the ladder and just went "Do it the way that don't have me cracking my head open on it, simple as," and that settled it there and then. Should've asked her from the start, honestly, would've saved everyone the breath.

Worst of it come when the building inspector's round checking the back wall and he's tapping on it, frowning, and turns out the whole thing's been built out of true, leaning ever so slight, cause the footing weren't level to begin with. Had to knock the whole thing down, the whole wall, back to nothing, and start it again proper from the ground up.

Me: "You are actually having a laugh."
Inspector: "Better now than after it's plastered over, innit."

Fair enough, but still, that was a violation and a half, weeks of graft standing there in a pile of rubble where a wall used to be. Nobody said much that evening, just cleared it and went home.

The day the loft frame finally stood up though, all the roof timbers in, squared off, standing there in the actual shape of two proper rooms for the first time, that was a good day, that. Stood up there with the cousin and man's mate, just looking at it, not saying much, and man's mate just went "Say less," and that was it, that said everything that needed saying between us.

Last stretch of it, final four nights, man dem was up there past dark every single one, headtorches on, extension leads running off next door cause our own circuit weren't sorted yet, finishing the skirting, the paint, all the little bits that somehow take longer than the big stuff ever does. Bare tired, all of us, running on tea and whatever the wife left out on the side. But nobody wanted to be the one to say let's leave it another night, not after all that, so we just kept going till it was done.

And then it stood finished. Whole thing, top to bottom, done. Stood in the middle of it that first night with the wife and the wife's mum and the eldest, all of us just looking round it, and the wife's mum's going "Oh my days, look at this, will you look at what you done," and I'm not gonna lie I had a proper lump in my throat standing there, styll.

Took the tally after, sat down with the pad again like at the very start. Eleven months in the end, not six, don't laugh. Six different people had a hand in it one way or another over that time: the cousin, man's mate, man's mate's brother, man's mate's brother's mate, the wife's mum's geezer, and my own old man come down for the last fortnight of it and all, wouldn't take no for an answer once he heard we was close.

Stand in it now, of a morning, up in that loft with the light coming through the window, and honestly you'd never know the half of what it took to get here. All the two weeks lost at the start, the wall come down and built up again, the rain that wouldn't stop, the fright the cousin give us falling off that scaffold. Just stands there now like it was always meant to be there, calm as you like. That's the maddest bit of it, innit. All that graft, all them arguments over a hatch of all things, all them nights past dark, and now it's just the house. Just home. You get me.
