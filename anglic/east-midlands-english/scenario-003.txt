The father: Ey up, me duck. I've had a think. What about summat like that footballer everyone's on about, off the telly?

The mother: Gerroff. There's three of him already down the nursery. I'm not havin our lad growin up as "the other one."

The father: Fair enough. Worra about that name yer sister were on about, the fancy one?

The mother: That's proper mardy soundin, that. Sounds like summat off a soap opera. Folk'll say it wrong every time and all, and I int havin it.

The father: Right, well we gorra call him summat, dunt matter which way I go you're knockin it back.

The mother: What about after your dad, then? Proper solid name, that.

The father: Can't, can we. Yer brother's already got it, and yer cousin's lad an all. That's three on 'em round one table at Christmas, all answerin to same name. Gerroff.

The mother: I'm sick to the back teeth of it now, mesen. Been at this since dinner time.

The father: Well summat's gorra give. Can't leave him wi' nowt on the form.

The mother: Who's decidin anyroad? Is it you what says it every day at work, or me what carried him nine month and int got the energy left for arguin?

The father: Dunt work like that, does it. It's not one person's, it's ours.

The mother: Dunt feel like it, way you've tret every idea I've had.

The father: And you've knocked back mine an all, so we're evens, int we.

The mother: (sighing) I'm cream crackered wi' it now.

The father: Aye. We'll sort it t'morrow, when we've both slept.

The mother: He'll just have to go wi' nowt for one more night, then.
