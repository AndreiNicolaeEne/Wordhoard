Ey up. Sit yerselves down, I'll tell yer how it went wi' yer gran, plain as I got it, so yer've all got it same as me.

I'd only just got in from work, snap tin still in me bag, come down the jitty same as I allus do, and I sat wi' her right through, just me and her, no one else in the room.

First thing she said weren't nowt about dying, not at all. She just looks over at the window and says, int it black over Bill's mother's, like her mind were only on the weather and nowt else. I told her aye, happen it'll come on to rain before night, same as we'd allus said to one another over the years.

Then she come out wi' the other thing, clear as owt, no messing about. Said the back gate wanted seeing to, the latch were gone and she dint want it left banging all winter through, and I were to gerrit done. I said I would, said it plain so she'd know I'd heard her proper.

She called us "me duck" then, same as she'd called us since I were nowt but a babby, soft, just the once, and I knew.

Then she asks us summat I weren't expecting at all, straight out, asked was I happy. And I telled her true, I said aye, mostly, and gerrin by. She just nodded, like that were enough for her.

Last full thing she said were, "yer've been good to me, now look after yersen." I've kept that whole ever since, word for word, and I allus will.

After that weren't nowt more said. Just the quiet, and mesen holding her hand, hers going weaker in mine, till she went off like somebody drifting to sleep of an evening. That's the whole of it now, plain, so yer've got it same as I have.
