Workmate: Ey up, me duck, sit thi'sen down a minute cos I gorra tell thee what I heard off the woman on the till at the cob shop. She reckons the eldest got sacked off the site for gerrin into it wi' the foreman, right bust up it were, chairs flyin', police an' all, and now he's not allowed back on no site in the whole city, nowt.

Me: Stop there. That int what happened. Not even close.

Workmate: Eh?

Me: There worn't no bust up. No police, no chairs, nowt like that at all. What happened is he asked for his snap break early cos he'd had nowt to eat since six that morning, and the foreman said no, so he just downed tools and sat in the van till dinner. That's it. That's the whole of it. He wornt sacked either, he's back Monday same as ever, same shift an'all.

Workmate: Get away. That's dead different to what I heard.

Me: It's not dead different, duck, it's a different tale altogether by now. By the time it got to thee it'd grown legs.

Workmate: (sheepish) Where'd tha hear it proper then, cos I only got it off the till woman.

Me: Off him mesen, straight after, round the jitty back of the yard. So wherever she got it from, it worn't off anyone that were actually there.

Workmate: She'll have had it off her fella then, he's on that site an'all, works alongside the foreman's brother.

Me: There y'are then. Foreman's brother's gone and tret it summat rotten in the telling and made it worse every time round, and it's got mardier and mardier since, like these things do.

Workmate: I'll say nowt else about it, then.

Me: Tell it the true way from now on, whoever asks thee. He downed tools over his snap, that's the whole of it, and he's back Monday. Nowt more to it than that.
