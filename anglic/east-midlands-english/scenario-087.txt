The one at the counter: Ey up, me duck, y'alright? Y'look proper wore out.

Me: I am an' all, I've had no sleep, none. Dog were badly last night.

The one at the counter: Gerroff, what were up wi' it?

Me: Dunno rightly what set it off. It were looking black over Bill's mother's teatime and by the time I'd got the tea on the dog wunt touch its bowl, just lay there off its feed, not moving right, ears down, looking proper sorry for itself. I thought, I'm not leaving it on its own tonight, no chance, so I stopped down wi' it from the first hour, sat on the floor next to it. Nowt happened for ages though, just sat there stroking it and talking to it low, the way you would to owt that's frightened. Tried gerrin a drop of water into it an' all, it wunt drink off its own back so I had to coax it in wi' me fingers, bit by bit.

The one at the counter: Did it get worse then?

Me: Aye, about two or three it took a right bad turn, started shivering summat awful and I thought, I've dun wrong here keeping it home, should've took it straight to the vet's, mesen sat there near enough scraitin thinking I'd made a mess of it. But I kept it wrapped up warm and kept talking to it low, dint dare leave it on its own not for a minute. Then near enough dawn I clocked its breathing had gone steadier, and its ear twitched at summat outside the way it normally would, and I thought, there's summat there, that's a good sign that is.

The one at the counter: Thank god for that.

Me: By morning it were steadier, not right yet mind, but steadier, and it were only once I were sure of that that I went, proper dropped where I sat and slept like the dead. Mardy as owt when I come to an' all, but I don't care, it's on the mend.
