Never in a month of Sundays did I think I'd clap eyes on her again, and not there of all places, in the queue for cobs with her basket half full of veg, when I'd only nipped in for us snap before a shift.

Me: Ey up! Worra shock, is that you?

The old friend: Gerroff, it can't be... it is! Bloody hell, how long's it been?

Me: Gorra be over ten year now, easy. Last time I saw thee we were still at the big school.

The old friend: Don't, you're making us feel ancient. Ten year, that's mad, int it. What you been doing wi yersen then, all this time?

Me: Working over at the depot since I were twenty odd. Married now an' all, two little uns, mardy as owt the pair of em some days.

The old friend: Get away! I'm still at the hospital, on days now finally, me feet couldn't tek the nights no more. No kids for me, just the dog, and he's bother enough, I'll tell thee.

Me: Remember cutting through that jitty behind the school every dinnertime, freezing, and you'd never wear a coat, dint matter how nesh it were?

The old friend: Oh my days, don't! I were proper mardy if anyone said owt about it an' all. And remember you falling in the beck after conkers, come home looking like summat the cat dragged in, your mum went spare.

Me: I did, scraitin all the way home an' all. Tret like a right baby over it, weren't I.

The old friend: You were and all. Here, dost tha ever hear from that lad off our street?

Me: Nowt, not in years. Heard he'd gone off down south for work but couldn't tell thee where, he dunt keep in touch wi no one far as I know.

The old friend: Shame that, he were a good un. Right, we can't be losing touch again, gi's your number, I mean it this time.

Me: Go on then, and I'll ring, no messing about. Looks black over Bill's mother's out there anyway, best get off before it chucks it down.

The old friend: Ta ra then, me duck. Proper made up seeing thee.
