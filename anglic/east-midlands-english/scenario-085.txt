The first: Ey up, me duck, sit y'sen down, I've just wet the tea. Get a cob down yer while it's warm.

The second: Ey up. Ta. Ooh, don't rush me down onto that chair though, these knees o' mine dunt bend like they used to.

The first: Tell me about it, love. Mine's been bad twenty-odd year now, stiff as owt of a morning, I can't gerrup off that settee wi'out summat crackin' first.

The second: Twenty year's nowt to write home about. Try three, four nights a month I'm wide awake wi' this ache right through me hip, gone three in the morning some nights, scraitin' near enough wi' it, it's that bad. Dint sleep a wink Tuesday gone neither.

The third: You two dunt know you're born, and that's the truth of it. I've had me back since before I knew either on yer, and that's the best part of forty year now. Started when I were nobbut a youth meself, and nobody's ever tret it right, doctor nor no one. Black over Bill's mother's out there an' all, that'll set it off proper by dinnertime, you watch.

The first: Eh, don't be mardy about it, we've all got summat wrong wi' us.

The second: Aye, but who's got it worst though, eh? Go on then, who's the worst off, at this table, int it?

The third: Not me, and not you neither, I'll tell yer that for nowt.

The first: I'm nesh wi' it an' all now, come to think, can't be doing wi' this cold on top.

The second: Gerroff, you're not nesh, you're just mardy same as ever, you.

The third: We're all summat, me duck, every one on us, and none on us gerrin' any younger, so worra good's there arguing over it.

They all laugh at that, none on 'em giving an inch, and the tea goes stone cold on the table same as it does every time.
