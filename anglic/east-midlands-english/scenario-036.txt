Ey up, me duck, y'ay heard the full tale yet then? Right, park thi sen down, cos this is gunna take a bit, I'm tellin yer proper, start to finish, cos it were a day and a half and no mistake.

So it were my turn to have everyone this year, right, first time ever, and I dunno what come over me but I says yeah go on then, I'll have it at ours. Soon as it were out me mouth I were bricking it if I'm honest. Every other year it's been the mother's or the eldest's, and I'd just turn up and help wi veg and that were me lot. This time it were all on me. I couldn't sleep proper for a week before, just lyin there thinking about gravy.

I drew up this menu, right, dead ambitious, cos I'd been watching them cooking programmes and thought I could just do it all mesen. Not just the usual bird and taters, oh no, I had it in me head I were doing a starter an all, and a proper trifle from scratch, none o that shop one, and some fancy stuffing wi chestnuts in, and pigs in blankets AND a joint of beef cos the father-in-law dunt eat turkey. Looking back it were mad, I were never gunna get all that in one oven wi one pair of hands, but at the time I thought, nowt to it, how hard can it be.

Then the numbers start creeping up an all. First it were just us and the mother and the eldest and her lot, that's what I'd planned for, portions and everything sorted in me head. Then me sister rings up, can her workmate come, her family's gone away and she's got nowt on, well course I can't say no to that. Then the eldest says can the youth from down her road come an all cos his mum's working over the holidays. By the end I'd gone from six to eleven and I hadn't bought owt yet.

Shopping were its own disaster. Supermarket were rammed, trolleys everywhere, and I'm stood there wi this list as long as me arm. Got everything in the end bar one thing, and I swear I went round three different shops looking for it and couldn't find it nowhere. Chestnuts. Just chestnuts, that's all I wanted, for the stuffing I'd got so set on, and every shop had sold clean out. I were that mardy about it by the third shop, proper strop on me, stood in the aisle going on to mesen.

Me: There's gorra be some somewhere, there's just gorra be.

The one at the counter just looked at us like I were mental.

The one at the counter: Sorry love, we've had a run on em, everyone's after the same thing this time o year, int it.

In the end I just got tinned and hoped for the best.

Night before were carnage an all. I were up till gone one in the morning, prepping veg, making the stuffing wi me tinned chestnuts, wrapping bacon round sausages, and I'd wrote mesen a proper timetable on a bit of paper, hour by hour, what goes in when, what comes out when, all planned lovely. Felt dead pleased wi mesen going to bed. Should've known better.

Cos that timetable dint survive five minutes into the actual morning, did it. I'm up at half six, half asleep, and straight off the oven's playing up, taking donkey's to get to temperature, so already I'm behind before owt's even gone in. Then the bird's bigger than I thought so that's another half hour on top. I'm stood there wi me bit of paper just crossing things out and rewriting times, gerroff, I thought, this is a joke.

And then come the first proper disaster. I'd got the pigs in blankets in the top oven and got that involved wi the gravy and the veg I clean forgot about em, and next thing there's this smell and smoke's coming out the oven door and they're black, proper black, ruined outright, the lot of em stuck to the tray. I could've cried, straight up, I were that close to scraitin over a tray of sausages, daft as it sounds.

Me: Oh no, no no no, they're gone, they're proper gone.

The mother, who'd only just walked in the door: What's up wi yer, what's burning?

Me: Me pigs in blankets, I've only gone and forgot em, they're ruint.

The mother: Well have yer got any more bacon and sausages left?

Me: Half a pack, maybe.

The mother: Right, gerra move on then, I'll wrap while you get the pan hot, we'll do a quick batch, nobody'll know the difference.

So we scrambled a second lot together dead quick while I'm trying to keep an eye on six other things at once, sweat proper running off us.

That's when the guest turned up early an all, bless her, must've been an hour before I were ready for her, and she just took one look at the state of the kitchen and me face and didn't even take her coat off.

The guest: Right, I'm not stood here doing nowt, gi's a job.

Me: Honest yer don't have to.

The guest: I'm not stood here like a spare part watching yer have a breakdown, gi's them sprouts, I'll do em.

So she were straight in there peeling and stirring like she lived there, proper saved me bacon, if yer pardon the pun.

By that point the oven and the hob were both rammed, everything wanting the same space at the same time, taters wanting to go crispy in the top oven where the bird had just come out to rest, gravy wanting a hob ring where the sprouts were on, stuffing wanting warming through, and me just doing this mad dance round the kitchen trying to get it all timed so nowt went cold and nowt got forgot again. Kept looking out the window an all, sky'd gone that colour, black over Bill's mother's, proper storm coming, which somehow felt about right for the morning I were having.

One good thing though, the trifle. I'd been dead nervous about that trifle, thought it'd be a right faff and taste of nowt, but it come out beautiful, layers all sat lovely, and even I were surprised how good it were, tasted better than owt I'd made before, honest to god.

In the end we sat down to eat gunna near on two hour later than I'd planned, everyone starving, kids proper mardy wi hunger by then, but soon as plates were out and people took first bite the table went dead quiet which is always a good sign int it.

The eldest: Ooh that gravy's lovely that, what've yer done to it?

The father-in-law, forkful of stuffing halfway to his mouth: This is nowt like the shop stuff, this is decent this.

But then there were the pigs in blankets, second batch and all, and me sister's workmate, who I'd only just met mind, just come straight out wi it.

Her workmate: I'll be honest wi yer, these are a bit dry, love, summat's gone wrong wi these.

Everyone went a bit quiet at that but she weren't wrong, they had gone dry, rushing the second batch and all, so I just laughed and said aye, tell us summat I don't know.

But the trifle, oh me duck, the trifle went in about four minutes flat, empty bowl, everyone going back for more, the youth even scraped the dish, and that were the one everyone went on about after, saying I should do that every year regardless of owt else.

So by the end, everyone fed, dishes everywhere, I'm sat there proper knackered, and the mother turns to us and goes.

The mother: Well, you gunna do this again next year then, or what?

Me: Am I heck as like, never again, I'm done, put us down for just bringing veg from now on.

But even as I were saying it I knew I dint mean it, cos secretly, deep down, I were dead proud of mesen, disasters and all, and I already knew I'd be daft enough to do the whole thing again given half a chance.
