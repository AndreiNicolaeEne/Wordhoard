Right, I'll tell thee this proper cos it's a good un, honest, even though I were bricking it half the night.

So by the end on it me friend were gone, gone gone, not just tipsy. Slurring that bad you couldn't mek out one word in three, eyes gooin all over the shop, and grinning like a big daft kid at nowt at all. I knew summat were up before that an all, cos when we come out the pub and stood up off the stool, me friend's legs just went, like they'd forgot what legs were for. Grabbed the bar rail and everything, and I'm stood there thinking, ey up, this int right.

"You alright?" I says.

"I'm fine," me friend goes, dead determined about it an all, like saying it hard enough meks it true. "Never bin better in me life, honest to God."

And then went to walk off and near went straight into the fruit machine.

Getting them to actually leave were a whole negotiation in itsen. Proper mardy about it, folded arms and everything, like a kid told it's bedtime.

"I dunt wanna gu yet," they kept saying. "One more, just one more, gerroff, I'm fine, I said I'm fine din't I."

"You've had your one more about four times already, duck," I says. "Come on, let's be having thi, I'll walk thi back."

Took a good ten minutes of that, me practically dragging one arm through their coat sleeve while they tried to argue the toss with a fella at the next table who they'd never met in their life. But I got 'em up in the end, arm round their waist and their arm slung ovver me shoulders, and out we went into the cold, which sobers nob'dy up as quick as folk think it does.

Walked 'em home the long way, cos it were the only way I knew with me eyes half shut an all by then. Had me arm round 'em the whole road, near enough carrying half their weight, and every few steps they'd list off to one side like a boat in a bit of wind and I'd have to haul 'em straight again.

"I need to sit down," they said, about halfway up the road, dead sudden, and just went down on this low wall outside somebody's front garden. So we sat. Then they were sick, right there, quite politely as sick goes, turned their head an all so as not to get it on us, which I did appreciate at the time.

While we were sat there a bloke walking his dog come past, and he give us this look, proper look, up and down the pair of us, me holding me friend's collar back and them retching away, and he just went, "night, this," dead flat, not even breaking stride, dog trotting on past like it'd seen it a hundred times, which round here it prob'ly had.

Got 'em up again after a bit, and that's when we went wrong, cos I took us down the wrong jitty, one that dunt even come out where I thought it did, just a twitchel between two gardens that goes nowhere useful at that time of night. Had to double back, me friend going "where we gooin, where we gooin, this int the way," which, credit to 'em, half drunk as they were, they were right an all.

Got sorted eventually, back on the proper road, and I says, right, you need some water in thi.

"I dunt want no water," they says, pushing me hand away when I got a bottle out. "Don't want it, gerroff."

"You're avin it," I says, "I'm not carrying thi home just for thi to be dyin of it in the morning an all."

Took a good five minutes of that, me holding the bottle to their mouth like they were about two year old, before they give in and had a few gulps, dribbling half of it down their chin and coat, but some on it went in at least.

And then, out of nowhere, dead soft all of a sudden, they went and said the maudlin thing. Grabbed me arm, stopped dead in the street, looked at me proper serious.

"You're me best friend in the world, you know that," they said, "I dunt tell thi enough, I love thi, honest I do, you're a good un, a proper good un," voice going all wobbly, nearly scraitin over it an all, over nowt, over me just walking 'em home like anybody would.

"I know, duck, I know," I says, "come on, nearly there now."

Getting through their own front door were its own battle an all. Key wouldn't gu in straight, me friend trying to help and just meking it worse, hand ovver mine, key scraping round the lock summat awful, both on us laughing at it by then even though it were half past three in the morning and freezing. Got it in the end, fell in the hallway together near enough, and I got 'em through to the front room and down onto the settee, coat still on and everything, cushion under their head, blanket over the top.

"Never again," they said, dead solemn, flat on their back looking at the ceiling. "That's it. Never drinking like that again as long as I live."

"Course you're not, duck," I says, tucking the blanket in a bit more.

"I mean it this time."

"I know you do," I says, and I did believe they meant it right then, just not that it'd last past the hangover.

Left a pint of water and a bucket by the settee just in case, and let mesen out, and went and got mesen home an all, absolutely knackered.

Rung 'em the next day, well past dinner, and they picked up sounding like death warmed ovver.

"How you feeling," I says.

"Rough," they went. "Dunt remember much past the pub, if I'm honest."

So I filled it in for 'em, bit by bit. The legs going. The state getting 'em out the door. The wall, being sick, the dog walker. The wrong jitty. The water fight. And when I got to the maudlin bit, telling 'em what they'd said, they went dead quiet a second.

"Did I really say that?"

"Word for word, duck."

"Oh God," they said, and you could hear 'em cringing right down the phone. "And the never drinking again bit?"

"An all," I says. "Meant it an all, at the time."

"I'll hold thi to that this time," they said, half laughing, half groaning.

"You always say that," I says.

And we both knew it for what it were.
