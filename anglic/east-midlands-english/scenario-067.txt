Ey up, come on then, I'll walk thee round, no need to say nowt, just tek it all in as we go.

See where the pavement goes wide outside the end house? Nobody parks there, not never, even when it's empty as owt of a Sunday afternoon. That's for the ambulance, in and out for the old feller two doors up, and there's no sign up nor nothing, tha just knows. Thy own car goes outside thy own door, or down the jitty round back if tha's got the room, and that's that.

Now that low wall wi' the privet gone all leggy, that's where next door's ground stops and ours starts. Nobody crosses it, not the kids on their bikes, not the postman even. Nowt marks it but everybody knows to the inch, always has.

Half nine of a night the whole street gerrs quiet, telly down low, no mower, no owt. Nobody's ever told thee that neither, tha just learns it or the curtains twitch.

When tha's passing folk, elders get the nod first, then whoever's got a babby wi' 'em, mebbe one scraitin in the buggy, then the rest of us, and it's just "ey up" or "y'awight duck", nowt fancy.

Borrowing's easy round here, a cob of sugar, a ladder, whatever tha's short of, tha just knocks and asks, mesen or yersen, dunt matter. But if summat dunt come back tret proper, tha'll not get lent nowt again off that one, and nobody says why to thy face, they just gerra funny look on and mek an excuse next time tha asks.

And the one thing everybody does and nobody says out loud? Bins out the night before instead of the morning, every last one of us, sooner or later. Nobody's ever mardy about it though, not really.

That's your lot, duck, come on in out the cold, it's proper nesh out here, and it's looking black over Bill's mother's an' all.
