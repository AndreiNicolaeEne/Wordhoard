Ey up, me duck, sit yersen down a minute, I'll tell thee the whole of it, cos it's been a right saga this one and I don't reckon I've told it proper start to finish to anybody yet.

So it were that wild night back end of the year, wind howling round the house summat rotten, banging about like it were trying to gerrin, and I says to my other half, that's bad that, that's black over Bill's mother's and no mistake, and sure enough come morning we go out the back and the fence between us and next door in't there no more. Just gone. Flat. And laid right across the top of it, roots up out the ground like a great hand had pulled it, is this tree, and I'm stood there in me dressing gown thinking, well that's a right mess that is.

First thing was working out whose tree it even were, cos it grew right along the line between the two gardens, half its roots one side and half the other by the look of it, and you could've argued it belonged to either of us if you'd wanted to be daft about it. But when you looked close it were planted a good foot or more into their side, always had been, everyone round here remembered it going in, so there weren't really no argument to be had about whose tree it were. It were theirs. Question were what to do about a fence that weren't neither of ours no more, just kindling.

So I go round, knock on, and the neighbour comes to the door and I says, ey up, seen the state of the fence, and straight off it's like talking to a brick wall.

The neighbour: Well it's the wind that's done that, in't it. Nowt to do wi' me, that. Act of God that is.

Me: Aye, the wind's blown it over right enough, but it's your tree that's took the fence down, and that tree's been leaning that way donkey's years, everybody's said it, it dint just start leaning last night.

The neighbour: It's a tree, they lean. Dunt mean owt.

And that were more or less how it went, back and forth, neither of us gerrin anywhere. Then me other half, who'd been earwigging from the kitchen window, comes out and says, oh go on then, tell 'em about the warning, and I remembered, aye, we did an' all, cos it were the old boy two doors up, lovely fella, allus out in his garden, and he'd said to me at the bus stop must be two summers back, that tree of theirs wants looking at, that does, it's leaning summat chronic, somebody wants telling before it comes down on somebody. And I had mentioned it, casual like, over the fence at the time, and it'd gone in one ear and out the other by the sound of it, cos nowt were ever done about it.

So next time I says that to the neighbour's face, and oh, that dint go down well.

The neighbour: I dunt remember nobody saying nowt about that.

Me: Well it were said. Ask him yersen, he'll tell thee same as me.

And that's when it turned proper stiff between us. Cos once you've said a thing like that, it's not just wind and bad luck no more, is it, it's whose fault, and nobody wants to be the one what pays for summat when they reckon it in't their doing, and I dint want to be paying for half a fence that got flattened cos they never sorted their tree out. So we went round and round it for the best part of a fortnight, both of us mardy as owt every time we passed each other in the street, barely a nod, let alone a word. I'd say we ought to split it down the middle, seeing as it's a shared fence after all's said and done, and they'd say why should they pay owt when it's the wind's fault and act of God don't come with a bill attached. And I'd say well your tree wants an act of God to fell it an' all then, seeing as it's been wanting felling for two year. Round and round, neither of us gerrin off our position an inch, both too proud, if I'm honest, to be first to back down, cos once you've dug in it's hard to climb back out without looking like you've lost.

Anyroad, it were the old boy what sorted it in the end, the same one what give the warning in the first place, and nobody asked him to get involved, he just did, cos that's how he is. Come round one evening, knocked on both doors same night I heard after, and got the pair of us out the front, and he says, now then, you two, this in't doing nobody any good, standing here like a couple of mardy kids, and I'll tell thee what we'll do.

The older neighbour: Tree's yours, that's true enough, and it were leaning, that's true an' all, so by rights it's your job. But the wind were a genuine wind, worst we've had in years, and there's trees down all over, so it in't like you were negligent exactly, more unlucky. So how's about you split the materials down the middle, and whoever's got more time or more back in 'em does more of the graft, and nobody has to say sorry to nobody, you just gerron with it.

And do you know, that were exactly the shape of it needed, cos it let the neighbour say they weren't admitting fault, just being neighbourly, and it let me say I weren't backing down over the warning, just being sensible about splitting a job. Nobody had to lose face over it, and that's all either of us really wanted in the end, if I'm honest with mesen.

So we split the cost of the new posts and the panels down the middle, and then come the Saturday we're both out there in the cold clearing the old tree, and by heck that were a stiff couple of hours, I'll tell thee. Barely a word between us past what were needed, him sawing his end, me dragging brash to the side, working like two strangers what happened to be in the same garden. Proper awkward it were.

But then round about dinner time his wife comes out with a plate of cobs, ham and cheese, still warm, made up like snap for the pair of us, and hands 'em over without being asked, just says, get that down thee, you'll be starving, and that were it really, that broke it. Cos after that he hands me the good saw when mine went blunt, dint even ask if I wanted it, just passed it over, and I got the kettle on for the both of us after, and by the time we'd got the new posts in the ground we were laughing about summat daft, I don't even remember what now, and it were like nowt had ever been wrong between us.

Took us the best part of two weekends all told, between the both of us, but the fence what's up now is twice as sturdy as the old one ever were, proper concrete in the posts this time, none of your rotten old wood job we had before. And things between the two houses, well, I'd say it's better than it were even before all this kicked off, cos we've been through summat together now, in't we, and there's a nod and a natter over that fence most days, and he keeps half an eye on our bin day same as I keep half an eye on his post when they're off. So it worked out, in the end, though I'll not lie, there were a fair few weeks there I could've cheerfully seen that tree land on summat else altogether.
