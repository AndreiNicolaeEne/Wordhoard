Me: Ey up, sit down a minute, me duck, I'll tell yer how it went.

My friend: Go on, tell her about the state of him first.

Me: Aye. One of the lads from our year, I dint even clock him at first, honest. Great big beard on him now, gone grey round the sides, I had to ask who he were. Worra state, I thought. He were skinny as owt back then, remember, all elbows, and now he's twice the size of that, filled right out. I said, "I'd never have known you off the street," and he just laughed, said he gets that all the time now.

My friend: And then there were her, dint change one bit. Same laugh, same way of stood with her arms folded, exact same as she always were. You'd have thought no time had passed at all.

Me: No time at all. Int it weird how some of 'em stay exactly as they were and others you gorra look twice at.

My friend: Tell her who wasn't there an' all.

Me: Oh aye, the one who used to sit at the back, he dint come, summat about his mother being poorly, had to stop home and look after her.

My friend: Shame, that. But did you hear about the other one, works down south now, in a bank or summat, earning a fortune they reckon. Nobody saw that coming, he were mardy as owt at school, never did a stroke of work in his life.

Me: Nowt like you'd expect, is it.

My friend: Mind you, look at us two. We've not changed much, have we.

Me: Gerroff, I've changed loads.

My friend: Yer have not. Yer still say ey up to everyone and mek us laugh same as ever, so we can't have changed that much between us.

Me: Well, mebbe a bit then, but not so much as some of 'em. We should sort another one, sooner this time, before another twenty year gets past us.

My friend: Aye, go on then. Next year, no messing about, and I'm not scraitin at the end of this one neither.
