It'd bin black over Bill's mother's since dinner, me dad said, so we knew summat were coming, but nobody thought it'd tek the leccy an all. Just gone dark outside and the wind got up a bit, and then click, off went everything, telly and all, right in the middle of it.

Me mam were straight up off the settee. "Ey up, dunt panic, I'll find summat." Right scramble then, everyone banging about the front room looking for owt that'd give a bit o' light. Dad had a torch out the shed but the batteries were dead as owt, dint even flicker. Nan found one candle stub in the drawer by the telly, and that were it, one for the whole lot of us, no more to be had anywhere in the house.

"Right," she says, "we'll have to share it then, mesen and yersens an all," and passed it round like it were snap going down the line, everyone getting a warm off it for a minute before it went on to the next.

The youth kept walking into the sideboard, same corner, three times he went into it. "Gerroff," he says to it like it'd moved on him itself, rubbing his shin, proper mardy about it by the third go. Me dad said he'd sort the fuse box in the morning, weren't fixing nowt tonight in the pitch black with one candle and a torch that dunt work.

So we just sat, the lot of us round that one bit o' light, close together on the settee, nesh as I am I had the blanket an all wrapped round me, and we just talked, nowt much, just whatever come to mind, telling each other to shurrup and laughing at nowt really. The eldest were scraitin with laughing at summat daft the youth said, not proper crying, just that carry-on sort.

Leccy never did come back that night, int it always the way. We sat till the candle burnt right down to nothing and then it were bed for the lot of us, feeling our way up them stairs in the dark, still laughing a bit going up.
