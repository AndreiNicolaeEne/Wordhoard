The aunt: Ey up, me duck. I've been hearing all sorts about that one from off the Nottingham Road, you know the one I mean.

The niece: Which one's that then.

The aunt: The one our neighbour's lad works with, down the depot. She says he's ever so steady, worra grafter, never a day off in his life, first one in of a morning with his snap packed and all. And a temperament on him like nowt I've ever heard, calm as owt, never raises his voice at nobody. And the family, well, you know the family, decent as they come, never a bit of bother out of any of 'em.

The niece: Mm.

The aunt: Mm what. Go on then, what's wrong with him. What could be wrong with a lad like that.

The niece: Nothing's wrong with him, I just... I int ready is all. I've gorra sort mesen out first, get settled at work proper, I dunt have the time to be thinking on it right now.

The aunt: Ha. That's what your cousin said an' all, dead against it she was, said she wanted nowt to do with it, dint want to know. Look at her now, married three year and glad of it, glad her mother kept on, she says so hersen every time I see her.

The niece: I'm glad for her. I am. But I'm not her, am I.

The aunt: No, you're not. Don't go all mardy on me now, I'm only saying what I'm saying.

The niece: I know. I do know.

The aunt: Well. I'll say nowt more about it for now. But he's not going nowhere, and neither am I, so when you come to yersen you know where I am, me duck.
