Right, so. Everyone else round here learned this when they were nowt but bairns, five or six year old, wobbling about holding onto their mam's coat. I dint do any of that. And that were more or less how it went for months after. Slow, and humiliating a fair bit of the time, if I'm honest. There were allus a few stood watching round the barrier, some of 'em right encouraging, giving us a nod when I stayed up, and some clearly having a right laugh at us and not hiding it much either, and you had to learn not to mind either sort too much.

What got us through, if I'm honest, were stopping comparing mesen to the youth flying past and starting comparing mesen to mesen a month back. Because against him I were nowt, always would be, he'd had a different start in life on the ice than I had. Then, about the fourth month in, summat changed. What I were proud of instead were the things I could do without even thinking on it by the finish, that I couldn't do a stroke of when I started, not one. Standing up off a fall without grabbing the barrier. Turning without me arms going everywhere like a windmill. And looking back on the whole daft season of it now, duck, it sits right fond in me memory, not sour at all, which I'd never have guessed at the time, lying flat on that ice with the breath knocked out us thinking I were done. So there it is, the whole tale, and now you've heard it proper, you can stop asking us to tell it every time we're stood having us tea.
