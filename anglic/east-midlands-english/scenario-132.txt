Eleven day that cold held for, straight through wi'out a let up, and I don't reckon none of us have known owt to touch it. Day before it come in the mother stood at back door and said it looked black over Bill's mother's, and sure enough by morning it were a different world, colder than anybody had said it'd be, no easing into it at all, just straight down like a switch flicked. Proper brass monkeys, worra start to it.

Mother: Come in, me duck, yer'll catch yer death stood there.

We gorra keep the stove going round the clock from that first day, banked it right up last thing and straight to it again first thing, never let it go out once in eleven day, because you daren't risk it, int it. Stood out back of a morning gerrin the coal in and it fair tret you rotten, that wind, cut right through owt you had on. The youngest were proper nesh with it, wouldn't gerrout from under the blanket for owt or nowt.

Pipe out the back went solid on the third night, first time that's ever happened here, and I stood there with the kettle trying to gerrit going again and nowt doing, frozen solid right through, lagging an' all, dint make a bit of difference.

The eldest: Ey up, that's not right that. I've seen some winters in my time but I've never known a pipe go like that.

Me: Worse than that one back when, is it.

The eldest: Aye, love, this has it beat and no mistake. That one were bad but it dunt hang about eleven day like this has.

Worst night were the eighth, cold enough it hurt to draw breath crossing the yard, and by morning two of the hens had had it, huddled in't corner and gone stiff. Proper upset the mother that did, scraitin over it she were, said she felt mardy about it for days after, couldn't help hersen.

Then eleven day in it broke, just like that. First drip off the guttering were the best sound going. Stood in the yard letting it thaw round us and nob'dy said much, just stood there glad of it, hands out like idiots, warm at last.
