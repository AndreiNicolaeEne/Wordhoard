Black over Bill's mother's since dinner, and the pair of 'em out on the yard trying to get the footings finished before it come down. The older hand hadn't even had his snap yet, trowel going smooth and even, buttering the brick, tapping it down with the heel of his hand, checking his level and moving on to the next.

The older hand: Now then, watch this, me duck. You dunt load the trowel up like that and dollop it on, you drag it, one long pull, so it goes on flat and even. See?

He laid one clean, tapped it level, straightened up.

The older hand: Your go.

The new hand had a go. Mortar everywhere, brick sat up proud on one corner.

The older hand: Nay, nay. Look what you've done, you've gone and piled it all on one end, that's why it's sat wonky. Drag it flat first, then lay the brick, then tap it level.

The new hand: What's the level even for, if you're just tapping it by eye anyroad?

The older hand: That's what tells you it IS level, that's the whole point of the thing. Load it, drag it flat, lay it, tap it. Same as I just showed you, exact same.

Second go, same mess again, brick rocking on a heap down one side.

The older hand: I've just told you that, int it! You're not dragging it, you're dropping it, there's a difference. This int rocket science, it's a trowel and a bit of mortar, nowt else to it.

The new hand: I'm trying, arright, I've never done owt like this before.

The older hand: I know, I know. Dint mean to snap at yer, and gerroff looking so mardy about it. 'Ere, think of it like buttering toast, thin, right to the edges, not a great gob in the middle. Try it like that.

Third go. Thin, level, true first tap.

The older hand: There y'are, duck. Now you've gorrit.
