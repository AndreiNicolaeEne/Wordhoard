Ey up, love. Gerroff, don't be lookin at us like that, I'm awright, just budge up and let us tell thee how it were, cause nob'dy tells thee proper and I wish somebody had've.

Them first days, honest, I gorra nowt off it. Sounds daft but it's true. There were that much gooin on, people in and out the house all hours, the arrangements, someone always wantin summat signed or fetched or asked, that you dint have two minutes together to feel owt directly. You'd catch yersen mekkin tea for the fourth time that mornin for folk you'd never normally see from one year to the next, and it were like the grief were happenin to somebody else, behind a wall somewhere, and you were just gerrin on wi the jobs. I weren't scraitin, not properly, not then. I were too busy bein useful for anyone to notice I wunt.

It's after, when they've all finally gone home, that's when it gets thee. I remember the night the house cleared out at last, door shut, and it were the quiet that dint half land on us. No phone gooin, no one askin if you wanted owt, just... nowt. That were the first time it were real, sittin in that quiet on me own wi no one left to be strong in front of. I've never known a silence like it, before or since.

Yer body does daft things an all. Couldn't eat for days at a stretch, just dint want it, everythin tasted of nowt whatever it were. And sleep, forget proper sleep. I'd drop off for an hour, wake up starin at the ceilin at three, doze off again at five for twenty minutes, up again at six feelin like I'd not shut me eyes at all. Gone proper nesh an all in them weeks, cold to the bone even wi the heatin on, couldn't warm through whatever I put on.

First month were a strange one an all, cause the world just kept on gooin, dint it, shops open same as ever, folk moanin about the bus bein late, and you're thinkin, dunt any on em know? How's it still Tuesday out there like nowt's happened. Walked down the jitty to the shop same as always and that were half the trouble, everythin just carryin on same as any other day, and you're miles behind and they've no idea they've left thee.

Then there were a day - nothin special about it beforehand, no date circled on owt, just an ordinary Wednesday - and it undid us completely, out the blue. I couldn't even tell thee after what set it off. Just stood there in the kitchen bawlin like a kid over nowt I could put a name to.

And it dunt stop there neither. Months on, you'd catch a smell off somebody's coat in the street, or see summat as daft as a certain mug at the back of a cupboard, and bang, you're back in it, full wave, no warnin, no pattern to when it'll come. Daft, int it, how a smell can do that. Still happens now and then if I'm honest wi thee.

Middle of it were the worst in its own way, cause nowt were sharp any more, it were like it were black over Bill's mother's every single day wi no sign of it liftin, dulled the good days same as the bad. Went to things I should've enjoyed and just felt like I were watchin mesen from the corner of the room the whole time.

There were this youth at work said summat to us in that stretch, meant well I spose, come out wi "at least it were quick" like that were meant to help, and I could've gone right mardy at him. But what actually got us through more than any of the sorted-out official stuff, the forms and the vicar and everyone's careful words, were somebody just turnin up one Tuesday wi a cob and a bit of cheese and sayin nowt much at all, just sittin wi us a while. Small thing. Meant more than owt planned ever did.

Anniversary of it, I dreaded that for weeks before, proper dreaded it, couldn't think past it however hard I tried. And when it come, I gorra through it, clumsy as owt, dint do nowt clever wi it, just gorra through the day, and that were enough. That were the whole point of it, love, just gerrin through, no medal gooin for doin it right.

Appetite come back slow, bit by bit, some weeks worse than others, one good week then a rotten one straight after wi no rhyme to it. Sleep an all, in fits and starts, never all in one go. You'd think you'd cracked it then it'd be off again.

I remember the day I caught mesen laughin, proper laughin, at nowt much, summat daft on the telly, and straight after felt sick wi mesen over it, like I'd no right to. That guilt come right in behind the laugh every time, uninvited, for a good while after.

By the end of the year I were carryin it different, lighter in bits of us, but it dunt go, love, not really, it's still there, just not sat on me chest the same way it were.

It dunt stay this hard forever. I can tell thee that straight, and I mean it. But there's no set time on it, nob'dy can tell thee when it eases off, so dunt go measurin yer year against nob'dy else's, mine included an all. Yours'll gan how it gans, and however it does, that's yours.
