Me mate: Ey up, y'alright? You look like summat's chewed you up and spat you out.

Me: Ey up. I'm tellin thee summat happened to mesen last night, comin back off lates, and I dint imagine one bit of it.

Me mate: Gerroff, you was half asleep, wannit, walkin home in the dark after a full shift does funny things to your head.

Me: I weren't half asleep, I were wide awake the whole time, thinkin about nowt but me snap box rattlin in me bag and gerrin home for me tea.

Me mate: Go on then, what "happened".

Me: I come down the jitty by the allotments and there's this shape stood dead still at the end of it, big as a man but low down on all fours, and its eyes was catchin the light like a cat's, only there weren't no light down there to catch, none at all, no lamp, nowt.

Me mate: That'll be a fox, me duck, foxes eyes do that, catch what little light there is even when you cor see where it's comin from.

Me: A fox dunt stand up on its back legs and look thee dead in the eye for the best part of a minute, dunt it. This were taller than any fox, taller than me an' all, easy.

Me mate: Might've bin someone's dog got loose then, big collie or summat, folk round here let 'em roam.

Me: There's only one dog down that end, owd feller's from the end house, black and white, and it barks its head off soon as it sees anyone comin. This never made a sound, not one, just stood there watchin me, and then it worn't there, gone, and I never heard it go nowhere. Tell me that's owt normal.

Me mate: Worra tale, I'm tellin thee, get yersen off to bed early tonight.

Me: I swear on me nan's grave every word of that's true. I'm not mardy and I'm not makin it up, I know what I saw with me own eyes.

Me mate: Aye well, you can swear on what you like, I'm still sayin it were shadows and a long shift talkin, nowt more.
