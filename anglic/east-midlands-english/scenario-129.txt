Ey up. I'll tell thee how it went wi' that fire, cos I'm still a bit shook thinkin' on it, honest.

Were early afternoon when I first clocked it, just a grey smudge ont' horizon, miles off by the looks. I were havin' me snap outside at the time, and I thought nowt much of it, just stood there watchin', same as you would. Said to mesen, that's nowt to do wi' us, that, it'll burn out over yon fields and that'll be that.

Then wind got up. Within the hour it had pushed that smoke right across and me eyes were stingin' summat shockin', streamin' like when it's black over Bill's mother's and the air goes thick afore the rain comes.

The neighbour: Ey up, duck, worra you still doin' out here, gerrin yer coat on, we're off, now, this in't waitin' about for nob'dy.

Youth from up the road were already runnin' door to door, shoutin' the same.

I dint need tellin' twice after that. Gorra get out, I thought, and I were out that place in twenty minute, only grabbed what mattered and left rest of it where it were. Me throat went raw sharpish, proper rasping, and heat were pressin' on me skin even that far back, fierce it were, like standin' too near an oven.

I looked back once, just as I got clear, and whole sky behind were lit up orange, glowin' summat rotten. Dint stop again after that, just kept goin'.

Two nights I were away, stoppin' wi' me sister, not knowin' owt, mardy and sick wi' worry, couldn't settle for it. Then word come we could gerr back, and when I got there the place had been tret alright, spared complete, not a mark on it. I just stood there near enough scraitin', duck, I tell thee, relief like you wun't believe.
