Me: Ey up, go on then, you gorra give us it proper this time, none o' this bit-o'-this bit-o'-that. Write it down while I've got a pen on me.

The aunt: Eh, I dunt know if I can, love, I've never had it wrote down in me life, it's all up here. Right. Well. You start wi' the flour, that's your base, that's about a pound, mebbe a bit under, our nan never weighed owt proper, she just went by the bag.

Me: A pound of flour, right.

The aunt: And the sugar goes in wi' it, and, ooh, I nearly forgot, you want a good pinch o' mixed spice in there an' all, don't leave that out, I nearly did meself first time I made it and it come out proper flat tastin', nowt to it.

Me: So flour, sugar, spice.

The aunt: Flour, sugar, spice, then you rub your fat in wi' your fingers, cold fat mind, then the fruit goes in after, then you loosen it with a drop o' milk, just enough, you'll see when it's right, and then it's in the oven. Low oven, and here's your bit, nan always said dunt rush it, whatever you do. She'd have your guts if she caught you turning the gas up to get done quicker, it'll sink in the middle and go all clarty, so you just leave it be, low and slow, hours if it wants hours.

Me: How do you know when it's done, though, if you're not watching a clock?

The aunt: You dunt watch no clock, duck, you watch it. When it's stopped singin' to itself, that hissin' sound, and a knife comes out clean, that's your done. And the spice, if I'm honest wi' you, I've never measured that in me life, it's just a pinch, whatever looks right, you judge it by eye.

Me: Could you use margarine 'stead o' the fat, then?

The aunt: You could, aye, it'll do at a push, but it dunt taste the same, not really, so I'd say only if you've gorra.
