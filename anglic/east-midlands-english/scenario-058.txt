The tenant: Ey up, have you got five minutes, me duck? It's this soddin roof of mine again, I'm at me wits' end wi it.

The neighbour: Ey up, gerrin out the wet, don't stand there scraitin on me step. What's up wi it now then?

The tenant: It's dropping right through in the back bedroom, over that corner by the chimney breast where our youth sleeps. Ceiling's gone all bowed and brown like a cob left too long in the oven, and I'm up half the night emptying the bucket under it. Been going on months now, since back end of summer.

The neighbour: And you've said summat to him about it?

The tenant: Twice. First time I rung, he says he'll gerra bloke round to look at it. Dint hear nowt for weeks, so I rung again, and he comes out wi the very same, "I'll gerra bloke round," and still nowt's happened. Gets mardy wi me an all if I press him, like I'm making a fuss over owt.

The neighbour: So that's two promises and dunt one of em kept. How long since that last one?

The tenant: The best part of two month now.

The neighbour: Right. Well you've got a few roads you can go down, none of em champion. You write to him proper, keep a copy, so you've summat in black and white if it comes to it. Or you ring environmental health and let them tell him straight, only he might try and gerroff of you come the end of the tenancy for it. Or you get someone in yersen and take it off the rent, but that one wants doing dead careful, he could come back on you.

My old landlord tret me the very same over a kitchen ceiling, years back. Called it nowt to fuss over till the plaster were down on the worktop. I got onto the council in the end and it were fixed inside a fortnight.

The tenant: I'll write to him first, then, get the dates down proper. Give him a week, and if there's nowt back I'm straight onto environmental health mesen.

The neighbour: That's your best bet, love. Go on, before that lot comes through and brains one of the kids.
