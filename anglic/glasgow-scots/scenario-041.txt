Right, I'll tell ye the whole thing, cause everybody's heard the wee version but naebody's heard whit actually went on in ma heid.

I was there two year. Two year almost tae the day, gied it aw I had and mair, so I did. And it wisnae one big thing that done it in the end, it never is, is it. It's the wee things, the drip drip drip, until one day some tottie wee thing happens and ye think, right, that's me, I'm done.

The final straw was daft, honestly, dead daft when ye say it oot loud. Somebody'd used the last of the milk in the wee kitchen and never replaced it, and I stood there wi ma tea bag gaun cold in the mug, and something in us just went. Cause it wisnae the milk. So that night I went hame and sat masel doon at the table and actually practised whit I was gonnae say. Said it tae the mirror n aw, God's honest truth, like some eejit. Me: It's no about any one thing in particular. I've just been thinking it's time for a change, that's aw.

That was the polite version. The real version I never said, and here's whit I held back frae her, tae yersel now cause I'll tell you straight: I held it back cause I didnae want tae be the one that started a stooshie, and I didnae want her thinking I was bitter, even though I was, right doon tae the bone. She looked surprised right enough, I'll gie her that, her face went.

Me: Naw, honestly, ma mind's made up.

The manager: Well, whit if we looked at your pay, we could maybe stretch tae a wee rise. Or if it's the hours, we could shift you tae mornings instead.

And it was tempting, I'll no lie, for aboot two seconds it was tempting.

Me: Naw, it's no aboot the money. It's just time, I think I need a fresh start.

Which was true enough, in its way, but it wisnae THE reason, no even close. The reason was I was scunnered, pure scunnered, and nae amount of extra money was gonnae fix somebody else no daein their share while I ran masel intae the grund coverin for them.

We agreed two weeks notice, the standard, and she said that's grand, we'll miss ye, and I said aye, likewise. But there was one moment, right at the end, when my workmate walked us oot tae the door, the yin that'd covered for the other one as much as me ower the years, the yin that kent fine well whit I wisnae sayin in that office. She just looked at us.

My workmate: Ye did the right thing, ye know.

Me: Aye. Well. We'll see.

There's days I still think aboot the folk I left ahind, wonder how they're gettin on, whether the somebody's still gettin away wi it, whether the manager ever pieced thegither whit I really meant that day. But I'll no lie tae you, I'm no sure I'd hae the nerve even now. Some things ye only ever say tae yersel, on the walk hame, in the dark, wi the rain comin doon dreich as anythin, and that's whit two year taught us, if nothing else: the polite words and the true words are hardly ever the same words, and maist of us gan through wur whole lives sayin the wan and meanin the other.
