Right, so you missed it, and I'm no gonnae lie tae you, it was some day. I'm still findin roast tatties doon the back ae the couch.

So it started wi me sayin, dead early on, "right, I'll dae it this time, I'll host." Naebody asked us tae, mind, I must have been feelin pure gallus that day, cause I just went and said it, before I'd even thought whit that meant. And soon as the words were oot my mooth I was cackin it. Actual dread. I've never hosted the big one in my life, I've always just turned up wi a bottle and a poke ae messages and let somebody else dae the worryin.

The mother: "You sure aboot this, hen? You've never even done a roast on your own."

Me: "Aye, how hard can it be."

Famous last words, eh.

So I sat doon wi an auld notebook, dead serious, and I drew up this menu that was pure ambition and nae sense whatsoever. No just a turkey, oh naw, I had it in my heid I was daein a turkey AND a wee ham AND homemade stuffing frae scratch, no the packet, and three kinds ae tatties because apparently one kind isnae enough, and a trifle, and a cranberry thing I'd never even made before in my puff. Lookin back at that list the night before I nearly greeted mysel, never mind the weans.

And the numbers just kept creepin up an aw. Started aff as "just us, mibbe six." Then my sister goes "aw can my auntie come, she's on her own the day," and then it's "well if she's comin the neighbour cannae be sittin in her lone next door either," and by the time I stopped countin it was gone fifteen, and I'm stood there like, right, whit poke am I even gonnae fit aw this messages in.

Speakin ae which. The shop. I'm no exaggeratin, hen, I went tae three different shops for a big enough turkey and by the third yin they were lookin at us like I was a bampot, askin the same question every time. I must have looked pure glaikit, standin there in the queue wi the same question aff by heart. But the ONE thing, the one single thing I could not get anywhere in this whole city, was fresh cranberries. No the jelly stuff, actual fresh yins, for the sauce I'd promised masel I was makin frae scratch. Every shop, sold oot or never had them tae begin wi. It was pure dreich ootside as well, rain siling doon the whole time, and by the third shop I was that crabbit wi masel, and that drookit fae runnin aboot between them, I looked like I'd fell in the Clyde.

The night before was a write-aff. I was up till God knows, prepping, choppin, wrapping things in tinfoil, the whole kitchen looked like a stooshie had gone right through it.

The mother, on the phone: "You'll be grand, hen, get your rest noo, big day the morra."

I never got tae bed till gone one, mind, cause I'd wrote oot a timetable an aw, on an actual bit ae paper, whit goes in the oven when, doon tae the minute. Dead proud ae masel, timed aw tae the second like I was runnin a wee restaurant.

That bit ae paper survived aboot twenty minutes intae the actual mornin before it went straight in the bin.

Because the day itself, when it came, was pure chaos frae the off. I was up at some ungodly hour, half asleep, and I'd forgot tae even take the turkey oot the fridge in time so the whole timetable was buggered before I'd even started. Then the stuffin I was that proud ae, I got distracted takin a phone call and next thing I know there's this smell fae the kitchen and I run through and the top ae it is black, pure black, ruined, deid, done. I nearly greeted. Actual tears in my eyes ower a bit ae stuffin, whit a state.

Me: "It's ruined, it's actually ruined, I cannae fix this."

The mother: "Whit dae you mean ruined, how ruined."

So I scraped the black bit aff the top, mixed the rest wi a wee bit ae stock tae save it, said a wee prayer tae naebody in particular, and stuck it back in and hoped for the best.

Right in the middle ae aw that, the door goes, and it's a guest arrived a good hour early, dead apologetic, standin there wi a bottle in their hand and me wi flour up tae my elbows and my face half done greetin ower stuffin. And rather than sit them doon wi a cup ae tea like a normal host, I just handed them a peeler and a bag ae tatties and said, "right, you're helpin noo, hen, naebody's sittin idle in this hoose the day."

The guest: "Whit dae you want me daein wi these."

Me: "Peel them, quick, we're behind."

By this point the oven was a warzone. Turkey needin its space, the ham needin in, tatties needin roastin, and me tryin tae juggle it aw on one wee cooker that wisnae built for a dinner this size. So obviously, wi aw that stooshie, we sat doon tae eat a good hour and a hauf later than I'd planned. But the second folk actually got a fork intae it, the table went dead quiet, which if you know this lot, is the highest compliment gettin paid.

Then the verdicts start comin in, one after another.

The eldest: "That stuffin's got a wee bit ae a burnt edge tae it, but naebody's gonnae say nothin."

Dead blunt, nae messin aboot, but that's this lot for you, they'll tell you straight tae your face and then go back for seconds anyway.

By the end ae it aw, stuffed tae the gunwales, everybody sittin aboot dead peely-wally and half asleep on the couch, somebody goes, "so, same time next year then," and I just laughed and said naw, never again, this is a one-time thing, I'm no daein this tae masel again.

Me: "Never again, I'm tellin you, my nerves are ruined."
