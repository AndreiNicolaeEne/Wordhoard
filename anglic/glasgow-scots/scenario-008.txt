The wean: Mammy, can ah no get the phone. Ah'm the only wan in ma whole class that's no got wan.

Mother: Naw, hen. No the day, no the morra either.

The wean: How no but.

Mother: Cause there's no the money for it, that's how no. Ah'm no gonnae staun here and dress it up for ye. The messages this week took near every last penny that wis in the poke, never mind a phone on tap of it.

The wean: Ah'll dae extra chores for it. Ah'll dae the dishes every night, even at the weekend, even when it's your turn.

Mother: It's no aboot chores, son. Ye could dae the dishes every night till ye were auld and grey and it still widnae stretch that far. It's no fifty pence we're talkin.

The wean: Whit aboot for ma birthday then, could ah get it for that.

Mother: If yer da's back in work by then, aye, mibbe, we'll see whit's spare. Ah'm no promisin ye a thing, mind, it's if, no when. Dinnae go tellin folk ye're gettin it.

The wean: But he's got wan already. His mammy got him wan and she's no even workin.

Mother: Ah dinnae care whit his mammy's daein wi her money, hen, that's their hoose and their business. Whit some other wean's got doesnae pit anythin on this table. Every hoose is different, and ours is skint the noo, that's a' there is tae it.

The wean: It's no fair but.

Mother: Away and stop yer greetin, ye'll gie yersel a heidache ower nothin. Tell ye whit, though. Ah'll get ye they wee trainers ye were on aboot, frae the shop doon the road. No the phone. But no nothin either.

The wean: ...The white wans?

Mother: Aye, the white wans. That's it the noo, no mair askin, ah dinnae want tae hear anither word aboot it the night.

The wean: ...Awright. Suppose that'll dae. For the noo.
