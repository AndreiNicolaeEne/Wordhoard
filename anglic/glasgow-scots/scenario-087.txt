Workmate: Here, you look wrecked. Whit happened tae ye?

Me: Aw, dinnae ask. The dug went aff its feed last night, wisnae moving right either, just lying there wi its heid doon like it couldnae be bothered wi the world. I wisnae leaving it in the kitchen its lone all night, no way, so I brought its wee bed through and sat wi it.

Workmate: Whit was wrong wi it but?

Me: Naw idea, hen, that's the thing. For hours there was nothing, just sitting there watching it breathe, trying tae get it tae take a wee drap of water aff ma hand cause it widnae touch its bowl. Then aboot the middle of the night it took an awfy turn, shivering and that peely-wally round the gums, and I'm sitting there thinking have I done the wrong thing keeping it hame, should I have hauled it tae the vet hours ago, pure feart I'd left it too late. I just kept it warm and kept talking tae it low, the way ye'd talk tae a wean that's greeting, on and on aboot nothing, just so it heard a voice.

Workmate: God, that's a stooshie and a half.

Me: Aye. It felt like the longest night going, dreich doesnae cover it. Near dawn though I catched its ear gie a wee twitch at a noise oot the windae, first thing it done right aw night, and its breathing had changed, no as laboured. By the morning it was steadier, still no right, still a bit glaikit looking, but steadier, and it was only once I was sure of that I let maself feel how done in I was. Sat doon on the flair and near fell asleep there and then, like a right eejit.

Workmate: Ye did good but.

Me: Aye, well. Couldnae have done anything else, could I.
