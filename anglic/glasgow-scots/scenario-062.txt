See, years ago, before you were even a thought roond here, us and them next door were as close as two hooses can get without living in the wan. In and oot ae each other's kitchens morning tae night. My man and her man went tae the fitba thegither every second Saturday, rain or shine, and I'm telling you it was aye raining. The weans grew up thegither, ower the fence, under it, through the gap where the wood had rotted, disnae matter, they were aye through it, greeting when they got skint knees and laughing again five minutes later. If I ran oot ae milk I chapped her door, if she ran oot ae sugar she chapped mine. Messages in a poke handed ower the fence, a wee bag ae something for the weans, nothing in it, just neighbours. That went on years. Good years.

Then they got the shed put up.

Now, I'm no against a shed, everybody needs somewhere for the lawnmower and that, but the fella they got in tae build it, whether he couldnae measure or just couldnae be bothered, put the thing right ower the line. No an inch or two, mind, a good six inches intae oor bit, and the guttering ae it hung ower further than that again, so every time it rained, and God knows it's aye raining here, the watter fae their roof came straight doon intae oor borders and drookit everything I'd planted that year.

So I went ower, nice as you like. Never raised my voice, never even mentioned the line, just asked, could they maybe get the guttering turned, or the shed shifted back a wee bit, no a big job, save us baith the bother later. She said aye, nae bother, she'd get her man ontae it. Weeks went by. Nothing. I let it go, thought, fair enough, folk get busy. Asked again, softer than the first time even, and again, aye aye, he'll dae it the morra. The morra never came. Third time I asked there was an edge on it, I'll no lie tae you, because by then it had been the best part ae a year and my borders were ruined every time it came on tae rain, and I says tae her, listen, this has been gaun on too long noo, when is he actually gonnae dae it.

Word came back tae me through the wife two doors doon, the one that's aye ferrying news up and doon this street whether you want it or no, and by the time it reached me it had grown itself a whole new shape. What I'd said polite the first two times had become me shouting the odds at her ower the fence, apparently, demanding it moved that day or else. I never said any ae that. But that's whit came back, and once a thing's said it's said, you cannae unsay it even when it's no true.

When I finally caught her tae ask whit that was aw aboot, she looked at me straight-faced and telt me she'd never once ignored me, that she'd never even heard me ask the first two times, that I must be mixing her up wi somebody else. Flat denial. No a flicker. And I knew fine well she was lying because I'd stood at that fence three separate times.

So I did something petty, and I'll own it, I'm no proud ae it. Her man used tae park his van half up on oor bit of the verge, always had, never bothered me before, but that week I got oot early and put oor bins right where he'd normally pull in. Said nothing. I tried tae mend it, mind you, I'm no somebody that holds a grudge for nothing. By that point the street had went and split itself doon the middle, near enough. Auld Mrs across the road, aye on their side, God knows why. The couple at the corner, on oors. Naebody said it oot loud but you knew fine who was speaking tae who and who wisnae. That's the last real word either ae us has said tae the other. Eleven years noo, near enough twelve. And if I'm honest wi you, hen, all these years on, I couldnae tell you for certain any mair who actually started it.
