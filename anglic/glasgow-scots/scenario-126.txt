My pal: Whit's up wi you the day, you're lookin awfy peely-wally.

Me: Ah've hardly slept a wink, hen. Somethin happened last night and ah cannae get it oot ma heid.

My pal: Aye, right. You were probably half-asleep in front the telly, dreamin away tae yersel and thinkin it wis real.

Me: Naw, ah wisnae. Ah wis wide awake, sittin up daein the crossword, thinkin as clear as ah'm thinkin the noo.

My pal: So whit happened then, come on.

Me: The clock on the wa jist stopped. Deid stop, hands and aw, right where they were. And the same second, ah mean the very same second, ma granny's photie on the mantelpiece fell flat on its face. No a breath ae wind in that room, the windae wis shut the whole night.

My pal: That's easy enough explained. The clock's battery wis deid, that's aw that wis, and the photie probably got chapped by somebody runnin past, or a lorry went by ootside and shoogled the hale hoose.

Me: There wis nae lorry. It wis that quiet ye could hear yer ain hert gaun. And there wis nae wean up either, they'd been in their bed since aboot nine, ah checked masel before ah sat doon.

My pal: Mibbe the frame wis just auld, gave way on its ain, wid does that in the heat.

Me: The fire wisnae even lit, hen, it's July! There wis nothin tae make it faw, nothin at aw, and it didnae just topple ower, it went face doon, like somebody laid it doon deliberate wi their ain hand.

My pal: Ah'm no buyin any ae that, ah'll tell ye that the noo, ye sound like a right bampot.

Me: Ah swear on ma weans' heids, every single word ah've telt ye is true. Ah wis wide awake and it happened jist like ah said, so it did.

My pal: Naw. Ye were half-asleep and that's the end ae it. Ah'll see ye the morra, wi a clearer heid, mibbe.
