Right, so, the plot. I'll gie youse the whole year of it, fae the first seed tae the last spud oot the groond, cause it wis a year and a hauf, so it wis.

Started aff back in February sitting at the table wi a cup ae tea and the seed catalogue, tryin tae figure oot whit we were even gonnae dae wi the thing this time. Every year ye tell yersel ye'll be sensible, plant whit ye can actually eat, and every year ye end up wi three types ae squash and naewhere near enough tatties. So I sat doon wi the auld notebook and worked it oot proper, tatties doon the far end, cause they take the maist room, a row ae leeks, the beans up the cane wigwam like always, some lettuce and cabbage for the summer, and I let masel one daft thing, which wis the courgettes, cause the weans like watchin them grow big as their airm near enough overnight.

Didnae get onythin in the groond till the back end ae March, mind, cause it wis a dreich start tae the year, that cauld that hung on and hung on, and ye're no wantin tae put young stuff oot when there's still a frost gonnae come and nip the heids aff everythin. So I waited it oot, few weeks after the last cauld snap, and once it turned we got a right good run ae weather, sun near every day for near a month, and honestly it wis magic, everythin came up that fast ye'd hardly credit it. The beans were up the canes afore ye knew it, the lettuce went in rows like a wee army, and I mind standin there ae a Sunday thinkin, aye, this is gonnae be a good yin.

Well. First thing that turned up wis slugs on the lettuce, and I mean turned up like they'd been invited. Wan mornin it wis a full row, next mornin it wis lace, honest tae god, holes right through every leaf. I tried the beer traps first, wee dishes ae auld beer dug intae the groond, and it worked a bit, caught a fair few ae the eejits, drooned in it, but there wis that many mair comin fae somewhere I couldnae keep up. Tried the eggshells roond aboot the plants an aw, crushed up, supposed tae pit them aff crawlin ower it, and again, helped, didnae solve it. Lost near a third ae that first sowin afore I got wise and started gaun oot wi a torch efter dark tae pick them aff by haund, which is a right glaikit-lookin thing tae be daein at ten at night in yer good jaicket, but it worked better than either ae the ither two thegither.

Then the dry spell hit, and my god, that wis the year's real fight. Weeks and weeks wi hardly a drap ae rain, everythin startin tae go peely-wally and droopin by the efternoon even though it looked fine in the mornin. Had tae haul watter up by haund every single day, cannae remember the last time my back wis that sore, cans back and forth fae the tap at the tap ae the plots doon tae ma bit, day in day oot, weeks ae it. And that's when the stooshie started wi the neighbourin plot.

See, there's only the wan tap serves a good six or seven plots, and it's aye been first come first served, naebody ever needed tae make a rule ae it cause there wis usually enough rain ye didnae need the watter that bad. But in a dry spell like that, every plot-holder's doon there mornin and night wi their cans, and the fella next tae me, nice enough usually, mind, started fillin up that early and that often it wis like he'd claimed the tap for his ain. I'd go doon at seven and he'd already been and gone and be back again by half seven.

Me: Here, you were doon at that tap three times afore I even got a look in.

Neighbouring plot-holder: Aye well ma toms are dyin, whit dae ye want fae me.

Me: So are ma beans, pal, we're aw in the same boat here, ye cannae just be hoggin it.

It got a wee bit heated, I'll no lie, few sherp words back and forth ower a couple ae days, him sayin I wis exaggeratin, me sayin he wis bein a bampot aboot it. In the end it wis the wumman fae the plot on the ither side sorted it, fair play tae her, said we should just dae mornins and evenins turn aboot, odd days him, even days me, and naebody fillin mair than two cans at a time when it's that dry. Worked a treat efter that, and him and me were fine aboot it inside a week, nae grudges held. He even gied me a bag ae his early spuds later on by way ae sayin sorry, so.

Noo, the tatties, I have tae say, were the star ae the whole plot this year. Clear win, nae argument. Dug the first row up in July and they were that good, big and clean and no a scab on them, and by the time I lifted the lot in the autumn I had mair than the hoose could get through, gied bags ae them away tae near everybody I knew. That's whit a good year looks like, that is.

The courgettes though, they were a different story. Got hit wi some kind ae rot doon at the base ae a few plants, right when they were just startin tae flower, and I lost near hauf the crop tae it afore I even clocked whit wis happenin, by the time ye see the leaves gaun yellay it's usually too late. Cut ma losses, pulled the worst affected yins oot afore it spread tae the rest, and got a decent wee crop aff whit wis left, but nowhere near whit I'd hoped fur startin oot.

Then there wis the storm. Came in ae a Tuesday nicht, wind that fierce it flattened three rows ae beans right doon tae the groond, canes and aw, and the leeks were lyin ower like somebody'd walked through them. Went doon the next mornin and could've greetin, honest, the state ae it. But ye just get on wi it, spent the best part ae a Saturday staundin the canes back up, tyin the beans back on wi bits ae auld string, packin the soil back roond the leeks that had come half oot the groond. Lost a fair few plants that just widnae come back up right, but the maist ae it pulled through, which surprised me.

So come the end ae it, when I finally tallied up whit come aff that plot ower the whole year, it wisnae bad at aw, tatties by the bagful, a good run ae beans once they got back on their feet, the lettuce that survived the slugs, leeks, and a smaller but still decent haul ae courgettes. Traded a big bag ae the spare tatties tae anither grower doon the row for a load ae his rhubarb, cause naebody in oor hoose can grow rhubarb tae save their life and the weans are daft for a crumble.

Put the plot tae bed aboot the back end ae October, dug it aw ower, cleared the auld canes away, covered ower the bare bits. And ma verdict, lookin back on the whole ae it, slugs, drooth, stooshie an aw, it wis a guid year. No an easy yin, but a guid yin. Ye take the rot wi the tatties, the storm wi the sunshine, that's just whit it is daein this. Already thinkin whit I'm plantin the morra, so tae speak, come February again.
