Right, yous asked me tae tell it, so I'll tell it plain, the way it wis, no dressed up.

She wis peely-wally that mornin, lyin there wi her heid sunk intae the pillow, but she wisnae crabbit wi it, no even a bit, and I wis sittin aside her, haudin her auld hand. First thing she says, and it wisnae aboot dying at aw, she goes, "Is it still dreich oot the day?" Just that, easy as onythin, easin intae the room afore onythin else got said.

Then she says, clear as ye like, mind and sort they messages oot the poke in the press, gie the wee tin tae the eldest, she didnae want it left lyin. Said it plain enough there wis nae mistakin whit she meant.

Then she just goes "hen," the wan mair time, same as she'd aye said it since I wis a wean masel.

Then she asked me somethin I wisnae expectin at aw. She goes, "Did I dae awright by yous?" And I telt her the honest truth, naw messin aboot, I says, "Aye, ye did. Ye did awright, mair than awright, ye did."

No long efter, she says, "Look efter each other, and dinnae let the weans greet ower much for me," and that wis the last full sentence oot her, and I've kept it whole in ma heid ever since, word for word, gonnae keep it that way an aw.

Efter that there wis just the quiet. I had her hand in mines the hale time, haudin on, till she drifted away frae us, peaceful, nae stooshie, nae fright in her at aw.

So that's it, that's whit happened, plain as I can gie it tae yous, so yous huv it the same as I dae.
