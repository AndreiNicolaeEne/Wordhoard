The owner: Awright hen, whit's the story - roll and slice, brown sauce, wee tea, milk nae sugar, aye?

The customer: Aye, gie us that, but naw, swap the roll the day, ah'll take a poke o chips insteid, ma stomach's no right this mornin.

The owner: Whit's up wi ye noo, ye're lookin a bit peely-wally so ye ur.

The customer: Didnae sleep a wink. The wean wis greetin hauf the night wi her teeth, crabbit as onythin, does ma heid right in.

The owner: Aw, the poor wee soul. Is she any better the day?

The customer: Aye, away tae her granny's for a wee bit peace. She's a right wee bampot when she's like that though, greetin the hoose doon.

The owner: Ye'll get yer sleep back. How's the work gaun wi ye?

The customer: Cannae complain, or ah could, but naebody'd listen tae me onyway. Whit aboot yersel, how's the stall daein?

The owner: Dreich as onythin aw week, drookit every day, naebody wants tae stand in the rain for their messages. Ah've been staunin here like an eejit some days wi nae customers ata.

The customer: Well, ye've me, three times a week rain or shine, so dinnae be moanin at me.

The owner: Aye, ye're a stoater so ye ur, no like some o they glaikit yins that never come back. Right, here's yer chips, hen, salt and vinegar's on already, and yer tea. That's three-fifty.

The customer: There ye go. Cheers, hen.

The owner: Nae bother. See ye Thursday then, same time, or ye gonnae let me doon again?

The customer: Naw, ah'll be here, if the wean disnae finish me aff furst. See ye then.

The owner: Away ye go, ya bampot, ye'll survive.
