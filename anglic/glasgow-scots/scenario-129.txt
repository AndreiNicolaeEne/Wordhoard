Right, so it wis the day started aff like any other, dreich enough but naethin tae worry aboot. Early efternoon ah'm oot the back hingin the washin and ah see this smoke away oot on the horizon, jist a wee grey smudge up ower the hill. Ye'd hardly gie it a second look, tae be honest, jist thought some fermer wis burnin somethin he shouldnae. So ah stood there a meenit, the wumman next door wis oot as well, baith ae us jist watchin it like it wis nothin tae dae wi us.

Within the hour but the wind had turned and that smoke wisnae distant nae mair, it wis right ower us, and ma eyes started stingin somethin rotten. That wis the moment it went fae watchin tae ah'm no stayin here. Ye ken that second when it clicks in yer heid, right, this isnae a spectacle, this is comin for ye.

Ah wis oot that hoose in twenty minutes flat, naw messin aboot, jist grabbed whit mattered, the weans' photies, the auld dug, ma good boots, and that wis it, the rest could burn for aw ah cared in that moment. Ma throat wis raw fae the smoke, felt like ah'd swallowed sandpaper, and the heat wis pressin on ma skin even fae that distance, like staunin ower close tae an oven.

Ah looked back the once, and the light of it against the sky wis somethin ah'll no forget, aw orange and roarin like the hale hill wis alive. Then ah wis gone, doon the road as fast as ma legs would take us.

Two nights away, naw knowin whit wis left, the weans greetin every night wi the worry, me no sleepin a wink either, jist lyin there in some stranger's spare room starin at the ceilin thinkin aboot the hoose and every wee thing in it.

The mother: Any word yet, hen?

Me: Naw, naethin the night. We'll ken the morra, they're sayin.

The morra came and we got the word at last, the place had been spared, no even singed. Ah near grat wi the relief, honest tae god, stood there like a right eejit greetin in the street.
