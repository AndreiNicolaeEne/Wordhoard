The eldest: Right, wee one, listen up afore the rest come doon, so youse aw ken whit's whit. It's tig the day, and ye need four at the least tae make it worth yer while, five's better.

The younger one: Whit's the boundary but?

The eldest: See the wall at the tap there, that's one end, and the big bin ower by the gate's the other. Naethin past the lamppost either, that's oot the game a'together, so dinnae even think aboot it.

The younger one: Who's on it first?

The eldest: We dip for it. Aw hauns in, and whoever's haun's oot last when the rhyme's done, that's you, yer It. Nae arguin efter, that's the rule, awright?

The younger one: Whit coonts as a tag?

The eldest: A proper touch, wi yer haun, on the body. No a shove, and no grabbin their jaicket tae haud them back, that's just bein a bampot aboot it. If ye cannae reach them wi yer airm oot straight, it didnae coont, hen.

The younger one: And if ye run ower the boundary while yer bein chased?

The eldest: Then yer caught onyway, same as if they'd tigged ye. Ye cannae just skip ower the wall tae get away, that's cheatin and everybody'll say so.

The younger one: Whit if ye get tigged?

The eldest: Ye freeze. Stuck deid still where ye are, and ye cannae move a muscle till somebody else that's still runnin comes ower and taps ye loose. Then aff ye go again, nae bother.

The younger one: How dae we ken when it's done?

The eldest: When everybody's frozen at the one time, or we're aw too wabbit tae run nae mair. Then the last yin caught is on next, gonnae be them chasin.

The younger one: Whit if I get it aw wrong?

The eldest: First go doesnae coont against ye, so dinnae fret yersel. Just run, hen, and dinnae be glaikit aboot the wall.
