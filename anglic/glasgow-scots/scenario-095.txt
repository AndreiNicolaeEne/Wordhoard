The sister: Right, that wee clock's mine but, so it is. She telt me that hersel, sat right there in that chair, she says you're to have the clock aff the mantelpiece when I go.

The brother: She never said any such thing to you. When did she say that.

The sister: When you were oot the room getting her messages sortit intae a poke, that's when. Am I supposed tae have made it up?

The brother: Aye, funnily enough, aye. Cause she never mentioned it tae me and I was in and oot that hoose every day God sent.

The sister: Naw you werenae. Don't you dare stand there and say that tae me.

The brother: Whit, so I'm lying noo as well as no carin.

The sister: I'm no saying that. Forget the clock. What's happening wi the money then, cause I know fine well there's mair should be left than whit's sittin in that account the day.

The brother: Here we go.

The sister: I'm just asking.

The brother: You're no asking, you're saying I've taken it, that's whit you're daein.

The sister: Well have you?

The brother: Naw. I have not touched a penny of her money and you can check every statement, every last one, the morra if ye like.

The sister lowers her voice right doon then, and it comes oot smaller than either of them expected.

The sister: I just cannae believe she's away and we're stood here fightin ower a clock and a bank book. That's no even it. It's that you were here. You were here and I wisnae, and I cannae get that back aff naebody, no even her.

The brother goes quiet an aw, and when he speaks it's the same wee voice back.

The brother: Aye. I know, hen. I know.

The aunt: Right, that's enough noo, the pair of youse. She'd be mortified hearin this, so she would, a right stooshie ower nothin. Sit doon, the two of yese, just sit doon a minute and get yer breath.

The sister: Sorry.

The brother: Aye. Sorry.

Naebody says onything for a good wee while efter that. Neither of them looks at the other, baith hearing it back in their ain heid, how it sounded, the two of them squabblin ower a wee clock and a deid woman's money.
