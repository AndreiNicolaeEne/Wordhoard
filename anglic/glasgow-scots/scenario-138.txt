The workmate: See her laddie, the one up the depot. Ah heard he goat the sack Friday. Some stooshie ootside the Bull, decked a boy stone cold, the polis had tae come and everything, away in the van and aw.

The aunt: Naw. Stop right there, hen, that's no whit happened, no even close.

The workmate: Whit dae ye mean, that's whit ah heard.

The aunt: Ah ken whit ye heard, but it's aw wrang. He never decked naebody. There wis a stooshie right enough, two eejits gaun at each other ootside, and he goes in tae pull them apart, no tae start onything. Goat an elbow in the coupon for his trouble, that's aw that happened tae him. The polis came cause somebody phoned it in, took statements aff the two that were fightin, never even looked the road ma laddie wis staunin. And he wisnae sacked either. He's in the day, went in wi a black eye, peely-wally lookin fae the fright of it, but he's fine, and he's still goat his joab.

The workmate: So he never hit a soul.

The aunt: No a soul. That's the bit that's completely arse-aboot-face, hen. Fae "broke up a fight" tae "goat the sack for battering somebody" - that's some distance for a story tae travel in two days.

The workmate: Aye, when ye say it like that. Where did ah even hear it fae... it wis the wee wan on the tills, said her man heard it aff somebody that drinks in the Bull.

The aunt: There ye go then. Some bampot at the bar seen a boy wi a black eye getting bundled intae a van and pit two and two thegither and made five. That's how it goes, every time it's telt it gets a wee bit mair gallus than the last.

The workmate: Right. Ah'm sorry, hen, ah shouldnae have jist come oot wi it withoot askin ye first.

The aunt: It's no you ah'm crabbit at, love. Just - fae the morra on, if onybody asks ye, ye tell it the way ah've telt ye the noo. He broke up a fight, goat an elbow for it, and he's still workin. That's the hale story, nae mair, nae less.
