The mother: Right, the lot ae youse, intae the livin room. Whae broke that mirror aff the wa?

The eldest: No me. Never touched it.

The mother: Naebody's askin whae it wisnae, ah'm askin whae it wis.

The middle one: It wisnae me either. Ah think it wis the wind, honest, the back windae wis open a wee crack and there wis a right gust came through efter denner, must've knocked it aff the hook.

The youngest: Aye, or the cat. She's been actin the bampot aw week, flingin hersel aboot the place like she's glaikit.

The mother: The mirror's on the wa in the hall, hen, there's nae windae anywhere near it. And that cat's been drookit and sulkin under the bed since this mornin, she's no been near the hall.

The eldest: Well it wisnae me, ah wis in ma room the hale efternoon.

The middle one: Naw ye werenae, ah seen ye comin oot the hall wi a face oan ye, right efter we heard the crack.

The eldest: So did you, ye were staunin right there an aw.

The middle one: Ah wis nowhere near it, ah telt ye, ah wis at the windae.

The mother: Haud on a wee minute. Two seconds ago it wis the wind at the windae, noo you're staunin at it yersel. Which is it?

The middle one: ...Ah wis just walkin past, that's aw ah meant.

The mother: Aye, right. Weans greetin, pointin fingers at each other and the cat, and no wan ae youse can gie me a straight answer. Crabbit as ah am the day, ah cannae be daein wi this stooshie every single time somethin gets broke in this hoose.

The youngest: That's no fair, it wisnae me!

The mother: Naebody's ownin up, so naebody's gettin their messages oot the poke the night, nane ae youse. When wan ae youse has the guts tae say it wis them, mibbe next time it'll no be aw three in the huff thegither.
