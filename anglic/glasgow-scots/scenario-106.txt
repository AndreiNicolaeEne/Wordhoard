I don't tell this one much, but I'll tell it the wan time, and I'll tell it straight, no bigger nor smaller than it wis. I'll no go through it again efter the day, so listen close.

We'd been on the road since afore first light, the coach grindin up through the hills, and everybody kent, withoot onybody sayin it oot loud, that we were comin up on the checkpoint. It sits there because that's whaur the border runs, has done since afore ony ay us wis born, and wi aw that's been gaun on the last while they'd doubled the manpower on it, so it wisnae jist a wave-through the wey it used tae be years back. Ye came roond the last bend and there it wis, the wee auld huts and the barrier doon and a line ay motors and coaches stretchin back further than ye'd like tae see.

It wis a dreich day and aw, the rain no heavy but never lettin up either, and once the coach stopped ye had tae get oot and staund in it wi yer bag, so by the time we were near the front half ay us were fair drookit. There wis weans on the bus in front greetin wi the wait and the wet, and I heard the driver ahent me gettin awfy crabbit wi somebody on his phone aboot how long it wis takin. We sat, or staunded, in that line a good half hour afore we even got near the front ay it. I'll no lie tae ye, I wis nervous the hale time, ma papers oot in ma haund, gaun ower them again and again like they were gonnae change if I looked at them enough times. Ma hert wis gaun like the clappers and I kept tellin masel there wis nae reason fur it, I'd nothin tae hide, but that disnae stop the nerves, does it. It's no a rational thing at aw.

When it wis finally oor turn, ye went up wan at a time tae the wee windae wi yer papers oot. The wan askin the questions wis a woman, mibbe forty-odd, tired-lookin aboot the eyes, and she took ma papers aff me withoot a word first, had a wee look at the photo, then at me, then back at the photo again.

The one at the desk: Purpose ae yer visit?

Me: Jist visitin ma sister. She's oot here workin.

That wan wis easy enough, said it plain, nae bother tae it. But then she asked the next wan and I had tae think aboot it mair than I'd like tae admit.

The one at the desk: How long ye plannin on stayin?

Noo I'd a return date on ma ticket right enough, but I'd tellt ma sister I might stey on a wee bit langer if things were awright at her end, and I didnae want tae gie a date that didnae match up wi somethin else in the papers, so I took a second, mibbe two, tae get it right in ma heid afore I opened ma mooth.

Me: Twa weeks. Back on the fifteenth.

She never said onything tae that, jist looked doon at the papers again, and it went that deid quiet while she went through every page dead slow, me staundin there tryin tae look like a person wi nothin on their mind. You could hear yer ain breathin in a moment like that, honest tae God.

Then she asked us for the wee yellow slip that goes wi the visa, and I near went peely-wally on the spot because I couldnae mind for the life ay me where I'd put it. I'd tae go through every pocket, every fold ay ma jaicket, ma sister's man's poke ay pieces he'd made up for the road gettin shoved aside in the panic, and there it wis the whole time, foldit up smaller than a stamp, stuck doon the side ay ma passport. Felt like a right bampot haundin it ower, but she never made onything ay it, jist took it and stamped it in wi the rest, nae look, nae comment.

There wis a young fella ahead ay me in the line, aboot ma ain age, gallus-lookin when he first got up there, cocky wi it even, but they took him tae the side, tae a wee room aff tae the left, and he wis in there a good while langer than the rest ay us. Naebody made a stooshie aboot it. Ye jist watched oot the corner ay yer eye, careful no tae make it obvious ye were watchin, and naebody said a word aboot it efter either, when he came back oot and got back on the bus, grey in the face, no near as gallus as he'd went in. That's jist how it goes at they places. Ye see it and ye leave it, because askin disnae help onybody, least ay aw him.

The whole time I wis up at that windae I wis workin hard tae keep ma face still and ma voice level, because ye ken fine that lookin nervous is the wan thing that gets ye a second look, even wi nothin tae hide at aw. So I stood there wi ma hauns as still as I could manage, ma voice flat, sayin whit needed sayin and nae mair than that.

She did wan wee kindness, mind, and I've no forgotten it. When she handed the papers back she looked up at me proper, for the first time the hale time, and said, quiet, jist between the two ay us, "Safe hame, hen." That wee bit ay softness efter aw that silence nearly did mair tae me than ony ay the questions had.

Then she gied us the wan nod, and that wis it, that wis whit let us go on. Papers back in ma haund, barrier up, and we were through.

I got back on the bus and it pulled away, and even efter, doon the road, it never lifted right away. It wis a good twenty minutes further on, past the checkpoint, past the next wee toon even, afore I felt ma shoothers come doon frae roond ma ears, afore I even noticed I'd been grippin ma ain hauns thegither the whole time withoot kennin I wis daein it. That's when it goes intae ye, no at the time, efter. Ye don't feel the toll ay it till yer safe past it, and then it hits ye aw at once, and ye're shakin a wee bit and ye don't even ken why noo, because naewhaur along the wey did onything actually happen tae ye.

That's how I tell it, and that's aw it wis. No a big story. Naebody shoutin, naebody dragged aff onywhere permanent, jist a line and some questions and a stamp at the end ay it. But it stays wi ye, that wee bit ay fear, even when there's nothin tae show fur it efter, and I'll no make it intae mair than that, because it wisnae mair than that. It wis jist a checkpoint, and I got through it, same as maist folk that day did.

You never said a word the hale time I wis tellin ye it, jist sat there, yer tea gaun cold in front ay ye, and that's how I kent it wis worth the tellin. Some stories, ye tell them fur the tellin ay them, no fur ony big finish.
