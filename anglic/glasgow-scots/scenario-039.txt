The foreman: Right, afore youse aw bolt aff hame - the morra. Get it settled the night or it'll be forgot.

The joiner: Aye go on then, whit's the script?

The foreman: The tenement joab, the roof. Seven o'clock sharp, cause the scaffold boys are in at eight and we need the felt doon afore they're clamberin aw ower it.

The young one: Seven? Away ye go, mak it half eight, ma back's wrecked frae the day.

The foreman: It's seven or naethin, son. Forecast's lookin dreich efter ten, if it comes in wet and we've no the felt doon we're stuck.

The joiner: Aye, seen that. Rain aff the west. If it's aw drookit up there we're back tae square one.

The driver: Ah'll bring the van roond wi the felt and the flashin, but there's no much felt left in the yard, so dinnae go slappin it doon like there's mair.

The foreman: That's you sorted wi the felt and the ladders then. Whae's daein the flashin roond the chimney heid, cause that's the fiddly bit.

The young one: No me, ah did it last time and ma hauns were freezin the hale day.

The foreman: Aye it's you but, yer the only wan wi the wee hauns fur it.

The young one: Every time it's me stuck wi the worst bit, honest tae God, it's no fair.

The foreman: Right, dinnae start a stooshie ower it. Joiner's on the felt, driver's bringin the gear, me on the guttering, and yersel on the flashin. That's the fower.

The joiner: Whit aboot the sparky, is he no needed the morra?

The foreman: Naw, he's away tae anither joab, cannae make it, he telt me the day.

The joiner: So that's yin doon awready.

The foreman: Aye, we're fower and that'll dae fine.

The young one: Seven sharp then, and whaever's late's buyin the rolls.

The foreman: You're buyin the rolls awready efter last week, so don't you start.

The young one: Away and gie's peace. Seven o'clock, ah'll be there.

The foreman: Aye, mind and be. See yiz aw the morra.
