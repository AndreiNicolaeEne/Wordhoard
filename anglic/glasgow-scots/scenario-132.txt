Honestly I don't think ony of us in this hoose will forget they eleven days. It come in colder than onybody wis sayin frae the very first mornin, the sky that hard blue and no a puff ae wind in it, and by dinner time yer breath wis hingin there like it didnae want tae leave yer face either. We had the fire gaun frae six in the mornin tae away past midnight, every single day of it, coal in first, then when the coal ran low it wis whitever wid burn, and the wee electric bar on top ae that just tae keep the weans frae greetin wi the cold in their fingers.

The pipe in the scullery went solid hard on the fourth day, first time that's ever happened in this hoose in ma mother's time nor before it, and we were away for messages in a poke tae the neighbour's just for a drop tae boil. The auld one sat by the fire the whole stretch, blanket ower her knees, crabbit wi the weans if they let the door stand open even a second.

The eldest: This beats the winter your grandfaither died in, son, and I thought that yin wid never be beat.

Worst night wis the eighth, naebody arguin that. Sky clear as onything and the cauld came doon like a hand roon yer thrapple. Weans greetin half the night, the windaes drookit wi ice on the inside, ma faither up and doon tae the fire like a man possessed.

Lost the doos that night, two ae them, deid stiff in the loft by mornin. My faither didnae say a word, just stood there peely-wally, lookin at them like it wis somehow his ain fault.

The morra after the eleventh day it broke, just like that, a drip aff the guttern first and then the hale street runnin wi it, folk oot their doors haein a right stooshie aboot naethin, laughin like eejits. Naebody said much in oor hoose, we just stood at the windae watchin it go, drookit wi relief mair than onythin, gled it wis done.
