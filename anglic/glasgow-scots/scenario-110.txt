The mother: Right, sit doon and get that tea intae ye afore it goes cold. So come on then, whit's it actually like doon there?

Me: Ach, it's mad, so it is. Different world.

The auntie: Different world, she says. Whit dae ye mean different world, it's no the moon, hen.

Me: Naw but honest, it's just... everything's that bit bigger. Ye could walk for an hour and no even leave the one bit of it. Here ye can get frae the toon tae yer bed in twenty minutes if the bus is behavin. Doon there it's near an hour some days just tae get fae one side tae the other, and that's no even the whole city, that's just crossin it.

The da: An hour? Tae get where, tae yer work?

Me: Tae anywhere. Tae yer maw's, tae the shops, tae see a pal. Ye plan yer whole day roond gettin there and back.

The eldest: Christ. And is it no deein yer heid in, aw that time on trains and that?

Me: Ye get used tae it. Ye just sit wi yer earphones in and yer heid doon. But aye, it grinds ye doon an aw. And the noise of it, that's the other thing naebody tells ye. It's never quiet. No even at three in the mornin. There's always somethin gaun on, sirens or folk shoutin or the traffic just never lettin up. Here ye get the odd drunk eejit roarin at hauf eleven and that's yer lot. Doon there it's like that every night, aw night.

The mother: Sounds like ye cannae get a minute's peace.

Me: Ye cannae, naw. Ye just stop hearin it eventually, is the honest truth.

The auntie: So where dae ye actually bide then? Whit's the hoose like?

Me: It's no a hoose, it's a wee flat. Wee, right, and I mean wee - youse would look at it and think how does a person live in that. Smaller than whit I had here even. But it costs a fortune, that's the daft bit. Whit ye'd pay for a room the size of oor bathroom would near buy ye a hoose up here.

The da: Away.

Me: Naw, straight up. Rent for that wee box is more than the mortgage on this whole street put together, I reckon.

The eldest: That's aff its heid. For that ye could be livin like a king up here.

Me: Aye, well, that's the trade ye make.

The mother: And are the folk friendly doon there? Or are they aw stuck up their ain backsides?

Me: That's no really a simple aye or naw, if am honest. Some of them couldnae be nicer, they'll hold a door or gie ye directions nae bother. But it's no like here where ye'd get a full conversation aff a stranger at the bus stop aboot nothin in particular. Naebody talks tae ye on the train, ye'd get looked at like ye were a bampot if ye tried. But then yer neighbours, once they know ye, they're grand, they'll take yer parcels in and that. So it's friendly, just... quieter friendly. Ye have tae work for it a bit.

The auntie: Sounds a bit cauld tae me.

Me: It's no cauld exactly. Just different rules.

The gran: And whaur dae ye get yer messages? Is there a decent market or is it aw they big shops?

Me: There's a market, aye, no far fae mine, does me for fruit and that, it's cheap enough there even wi everythin else bein dear. But mostly it's the big shops, ye're in and oot wi yer poke of messages and that's that. No much crack in it, no like doon at oor Co-op where ye cannae get served withoot hearin the hale street's business.

The eldest: And whit's the work like, how did ye even get sortit for a joab doon there?

Me: Ye have tae hustle for it, is the truth. It's no like up here where somebody's cousin puts a word in. Ye're sendin yer stuff aff intae the dark and hopin somebody looks at it. Took me a good while, mind, and a load of knockbacks afore anythin landed. But once ye're in somewhere, it moves fast, they dinnae hing aboot. Long hours an aw, longer than whit's normal up here I'd say.

The da: Sounds like a stooshie just tae get gaun in the mornin.

Me: It is that. And I'll tell ye, first few month I was that lost, honestly, lost aboot three or four times a week, comin oot the wrong exit and windin up gaun the wrong way doon some street that looked the same as every other street. Took me ages tae get the layout of it intae ma heid. Naebody tells ye the streets dinnae run straight there, they just go wherever they fancy.

The mother: You, lost? You that knew every back close in this scheme since ye were wee?

Me: Aye, well, it's no the same place, is it. I'm tellin ye, I felt like a right glaikit eejit, staunin there wi the phone in ma hand tryin tae work oot which way was which.

The auntie: And whit aboot the food, is it any good?

Me: Better in some ways, worse in others, honestly. Ye can get near enough anythin ye fancy, any hour, any kind of food fae anywhere in the world, and some of it's brilliant. But a proper roll and sausage, forget it, they dinnae have a clue. And everythin's dearer, obviously. So it's swings and roondabouts.

The eldest: Right, but is it actually better than here, or is it worse? Straight answer.

Me: Ach, ye cannae compare them like that, it's no that simple.

The eldest: That's no an answer, that's a swerve.

Me: Aye, well. It's different, is whit I'm sayin. Some days I'd tell ye it's brilliant and I widnae come back for anythin, and other days it's dreich and grey and I'm that homesick I could greet, and I just want ma ain bed and ma maw's tea. Depends whit day ye catch me on.

The mother: Aw, hen.

The da: Right, well here's the one that matters then. Are ye ever comin hame for good, or is that it noo?

Me: I dinnae know, honest tae God. Maybe. No the day, and probably no the morra either, but I'm no ruled it oot. We'll see whit happens. Ask us again next time am up.

The gran: You and yer "we'll see". Same answer since ye were wee.

Me: Aye, well, some things dinnae change, dae they.
