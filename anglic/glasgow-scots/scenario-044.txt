The brickie skims a trowel of mortar off the board, pats it ontae the brick dead even, sets it doon intae the bed wi a wee twist, then taps it level wi the end of the trowel. He steps back and hauds the trowel oot.

The brickie: Right, you're up. Butter the brick, no the wa, then doon it goes and gie it a wee twist tae settle it. Simple as that.

The new start: Aye, nae bother.

He lifts a great dollop and slaps it straight ontae the wall itself, then plants the brick doon on tap of it wi nothin on the brick ata.

The brickie: Naw naw, haud on. It's the brick ye butter, no the wa. Ye've done it backarts again.

The new start: So dae I no pit any on the wa at all?

The brickie: Naw. The brick gets it. The wa gets nothin. That's whit I just telt ye, is it no?

He tries again and this time he butters the brick right enough, but drops it straight doon wi nae twist, so it sits high and squint against the line.

The brickie: For God's sake, look at the state ae that. It's aw squint. Ye cannae just drap it doon, ye've tae twist it intae the bed.

The new start: I did whit ye showed us but.

The brickie: Ye didnae. I've showed ye three times the noo and it's still comin oot wrong.

The new start: Awright, awright, keep yer hair oan, I'm just no used tae the trowel yet.

The brickie: Right, forget aw that. Butter it thin, like ye're spreadin jam on a piece, right tae the edges. Then doon it goes and gie it a wee wiggle, no a drap, like you're settlin it intae its bed for the night. A wiggle.

He hands the trowel back ower wan mair time. The new start butters it thin and even, sets the brick doon, gies it the wee wiggle, and it settles level, flush wi the line, first go.

The brickie: There ye go, that's it, ya beauty. Dae that a hunner mair times the day and youse'll be gallus at it by the morra.
