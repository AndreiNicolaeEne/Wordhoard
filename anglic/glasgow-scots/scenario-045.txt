The customer comes in for the jacket that's been hinging ready on the rail, takes one look and stops deid.

The customer: Right, whit's this then. This isnae whit ah ordered, hen.

The tailor: That's your jacket, that. Made it aff the exact specs.

The customer: Naw it's no. Ah asked for three buttons doon the front and a straight hem. No this, two buttons and the hem aw angled like that.

The tailor: Ah'm tellin ye, that's whit we agreed the day ye came in. Ah dinnae make these things up.

The customer: Ah'm no glaikit, ah ken fine whit ah asked for. Three buttons. Straight hem. Look at it, it's squint aw the way roon.

The tailor: Haud on. Ah'll away and check ma note frae the fitting.

The customer waits, wean greeting somewhere ower at the next close, weans always greeting aboot something.

The tailor: Right, here it is. Two buttons, aye, that's whit's doon on the sheet, so that's no changed. But... the hem line says straight. Ah must've read it wrang aff the auld measurements.

The customer: See. Telt ye it wisnae right the minute ah clapped eyes on it.

The tailor: Aye, fair's fair, that yin's on me. Ah'll no argue wi ma ain writing when it's staring at me in black and white.

The customer: So whit noo? Ah'm no wantin tae come back here a third time ower this.

The tailor: Ye'll no need tae. Gie it back tae me the noo, ah'll get the hem sorted straight, and it'll be ready for ye the morra efter next. Two days, ah promise ye that.

The customer: And the buttons stay two, since that's whit's on the sheet?

The tailor: Aye, that bit's correct, so that's no touched. Just the hem.

The customer: Right ye are then. Two days.

The tailor: Two days, hen, on ma life. Ah'll dae it masel the night so it's done right.
