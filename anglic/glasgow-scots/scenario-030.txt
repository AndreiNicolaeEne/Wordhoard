The niece: Right, I'm no leavin here the day withoot that tablet recipe, hen. Ye've had me askin since the wean was born and aw I get is "oh I just make it."

The aunt: Away ye go, it's no a big stooshie, I just never wrote it doon. My ain maw never wrote naethin doon either, ye just stood at her elbow and it went intae yer heid, so bear wi us.

The niece: Well tell us then. Whit's first intae the pot.

The aunt: First it's the butter, a good knob of it, mibbe four ounces, gie or take, ye're no exact wi it, that's no how she done it. Intae the pan wi a pound of sugar and -

The niece: And?

The aunt: Aw haud on, I nearly left oot the milk, there's a wee drop of ordinary milk goes in an aw, forgot that the last time and it come oot ower thick. So butter, sugar, milk, then the tin of condensed milk on tap, aw thegither on a low heat till it's melted.

The niece: Then whit.

The aunt: Then ye bring it up tae the boil, but my maw aye said never rush the boil, hen. Ye turn it up tae get it done quicker and it catches on the bottom, and burnt tablet's fit for naebody. Slow, and keep stirrin.

The niece: How d'ye know it's ready but, there's nae clock for it.

The aunt: Ye drap a wee bit intae cauld watter and if it goes intae a soft ball ye're there, or ye go by the smell, it turns fae milky tae mair like toffee. The bicarb though, that's the bit I never measure, just a pinch between the fingers, judged by eye. Too much and it's bitter.

The niece: Could ye no use margarine instead the butter?

The aunt: Aye, ye could, at a pinch, it'll still set, but it's no the same, ye lose that wee taste, so I'd no dae it unless ye had tae.
