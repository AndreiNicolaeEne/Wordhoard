The three of them were sat outside the kena of an evening, the tea gone stone cold in the mugs beside them, and sure enough the talk went round to health the way it always does when there's nothing else left to be complaining about.

The first: God bless the mark, this knee of mine has gone gammy on me, years now. Fourteen years if it's a day, ever since the fall off the back of the wagon below in Athlone, and it never right since. Stiff as a board of a morning, I have to be near lifted out of the bed itself.

The second: Stiff is it. Sure that's nothing only the weather in it. I'm after having three and four bad nights of it every month this long while, the shoulder roaring at me till three and four in the morning, up and down, up and down, not a wink got the whole night. That's a different class of thing altogether, that is.

The first: Ah now, don't be talking to me.

The third: The pair of ye can whisht a minute. I have the back on me since before either one of ye was reared, God's truth, since I was no age at all, a lackeen hauling water below at the old site on the tober. Older than the two of ye's troubles put together and it never left me one day since, not one.

The second: Who's to say now who has it worst so, will we put it to a vote on it?

The third: There's no vote in it, sham, sure everyone at this table knows their own pain is the worst pain going and no other will tell them different.

The first: True for you there. Mine's still the worst though, all the same.

The second: Gloke at him now, God help us all.

And the three of them roaring laughing then, the way they always did, not one of them giving an inch, the tea gone stone cold in the mugs and none of them minding it one bit.
