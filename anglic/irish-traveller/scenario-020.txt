The friend: God, I don't know where to start with it. He's after telling me it's over, just like that, in the middle of the tea, like he was telling me the milk's gone off.

Me: Mm.

The friend: Three year we're together, and now he says he needs space. Space, God help us. Sure the man had the whole kena to himself every time I went off to me mother's of an evening.

Me: Mm, aye, he did.

The friend: And I'm after going back over the whole of it in me head since, wondering was it me, did I go on too much about getting married, did I say too much to his mother that time, should I have held me tongue more, should I...

Me: Stop that now, would ye. Stop it there. That's not a thing you did, that's him, and his gammy carry-on with the drink, out misli-ing round the tober with them fellas of his till all hours, skimished more nights than not, and you sitting home minding the child on your own. That's the whole of the reason, and not one bit of it belongs to you.

The friend: D'ye really think that?

Me: I know it. There's not a thing wrong with you, sham, not a thing. You're the same girl you always were, good and true and soft-hearted, and any feen with sense in him would be glad of ye.

The friend: Sure who'd have me now, God love me.

Me: I would, only I've me own fella to be minding! Look, I'm stopping here the night, I'll bring a drop of something over and we'll say no more about him. Or if ye'd sooner, we'll get out of this trailer altogether, get the child seen to and walk down the site, see who's about, anything but sitting here staring at the four walls.

The friend: Ha. God, would ye ever stop, you're worse than a priest for the talking.

Me: Someone has to be, sham. Come 'ere, put the kettle on again and we'll sort ye out.
