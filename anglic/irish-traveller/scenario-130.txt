The water was at the door first thing, only ankle deep itself, and I said nothing of it only stood there in me bare feet looking down at it creeping in under the step. Didn't think much of it, only a drop of rain and the drains backed up again, sure it does that every winter, please God it'd go as quick as it came.

Checked it again an hour after and it was up past the ankle now, near the shin of me, and that's when I started lifting. Bug the good delph up onto the counter, bug the blankets up onto the bed, anything soft or dear to us went up first while there was still time in it. The child's shoes, the good coat, the box with the papers, all up as high as I could reach.

By midmorning it was at the knee and rising still, and I'm after moving everything that could be moved onto whatever stood the highest in the place, the table, the dresser, the top of the wardrobe, God help us. The eldest was out at the tober trying to gloke was any of the other kenas as bad, and came back soaked to the waist saying the whole site was the one story.

It kept on climbing, no stop in it at all, till it peaked early afternoon, higher than anything we'd raised, God bless the mark, over the top of the dresser itself.

The mother: Don't be standing in it, get up on the bed with the child, would ye.

Me: I'm alright, I'm only watching does it come any higher.

We sat then and only listened to it lapping off the walls for hours, the whole place gone quiet only for that sound, waiting on it, saying nothing only God love us get us through this.

It was a good few hours after the peak before it started to go down at all, dropping that slow you'd swear it wasn't moving only you'd look back an hour later and see the difference in it.

Went back the next day and there it was, the line of it dried into the wall, dark and high, God bless us, further up than I ever thought water could reach in that place.
