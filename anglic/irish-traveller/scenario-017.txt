The aunt: God bless the work, are ye above in the kena at all? I'm after coming the whole tober down from your woman's to have a word with ye, so put the kettle on and sit down a minute till I tell ye.

Me: What is it now, aunt, come in out of it.

The aunt: I was talking below to your woman that knows the Galway crowd, and she's after telling me all about that feen I mentioned to ye a while back. She says there isn't a better worker going on the road, up before light every morning, never a day idle in his life, never mind the drink or the messing some of them do be at. And soft in himself with it, not a temper on him at all, ye'd never hear him raise his voice to a soul. And the family behind him, sure they're solid people, respected the length of the road, no trouble ever out of them, God-fearing and decent the whole lot.

Me: I heard he was alright, aunt, I did hear that.

The aunt: Alright? What'd be wrong with a man the like of that, will ye tell me now? Working steady, easy in himself, good stock behind him. What more are ye looking for in this life?

Me: Ah I'm not saying anything against him, aunt, I'm only saying I've things to sort first, I'm run off me feet as it is.

The aunt: Run off your feet. Sure your own cousin said that exact word to me, cried the door down saying she wasn't ready, and look at her now, delighted out of her life she didn't win that one. She's after thanking me for it since.

Me: I know, aunt, and I'm grateful to ye for thinking of me, I am. I'm only saying not yet, I have to think on it myself.

The aunt: Well. I'll say no more about it so. Only the offer's there whenever ye come round to it, it's not going anywhere, and ye know well where to find me.
