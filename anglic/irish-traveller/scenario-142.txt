I woke this morning and the first thing landed on me was the promise, sitting there in me chest like a stone before me eyes were even open. Months back, after the uncle was on at me one time too many about jobs started and never finished, I swore to him plain, in front of the whole kena of them, that I'd give the full day to clearing that scrapyard out the back of the site, every last bit of it, and today was that day, God help me.

I near didn't get up at all. Lay there another twenty minutes cursing meself for a fool, for ever opening me mouth and swearing anything to any man living. Out the door anyway, muttering the whole way down the tober to the yard, not a soul out only the dog and a gloke of grey sky sitting over the trailers.

The brother-in-law was there before me, God love him, not a word out of him only nodding at the heap of it.

The brother-in-law: Sham, will ye hurry up out of that, the day's not getting longer on us.
Me: What kind of a header was I, swearing to a full day of this.
The brother-in-law: Ye said it yerself, no one held a gun on ye.
Me: Amn't I after knowing that.

Sorting the metal near broke me back, the length of it never ending, the sun gammy hot by noon and not a drop to drink only the flask gone warm on me. Round dinnertime I was near ready to misli off home and leave the rest for another day, and I said as much.

Me: I'm about ready to leave the whole lot of it where it stands.
The brother-in-law: You swore it though.

That was the end of that thought, closed off clean as a door shutting. I gritted through the worst stretch of the afternoon, the heaviest of it, arms shaking, still going, still going. By the time the light was after going soft and gold over the site I had the last of it loaded and the yard swept bare behind me. Walked back up the tober complaining still, under me breath, but walking home a man who'd kept his word, thanks be to God, and not one step of the whole long day turned into walking away from it.
