Me: Right, say we're after gettin' a great whack of gred from nowhere, fell into our laps out of the sky, what's the first thing yourself'd be at?

The sham: I'd have a new cob off a fella above in the yard before the sun was down, a proper trotter for the tober, and I'd have him out skimished with pride at every show between here and Galway.

Me: Ye would in your eye. The shades'd be round the site inside the week askin' where a feen the likes of you got the price of a cob like that.

The sham: Arrah stop, a man's allowed the one dream. What'd yourself be doin' so, since ye've gone all sensible on me all of a sudden.

Me: I'd solk a good bit of it and bug it straight into the post office, so I would, put it by for the lackeen goin' for her confirmation and the sublik after startin' school below. Not have the whole lot gone in a gulp.

The sham: Puttin' it by, is it. Sure money's for livin' off, not for lookin' at in a jar behind the trailer door.

Me: And yourself'd have it gone on drink and presents for the beoir inside the month, and be back below at the gather's door lookin' the loan of the price of a dinner.

The sham: Musha, would I now. Sure I'm after doin' enough gruber in me life to know better than that. Alright so, half away safe and half for the divilment, that suit ye?

Me: It would. But there's to be no golden sulky out of it, God bless us, don't be gettin' notions above yerself.

The sham: A golden sulky! Gloke at the pair of us here, plannin' out gold sulkies and diamonds on the harness, and not the price of the messages between the two of us this minute.

Me: God between us and all harm, we'd want our heads examined, the two of us. Go on so, put the kettle on, we'll leave the gred where it is, in nobody's pocket at all, for now anyway.
