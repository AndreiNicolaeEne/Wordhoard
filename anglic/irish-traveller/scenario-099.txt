I was above at the green beyond the site, watching a cob out sulky trotting with some young sham up behind him giving it socks, when who did I see coming along the tober only my friend from years back, that I hadn't clapped eyes on since we were the height of nothing.

Me: God almighty, is that yourself? I nearly didn't know you, so I didn't.

My friend: I'm after saying the very same about you this minute. Look at the cut of you, you're after getting very grand altogether.

Me: Grand my eye. How's all belonging to you? The gather still keeping well?

My friend: He is, thank God, though he's slower on his two feet than he was. And yourself, how's the beoir and the kids?

Me: Ah, we're all rightly, please God. Three kam and a lackeen now, if you don't mind, and the youngest of them the very same divil his father was.

My friend: God between us and all harm, another one of him let loose on the world.

Me: Don't be talking. Sure it's mad seeing you though. Do you know who I was thinking of only the other week?

My friend: Who now.

Me: The teacher. Ye know the one I mean. The one that had us all afraid of our shadows.

My friend: Ah here, don't. My stomach's after turning just hearing her mentioned.

Me: I know well. If she caught you so much as whispering while her back was to the class, one word out of you, that was it, you were for the line.

My friend: A hundred times. Not fifty, not eighty, a hundred, and she'd count them herself after, God help you if you were shy a few.

Me: I must not talk in class. A hundred times. My hand used to be crippled after it.

My friend: And she'd stand there with the chalk in her hand and say it, do you remember, "I have eyes in the back of my head and don't any of ye forget it." Nearly word for word she said it, every day near enough.

Me: God, I can hear her saying it this minute. The two eyes of her going round the room like she was daring you to try her.

My friend: Do you remember the time she had the poor sublik up out of his seat, made him stand out in front of the whole class with his hands out flat, and she going at the fingers with the ruler? I can still see the face on him.

Me: I do remember it. We were all sat there like mice, not one of us breathing, only delighted it wasn't ourself.

My friend: I still freeze up thinking of it, God's honest truth. Even to this day if someone raises their voice sudden near me I'm braced for something.

Me: Do you know I still go scarlet if I'm called on sudden, even now, even at my age, standing in a queue somewhere and someone says my name loud. I'm straight back in that room.

My friend: Sure amn't I the very same. But do you remember the one time that sham talked back to her and got away with it? I still don't believe it happened, even now, and I was there.

Me: I do. What was it he said to her again.

My friend: She was after giving out to him for something and he only looks up at her, cool as you like, and says, "Sure you'd want to catch yourself on." The whole room went dead still, we were all waiting for the roof to come down on him.

Me: And she only laughed.

My friend: She laughed. Actual laughing, out loud, the two of us there not believing our eyes. Never touched him. To this day I don't know what came over her.

Me: Nobody ever worked that one out. Do you remember her other thing though, the little habit of hers, the way she used to clear her throat before every single sentence, a little "hem hem" like that, and we used to do it behind her back the minute she turned round.

My friend: The whole class at it, hem hemming away, and one of us would always get caught mid hem hem and near die of the fright.

Me: And her twisting the ring on her finger while she read out the roll, round and round, we used to watch it more than we listened to our own names.

My friend: God, the state of us. But do you remember the time she was good to that one poor lackeen, the child that had no coat that winter? She never said a word in front of anyone, only kept her back after and gave her one of her own, an old one mind, but a good warm one. Not a word said about it after, ever.

Me: I remember that alright. It didn't fit with the rest of her at all, and that's why it stuck with me.

My friend: What do you hear of her these days at all? Is she even above still?

Me: Someone told me she went out to a mission house below in Galway, gone into it fully, giving her days to it. Another one told me she passed on a good few years back now. I couldn't say which is the truth of it, nobody seems to know for certain.

My friend: It's a strange thing, the not knowing. But looking back on it now, and the fear we had of her, I think it was never only fear at all, if I'm honest. I think it was something closer to respect, only we hadn't the word for it then, being childer.

Me: I'd say that's exactly it. She wanted something for us, I know that much now, even if she went the hard road to get it into us.

My friend: The hem hemming though. God, I can't think of it without laughing. The state of us mimicking her the second her back was turned.

Me: And getting caught at it more than once and standing there red as a beetroot. But I'll tell you this, if that woman walked up the tober toward me this very second, I'd stand up straight as a poker before I'd even think about it.

My friend: Ah, wouldn't we all. Some things don't leave you.

Me: They don't. Anyway, enough of her, God rest her or God keep her, whichever it is. Tell me what's the story with yourself these days.

My friend: Ah, not a lot new. We're above at the halting site still, near the mission, in the same kena more or less, only a bigger trailer now with the family grown. The gruber's scarce enough this while, but we're getting by.

Me: Same as everyone. There's no gred going spare anywhere these days, that's for certain.

My friend: The aul feen has the cob out sulky trotting most evenings still, keeps him out of trouble more than anything. Better than having him under my feet, God love him.

Me: Sure the same here, mine's the very same, always some horse or other to be fussing over.

My friend: And how's your own gather keeping, is he still able for himself?

Me: He's grand, thank God, slower but grand. He was asking after your crowd only last week, wondering did I ever run into any of ye.

My friend: Musha, tell him I was asking for him. And the shades were up at our site again last week, causing their usual trouble over nothing.

Me: They never leave well enough alone, that shower. Anyway, I'd best be misli-ing on, I've the dinner to see to below, but it's done my heart good running into you, so it has.

My friend: It has mine too. Sure I'll bug you a shout when I'm passing your way again, we'll have a proper sit down and a cup of tea over it.

Me: Do, please God. Mind yourself now, and God bless.

My friend: God bless yourself too, and all belonging to you.
