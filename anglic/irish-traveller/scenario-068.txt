Now listen here, love, for I'll only be after saying this the once, and you only landed on the site today so you want it before any fella comes round all smiles on ye.

There's always one about, a feen ye'll know the minute ye clap eyes on him, he's after being everyone's best friend before he's even proper introduced. Lovely talker, holds yer hand too long in the shake, asking after the childer before he knows are they yours at all. He'll have a deal going, always, a load of gear or a motor or work below in some yard, and he's never short of a story for why it's you above anyone he's telling.

The pitch is always grand, too grand, and that's the whole tell of it right there, if a thing sounds like it fell off the back of heaven itself, ye walk on past it. He'll say he's a trailer going, or a horse, or work paying good, and all he wants off ye is a small bit of gred up front, just to hold it for ye, sure he trusts ye. And that's the hook set, the minute the gred crosses out of yer hand into his, that's the last ye'll see of him. He'll have misli'd off that site and off the county before yer tea's cold.

The cousin's fella below in Galway, God help him, handed over forty pound for a motor was never there at all, and the man was gone within the hour, gammy business the whole of it, and no moniker he gave was his own besides. He'll tell ye himself now, quiet, not for the gossip of it, only so it doesn't happen ye.

The signs, before it gets that far: too friendly too quick, no straight answer on where he's stopping, and himself the one bringing up money first, not you.

If one comes to ye with talk like that, ye say nothing back, ye give him nothing, and there's no good running to the shades after neither, sure they'll do nothing for our own. Go first to the eldest on the site before ye put a penny into any strange hand asking up front. God bless and mind yerself now.
