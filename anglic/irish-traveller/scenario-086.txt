Sure you know the cousin was getting married, the match was on since the springtime, and the whole of us were below at it, both sides of the family and half the site besides. My sister: I'm only asking. You were roaring "one more song" at half three this morning like it was your own wedding and not hers. God love you, you'll not do that in a hurry again, will you.

My sister: Sure you said that the last time.

My mother came by above the din in my head and gloked at me and shook her head and said nothing, which was worse than if she'd said plenty.

Round dinnertime I made myself a bit of dry toast and a strong cup of tea, nothing more than that, my stomach wouldn't have taken anything heavier, and I sat there sipping it slow and staying quiet, not moving more than I had to, until the worst of the sickness passed off me by degrees.

By the evening, thank God, I was near myself again. I ate a proper dinner, the first real one in two days, and I could think straight for the first time since the wedding, no fog on me, no gammy head, nothing only the shame sitting there quiet where the sickness had been.
