The uncle: Come here to me now, kam, and gloke at this before you'd go putting a hand near him at all. You take the collar so, over the head first, easy now, and slide it down till it sits proper on the shoulder. Then back him in between the shafts nice and slow, God bless him, he's no bother in the world once you're not rushing him.

The new hand puts the collar on upside down and hauls the cob back too quick.

The uncle: Ah now, hold on, hold on. Not like that. The buckle wants to sit on the top of him, not under his throat where you have it. Take it off again and we'll go again, slower this time.

The new hand: Is it the britchin buckle you mean, or what?

The uncle: No. The collar. I haven't even got to the britchin yet, sham, will ye listen to what's put in front of ye instead of thinking ahead of yourself.

He shows it again, backs the cob in himself, hooks the traces, reaches for the britchin. That's your britchin there, goes under the tail, holds him back going down the tober, so it wants to be snug on him, not slack.

The new hand buckles it loose and crosses the traces over.

The uncle: Would ye ever - I'm only after showing ye that! Look at the state of that now, it's tangled itself the way you have it, the poor cob'll be lame in his mind before we're out the gate at all.

The new hand: I thought that was the way you had it. I was watching, so I was, I don't know how it went like that on me.

The uncle: Right, forget the way I said it. Think of it this way - the strap nearest your own hand stays nearest your own hand, the whole way round, it doesn't cross itself, same as lacing a boot. Solk that one, bring it round to your side. That's it. That's it now, don't let it wander off on ye.

The new hand does it right.

The uncle: There. Now you have it. That's the whole of it, please God, and don't be having me show ye a fourth time.
