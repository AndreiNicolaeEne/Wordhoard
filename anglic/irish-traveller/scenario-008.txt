The child: Ma, will ye get me the phone, the good one I was after showing ye on the box.

The mother: Amn't I after telling ye that three times this week now, and the answer's the same. Sure there's no gred for it, not this side of Christmas.

The child: Why not though. Why can I never have anything I ask for.

The mother: Because your father's not working since the job above in Galway fell through on him, and what comes in goes on the site fees and the diesel and feeding the four of ye first. That's the whole of it, love, there's no big mystery to it. There's no money.

The child: I'll do the messages for ye every morning for a month, I will, and mind the young ones when ye're above at the meeting.

The mother: You'll do the messages anyway, that's no bargain, that's you living in this trailer same as the rest of us and pulling your weight.

The child: What if I wait for the birthday so.

The mother: If your father's back working by then, please God, and there's a bit spare after the bills is paid, we'll see. I'm not saying never. I'm saying not now.

The child: The sham above in the next trailer, the young fella, he's had one since the summer, and his mammy's on nothing only the children's allowance same as yourself.

The mother: I don't care what's above in his kena, that's his mammy's business and this here's mine, and him having it doesn't put a pound in my pocket nor take one out of it either. Ye can't be measuring your own life off what's sitting in somebody else's hand.

The child: That's not fair though.

The mother: Fair or not, that's how it is. Here now, don't be gammy about it. I'll bring ye down to the shop Saturday and get ye a top-up for the sister's auld phone, will that do ye for the minute?

The child: Suppose it'll have to, so.
