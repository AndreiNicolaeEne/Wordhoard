Me: Sit down there now, love, and give me your ear a while, for you should have the whole of it before you're properly one of us, and I'm the only one left who has it start to finish, so it falls to me to give it to you straight.

It started above at the fair, would be ten year ago now, maybe a small bit more, God knows the time does fly on a body. My own cousin was the one that pulled the rest of us in, and I'll tell you now she never meant to do it, she only wanted a hand minding the cobs while her gather went off to see a man about a trailer, and didn't she rope in every sham and beoir standing near the rail that day, and sure amn't I still thanking her for it. She had the way of talking to anyone, that one, God bless her, she'd have the shades themselves laughing if she'd a mind to. That's the day the whole of us first stood together, and we never rightly parted after, not properly, not since.

Now there was one among us near didn't stay, and I won't say who only that you'd know him well enough once you meet him proper. He was quiet that first year, kept himself to the edge of things, misli-ing off home early every time, and the rest of us thinking he wanted no more of us at all. But then didn't his own cob go gammy on him at the fair the following year, lame as you like, and it was two of the group stood with him half the night walking it up and down the tober trying to ease it, no complaint out of either of them, and after that he was a different man entire. He's after telling me since that was the night he knew he'd found his crowd, and he's never missed a gathering since, not one.

There was a falling out early on as well, between two of the feens, and it was over gred if you don't mind, a bet gone wrong on a trotting race, one swearing blind he was owed and the other swearing just as blind he wasn't. Went on for weeks that did, the pair of them not speaking, the rest of us caught in the middle of it. It was the mothers in the end sorted it, sat the two of them down and wouldn't let them up till it was settled. And out of that whole business came the joke that's stuck to us since, for the cob that started it all was meant to be the surest thing walking, and to this day if any one of us says a thing is a dead cert, sure the whole group falls round laughing, because that cob came in last of the field and near put the two of them in the poorhouse over it.

We lost one along the way an all, I won't lie to you. Said at the time it was the gruber that took him, the work dried up below and there was more of it over in England, and that's the reason given and no one pushed him on it further, though there's them among us thinks it was more to do with his young one marrying in with another crowd and the two families not sitting easy together after. Whatever the truth of it, he's gone from us these three years and we don't dwell on it beyond the once in a while someone says his name and we go quiet a minute.

And we took in one after, a fine sublik that was, vouched for by his own cousin that was already sat among us, and even so we didn't take him at his word straight off, no. We tested him a good while, mind, had him prove himself at this and that before anyone called him one of us proper, same as I suppose we'll be doing with yourself a small bit yet, though don't take that the wrong way, it's only ever done out of care for what we have.

The thing that bound the rest of us tighter again was the winter the shades moved the whole site on with no warning near given, dead of the cold it was, and we'd nowhere sorted for it. Ended up the lot of us packed into the one kena that night, more of us than had any right fitting in it, sharing what bit of food and drink was going, and nobody slept a wink, but God between us and all harm, we laughed more that night than many a good one since. That's the night I'd say properly made us what we are.

There's an absence still felt among us an all, one that moved off to a site over beyond Galway a good while back now for family reasons of her own, and though we do be on the phone to her regular enough, it's not the same as having her stood at the wall with the rest of us of an evening, and there's them still says her name expecting her to answer.

As for how we stand now, sure everyone has their bit. There's one always organising the whole crowd, on the phone morning noon and night sorting who's bringing what and who's collecting who, and God help the fair or the wake that crowd wasn't at because of her. There's a sham among us always the last through the door, always some story for it, and we do only laugh now for we've long stopped expecting different of him. And there's one keeps the peace above all, quiet spoken, never raises herself, but she's the one the rest of us go to when there's bad blood needs cooling, and it cools quick enough once she's spoken to it.

We nearly went under once, mind, a good few years back, over a row so bad I won't put the details of it to you now, only that it split the group clean in two for the best part of a year, hardly a word passing between the two halves of us. It was a removal brought us back together in the end, standing shoulder to shoulder for one of our own being buried, and whatever was between us before seemed a small thing next to that, and it's never come up between us since, not properly, though we all know it's there under it.

The one thing's held from the very first day and never faltered is the fair itself, the same one every year, we go together whatever else is going on among us, good times or bad between us, we stand at that same rail together, and there's not one of us would miss it for the world.

And you, love, you're near there already whether you know it or not. You were up half the night with the rest of us the last time a cob needed minding, no complaint out of you, and you brought food up to the site without a word said needing to be asked, and that's more than plenty of grown feens among us ever managed their first year. So there you have the whole of it now, start to finish, and you're one of us, close as kin, and God bless the mark, may there be many a fair yet with you standing at that rail alongside the rest of us.
