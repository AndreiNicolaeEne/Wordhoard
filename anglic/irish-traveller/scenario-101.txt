Me: Well I'm only after getting back and I have to tell you, the sham that used be beside us above in the school, I couldn't have put a moniker on him at all, he's changed that much. Big grey beard on him now and the belly out to here, and I swear to God I looked straight past him twice before it clicked.

The cousin: Is that the one that went off to England years back?

Me: The very man, home special for it. But God love her, your one that always had the head on her, the smart one, exactly the same. Not a day put on her, still talking the ninety mile an hour, still bossing the whole hall round her.

The cousin: She was always that way though, even as a young one. Do you remember her below at the site giving out to the lads over the state of the cobs, and she only about twelve?

Me: I do well. And look at her now, no different, only taller.

The cousin: Who didn't show at all? I was asking round and nobody had much of a word on it.

Me: The gather's youngest, the one that used be gruber-mad, always under a car. He's after being put away, God help him, over some carry-on with the shades a few year back. Nobody wanted to say it out loud in the hall but sure it got round anyway.

The cousin: Lord have mercy on him. Did you hear the news on the lackeen that used be knocking round with the sulky trotting crowd?

Me: I did, and I near fell over. She's after doing the training below in Dublin, has letters after her name now, a nurse no less. Who'd have said it, out of all of us.

The cousin: God bless her, she done well for herself. Sure look at the two of us anyway, are we changed itself?

Me: Ah we are and we amn't. Bit more grey, bit slower on the tober, but we're the same pair of eejits underneath it still.

The cousin: We are, please God. We won't leave it as long the next time, will we. Get the whole crowd together again before we're all after forgetting each other's faces.

Me: We will, please God. Before the year's out even.
