The youngest: Amn't I after having the queerest dream, sister, and I can't get shut of it since I woke. It's stuck to me like glue so it is, I've it going round in my head since before I'd the kettle even on.

The eldest: Go on then, tell us the whole of it. Sure the tea's only wet, we've time enough before anyone's stirring.

The youngest: It started ordinary enough, that's the mad thing about it. On and on it went, past the turn where home should have come up, and I kept saying to myself, sure it'll be round the next bend, and it never was. I misli'd on and on and the road never gave out.

The eldest: That'd put the heart crossways in you alright, a road with no end to it.

The youngest: When I did get there at last, God knows how long that took me, the kena had a room in it I never seen before in my life, waking or otherwise. Never existed, that room, not in any trailer we ever had, not in this life.

The eldest: What kind of a room was it at all? Was there something in it, or was it only empty on you?

The youngest: There was something in it alright. Our father was sat in it, God rest him, calm as you like, like he never left us at all, like no time had gone by on the man. Sitting there easy as a Sunday after Mass, and he was talking away to me, ordinary as anything.

The eldest: Ah here now. God bless us and save us, that'd put years on you, that would.

The youngest: It would. I've been trying since I woke to get the words back, what it was he said to me, and I can't get them, sister, no matter how I go at it. I know I was told something, I have that much of it still, the feeling's on me yet, but the words themselves are gone from me entirely.

The eldest: Try again now. Was it something about the family, or was it -

The youngest: I did try. Three or four times I went at it since I woke, lying there with my eyes shut trying to hear it again inside my own head, and I get near it and it's away from me again. It's like trying to solk hold of smoke, sister, you close your hand on it and there's nothing there. I'll not get it now, it's gone.

The eldest: Sure maybe it wasn't meant to be got. Some things aren't for keeping, only for feeling once and letting go.

The youngest: Then it shifted on me, the way dreams do be shifting, and I was searching the whole place over for something. Looking under everything, into every room of that kena and it grown bigger than any trailer has a right to be, and whatever it was I was after, it wasn't where it should be, and it wasn't turning up anywhere else either. I went at it harder and harder, in a right state, and the more I looked the worse the fright in me got.

The eldest: Looking for what, though? What was it had you that destroyed?

The youngest: That's the thing of it, I couldn't tell you. I didn't know inside the dream itself what I was after at all. I only knew it had to be found and it wasn't being found, and that was near killing me with the want of it.

The eldest: Mother of God, that's a fright and a half.

The youngest: The panic in me kept climbing and climbing till it broke me clean out of the sleep. I woke with the heart going in me like a hare, and I lay there a good while after, not rightly sure which of the two was the real one, the room with our father sat in it or the kena I was after waking up in. I had to look round me twice before I'd trust the roof over my own head.

The eldest: Sure it takes a minute alright, coming back to yourself out of a thing like that.

The youngest: It does. I was staring up at the roof half thinking it'd turn into that room on me again if I looked at it too long.

The eldest: And what do they say it means, a man gone from us this many a year turning up that settled in a dream, sitting there like he only stepped out for the messages and back in the door again?

The youngest: I couldn't tell you, honest to God. My own mother, God rest her too, used always say if the dead come to you easy like that, not raging or roaring at you, it's no bad sign at all, it's only them minding you still.

The eldest: Wisha, could be that. Or it could be you're only after eating something wasn't sitting right with you before bed.

The eldest: Well there you are then, it must be him minding you so, and no more about it. What else would it be.

The youngest: D'ye think would he come again, if I let myself think on him before I go over tonight? Might I get the words that time.

The eldest: I wouldn't be trying that, love, if it was me. Sure you'll only be lying there half the night waiting on him and getting nothing only your own thoughts going round and round on you.

The youngest: True enough, sister. I'll say nothing to him at all so, and see does he come to me anyway of his own accord.

The eldest: That's more than plenty of people get out of a dream, sister, and don't be saying different. Now drink your tea before I throw it out on you and put the kettle on again for a fresh one.

The youngest: God, you'd have me drowned in tea this morning.
