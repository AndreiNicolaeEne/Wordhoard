The cousin: Are ye after hearing what I heard below at the fair?

Her sister: What is it now, God bless us, tell us quick, don't be leaving it hanging.

The cousin: I'm only after talking to her one, the sister of your woman, and she seen it herself with her own two eyes, so she did. The feen was standing off with the beoir, the two of them well away from the rest of us, down past the trailers, laughing away at some class of a joke between themselves, and standing closer than any two friends'd stand, I'm telling ye.

Her sister: Go way outta that! Which feen is this now, who is it at all?

The cousin: Ah the sham ye know well, does be always about the sulky trotting of an evening with the uncle. Him and her, God, laughing their heads off and not a bit of space between the two of them.

Her sister: Ah stop it now! That's an awful lot to be nothing, is it not? Or would we be reading it in too far altogether, the pair of us?

The cousin: That's what I said to meself and all, but hold on, there's more to it. The one that does be minding the cobs seen the same two earlier on as well, off on their own near the kena before the music even started. Twice in the one day, mind, that's no accident.

Her sister: God almighty, the whole site'll have it by the tober tomorrow, ye know the way it does misli round, one mouth to the next and back again before ye'd blink.

The cousin: Could be nothing still, God bless the mark, don't go saying it to a soul yet. Sure the only way we'd know for sure is to ask one of the two of them straight out to their face.

Her sister: True for ye. I'm dying to know all the same, God help me.
