Me: Did you hear the mother going on about the dinner, like I never boiled a spud in me life?

Himself: Ah she's only oul in herself, don't mind her.

Me: Oul in herself, me eye. She said it straight out to the beoir doing the washing up, in front of the whole kena, that the cabbage was gammy and I'd no notion how long to leave it on.

Himself: She meant nothing by it, sure that's only her way.

Me: She meant plenty, and you're only taking her part because you're still afraid of your mammy at your age.

Himself: I'm not afraid of her. I only don't want the two of ye at it over a Sunday dinner.

Me: And the father, God love him, the very same story about the fair a third time, word for word, I'm after hearing that story more times than I've had me own dinners.

Himself: He does love that story.

Me: He loves the sound of himself telling it is what he loves. Called me be the wrong moniker twice an' all, and never once corrected himself.

Himself: He does that to everyone.

Me: And then the goodbye at the door. Forty-five minutes, God between us and all harm, standing there in the cold, ye'd swear we were misli-ing off to America and not back up the tober to our own kena.

Himself: That's true enough now, she does go on something shocking.

Me: She does. And that sham that showed up at the gate, skimished before the dinner was even served, gloking in the window the whole time we ate, solking chips off the plates when nobody was minding him.

Himself: I seen him. Gammy article altogether.

Me: He is surely, God help whatever beoir ends up stuck with him.

Himself: Ah here, you're right, I couldn't be doing another Sunday the like of it.

Me: Not a bother, love. We'll say we're working the next one.

Himself: We will. God forgive us the lie, but we will.
