The wife: Nine days left in this month, God help us, and will ya gloke what's below in that tin there.

The husband: Tip it out on the table so, don't be keepin' me in suspense over it.

The wife: Twenty. That's the whole of the gred, no more comin' in till it turns over, and nine days to be done on it.

The husband: We'll do the big pot then, spuds and the onion and whatever's after bein' left of the mince, stretch it three days runnin', four if we're careful with it.

The wife: Four days on the one pot, God bless the mark, and bread and a drop of tea the other days. That's the plan so.

The husband: Sure I might put a fiver on the trotting Sunday, ye never know, could come good for us.

The wife: Ye will in your eye put a fiver on anything, are ye gone soft in the head, a gammy notion entirely with the state we're in, keep your gred in your pocket where it belongs.

The husband: Ah it's only a-

The wife: It's a quarter of what has to do us the nine days, will ya gloke sense for once instead of the horses.

The husband: Alright, alright, keep your hair on. I'll not touch it, not a cent of it.

The wife: And when the sham comes round for the club money Friday, tell him we're after bein' caught short this week, he can call again the next.

The husband: He won't be delighted, that fella.

The wife: He'll do rightly, sure God between us and harm, we'll square him after. Twenty over the nine days, that's what, itself, not even three the day, and that's bread and milk and the pot stretched thin.

The husband: The gruber below the road might have a start for me Wednesday. A few euro in me hand before the week's out, please God.

The wife: Please God is right. That'd see us right, that would, and we'll get to the turn of it yet.
