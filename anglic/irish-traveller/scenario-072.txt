Me: Right, what about half seven below at the site, top of the tober by the big kena?

My friend: Half seven's no good at all, sham, the young fella's after taking the car below for the tyre and he won't be back till near eight.

Me: Eight so. Did ye ask round who's coming anyway?

My friend: I did. The sham's brother's game, and the sister above said she'd come once she's back from the mission.

Me: What about the uncle, the one with the sulky?

My friend: Ah he's after telling me no, he's gruber till late, some job for a man beyond in Galway, won't be back before ten itself.

Me: Sure that can't be helped. And the two lackeens that were below yesterday?

My friend: They're coming, and God bless her, their mother said she might look in herself for a while and all.

Me: Right so, eight's shot with the young fella not back. Half eight?

My friend: Half eight's gammy as well, the mother has the dinner going that hour and she won't have it moved for man nor beast.

Me: Quarter to nine so, will that suit ye?

My friend: Ah go on, quarter to nine'll do rightly. Sure nobody's ever there on the dot anyway.

Me: I'll bug the word round to the rest of them so, so it's not left on yourself alone.

My friend: Good man. Who have we now, counting it up?

Me: Yourself and me, the sham's brother, the sister, the two lackeens, and the mother maybe if she's able. That's six so, seven if she comes.

My friend: Six or seven so, that'll do rightly. Tell them quarter to nine, top of the tober, and not a minute after or we'll be standing there gloking at each other for nothing.

Me: Will do, sham. God bless.
