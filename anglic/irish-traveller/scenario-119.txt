Sit down there now and I'll give you the whole of it, because you weren't there for a second of it, and it's the best telling of a bad Tuesday you'll hear all year, God bless the mark.

It started over nothing at all. We were sat out the back of the trailers after the trotting, half the site skimished already off a naggin some cousin had going, and the cards come out the way they always do of an evening when there's nothing on the telly and the light's still good. Meself and the sham beside me, a great feen for the cards he was, we'd a few games of twenty-five going for nothing, just for the crack, and I don't know which one of us said it first, but one of us says, sure we might as well put something on it so. And I went in easy about it, sure I was flying that evening, cards falling for me every hand, but the sham wasn't half as sure of himself and you could see it on him.

Well the minute that's said the whole crowd standing round the wall has an opinion on it. Someone shouts up a tenner, another one says ah go on, twenty, don't be soft on it, and the sham looks over at me and I look at him and neither of us wanting to be the one backs down in front of the whole wall. So it settled there. Twenty euro, small enough it wouldn't sting either of us and small enough we both said aye to it easy, and that was the deal, plain as that, agreed before a single hand was played, and every soul standing round heard it so there was no getting out of it after.

Back and forth the two of us for a good while, neither one able to pull clear, and you'd swear it could go on the whole night the way it was tipping. But then it turned. It turned hard and it turned sudden, and I knew before that last hand was even played out that I was bet. You feel it in your own hand, a gammy run of cards that's not coming right no matter how you play it, and there's nothing to be done only sit there and watch it happen to you in front of the whole site looking on.

And when it was over, over proper, no arguing the toss on it, I threw the cards down and I groaned like a man after losing a cob at the fair, and I says, fair enough, ye have me, I'm bet fair and square.

Now here's the turn in it. I go for my pocket, ready to bug him his twenty and be shut of it quiet, and the eldest standing there says no. And I'm looking round and the whole crowd's grinning at me like they'd it planned from the very start of it, and the eldest says, no money's changing hands over this, you're going to do a performance for it instead, in front of the whole site, and we'll tell you exactly what's in it so there's no cutting corners on us.

And they did. God, they laid it out like a solicitor reading a will off you. I was to get up on the roof of the car, the good car mind, not the banger, up against the wall where the whole site could see me proper. I tried to talk them down out of it, I'll admit that much to you now. I says, look, I'll bug ye the twenty and we'll say no more about it, sure isn't that easier on the whole of us.
