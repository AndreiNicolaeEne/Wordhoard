Me: God, I'm foundered with this roof of mine, it's leakin something shockin, and it's all over the one corner above the childer's bed. The plaster's gone soft as a wet sponge and last night didn't a big lump of it come down on top of the blankets.

Me: I rang the landlord back after Easter about it, first time, and he says to me, "I'll send a man out to look at it," so I says grand, no bother, and sure nothing come of that at all.

Me: I'm after askin him twice more since then, ringin him meself, and it's the very same aul answer comes back out of him every time. "I'll send a man." No man ever seen, no ladder ever put up, and the water still runnin down behind the press.

The friend: And how long is that goin on you now, all told?

Me: Four months if it's a day. Since before the summer.

The friend: Four months, and never a date put on nothin, nor a word in writing?

Me: Not a word. Just "I'll sort it" out of him, and that's as much good as nothin.

The friend: Right, well look, ya have a few roads ya could take. Ya could write it down to him, everything, the date, what he said, and keep a copy for yourself, so the sham can't turn round and say he never heard a thing about it. Or ya could ring the council or the RTB, only that can drag on ya for months and he'd have the head reared at ya over it. Or ya could hold a bit back off the rent till it's done, but that's a gammy aul game to play, he could come lookin for the lot of it off ya after.

The friend: I had one the very same, God rest the days I put up with him, sendin no man near it, and didn't the whole ceilin come in on me over the fire in the end. Cost me far more than if he'd have fixed it the first day I asked.

The friend: Write to him first so, keep your copy, and tell him straight ya'll be onto the RTB if it's not seen to inside the fortnight. That'll put manners on him quicker than any phone call.

Me: I will so, sure. What else is there only to try it.
