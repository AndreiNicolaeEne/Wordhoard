Me: Before we're all skimished and no use to man nor beast, we better sort the morning now while it's in me head. The drive below in Oranmore, what time are we starting on it?

The brother-in-law: Half eight, sure, nothing's ever ready before that anyway.

Me: Half eight me eye. We'll be tarring in the dark at that rate.

The eldest: Seven, and that's the end of it, I'm after saying it three times already and ye all nodding and forgetting.

The young fella: Seven so. Who's solking the mixer down there?

The brother-in-law: I'll bring the mixer on the trailer, it's loaded already, God bless the man that thought of that last night and not this morning.

The eldest: I'll do the tar meself. The sham there can do the barrow, and yourself can do the edges.

The young fella: The edges. Sure that's the worst gruber of the whole job, ye have me on me knees the whole day while the rest of ye's standing round gloking at me with the shovels.

Me: Someone has to do it and you're the youngest below, God help ye.

The eldest: There's a worry on me about the tar regardless, we're that low on it, and if the weather turns on us we're finished before we're started.

The brother-in-law: They have it dry till evening please God, we'll be grand once the gred stretches to another load.

Me: Right, is everyone able for the morning? Any word from the cousin?

The eldest: The cousin's not able at all, he's after the hospital with the young one for the check-up.

Me: That's the four of us so, without him and his digger.

The young fella: Which means I'm still stuck below in that hole on me own, that's twice running now.

The eldest: Twice is right, and don't be starting on it, I'll stand ye the pint tonight and we'll call it even.

The brother-in-law: Ye owe him more than a pint at this stage.

Me: Seven o'clock, all of ye, no one late or we'll lose the whole morning to it. God bless now, I'm away to the kena.
