Sit down there a minute till I tell you the whole of it, because you weren't within an ass's roar of it and you'd want to hear how it went.

We were all above at the mother's for the dinner, a grand spread of it, so we were, the bit of bacon and cabbage first and the spuds after that, and then the apple tart to finish, and everyone well fed and easy in themselves by the time the tay was wet. The whole crowd of us round that table, the mother and the father, the aunt and the uncle, the eldest above beside the window, the young ones running in and out under everyone's feet, and every last one of us with the belt loosened a notch and the guard well down, the way you do be after a good feed among your own.

Now there's one thing at that table, and there's been one thing at that table for years, that nobody in that family ever raises. Nobody ever stood up and said it outright, mind you, no one ever said "we won't be discussing that above at the table," only every last one of us has felt what it does when it's let out, and it's known the same as if it was written up on the wall of the trailer itself: you don't go there. You just don't.

So we're sitting there, easy as you like, tay going round, and someone, and God knows they meant nothing by it, it wasn't even said to start anything, only a thing said in passing, the way you'd say the tay's gone cold or the weather's turned gammy, someone brought it up. Sideways like. Not even looking at anyone when it was said.

And the table went quiet. Not a big quiet, only the one second of it, but you'd have felt it drop, the whole lot of us knowing before a word of argument was even out what was coming, the same as you'd know rain was coming off the smell of the air.

The aunt couldn't let it go by her. She come in mild enough at the start, only testing the ground, like.

The aunt: Sure now, there's no need to be bringing that up again, is there. Not at the dinner.

But the uncle, and he was a small bit skimished already, mind, a few pints in him before he ever sat down, wasn't having it left at that, and he come back at her hard, and it went up from there quick enough, the two voices climbing over each other.

The uncle: I'll say what I like about it, so I will. It's the truth of it and well ye all know it's the truth.

The aunt: Sure you don't even understand the first thing about it, so you don't. You never did, and you never will.

The uncle: I don't understand it, is it. I understand it better than any of ye ever will, and I'll not be told different by you nor anyone else at this table.

No ground given in it at all, not an inch.

The eldest tried then to pull it somewhere else altogether, God love him, reaching for anything at all that might do.

The eldest: Did anyone hear about the sulky trotting above at the green last Sunday, the young fella's cob took some class of a fit and near went through the rope on him...

But sure it didn't take. It never does. The whole table's ears were only half on him and the rest of them dragged straight back to the row, the way water finds the low ground no matter what you put in front of it.

And then the old one got dragged into it, the way it always does, someone throwing back to the last time this same thing come up, above at some other dinner years before this one.

The uncle: This is the very same as the last time, the very same, and look how that one ended and all.

The aunt: Don't be bringing that up on top of it now as well.

Only they both knew rightly it proved the very same thing to the pair of them, each one dead certain it proved them right and the other one wrong, the way an old row never really dies on ye, it only goes off to sleep a while and waits.

The mother tried the direct way then, God bless her, saying it plain out.

The mother: Didn't we say, the whole of us, that we wouldn't be bringing this up at this table, of all places. Haven't we said it a hundred times.

But sure that didn't stop it either. The row went on straight over the top of her, the same as if she hadn't said a word at all, the two of them well past hearing anyone else by then.

The uncle went to misli off out of it altogether, only he didn't get that far, he pushed his chair back off the table, not storming out of the place, mind, only putting a bit of ground between himself and the rest of them, over to the settle by the window with his arms folded, done with it, letting the rest of them at it if they wanted, but not himself, not anymore.

And after that there come down a class of a silence over what was left of the dinner, the whole feed gone gammy on us in the one minute, the knives and forks going against the plates far too loud in it, everyone eating away and looking at nothing much, the way you do when you'd sooner be looking at your own plate than at anyone's face across from you.

It was the mother again after a bit, God love her, that tried to smooth the whole of it over, saying something about was there enough of the tart left for seconds, or some bit of news about a cousin's wedding above in Galway, and it worked, a small bit, the odd word passed here and there again, someone even laughing once. But the table never got back to how easy it was before it. You'd have felt the difference in it the whole rest of the night, everyone that small bit more careful with themselves than they were an hour before.

And that was the end of it, near enough. No one said sorry, not in so many words, no one ever does, sure. The whole family only went quiet on it the way they always do, the lot of them agreeing without a word passing between them that it wouldn't be brought up again. And amn't I only after telling you, every one of us sitting there knew rightly, in the back of our heads, that it will be brought up again all the same, some other dinner, some other table, sooner or later, God help us. It always is. It always will be. And we'll all sit there again and say nothing about it beforehand, the same as we said nothing about it this time, and it'll happen anyway, the very same as it happened tonight.
