It was early afternoon, after the dinner, when I seen the smoke first, away off beyond the far field, only a thin streak of it going up soft into the sky. I says nothing much of it to meself, sure you'd see that itself plenty a summer and it burn out over the other side and never come near ye at all. We were sat out at the kena having the tea and I was only half minding it.

But God love us, within the hour the wind was after turning and it come on hard, and that streak was thickened into a great black roll tumbling over itself, closer than I'd have thought any fire could travel in that time. The smell hit first, then the sting of it in the eyes, and the eldest come out to me.

The eldest: Are ye not seeing that? That's not stopping where it is.

Me: I'm looking at it now, aye. It's after coming on fast on us.

And that was the minute the watching stopped being watching. We were up in a flash, solking only what mattered and leaving the rest sit, no time going into anything else. Twenty minutes, maybe less, and we were misli down the tober, the van loaded, the dog up on the seat, and nothing behind us only smoke.

The throat of me went raw off it, God, like I'd swallowed the fire itself, and the heat was pressing on the back of my neck the whole way down that road, so it was. I looked back the once, only the once, and the whole sky behind us was lit up gammy orange, the site glowing like it was after dark already though it wasn't near it.

Two nights we were away, stopping wherever we could get a bit of ground, not a word out of anyone about what state we'd find it in, only the waiting and the not knowing, and the mother saying her prayers over it morning and night.

And when we come back, God bless it and keep it, the place was spared entire. Not a stick of it touched. We said our thanks for it and got the tea on.
