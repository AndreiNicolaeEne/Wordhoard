The leccy was only after going on us when the dark was coming down proper, no warning given at all, one minute the telly going grand and the next the whole kena black as the inside of a cow. The card was after running out, gammy old article that it is, and not a one of us had it topped up, sure who'd think of that of a Tuesday evening. Gather said it was the young fella left it below in his jacket and never told nobody, and the young fella swore blind he never touched it, and the two of them went on at each other in the dark a good while till the mother roared at them to give it over and go find a match.

We were below in presses and drawers a good five minutes, gloking round with the phone light near dead itself, before anyone solked up so much as a candle. Turned out there was but the one stub left from the missions candle above on the shelf, no bigger than a finger, and that had to do the whole lot of us, passed hand to hand round the table, God help us.

The mother: Will ye quit knocking into that press, the pair of ye.

The eldest: Sure I can't see me hand in front of me face, how am I not to knock into it.

She banged her shin off the same press three times going for the kettle, cursing blue murder every time, till she gave it up altogether and sat down rubbing her leg.

Gather: Leave it be till the morning. There's no fixing nothing in the dark.

So we all drew in round the one small light, close as chicks under a hen, telling stories and slagging one another, the little sublik falling asleep against the mother's arm before the candle was half gone.

The leccy never came back that night. We went off to our beds in the pure dark, the whole household, please God it'd be back to us come morning, and glad enough of the sleep after the day that was in it.
