Two year and a bit I was above in that canteen, the hospital one, dishing out the dinners and doing the trays for the wards after, and I'll tell ye now the whole of it, start to finish, what was said and what I meant by it, because they're two different animals half the time.

I started there the back end of one year and I'm after leaving it the start of this one, so call it two year and three month if ye want it exact. Steady enough work. Early starts, but I never minded an early start, sure my gather had us up with the birds our whole life, gruber's gruber he'd say, doesn't matter what shape it comes in. I did me shifts, I did me overtime when they asked, I never once rang in with a story that wasn't true. And still.

It was never one big thing. That's the part nobody believes when ye tell them after. It was the bag checked at the door every single shift when the rest of them walked through waving, it was me moniker never once said right though I told the woman on the desk three times, it was the little laugh some of them give when the word Traveller comes up on the telly in the staffroom and then all going quiet when they remember I'm sat there with me tea. Small things. A body can carry small things a long while.

The last of it was the roster. There was a removal below in the family, and I'm after asking two week in advance, plenty notice, could I swap me Thursday, and the manager looks at the sheet and says no, that's that, in front of the whole canteen, when she's swapped shifts for half the ward for less. That was it. That was the one that finished it. Small on its own, but it was sitting on top of a big pile, and the whole pile went over.

I rehearsed it for a week before I said a word to anyone above. Said it out loud in the kena to meself doing the dishes, said it to the mother on the phone, said it to me fella in the van on the way to work of a morning.

Me: I'm going to tell her it's not about any one thing, that it's just time for a change, that I've loved me time here and I'm very grateful for the opportunity.

The mother: God bless you and your grateful. Tell her the truth of it.

Me: I can't be telling her the truth of it. I have to work me notice.

I had it polished like a good boot. Every word sanded down so there'd be nothing sharp in it for anyone to catch a finger on.

So I asked would I have a minute with the manager, and she said of course, come into the office, and I sat down across from her with me hands folded like I was above at Mass, and I said the line I'd been saying to the dishes all week.

Me: I wanted to have a chat about me position here. It's not about any one thing in particular, I just feel it's time for a change for meself.

And that was a lie and it wasn't, both together. It was about one thing in particular, the roster and the removal and the not being let go to me own, but it was about a hundred small things stacked up behind that one thing too, and I couldn't be saying all hundred of them sitting in that office with the tea going cold between us. I held back the real reason because there's no future in telling a person to their face that ye've felt like a stranger in their canteen for two year running, when ye still have two weeks left to work under them and ye'd sooner leave with your name clean than your chest emptied.

The manager was surprised, I'll say that for her, or she put on a good show of it.

The manager: Ah now, don't be rushing into anything. Is it the money? We could look at the money.

Me: It's not the money, no.

The manager: We could shift you off the early roster if that's what's wearing you. Give you a later start, four days instead of five.

Me: I do appreciate that, I do, but me mind's made up. It's just time, that's all it is.

I gave her the polished reason, time for a change, a person needs a change, and she nodded along like she believed every word of it, and maybe she did, and I sat there thinking, ye offered me the shift a fortnight after ye wouldn't give me the one Thursday, and isn't that gas.

We agreed the two week, standard notice, nothing more said about it, and I walked out of that office delighted with meself, walking on air near, thinking I'd done the whole thing right and dignified. And then that night lying in the bed I'm after getting the doubt on me something fierce. Two year of steady money for the sake of one bad roster and a manager who couldn't say me name proper. Was I after making a right eejit of meself. Please God I hadn't.

The two weeks went the way two weeks go, quick when ye don't want them to be and slow when ye do. The last day itself was quiet enough. A few of the girls got me a card, and there was a bit of cake in the staffroom at the break, and everyone said the usual.

A workmate: Sure we'll miss you above, you were great craic on a Saturday shift.

Another workmate: Keep in touch now, don't be a stranger.

Half of it was said the way ye'd say God bless you to a sneeze, out of habit and nothing under it. But there was one moment, packing up me locker, one of the older women who'd always been decent to me, no more, no less, just decent, she come over and she near said the real thing.

The older workmate: I know it wasn't just the roster, love.

Me: Ah sure.

The older workmate: I know. Mind yourself.

And that was as close as anyone come to saying it out loud, and as close as I come to saying it back. I gave her a gop on the cheek going out the door and that was that.

It's a few month now since, and I'll tell ye straight, it's more relief than regret, by a good stretch. I sleep easier of a morning not dreading the bag check. I've picked up bits of work here and there since, cleaning for a woman above in the town, a few shifts doing the school dinners, nothing as steady yet but nothing eating at me either. There's days I do think of the money, regular and reliable, and I think of the girls I got on well with, and I miss the two or three of them fair enough. But I don't miss the rest of it, the little looks, the checked bag, the moniker never right.

If I'd that conversation to have all over again, I'd say less of the polished line and more of the truth, I think. Not to cause a row, God between us and all harm, I'm not one for rows. But there's a cost to swallowing it all down every day for two year for the sake of being thought grateful and easy going, and I'd tell any young one starting out, sublik or lackeen, say the true thing sooner, because the polite version only spares the other person's feelings, and sure why would that be your job in the first place.
