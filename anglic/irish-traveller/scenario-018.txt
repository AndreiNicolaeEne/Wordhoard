Right, so ye want it start to finish, the whole of it, and sure amn't I after telling it enough times over the years that it comes out the same track every time now, worn smooth as an aul road. Herself is sitting there beside me only smiling away to herself, leaving me to it tonight, so I will.

It was no grand thing at all, the meeting of us, whatever ye have built it up to be in your heads. It was out at the trotting of a Sunday, my own gather's cob running in the open race and me standing at the rail with a sham off our site, the two of us more interested in who was about than what horse was ahead of what. There was a beoir standing down a bit with her sisters, a fine-looking lackeen in a red coat, and I'll tell ye now I was gloking over at her that much the cob near ran out from under me for the want of me watching him at all.

I went down after the race, full of myself, and said something to her about the cob.

Me: That's a fine animal you have there.

The wife: He is a fine animal. Pity his driver's no use to him at all.

Me: And that was that. Thirty years on and I still haven't lived that one down. I only have to say the word "driver" to her at a wedding and she's away laughing on me.

That line has followed me the whole road since, and I'll tell ye, she was well able to knock the wind out of a fella from the very off.

Now I won't lie to ye, she wasn't sure of me at all in the beginning, and there was good reason in it, no harm to her for it. There'd been bad blood between the two families going back years, over a horse deal that went wrong between her gather and mine, the kind of thing that sits in a family a long time and doesn't shift easy at all. She had it drummed into her not to be looking twice at anyone off our site, and for a good while she wasn't. What turned it was nothing romantic in the slightest, their van broke down on the tober out past the town one wet night, and I came on them stuck there and stopped and put it right for them, no gred looked for and none offered. Her gather didn't say much to me that night, but he said enough after, and it started to soften from there.

The courting of it properly started when I bugged her to a cousin's wedding above in Dublin, the first time we were ever out together that wasn't only by chance. And the trouble in it was the distance more than anything, she was beyond in Galway with her own crowd and I was up and down the country half the year on the tarring work, and her gather, God bless him, still hadn't come the whole way round on account of the old business. It took me months even to get a gop off her, let alone anything more, and she'll tell ye herself she made me earn every inch of it. There were Sundays I'd misli the length of the country for the guts of an hour standing at a wall talking to her, with the shades moving us on twice before the evening was out, and turn round and drive back the same night. We got past it slow, not easy, letters, the phone when there was a phone to be had, and me showing up steady enough for long enough that her gather couldn't say a gammy word against me no more. He stood with me at a wake not long after that and that was the real turning of it. After that there was no more talk of the old business.

There's a cardigan of hers I borried off her the night of that wedding, for the walk back in the cold, and I never gave it back to this day, it's above in the kena of ours still, in the press, and every anniversary she asks me have I the cardigan yet and I tell her I have, and that's the two of us settled for another year. Most of ye know that one already.

The night I knew it had turned serious was the night I brought her back to my own mother's trailer for her tea, and my mother took one look and says to me after, when herself had gone back out to the van...

My mother: You needn't be telling me anything. I have eyes in my head.

Me: And within the week it was all round both sites, the way these things do be, before I'd said a word to a soul myself. Her own mother heard it off a woman at the mission before I'd the nerve to say it to her face at all.

The asking itself was no big production, whatever ye're expecting. It was outside her mother's trailer of an evening, the two of us leaning against the wall of the site, and I hadn't a ring bought nor a plan made, only the words came out of me plain, will you marry me, and she said she would, and that was the whole of the romance of it, God help me. About a year after that we were married, near enough to the day.

The families took it well enough in the end, though it took its time getting there, more on account of the old business than anything against her or me. Both gathers stood up together at the wedding and had a drink and shook hands on it, and whatever was between them before, it was let go that day, please God for good.

The one thing that comes back to me from the wedding day itself, and no photograph ever caught it, was a young cousin's cob getting loose off the sulky below at the site during the do and trotting straight up through the middle of it, in among the tables, with half the men in their good suits after it and roaring, and herself standing there in the dress laughing so hard she'd to hold onto the wall to stand up. That's the picture I have of it in my head, not the posed ones at all.

We've had our stretch since, same as anyone does, a hard patch of illness in the family a few years back that near broke the both of us. What carried us through it was each other, plain and simple, and the two families rallying round the way they do, and prayer, and God between us and all harm, we're after coming through the far side of it together, and stronger for it please God.

And I'll tell ye the one thing that hasn't changed one bit since that day at the rail, she still has the same head on her, still able to bring me down a peg with one line quicker than any feen alive, and I love her for it the very same as I did that day, not one bit different.

So. Ye have the whole of it now. Raise them up, the lot of ye, and we'll drink to her, and to the two of us, and God bless the room and everyone in it.
