The wife: God bless, you're a state. Sit down there and tell me how it went.

Me: I will, only give us a minute, I'm only after sitting down and me back still going. Started before ever I felt ready for it, I'll tell ye that much, six o'clock and the tea barely in me when the sham was banging the side of the van for us to be misling out the tober. No proper breakfast, only the one cup, and straight to the digging.

The digging out was the first job, taking up the old path before a scrap of tar went near it, and that set the pace on the whole day, for once you're down on a shovel at seven in the morning your back knows rightly what's coming and it doesn't let ye forget it. Wasn't the height of an hour in and the hands were roaring at me, blisters coming through the gloves same as ever, and the back not far behind them, gone gammy from the first barrow-load near.

We got the break about half nine, tea out of a flask standing on the wall, and God knows it was barely enough only to draw the breath before we were up again.

Then the afternoon. Two till four was the hard stretch of it, the sun beating and the tar steaming off the ground and not a stir of wind, and I'll tell ye now I thought more than the once about packing the tools in and telling the man of the house I'd finish it another day. But ye don't. Ye stay with it.

One good bit though, the edging round the gate we thought would take the guts of an hour was done in twenty minutes flat, the two of us moving well together, and that was a small lift in it, God bless it.

Forty was owed me for the day and forty I got, no messing, straight into me hand.

Tomorrow's another the same if the weather holds. If it doesn't, please God, I'll get the day to lie the back down.
