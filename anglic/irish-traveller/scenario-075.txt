Never thought I'd clap eyes on you again, not in this place of all places. Above at the cousins' near the fair, ducked into the shop for a drop of milk and there she is at the till in front of me, the beoir I ran the roads with when we were nothing but sublik and lackeen the pair of us, dragging our mothers mad.

Me: Musha, God bless us and save us, is that yourself? I don't believe it. How long is it now, sure it's over ten year if it's a day.

My friend: More than that I'd say, more like twelve. Sure the last time I seen you, you were only after leaving the school below.

Me: Twelve year, God almighty, and look at the state of us now. I'm married this while, three young ones on me, the eldest gone eight already. Himself does a bit of tarmac, fierce little gred in it these days but it keeps us going, and I do the door-to-door when I can get out. Yourself, how's things gone with you?

My friend: I'm below in Galway now, married into a big family there. Two young lads and a lackeen only after arriving in the spring, God bless her. Himself's at the scrap, does well enough out of it, thanks be to God.

Me: D'ye remember the time we robbed the gaffer's sulky above at the green and near put it in the ditch, the two of us laughing so hard we couldn't misli a step, doubled over on the tober home?

My friend: Ah stop, I'm never after forgetting that. Banjaxed we were, the state of us, and the shades after coming round asking who done it.

Me: D'ye ever hear tell of the sham that was always with us, the one from the next trailer beside ours?

My friend: Not a whisper, God knows where he's gone to. Hope he's keeping well wherever he is, whatever kena he's landed in.

Me: Give us your number there so, and I'll not let it go this time like the last time. I'll ring you so I will, please God.

My friend: Ye'd want to and all. Mind yourself now, God bless.
