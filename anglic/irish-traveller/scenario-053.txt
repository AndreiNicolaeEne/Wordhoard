My friend, listen till I tell ye about the time I was above in the office beyond, the paper I wanted was the passport for the cob, the stamp off of them so as I could bring him below to the fair. The fair was on for the morning after, and no stamp, no sale, so it had to be got that same day itself, no way round it, and I after telling the feen behind the counter three times over why it couldn't wait.

He takes the form off me and has me put me moniker to the bottom of it, and then starts flicking through the rest of the papers slower than ever he need, and says he, "These things do take a while now, unless a body's in a great hurry, of course." Just hung it there in the air like that, looking at me over the top of the glasses.

I gave him back nothing only the gloke, the blank one, and says I, "Sure I've the whole day given to it, take yer time, no bother on me at all."

He tried it again after a small while, plainer. "There's them that gets it done quicker, if ye follow me, a small consideration, like." And a gammy oul smile with it.

Me: Is there a fee on the notice above on the wall for that? There isn't. Sure I'll come back tomorrow so, and I'll bring the sham with me that does be dealing with ye lot every week of the year.

The one behind the counter: Ah now. Sit down there a minute, there's no need for all that.

And God, the quiet that sat on us then. I couldn't tell ye would he throw the form back at me or make me sit the whole day out for spite. But didn't he stamp it after all, not a bob out of me, only two hour lost on a hard chair.

And that's the whole of it, a small man with a stamp in his hand thinking he's the president of Ireland, only wanting a few bob of gred bugged into his hand for doing the job he's paid to do anyway. Ye don't hand it over, ye just sit there quiet and let the silence be his to sit in. God bless us and save us from the whole crowd of them.
