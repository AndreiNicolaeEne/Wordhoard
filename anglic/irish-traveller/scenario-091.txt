The aunt, God love her, was on the tober the guts of a full day getting to us, a boat and three buses and a car after that again, and she landed in the door with barely the time to wash her face before the kena was filled up round her. I don't know to this day how she wasn't dead on her feet, but she never solked a seat the whole day, not properly, and never once passed a word of complaint about the journey she'd made.

Partway through it all the mother went. The eldest: "He's finished with the tober now, and God knows he walked enough of it for all of us."

That's the line, love. That's the one going round every mouth on the site since, and I'd say it'll be said long after the rest of us are gone too.

The walk to the resting place felt longer than I ever remember it feeling, and I've walked that tober more times than I could count for one reason or another over the years.
