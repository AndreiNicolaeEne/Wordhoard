Now this here's the tober in, see the way it opens out past the shades' hut, that's where ye'd leave a trailer if ye've one, nose out towards the road, never blocking the turn for an ambulance nor nothing, God forbid the need ever come. But never, and I mean never, leave a jeep or a cart sitting up against the wall past the last kena on the left, even if it's standing there empty as a church of a Tuesday. That ground's spoken for, has been long before I was ever born, and nobody puts so much as a wheelbarrow on it without asking first, and asking gets ye a no more often than not.

See where the grass goes a bit longer, past the third trailer along, that's the edge of the old fella's bit. Nobody misles onto it, not the childer chasing a ball after them, not a soul. There's no fence, there's no sign, ye'd never know it to gloke at it, but everyone living here has it in their bones and that's the end of it.

Quiet's kept from about ten of a night till the men are stirring for the yard, no radios, no dogs left out barking, no engines turning over for the sake of it. Nobody's ever told to keep it. They just do, and God help the one that doesn't.

Passing on the street, the elders get the nod first, always, then whoever's carrying a child, then the rest of us, a "God bless" or a nod does the job, ye're not made stop and talk unless ye want to.

Borrowing's easy going, a sham'll bug ye a hammer or a drop of gred if ye're stuck, no bother, no writing it down. The mother above is after lending her twin-tub out three times this month and not a word out of her. But if what's borrowed doesn't come back, that's it, quiet as ye like, ye're not asked twice for anything, ye're just never asked again after.

And the one thing everyone breaks sooner or later, the dog off the lead when it shouldn't be, and not a soul, ever, says a word about it.
