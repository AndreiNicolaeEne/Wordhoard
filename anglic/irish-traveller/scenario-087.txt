The one at the counter: Well, how'd ye get on with the cob, I heard he wasn't great.

Me: Ah, a fright of a night I'm telling ye. He was off his feed since the evening, gammy on the one leg and not misli-ing at all, stood in the one spot below the trailer like he was nailed to it. I said to meself I'm not leaving that below on his own tonight, whatever else. Stopped out with him from the first hour, gloking at him every few minutes for any change, and sure there was none, not a thing, for the longest while.

I tried to get him to solk a drop of water, holding the bucket up under his nose meself, coaxing him the best I could, for he wouldn't go near it on his own at all.

Round the middle of the night he took an awful turn on me, went down proper, and I nearly lost the head altogether. Saying to meself, are ye after doing wrong keeping him here, should ye have bugged the vet out hours before it come to this. But I stopped with him anyway, threw the auld rug over him to keep the heat in him, and I was talking to him low the whole time, the way ye'd talk to anything frightened, God help him.

Near the dawn I seen the first small sign of him, the breathing after easing a small bit, and the ear going up at a noise the way it always would, and I said thank God, thank God for that itself.

By the morning he was steadier. Not himself yet, but steadier. And it was only then, once I was sure of him, that the tiredness came down on me all in the one go, like I'd been hit with something.

The one at the counter: God bless him, he'll do now please God.

Me: He will please God. I'm after being up the whole night with him and not a wink, but sure what harm, he's worth it and more.
