Eleven days that cold held over the site, no let-up in it at all, and I'm after telling the whole of it now for there's not one of us will forget it in a hurry, please God we never see its like again.

It was gammy from the very first morning, colder than any of us reckoned. The frost was thick as your thumb on the trailer windows before the light come up, and we'd the stove lit and never let it go out, eleven days and eleven nights of minding it turn about, the mother taking the middle of the night and myself the last watch before the day broke.

Nobody could misli far from the door itself, the tober outside glassed over solid, and any sham that tried it went near over on the ice. Had to solk more turf in off the trailer than we'd ever solked before, and still it wasn't heat enough some nights.

The standpipe went hard on the third day, froze solid where it never froze before in all the years the site's stood, and we were after melting snow in a pot for the tea and the washing both.

The eldest sat in by the stove of an evening and gloked at the two of us shivering, and said his piece.

The eldest: I mind a cold below in the sixties would skin ye, but this one's bet it clean, God bless us and save us, I never known the like in all my days.

The worst of it come the ninth night, the ground ringing like delph underfoot and not a beoir nor a feen stirring past the door. We lost the old dog below in the shelter that night, and there's no bringing a body back once the cold's got that deep set in it.

Then after the eleven days the thaw come soft and grey of a sudden, dripping off the trailers and running in little streams down past the kena, and I stood out in it and let it wet me through, near crying with the relief of it, thank God and his Blessed Mother it broke at last.
