The eldest: I heard the sham with the grey cob, that the shades lifted him off the tober Tuesday night, skimished he was, langered drunk on the sulky, and the beoir left standing there with it wrecked and him hauled below to the barracks.

The mother: Stop there now. That's not what happened, not even close.

The eldest: Sure that's the way it came to me.

The mother: Well it came to ye wrong then. There wasn't a shade glokin' near him at all. The cob spooked below at the bridge, a bag blowing across the tober or some such, and the sulky went over on the turn. He's after breaking the leg bad, gammy from the knee down, and he's lying in the hospital in Galway this whole week, God help him. He wasn't skimished, not a drop taken, his own gather was above at the kena with him not an hour before it happened, and he'll tell ye the same.

The eldest: God, that's a whole different story to the one I have.

The mother: It is surely. By the time it reached ye, that poor man went from an accident with a frightened animal to a criminal in the cells. That's some distance for a story to drift, and no truth left in it at all.

The eldest: I'm ashamed of meself now, saying it around the way I did. Where did I even hear it, was it the workmate told me?

The mother: Ask yerself that. Who told her?

The eldest: She got it off one of the young lackeens above at the other site, I'd say, or the fella that does be at the scrap.

The mother: There's yer answer, embellishing it along the way it went, one to the next. Well ye know the truth of it now, so mind ye tell it that way from here on, however far it travels, whoever asks ye.
