Me: I'm after thinking about that opening of yours again, that double corner ye went with, and I'm telling ye now same as I told ye then, if ye'd have gone a different road at the start the whole tober of the game changes on ye.

The cousin: Ah would ye stop. I stood by that move the day I made it, sitting there at the board, and I'll stand by it to ye now. It was the right move with what was in front of me that minute.

Me: Sure that's what ye'll always say, ye never budged off a move yet in your life, gammy or not.

The cousin: It wasn't gammy. Gloke at the early part of it if ye don't believe me. Neither of us gained an inch off the other for a good stretch, fair's fair between the two of us there.

Me: That bit's true enough, I'll give ye that much, it was even going. But then we come to the one in the middle, and ye know rightly the one I mean.

The cousin: I know well what ye mean without ye saying it.

Me: That was a plain mistake. Nothing complicated about it at all. Ye seen it wrong and the board made ye pay for it after.

The cousin: I did not see it wrong. With what I had sitting in front of me that minute, that was the sound move, the only move a feen would have played. Ye can't be judging a man off what come three moves later, that's not fair judging, that's judging with the answers already wrote down.

Me: I'm only telling ye what happened, sham, not what might have.

The cousin: And what about the exchange after that then, the costly one, the one that took the two men off me at once? That was your doing and not mine, ye pushed on when ye should have held where ye were.

Me: My doing, is it? Sure it was your play left the door open for it in the first place, I only solked what was sitting there to be solked. Bug me that, I never took nothing that wasn't offered.

The cousin: Ah here now, don't be talking.

Me: By the time we come round past that, the game was gone, sham, I could lay it out for ye move for move if ye want it. Lost from there on, plain to see.

The cousin: It was not lost. Not then. There was plenty of tober left in that game yet, plenty.

Me: There was a comeback tried after, I'll give ye that much credit at least.

The cousin: There was, and it near worked an' all, it near worked.

Me: Near worked. It come too late is what it did, God bless the mark. Ye were fighting when the fighting should have started three or four moves before ye ever started it.

The cousin: If I'd have played the move I had in mind that time, instead of the one I went with, the late one, that saves the whole game on me. I've gone over that one move more than any other move in the whole match, lying in the kena at night thinking on it.

Me: Maybe so, maybe so. But ye didn't play it, sham, and what actually happened after was the last move, and we both know well what that move was.

The cousin: We do. There's no argument between us on what it was.

Me: It's what it meant we're at odds over.

The cousin: Because it didn't mean what ye're after making it mean.

Me: Forty-three moves the whole thing ran to, and that much we're agreed on anyway, God help the two of us, one of the only things we are agreed on out of the whole match.

The cousin: One of the only things, aye, that's God's truth.

Me: And straight after it, sure, we shook hands and said no more about it. Good game, fair enough, that was the whole of what passed between us that evening.

The cousin: It was. Nothing more said.

Me: But going back over it now it's a different story altogether, isn't it. Now it's who had the stronger game the whole way through, never mind how it finished up on the board.

The cousin: I had the stronger game the whole way through and I'll say it to your face same as I'd say it to any feen's face.

Me: Ye would say that, sure, ye'd say the sky was gammy if it suited ye.

The cousin: Gloke at the match before that one an' all, the two of us, do ye not remember that. Same story that time too, the very same.

Me: That's a different match altogether, don't be dragging that one in on top of this one.

The cousin: It's the same pattern, sham, plain as the wall of the site there. Ye do the one thing every time we sit down, every single time.

Me: I do not. That's you looking for a pattern where there's none in it at all, because ye can't let a game lie without making a speech out of it after, God bless ye.

The cousin: I'm not conceding a point to ye on this, not one.

Me: Nor me to you, not one either.

The cousin: There was gred on that match an' all, don't forget, and I never seen a penny of it handed over right either, now that we're at it.

Me: Ah here, don't be starting that again, that's a whole other row for a whole other evening.

The cousin: It is, aye. Leave it so.

Me: Leave it so.

The cousin: Same time next week and we'll go through it again, I'd say.

Me: We will, sure, and we'll be no nearer agreeing then than we are this minute.

The cousin: No nearer at all at all. God bless it anyway.

Me: God bless it.
