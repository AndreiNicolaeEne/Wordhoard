My friend: Go on then, I'm not letting ye away with it, I heard ye above through the wall the other night, roaring at each other like two bulls in a field. What was that about at all?

Me: Ah God, don't be talking. It started over nothing, that's the worst of it. Himself was after being off above at the fair all day selling on a bit of a cob for a fella he knows, and he said he'd be back for the dinner at six and there wasn't a sight nor sound of him till near ten, and no ring, nothing. The dinner was destroyed on the plate, ruined altogether, and the young ones were asking where's their father and I hadn't a clue what to tell them.

My friend: So who started it, when he did land in?

Me: Me, I'll be honest with ye, I'm not going to sit here and make out I was the quiet one. The minute I heard the jeep pulling in I was above at the door before he even had the handle turned, and I let a roar out of me that'd wake the dead. I said is this the time of night to be coming home to your family, and not a word out of ye all day, do ye think I've nothing better to be doing than standing over a cooker for ye. And he wasn't slow answering me back, he had the drink on him a small bit, not skimished exactly but near it, and he roared back that a man can't have five minutes to himself without being roared at the second he's in the door, that I was never done at him.

My friend: And it went on from there.

Me: Oh it did, up and up, the two of us at the top of our voices, and sure the young ones went off into the room out of it, God love them. And I said a thing then I'd no business saying, I said I'd be as well off on me own altogether, that I don't know what I'm doing tied to a man that can't lift a phone. I didn't mean a word of it, sure I know that and he knows it too, but it come out of me in the heat of it and once it's out you can't be calling it back in.

My friend: Did he say anything back as bad?

Me: He did worse again, God forgive him. He brought up the money, the auld sore point that's better left lying, about the gred his mother put by for us years back for the good trailer, and how I'm the one that let it go on foolishness, and he knows rightly that's a thing I can't hear without going cold inside, and he said it just to cut me, the same as I said mine just to cut him. Neither of us fighting fair by then.

My friend: God. And then what, did something get broke?

Me: The door of the kena got the worst of it. I went to walk off out of the row altogether and he wasn't having that, he wanted the last word, and when he didn't get it he hauled the door shut behind him so hard the whole trailer shook, and the good delph on the shelf near went off onto the floor. Didn't break, thank God, but it near did, and I remember standing there looking at it shaking on the shelf thinking, that's the two of us all over now.

My friend: And after the door went?

Me: Silence. Dead silence. I didn't answer another word out of him the rest of that night, not a syllable, and he the same, the two of us lying there like two strangers in the one bed, backs to each other. And the whole next day we were like it, him out at the front of the site with the cob and I above in the kena with the young ones, and the pair of us going round each other all day without a word, the best part of a full day gone on it. It's an awful cold way to be, I don't recommend it to anyone.

My friend: Who broke first?

Me: He did, God love him, he's not able to hold out long, never was. It was near dark and he come in and stood in the door a minute and he says, quiet now, none of the roaring left in him, he says, I'm after ruining your dinner and your evening and I never even rang ye, and that's the God's truth of it, I've no excuse. That was it. Not a big speech, just the once sentence, but it was the right one.

My friend: What did ye say back to that?

Me: I told him I was sorry too, for the thing I said about being better off on me own, sure that was a cruel thing to throw at a man and I never meant a syllable of it, I said it only because I was raging and looking to hurt, and I was ashamed of meself the whole day thinking on it.

My friend: And he took that alright?

Me: He did. And then he called me a stór, first time in God knows how long he'd said it, and the minute that word come out of him I knew we were grand again, that's always the way it goes with us, the auld word comes back before anything else does. And he went and made the tea after, put it down in front of me the way I like it without me having to ask, and sat in beside me on the couch, and put his arm round me, and that was that, that was the whole thing sealed and done, no more needed saying after that.

My friend: Ye made it up quick enough in the end so.

Me: We did, thanks be to God, we always do, we're no good at the silence, either of us, it doesn't suit us. But here, don't be telling the whole site now that ye heard it through the wall.

My friend: Sure amn't I only delighted with meself, I got the whole picture and the two of ye never had to say a word to me straight out.

Me: That's the thing about that wall, it's thinner than the both of us ever gave it credit for. I said it to himself after, I said, ye do know the whole road had our business last night, and he only laughed and said well let them have it, sure it's better crack than the television. We do be laughing about it now every time we pass ye, ye've the two of us blushing.

My friend: God between us and all harm, ye're a right pair. But ye're grand now.

Me: We are, please God. Better than grand. There's worse ways to end up than the two of us sitting there with the tea gone cold again, only this time laughing about it instead.
