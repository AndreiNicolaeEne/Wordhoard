Sit down there so, and I'll give you the whole of it, but mind now, I'm only giving you my end. There's two ends to every fence and I'm not the one to be standing on the far side of it telling you what's over there. You asked me and I'll tell you, but you'll get no interrupting out of me either, so just sit and listen and you'll have it as it happened, or as near as I can carry it to you now with the years on it.

Years back, and I mean years, we were closer to that kena than to half our own crowd. God's truth. The mother above and myself, we were rared nearly on top of one another, in and out of each other's trailers morning noon and night, borrowing sugar, minding each other's babbies, sitting out the two of us of an evening with a mug of tea watching the young ones chase the dog round the wall of the site. If one of us was skint the other one had it covered till the week's end and no word said about it after. Her sham and my own were the same, out training the cobs together of a Sunday, the pair of them below at the field talking sulky trotting for hours, happy as you like. I'm not saying that for effect. I'm saying it so you'd know what was lost, because what came after didn't come out of nothing, it came out of something that was good once and that's the sorest part of it if you want the truth of me.

It started small, the way these things always do. Small enough you'd nearly laugh at it now if it hadn't grown the way it grew. The eldest young fella of theirs put up a bit of a block shed for the cob's tack and the hay, and fair enough, a man's entitled to shelter his gear, I'd never begrudge him that. But he put it hard up against the line, closer than it should've been by a good stretch, taking the light off our side and near blocking where we'd pull the trailer in and out. I said nothing for a good while. You don't want to be the one starting rows over a shed.

So I went over, civil as you like, and I said it to the mother of them straight, no edge on it at all. Says I, would he ever think about pulling that shed back a few foot, it's cutting off our light something shocking and we can hardly get the jeep round of an evening. And she said sure she'd mention it to him, no bother at all, wouldn't be a thing. And I left it at that, sure why wouldn't I, we were after being like sisters all them years.

Weeks went by and nothing moved, not an inch of it. I let it go again, said maybe it slipped her mind, sure isn't everyone busy. So I went back a second time, still polite, still soft about it, and got the same again, sure I'll say it to him, don't be worrying yourself. And still nothing. Third time I went, I won't lie to you, there was an edge on me by then and I couldn't keep it out of my voice, and I said it plain, that shed wants moving and I've asked twice already and I'm asking a third time now and I shouldn't have to.

Word came back to me after that, the way it does on a site, through the neighbor that carries the talk between the two doors, God love her, she means well but she's no messenger for accuracy I'll tell you that much. By the time it reached me it wasn't that I'd asked three times polite, it was that I'd gone over roaring at the woman in front of her own children, which I never did, never raised my voice to her once in all them years. That's the way a story travels on a tober like this one, it picks things up and drops things off and by the time it's back at your own door it's a different animal altogether.

And when I went to her about that, to say that's not what happened at all, she looked straight at me and said she never once had a request off me about any shed, that it was news to her, that I must be thinking of someone else's business entirely. Flat out denied the whole of it, the two visits before it, the lot. And that stung, I won't lie, because I know what I said and when I said it and she knows it too.

So I did a thing I'm not proud of, and I'll say it to you now because you asked for the truth of it. I stopped minding for her of an evening when she was above at the missions. Small thing. Petty, even. But it was mine to do and I did it quiet, no drama, just stopped.

And then the comment came back to me, through the same neighbor, that the mother of them had said I was always jealous of what they had, that I never had two bob to rub together and couldn't stand to see another crowd doing well for themselves. That one went in deep, deeper than the shed ever did, because gred was never what it was about, not one bit, and for her to say that after everything, that cut me worse than anything since.

I tried after that, God knows I tried, I brought a fry down to the kena one Sunday, eggs and rashers and the whole of it, soft as I could make the offer, just to say we needn't be like this. She took it at the door and said thanks very much and never asked me in, and that was the last bit of that.

The street split near enough down the middle after, half of them still saluting me grand as ever, the other half gone very cool, very quick to be over at her side of an evening instead of mine. You'd feel it walking the tober, who'd stop and who'd only nod.

It all came to a head one evening out where the whole block could hear it, the two of us going at it hip and thigh in the middle of the road, everyone's doors open, young ones and all standing gawking. Her sham skimished as it happens, which didn't help the temper of it none. There was accusing flying then too, that I'd solked a bit of harness off their shed that went missing, which I never touched in my life, and I told her that to her face and I'll tell you the same, I never laid a hand on anything belonging to that family, and she can say what she likes about it.

That's, what, going on nine years now since a word's passed between the two doors. Nine years of God-blessing each other stiff on the tober and nothing more than that. The children still play together above at the wall when the mothers aren't looking, and I let that be, I've no quarrel with the young ones. But of an evening, if I'm honest with you now, and I will be honest, because you asked me straight, I couldn't tell you for certain anymore whose fault the whole of it was in the end. I know my own part of it clean enough. What I don't know, God forgive me, is whether it was ever really about a shed at all.
