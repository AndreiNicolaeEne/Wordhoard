The first elder: This knee of mine, ah, it has refused since I retired from the railways. I'm busy telling you, cold season or hot season it doesn't care, it just sits there and holds me, tight tight, and even the doctor at the clinic just shook his head, is it, like there's nothing more to say.

The second elder: Eh, knee. You call that suffering? Try not sleeping properly, three, four nights every month, for years now. Not now-now, not just now, I mean this thing has been going on and on. I'm busy lying there at two in the morning staring at that ceiling like it owes me something.

The third elder: The two of you are still children in this matter. Me I have carried this back since before either of you even knew me, since I was young and still lifting sacks at the market. Slowly slowly it started, and now-now it has become the whole story of my life. You are counting nights. I am counting decades.

The first elder: Is it now. So we should just fold our hands and let you win, hey?

The second elder: Eish, who is winning anything here. Somebody just tell me plainly, who among us is worst.

The third elder: Nobody is worst, that's the truth of it, si. Tomorrow it'll be your knee finishing you worse than my back, and you'll come reminding me that I told you so.

The first elder: Shame, look how small we've become, three old people arguing over who is more finished, like pains are goats you can count at the market.

The second elder: We're not finished, we're only busy suffering, that's all, though this laughing has left my chest somehow, sindiyo?

The third elder: Sindiyo. Nobody is dying today, is it.

They laugh together, all three, the way it always ends, and nobody has agreed on anything.
