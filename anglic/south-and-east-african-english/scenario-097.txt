Me:
Let me just talk, my friend, you don't have to say anything back, sindiyo? Just now, before you arrived, I was sitting here thinking I should just tell you plainly how my year went, so let me do that.

Those first days, honestly, I don't even remember them properly. It was mostly numb, the whole thing. People coming, people going, phones ringing, someone's cousin arriving from upcountry needing tea, another one asking where the programme has been printed, an aunty on the phone saying she's coming now-now and then showing up three hours later. There was so much moving around that the grief itself did not even get a chance to sit with me. You are busy receiving people, busy signing for this, busy telling the same story of how it happened to every new face at the door, and somehow in all that busy-ness there was no time left over to actually feel the thing that had happened to you. People would tell me things like "you are being so strong" and I would just say, is it, because I did not feel strong at all, I felt nothing, and there was no room to feel nothing either. That is how those first days were, hey.

Then everyone finally went home. That is the one I want you to hear properly, because nobody warns you about it. The house had been full for two weeks, full of relatives, full of neighbours, full of church people bringing plastic containers of food you will be eating for a month. And then one evening it was just me. The door closed on the last person and the house went so quiet, si, I have never heard a silence like that one. It landed on me like something physical. I stood in that empty sitting room and I could hear my own heartbeat, honestly, and that was the moment, not the funeral, not the burial, that was the moment it actually reached me.

My body did strange things after that. Me I would not eat, sometimes since two, three days, nothing, not even tea. Then one day I would eat like I had not eaten in a month, finish everything in the house, and then go back to nothing again. Sleep was the same somehow. I was not sleeping properly, whole nights, I mean whole normal sleep, no. It was short pieces, forty minutes here, one hour there, waking up at odd times not knowing if it is two or four, and being tired the whole day, carrying that tiredness like a bag on my back.

That first month was a strange one because of how fast everybody else kept moving. You walk to the shops and people are just going about, greeting each other, sasa, mambo, and you say poa poa because what else do you even say, and inside you are thinking, does this person know the whole world just ended for me. The matatus are still hooting the same, the vegetable seller is still shouting the same prices, everything just continues, and you are the only one standing still in it, and that is a very lonely feeling, that one.

Then there was one ordinary day, nothing special about it, I had not even marked it in my mind beforehand, and it just finished me completely. No warning at all. I do not even fully know why that particular day, out of all of them, but I remember sitting down in the middle of whatever I was doing and just breaking, properly breaking, like something had been waiting for exactly that moment to come out.

Months later, even, it was not finished with me. A smell would do it, just a smell passing in the street, or something small, an object lying somewhere, and a wave would come out of nowhere. No pattern to it at all, sindiyo? You cannot predict it, you cannot brace for it, it just arrives.

Then there was that long middle stretch, and honestly that one was the heaviest, because it was not sharp pain anymore, it was more like a fog sitting over everything. Even the good days, days that should have felt like something, felt somehow flat, grey, like I was watching my own life through a window instead of being inside it. That fog took long to lift, longer than I expected, longer than I wanted to admit to anybody.

Imagine, someone actually told me, meaning well probably, "at least he is not suffering anymore, you should be happy for that." I tell you, that thing cut deeper than it had any right to. I was quietly angry for days after that one careless sentence. But then, right next to that, there was a small thing, nobody official, nobody with a title, just someone who came, sat, held my hand and said "shame," just that, shame, look how small you have become in this, and that one small word did more for me than every organised comfort, every scheduled visit, every rehearsed line anybody brought me.

Then the anniversary was coming, and weeks before it I was dreading it properly, that kind of dread that sits in your stomach every morning you wake up and count how many days are left. When the day itself came, I got through it, somehow clumsily, I will not lie to you, I did not handle it gracefully, I just got through it, and that was enough. That was the whole point of it, just getting through, nothing more required of me that day, hey.

Appetite came back slowly slowly after that, not all at once, some weeks better than others. Sleep the same, slowly slowly, uneven, one good night then three bad ones, then maybe two good ones. Nothing arrived whole, everything arrived in bits.

Then one day I caught myself laughing, really laughing, at something someone said, forgetting myself completely for a moment, and right behind that laugh came this guilt, uninvited, nobody accused me of anything, it was just there in me on its own, like I had betrayed something by feeling light for even one second.

By the time the year finished, I was carrying it differently, I can tell you that much. Lighter in some places, definitely, some days I go a whole stretch without it pressing on my chest. But it has not gone, not fully, and I do not think it is meant to.

So that is my year, plainly, and I am telling you because I want you to hear it does not stay this hard forever, it really does not, hey. But there is no fixed timeline for when it eases, none, and whatever anybody tells you about stages and months, do not measure your own year against anybody else's year, not your workmate's, not your aunt's, not mine either. Your grief will go the way it goes. Just let it.
