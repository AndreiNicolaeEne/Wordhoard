The tailgate's hanging open and the whole roll of spanners gone from the bakkie, and the boss doesn't even look at the ones who were actually busy loading up just now. He walks straight for the young one, standing there by the robot corner with the others, and points.

The boss: You. Come here now. You took my spanners.

The young one: Aikona.

The boss: Don't tell me aikona, boet. You were busy with that toolbox just now, I saw you myself.

The young one: Wasn't me.

The boss: I'm asking you again, hey. Those spanners don't grow legs. You had that box last, so tell me now-now, where they went.

The young one: I swear, I had no part in it.

The others by the fire drum keep quiet, takkies scuffing in the dust, somebody murmurs a yoh under their breath, but nobody looks up, nobody says a word about who was really at that box the whole time.

The boss: Ag shame man, look how small you're making yourself over this. Just tell me straight.

The young one's face stays flat, eyes steady, giving him nothing back.

The boss: Is it. Sharp. We'll see about that, hey.

Nobody says sorry. Nobody says anything at all. The young one picks up the bag, walks off past the robot toward the taxis, face composed the whole way, and the thing he never did stays sitting there exactly where he left it.
