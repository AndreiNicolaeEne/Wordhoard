Imagine, one candle for the whole house, and even that one had to be shared round like a plate of food.

The magetsi went just now, just after dusk, no warning at all. The father was just reaching home from work when it happened, and the mother greeted him at the door, well done, but see there's no light in this house. She was busy cooking posho when the whole place went black, and she just stood there over the pot going eh-eh-eh like it will cook itself now. The little one started crying like the world had ended, shame, look how small she looked, holding onto mother's dress like that.

Me I was busy with homework at the table, lost my place completely, hey. The eldest was telling everyone, you come this side first, don't just walk anyhow in the dark, and he borrows me his phone for light since mine had gone flat. We started searching, moving slowly slowly so as not to add more damage to the damage already there, but he still knocked that same stool by the door, not once, twice, same leg, and the second time he was really complaining.

Only one candle stub in the whole drawer, no matches near it. The father went out to check the fuse box and came back saying there's nothing to fix tonight, the whole transformer is down, we'll just manage now-now.

Someone's phone rang and dropped before we could hear well, so we flashed back.

Me: Sasa, mambo, why you people also in darkness?
Neighbour: Poa poa, but the whole street has gone. KPLC has eaten that money again instead of fixing that transformer, that's why the lines are somehow these days, si you know how they are.
Me: Sindiyo, it's always like that, hey.

We gave up trying to fix anything that night. We gathered close around that one flame in the sitting room, all of us, shoulder to shoulder, the little one on mother's lap till she slept. Around ten the mother said sasa, that's enough, off to bed, the magetsi will come when it comes.

It didn't come. We went to bed in the dark, all of us, and in the morning the lights were just there again like nothing had happened, and the father only said, is it, and went to make tea.
