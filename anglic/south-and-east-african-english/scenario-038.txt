Ah, that job finished me today, my friend. Si, from the morning my body was not even ready, the foreman was already shouting for us to line up by the gate before the sun had properly come up. Me I was still yawning, still cold, and there we were sent straight to carry blocks from the lorry to the back of the plot. That one set the pace for the whole day, I'm telling you, because by the time we finished stacking, my hands were already shouting.

We just greeted quick, sasa, mambo, poa, no time even to sit. I was busy mixing cement the whole morning, slowly slowly at first because the body was still warming, then faster once the sweat came properly. By midmorning my back had started complaining proper, that low pain that tells you the day is long. I told the foreman my hands were finishing, he just said is it, and sent me to carry more.

We got a short break for tea around ten, but it was somehow, too short, we had barely sat when he was calling now-now, saying the trench people are waiting. Then not long after, now, he wanted us moving stones. And just now, when the sun was already going down, he wanted the whole back wall plastered before we left, as if our hands are machines.

Imagine, after all that. That afternoon stretch was the worst, my friend, sun up, body tired, and more than once I thought, let me just go home and lose today's pay, forget it. But I stayed.

One good thing though, the small gate we were fixing finished faster than I thought, maybe an hour only, and the owner was pleased, gave me forty extra for it there and there. That contractor eats money if you don't count it well, so I counted mine twice. We ate lunch over posho and beans, sat under the mabati shade, legs finished.

Ten hours gone, just like that. We've made a plan already, if my back is still like this tomorrow, I rest, if not, same site again. Either way, I'm not lifting anything more tonight, sindiyo?
