That week I was minding a house that was not mine, the full seven days, and I am telling it now end to end, because it did not want to let me rest even one single day of it.

The first night already there was something not right. That big lamp near the gate was flickering, on and off, on and off, like it was greeting me badly. I said let me just sleep, I will look at it in the morning, but even the sleep itself was somehow. The fridge in the kitchen was making a sound like it was arguing with itself, humming then stopping then humming again, and I kept waking to go and check it was not about to catch fire on me. By morning I was more tired than when I lay down, but I told myself, it is a new house, you will settle, hey.

Second day the dog got loose. I had opened the gate small small for the milk boy and that dog went through that gap like it had been planning it since the owners left. Imagine, that boy did not even push the gate to close it properly behind himself, that's how the whole thing started. I was busy chasing it down the road in my slippers, one hand holding my wrapper, everybody on that road looking at me like I was the one who had stolen somebody's dog and was now struggling to control it. The neighbour's watchman helped me corner it near the ditch. Within about an hour we had it back inside, panting, looking at me like nothing at all had happened, and I stood there and told it, you will finish me before these people even come back.

The garden trouble I did not even see coming, it crept up on me slowly slowly. In the beginning those flowers by the veranda were fine, I was watering morning and evening exactly like the madam had shown me. But by the third day I started noticing the leaves going yellow at the edges, then the tomatoes at the back drooping, and I could not tell if it was too much water or too little or some insect working underneath there quietly. By day four that whole garden was looking somehow, and I would just stand there in the evening staring at it like it owed me an explanation.

Then the water heater went. I was busy preparing water for a bath one afternoon and heard a small pop, then nothing, then cold water only, forever, from that pipe. I tried the switch, on and off, nothing. I called the owner and the line dropped before she picked, so I flashed her and waited.

Me: The heater in the bathroom has stopped, it just made a sound and died.

The owner: Is it? Which sound?

Me: Like a small pop, then it went quiet completely.

The owner: Ah, don't touch that one, just boil water on the stove for now, we will sort it when we are back.

So for the rest of that week I was boiling water in a saucepan like the old people used to, carrying it in a basin to the bathroom, and that alone added a whole hour to my evenings.

That same night the rain came without any warning while I had left the small bedroom window open because the day had been hot. By the time I remembered it, the curtain was soaked through, the little mat on the floor was swimming, and the water had reached the corner of the mattress. I mopped that room like my life depended on it, hung the curtain outside to dry, and prayed the whole time it would not leave a mark for the madam to find.

Then on the fourth day, without any warning at all, a visitor showed up. I heard the gate, went to check, and there was a relative of the owners standing there with a bag, saying she had come to collect something she had left in the house a long time back.

The visitor: Good afternoon, is the madam around?

Me: No, she travelled, she comes back end of the week.

The visitor: Ah, that's fine, can I come in small, I left something in that back room.

I did not know this person from anywhere, but she knew the house too well to be a stranger, so I extended a seat for her in the sitting room and gave her water. Before she left she said, borrow me a pen, I want to write my number for you, and I found her one, and she wrote it and went and searched the back room and found her thing in a box, maybe twenty minutes altogether. All that time she was inside I was standing near that door like a real guard, watching her hands.

The jolt of alarm came on the fifth day. I was cleaning that same back room and could not find one small envelope the madam had specially pointed out before she travelled, told me keep an eye on this one, it has documents. I searched that room top to bottom, my heart was somehow, already rehearsing what I would say on the phone, that I have lost your documents, forgive me. Then I opened a drawer I had already checked twice and there it was, underneath a folded cloth, exactly where I myself had put it for safekeeping and forgotten. I sat down on that bed and just breathed for some minutes. Shame, I had given myself such fear for nothing at all.

The locking out was the same week, an afternoon I had gone to throw the rubbish at the gate, since those garbage people who collect had eaten the money for two weeks now and not come, and I pulled the gate shut behind me without thinking. That door has a spring lock that catches itself. I stood there I don't know, three hours, four hours, trying every trick, flashing the neighbour, flashing the watchman. I told the watchman on the phone, you come now, don't delay, and he said don't fear, you reach, meaning I should just wait, help was coming. While I waited I bought a rolex off a boy passing with his tray and ate it standing right there at that locked gate, and later took a bodaboda with the watchman to fetch a spare key from somebody who used to keep one. By the time we got back it was nearly evening and I had lost the whole afternoon to that one gate.

Then there was the noise in the night. Maybe the fifth or sixth night, I do not remember exactly which, something was moving on the roof, a scratching, dragging sound, back and forth. I lay there telling myself it is only a cat, but the sound was too heavy for a cat. Eventually I got up, took the torch, went outside in only my shawl, and stood there shining the light up at the roof edge like that would help anything. It was a cat in the end, a fat one, sitting there looking down at me like I was the one disturbing its night.

The fix I finally landed on was for that same flickering gate lamp that had bothered me from the very first night straight through. On the sixth day I asked the watchman about it and we made a plan together, he showed me the bulb was loose in its holder, not even broken, just not sitting well. We turned it properly, tightened it, and that lamp stood steady the rest of the night like it had never given me a single problem.

The last day, the seventh, I was up early doing the frantic tidying, because you want the house to look like nothing at all happened while they were away. I straightened everything, hid the saucepan I had been boiling water in, watered that garden one more time hoping it would perk up by evening, cooked myself a quick posho and beans so I would not be caught cooking when they arrived, swept, dusted, arranged the shoes at the door properly, and by the time I heard the car at the gate I was sitting there like a person who had spent seven quiet days doing absolutely nothing.

When they came in, the madam went round the house with her eyes, and I gave my account.

The owner: So how was everything, no problems?

Me: Ah, it was fine generally, small small things only. The dog escaped one day but we got it back within about an hour. The heater stopped, I was boiling water since. There was a visitor, she said she is your relative, she collected something from the back room.

The owner: Is it? Which one?

Me: The one who came for the box.

The owner: Ah okay, that one, she is family, that's fine.

Me: And your garden is somehow, I don't know what happened there, I was watering it every single day.

The owner: We will look at it, don't worry yourself.

The owner: Well done anyway, for staying with the house alone that whole week.

I did not mention the locking out, or the rain that reached the mattress, or the night I stood outside talking to a cat with a torch in my hand. Some things you just carry quietly, hey, and be grateful the house is still standing and so are you.
