The bakkie's up on the jack in the yard, wheel off, and the older hand is busy showing him how it's done before he even opens his mouth about it - nut runner going one, skip one, one, skip one, round the star. Nut runner down, wheel doesn't even wobble. Now you, he says, and hands it over.

The new hand takes it and just goes round the wheel like a clock going one way, one two three four five, no skipping.

The older hand: No no no, hey. Aikona, not like that. You go round like that you'll crack the disc, or worse. Star pattern, boet. Top one, then the one across from it, then the next, then across from that. You see how I did it just now?

The new hand: Ja ja. So which one's across again, this one or that one next to it?

The older hand, shorter now: The one straight opposite. Not the neighbour. Across. I showed you now-now, hey.

They go again. First two nuts fine, then halfway he loses it and snugs three in a row before he's even touched the fourth point of the star.

The older hand: Eish, man! I just told you two minutes back and you're doing the same thing again. This wheel comes off on the highway it's not me driving, hey.

The new hand: Sorry, sorry, I was rushing, I thought -

The older hand: You weren't thinking, that's the whole story. Okay, forget my words. Wheel's a clock face. Twelve, then six, then three, then nine. Say it.

The new hand: Twelve, six, three, nine.

The older hand: Now go, slowly slowly.

Twelve. Six. Three. Nine. Clean, even, no drift.

The older hand: There! Sharp-sharp. Is it, you got it now?

The new hand: Ja, I got it.

The older hand: Lekker. Shame, everyone battles it the first time. Now do the other three and don't make me stand here whole day, hey.
