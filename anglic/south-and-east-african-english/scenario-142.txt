The day starts before the day even starts, because my eyes open and the promise is already sitting on my chest, heavy, waiting. Months back, after the uncle had asked me one time too many to come and clear that plot behind his house, I stood there and swore it plain, that I would come and finish it, the whole day, no matter what. And today is that today.

Not one part of me wants to get up. Is it, I ask myself, is it really today, and the answer doesn't change. But I swore, so I get up, and I complain the whole way there, muttering to myself on the bodaboda, why did I even open my big mouth that day, why couldn't I keep quiet like everybody else who is also not going to dig anybody's plot today.

The rider extends the seat for me though there's barely room, and I hold on complaining the whole stretch, past the one frying a rolex off the street, past the conductor shouting for people to extend, extend, somebody give this one space, hey.

I reach and the uncle is already standing there, greeting me well done, as if the greeting alone will put the hoe in my hand faster.

Uncle: Well done, my friend. You start from that corner there.

Me: I'm busy regretting my own big mouth, that's what I'm busy doing this morning.

The hoe is somehow, blunt like it hasn't cut grass since I was small, and the grass itself is up to my waist. By midday my hands are finished, blistered, my back complaining in its own language. I complain about the length of it, the timing of it, the sun choosing today, and mostly about the mind I had that evening I swore such a thing, shame, look how foolish a person can be.

Over posho and beans I tell my friend the whole story, imagine, I stood there and swore in front of everybody, and now-now here I am. He just laughs, says we'll make a plan, we'll finish just now.

At one point I put the hoe down and think, just leave it, but no, I swore, and that closes the thought before it finishes.

By evening the plot is cleared, flat, done, exactly as I promised, and I'm still complaining, hey, even walking home, but I never once turned and walked away from it.
