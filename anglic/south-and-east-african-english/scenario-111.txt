Quarter past ten and I'm busy talking to this empty room like it can answer me back. He said half seven, he said it himself, half seven sharp, and now look at this clock, is it? Half seven to quarter past ten, that is not thirty minutes we are talking, that is three whole hours. Three hours overdue and the posho on the plate is cold, cold like it was never even cooked for anybody.

Maybe the boda had no fuel. Maybe he is standing somewhere waiting for the pump to open, or he stopped for a rolex on the way and lost track, these things happen, he will make a plan and appear now-now. I want to believe it, half believing it the way you believe a thing because the other thing is too much to hold.

I keep going to that window and then to the door and back to the window, not deciding to, my legs just doing it on their own. What if the traffic ones stopped him again, eating his transport money, or worse, what if there was an accident on that road, God forgive me for even letting that word sit in my mouth.

I remember one time, shame, he was small then, and I sat exactly like this, same chair, same fear, and he walked in laughing like nothing. Look how small he was then. It gives me a little comfort and a little more dread, both together, somehow.

I think of going out to the corner myself to ask the boda men there. Not yet. Just now, maybe. I check the clock again: past four hours now.

A dog barks outside and my whole heart jumps up before I even stand, and it is nothing, just the neighbour's gate.

Then the real sound, the latch, the steps I know.

Me: Imagine, all this time and you can't even flash me one time to say you're coming?

The brother: Sorry sorry, the boda got a puncture, I'm sharp, I'm sharp, don't fight me, eh.
