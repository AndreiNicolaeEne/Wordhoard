The relative: Howzit, boet. Shame, I heard you're not right. How bad is it really, hey?

Me: Ag, it's not so lekker, I won't lie. Started three days back now, this ache in the joints, and my throat scratchy scratchy. Then yesterday the fever came on top of it, on top of everything.

The relative: Is it? Shame man.

Me: Ja. Today's a little easier than yesterday, but still far from right, hey. Not sharp yet.

The relative: What have you been doing for it then, hey, just lying there?

Me: No no, I'm busy sorting myself out. First thing, warm salt water, gargle gargle, for the throat. Then when the fever wasn't breaking on its own, cool cloth on the forehead, changed it through the night, now-now, then again just now.

The relative: And eating, sleeping?

Me: Just broth and weak tea, that's it. Sleeping whenever the body lets me. Some hours nothing, then just now I'm up again at some mad hour.

The relative: You must rest properly hey, don't push through this thing, you hear me. Don't be a hero.

Me: I hear you, I hear you. My own line is, if the fever climbs any more, or the breathing goes hard, then I'm going, sharp-sharp, no messing. But not before that, hey.

The relative: I can bring you something, or come sit with you an hour, keep you company.

Me: Ag, you don't have to, boet... okay, maybe just a small plate, if you're passing. Don't make a whole thing of it, hey.

The relative: No trouble at all. I'll bring soup, sit a bit, make sure you're okay.

Me: Sharp. Thanks hey, my friend.
