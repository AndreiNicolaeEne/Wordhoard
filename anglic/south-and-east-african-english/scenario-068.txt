Howzit, my friend. Before you start walking around here like everything is sorted for you, let me put you onto something.

There's a chap ,  you'll meet him, everyone does ,  standing by the rank where the kombis are loading, or by the robot when the light's long, or right outside the bank when the queue is stretching round the corner. Always smiling, always got a deal going, calling you boet or shamwari like you've known each other since Grade One. Too friendly-friendly, that's the first sign right there, hey.

He'll tell you he's got forex, rate better than the bank's, or a bakkie for sale, cheap-cheap, papers all sorted, or takkies, brand new, still in the box, murungu's price because he likes your face. Lekker deal, he'll say, and he'll make a plan for you right there. Sounds too good to even ask about, and that's the whole point of it, it's built that way so you don't ask.

Now hear this part well. The moment you pay anything upfront, even a small deposit just to "hold" it for you, that is the last you will see of him. He'll say call you now-now, he'll say he's coming just now, and then, gone. Your money gone with him.

There's the one, my cousin's friend, just arrived last month, busy looking for a room, hadn't even had his first plate of sadza in this town, this one found him waiting at the robot, promised him a stand out past the growth point, only needed a registration fee first. Paid it. Went back next day to see the stand. Aikona, no stand, no man, nothing. Ag, it was sad. Eish, shame, the boy was so happy that morning, look how small his face was after.

Signs before it even gets that far: he knows you're new before you've said one word, he's in a hurry, deal ends today-today, he won't walk you to any office, everything happens standing right there on the pavement, cash in hand. If somebody like that finds you, you say sharp, let me think about it, and you walk, you don't look back. Ask the vendor at the tuckshop, or the hwindi who's worked that route for years, is it, they know these ones, they'll tell you straight, and you'll know soon enough. Never pay anyone on the street before you've checked, my friend. Are we together?
