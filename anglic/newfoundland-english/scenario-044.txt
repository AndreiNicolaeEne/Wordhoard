The older hand had the knife out on the bench before the new hand even got the apron tied right. Fish laid out, belly up, and the older hand just done it slow, no talking, blade going in under the gills and down along the backbone in one draw, board clean after.

The older hand: There, now. Ye sees how she opens up like that, one draw, ye don't saw at her.

The new hand took the knife and hacked into the next one like it owed him money, blade catching bone halfway down, fillet all tore to bits.

The older hand: No, b'y, no. Ye're going too deep, look, ye stays along the bone, ye don't dig for it. Try her again.

The new hand: Wait, so am I cutting toward me or away?

The older hand: Away from ye, b'y, same as I just showed. Along the bone, one draw, light hand.

Next fish, same thing, knife buried right through, meat ruined again, guts everywhere.

The older hand: Jesus, b'y, what did I just say to ye? That's the second one ye wrecked the very same way. Ye're pressing down like ye're splitting wood, this en't wood, b'y, it's fish, light hand!

The new hand: I am doing what ye said, I swear.

The older hand: Ye're not, or she wouldn't look like that. Go way with that.

The new hand: Sorry, sorry, I'm after rushing it, that's all.

The older hand let out a breath, some frustrated, and picked up another one himself.

The older hand: Right, forget the words. Watch me hand only, not the knife. See how she's flat to the bone the whole time, riding along her, never tipping down. Now you.

The new hand took it slow this time, wrist flat, blade riding along easy, and the fillet come off whole, clean as anything.

The older hand: There ye go, b'y. Now do that a few hundred more times the day.
