Wakes up this morning and it hits me before me eyes is even proper open, b'y, today's the day. Months back I stood in that kitchen and swore up and down, right loud, that I'd give the whole day to gutting out that shed once and for all, no more of it dragged on. And soon as I remembers, oh me nerves, the regret comes down on me like a wet tarp. Don't want to move a muscle. Lies there a good ten minutes thinking of every reason a body could invent for staying put.

Gets up anyway. Grumbling the whole way down the road, muttering to meself what kind of fool swears to a thing like that in front of witnesses. Some foggy out too, proper mauzy, can't hardly see the tickle from the top of the hill.

The neighbour, out on his step: Where ya to this hour, b'y?
Me: Off to do the shed, finally.
The neighbour: Shockin day for it. Mauzy as anything.
Me: Don't I know it.

In I goes and it's worse than I remembered, junk piled to the rafters, some dust, some mess altogether. Grumbles about the length of it, grumbles about doing it of a day this mauzy, grumbles most of all at meself for ever opening me mouth. By the middle stretch, arms aching, back gone, I'm after thinking, sure I could leave the rest for another go. But no, I swore, and that's the end of that thought, shuts it right down before it gets its feet under it.

Grits through the worst of the boxes, dust and all, no stopping now. By duckish it's done, every last bit of it, swept and sorted proper. Me mother comes out.

Mother: Well, ye actually done it.
Me: Said I would, didn't I.

Sits down after with a mug-up, aching all over, complaining still, but done is done.
