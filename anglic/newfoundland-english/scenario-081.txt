The aunt: Where ya to, my love, I'm after hearing you got the sickness that's going round. Whadda y'at, tell me straight now, how bad is it really.

Me: I'm home, been in the bed half the day. Started three days back now, just an ache in me joints and a scratchy throat, nothing too shockin at first. Then yesterday the fever come on top of it, and I tell ya, some miserable I was. The day's a little easier than yesterday, I'll say that much, but I'm still far from right, still.

The aunt: And what've ya been doing for it, b'y.

Me: First thing I done was a gargle, warm water with salt in it, for the throat. When the fever wouldn't break off on its own I put a cool cloth across me forehead, kept changing it right through the night. Ye knows how that goes, up and down, up and down.

The aunt: Lord, ye must be wore right out.

Me: I am, some tired. Been keeping to broth and a drop of weak tea, and sleeping whenever me body lets me, which isn't near enough, I'll tell ya.

The aunt: Well ye needs to rest proper now, love, and don't be pushing through it like you always does.

Me: I knows, I knows. But luh, if the fever climbs any higher, or the breathing turns hard on me, I'm going and getting seen, I promises ya that much. Not before it, though.

The aunt: I could bring ya over a scoff, or come sit with ya an hour, keep ya company while you're laid up.

Me: Ah, you don't have to do that. But... sure, if ye wants to, I wouldn't say no to the company for a spell. Just don't be fussing over me now, I'm not that bad off.

The aunt: Go way, b'y, it's no trouble at all. I'll bring a bit of soup over the day, and we'll see how ye's getting on.

Me: Oh me nerves, you're some good to me. Yes b'y, that'd be lovely. Thanks, my love.
