Workmate: How'd the night go, then, b'y? You looks wrecked.

Me: Wrecked don't half cover it. The dog was some bad off yesterday evening, off her feed entirely and wouldn't get up right, walkin' like her back end wasn't hers at all. I says to meself, no, I'm not leavin' her out in that shed the night, not like that. Brought her in by the stove and stayed right there with her.

Workmate: All night, b'y?

Me: All night. First long while there was nar change at all, just her layin' there lookin' at me with them big sad eyes. I'm after tryin' to get a drop of water into her the whole evenin', holdin' the bowl right up to her nose, and she wouldn't take it on her own, so I was after cuppin' me hand and gettin' a small drop down her that way, coaxin' and coaxin'.

Workmate: Must've been shockin' hard on the nerves.

Me: Oh me nerves, don't talk to me. 'Round two or so she took a right bad turn, shakin' all over and her breathin' gone wrong, and I had meself half convinced I'd done the wrong thing, not takin' her in the minute it started. But where was I gettin' to at that hour, mauzy as it was out, not a soul on the road, nothin' open? So I stayed put, kept her warm, talked to her low the way you would to a youngster that's frightened, foolishness mostly, don't matter what you says so long as you says it soft.

Workmate: And?

Me: Near dawn I catches her breathin' gone easier, and her ear give a little twitch at somethin' out the window, same as she always done before all this started. That's when I knowed. By full mornin' she was steadier, not right yet mind, but steadier, and it was only once I was sure of her that I went and fell right apart meself. Some tired, b'y. Shockin' tired.
