The aunt: Now, love, I'm after hearing something the day and I couldn't sit on it, I had to come tell ya. The one down the wharf, ya knows the one, the missus next door's crowd, well her sister-in-law's after telling me he's some hard worker, out on the water before light and never a word of complaint out of him.

The niece: Is he now.

The aunt: He is, b'y, and that's not the half of it. Quiet in himself, steady, none of that sleeveen carry-on some of them gets up to round here. And the family, sure, decent crowd altogether, church every Sunday, the father a fine man, the mother does a mug-up for the whole street come Christmas. What more do ya want out of a man, tell me that.

The niece: I don't want for nothing, I s'pose. I just, I don't know.

The aunt: Just what, luh? What's wrong with him? 'Cause I'm buggered if I can see it.

The niece: There's nothing wrong with him, Aunt. I'm just some busy with the work right now, and I idn't ready for all that yet. That's all 'tis.

The aunt: Not ready. Lord, I heard that one before. Your cousin said the very same thing, near word for word, gave me the nerves something shockin, and look at her now, married eight year and some glad she never won that argument.

The niece: I hears ya, and I'm glad it worked out grand for her. But it idn't the same. I got things I'm after doing first.

The aunt: Well, I'm not gonna push ya, love, I only come to say me piece. He idn't going nowhere fast though, and when ya changes yer mind, and ya will, ya knows where I'm to.
