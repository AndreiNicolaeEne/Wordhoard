So I'm after minding the house for the whole seven days, and I'm telling ye now, b'y, it was one thing right after the other the entire time, start to finish, I never got a full night without something.

First night in, I'm not even unpacked proper, just the bag on the bed and the kettle on, and already there's something not right about the place. The furnace is making a sound down below like it's clearing its throat, this low knock knock knock every time it kicks on, and I'm standing there in me socks at eleven o'clock listening to a strange house talk to itself. I never said nothing to nobody, just told meself sure it's an old house, they all got a voice, and went to bed with the light on in the hall like a youngster.

Second day the dog was gone. I opened the porch door for two seconds, no more, just to shake out the mat, and he's out past me legs and up over the hill before I even got the word out of me mouth. I'm out there in me slippers roaring after him down the street, half the neighbourhood looking out their windows at me, and I'm thinking, that's it, that's the whole week ruined on day two, I've lost the family dog. Took the better part of an hour, mind, chasing him tickle to tickle round every lane off that road, before chummy from two doors down come out with a bit of baloney and coaxed him right into his arms like nothing to it at all. I could've kissed the man.

The garden trouble I never even seen coming, it just crept up on me slow the way that sort of thing does. There was tomatoes in behind, good ones too, and I was watering them the way you'd think was right, but by Wednesday the leaves is gone yellow and curled up something shockin, and by Thursday there's little brown spots eating into the lot of them. I don't know if it was too much water or not enough or the mauzy weather we had rolling in off the water half the week, that thick fog just sitting on everything and never lifting till duckish some evenings. I did what I could, pulled off the worst of the leaves, propped the rest up, said a prayer over them, moved on.

Then Thursday the dryer packs it in on me, right in the middle of a load, just stops dead with a smell off it like something electrical. I'm standing there thinking, oh me nerves, that's broke now under my watch, they're going to come home to a dead dryer and think I done something to it. Had wet towels strung up over every chair in the kitchen for two days after that, looked like a boil-up gone wrong in there.

Friday I had a visitor and no warning whatsoever. I'm in me housecoat, hair like a mop, and there's a knock at the door.

Me: Who's this now.

I open it up and it's her workmate, standing there with a tray of something.

Her workmate: Oh, sorry b'y, I didn't know you was minding the place, I thought she was home, I brought over a bit of a scoff for the two of them.

Me: No, no, they're away, I'm just here keeping an eye on things.

Her workmate: Go way b'y, I feel shockin foolish now.

Me: Not at all, sure come in and have a mug-up while you're here, no sense you going back with all that.

So we had a bit of a laugh over the mix-up and a cup of tea and she went off again, and I'm sitting there after thinking, well that's a fright and a half for a Friday morning.

Saturday I nearly had the heart attack. I couldn't find the good ring off her jewelry box, the one she leaves out on the dresser to soak in cleaner, and I'm after picking it up meself the day before just to have a look and I could not for the life of me remember where I put it back. I was into every drawer in that room, sweating, thinking, that's it now, I'm after losing the woman's ring, how do you even explain that to somebody. Turned it up an hour later in the little dish by the sink where I'd set it meself, plain as day. Me nerves was shot after that one, I'm telling ye.

Sunday, that was the locked out day. I went out to the shop for milk, quick as you like, and the wind caught the door behind me and it clicked shut, and there I am on the step with no key, no phone, nothing but me coat. Stood out there a good three hours, walked round the block a few times, sat on the step some more, before the neighbour on the other side come home and let me use her phone to ring a fella with a slim jim. Whadda y'at, he says to me, coming up the walk, like it's nothing at all, like this happens to him every day of the week. Might well have, the look of him.

Monday night, that's when the rain got in. I'd left the window cracked in the back room for a bit of air, forgot all about it with everything else going on, and we had a downpour come through off the harbour in the small hours, right into that room, soaked the sill and a good bit of the rug before I woke to the smell of it. Up at three in the morning with towels down, mopping like a mad thing, cursing meself the whole time.

And it was that same night, or maybe the one after, I got the fright of the noise. Something banging away down in the basement, this steady thump thump thump in the dead of night, and I'm lying there thinking, right, this is it, this is the one, somebody's after breaking in below. Took me a good ten minutes to work up the nerve to go down with the flashlight, heart going like a hammer, and it's only the dryer vent hose come loose off the back, banging against the wall every time the furnace fan kicked the air round. Some relief, I'm telling ye. I nearly had to sit down on the stairs after that one.

The dryer, though, that kept acting up right through, on and off, all week, and it wasn't till the second last day I finally sorted it proper. Turns out it was the belt slipping, and I found a bit of the same rubber tubing in the shed and jury-rigged it on there meself, and it run smooth as anything after that, not a peep out of it. Felt shockin good to have that one licked at last, I don't mind telling ye.

Last day before they was due back, I was into it from first light. Scrubbed the whole kitchen down, got the dryer running proper loads to catch up all them towels, hauled the vent hose back on tight, pulled the worst of the tomato plants and tidied round the rest so it wouldn't look too shockin, vacuumed every room twice over. By the time I was done I hadn't a bit left in me.

They come in the door that evening, and the mother's first word out of her mouth was, luh, the place looks lovely, and I near fell over with the relief of it.

The mother: How'd he get on, was he any trouble.

Me: Oh, he got loose the second day but we had him back within the hour. Right good as gold the rest of the week.

The mother: And the tomatoes, they don't look the best.

Me: No, they took some trouble round the middle of the week, I'm not sure what got into them, I did what I could for the poor things.

The eldest: What's this with the dryer, is it running funny.

Me: No, no, she had a bit of a moment on Thursday but I got her sorted, running some smooth now.

I never did tell them about the ring, mind, nor the three hours on the step in me slippers, nor the water in the back room half soaked into the floor before I caught it. Some things a body just keeps to themself. But I told them the rest of it after, over a mug-up at the table, the whole business start to finish, and the mother laughing so hard at the bit with her workmate and the scoff that she near cried, and I'm sitting there thinking, well, some week that was, but sure they're home now and the dog's asleep by the stove and that's the whole of it done.
