The first elder: Me knees is some bad the day, I tell ye. Fifteen years now they been at me, ever since I done them stairs down to the old house on the hill. You knows the ones. Locks right up of a morning, can't hardly get down to put the kettle on before they eases off.

The second elder: Knees. Luh, don't be talking to me about knees, that's nothing atall. I'm after three, four nights a month can't get a wink of sleep with me hip, right down the leg of me it goes, like somebody's after taking a poker to it in the dark. You gets to walk round on yours all the day still. I gets no sleep, none.

The third elder: The two of ye don't know what pain is, not a clue. I had this back since before either one of ye was even born, I'd say, long before I ever laid eyes on the pair of ye and that's some years now, b'y. Comes and it goes, that one, but mostly it comes.

The first elder: Well who's got it worst then, the three of us sittin here moaning like a load of old accordions over a cold mug-up.

The second elder: 'Tis me, sure, no sleep and you two still able to get about.

The third elder: Go way b'y, mine's older than the both of yers put together and then some.

The first elder: Longer don't make it worse.

The second elder: No it don't, girl, don't make it worse atall.

The third elder: Oh me nerves, the pair of ye. Some crowd to be sat with of an evening.

And they laughs, all three, nobody backing down an inch, the toutons gone cold on the plate between them and the tea near froze in the mugs, and not one of them any closer to saying who's worst off, nor caring to be, really.
