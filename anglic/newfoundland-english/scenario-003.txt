The mother: Go way b'y, you're not saddling that poor maid with some name off the telly. It's some trendy this minute and it'll be a laughing stock in five years, mark me words.

The father: Fine, fine, don't get on with your nerves. What about your granda's name so, the old one? Solid name, that.

The mother: That's an old man's name if ever I heard one, she'll get teased something shockin in the schoolyard over it, and it don't even sit right on a girl anyway.

The father: You're a right sleeveen, you knows that, turning down everything I comes up with and offering nothing yourself.

The mother: I offers plenty, you just don't listens. Yes b'y, what about we name her for me own nan, God rest her, give the old dear her due before she's forgot altogether?

The father: Your nan's name is already going round this family twice over, your sister's maid got it, and Nan's own sister carries it still. We'll have three of them answering to the one name at every mug-up from now till Christmas, and the mummers at the door won't know who they're calling out to either.

The mother: Well who's got the final say in all this anyway, you or me? Since you're that sure of yourself.

The father: There's no final say between the two of ye, no matter how sure you sounds.

The mother: Oh me nerves. Everything's gone mauzy in me head with all this back and forth the day.

They said no more on it after that, the mug-up gone stone cold between them, the wires outside gone quiet with a silver thaw, and the child below in the crib still with no name at all.
