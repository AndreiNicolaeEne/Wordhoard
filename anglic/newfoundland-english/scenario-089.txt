So I started skating, my friend, if you can believe that, forty-odd years old and never had a blade under me in me life. Everyone around me learned when they were three foot nothing, wobblin round the rink with their mother holdin the back of their coat, and here I am signin up for lessons with a crowd half my age starin at me like I'm after landed from Mars.

What pushed me into it, I don't rightly know. I s'pose I got tired of standin at the boards every winter watchin everyone else go round and round, and thinkin, sure b'y, why not, you're not dead yet. So down I goes to the rink one duckish evening in November, and the instructor takes one look at me and says,

The instructor: Now don't be expectin miracles. Takes longer when you're older, the body don't learn the same.

Blunt as anything, but fair enough, I s'pose. First lesson was shockin. Absolutely shockin. I couldn't stand up straight for two minutes together, never mind glide anywhere. Me ankles were goin every which way and I was hangin off the boards like a drownin man off a boat rail. Little chummy next to me, couldn't have been more than seven, was doin backwards crossovers round me while I was still tryin to figure out which way was forward.

The whole first week me body ached in places I forgot a body could ache. Me hips, me shoulders, the backs of me legs, muscles I hadn't used since I don't know when, all makin themselves known every time I so much as stood up off the couch. I'm after wakin up some mornings feelin like I was run over by the ferry.

And then there was the fall. B'y, the fall. I still think about it. I was tryin to turn, showin off a bit I s'pose, thinkin I had it, and down I went, hard, flat on me back, and it knocked the wind clean out of me. I lay there on the ice starin up at the lights not able to draw a breath, thinkin, that's it, I'm done, this is not for me, I'm too old for this foolishness. Buddy from the lesson skated over, all concerned, and I could hear meself sayin,

Me: I'm finished with this. I'm not comin back.

And I meant it and all. But that evening me workmate, who I'd been draggin on about the whole thing to for weeks, rings me up and says,

Her workmate: Go way b'y, you're not quittin after one fall. Come on Thursday, I'll come watch ya make a fool of yourself, sure that's half the fun of it.

So I went back. Reluctant as anything, but I went.

After that it was slow, my friend, slower than anything I ever tried in me life. Weeks of gettin the same little thing wrong over and over, one small crossover finally landed after a month of tryin, and a small crowd always seemed to gather round the boards, some of them right encouragin, cheerin me on like I was after winnin something, and some of them, I could tell, findin the whole thing shockin funny. Can't blame them. I must've been some sight.

The only thing that kept me sane was not lookin at the young ones flyin round like they were born with blades on. I stopped comparin meself to them altogether, b'y, no good ever come of that. Instead I'd think back to meself a month before, and if I was doin somethin now that I couldn't do then, that was the win. That's the only measure that ever meant anything.

Christmas came round in the middle of all this, and I'll tell ya, between the mummers goin round on Tibb's Eve and the whole run up to it, I nearly let the skating go altogether, too busy, too tired, too discouraged if I'm honest. But I kept at it, a lesson here, a lesson there, draggin meself down through the mauzy evenings when I'd rather have stayed in with a mug-up and me feet up.

Then, my friend, round about the fourth month, somethin clicked. Can't tell ya what it was in words even now. Wasn't nothin I thought me way into, it was more a feelin in me legs, in me balance, like me body finally understood somethin me head had been tryin to tell it for months. One evening I pushed off and just went, no thinkin about it, no white-knuckle grip on the boards, just goin, and I nearly cried right there on the ice. The instructor seen it and just nodded and said,

The instructor: There it is now. That's what I was after waitin on.

After that it wasn't easy, mind, nothin about it was ever easy, but it went from impossible to just difficult, and difficult I could live with.

I never did catch up to the young crowd, never will, and I've made me peace with that entire. I'm not out there doin backwards crossovers or any of that foolishness. But by the end of it I could get round that rink upright, under me own steam, turn a corner without landin on me arse, and stop meself without hittin the boards like a fool. Small things, I know, things a seven year old does without thinkin, but things I could not do at all when I started, and that's somethin.

Lookin back on the whole season now, it sits fond in me memory, not sour at all, which surprises me some when I think how miserable I was in the middle of it. The fall, the soreness, bein laughed at by half the rink some evenings, all of it just folds into somethin now I'm right glad I went and did. If you'd have told me back in November I'd look back fondly on gettin me breath knocked clean out of me on the ice, I'd have said go way b'y, you're not serious. But here I am sayin it anyway.
