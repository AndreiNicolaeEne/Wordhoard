My friend: Luh, don't think you're getting off with just tea and toutons. I heard ye the other night, right through the wall, I near knocked to see if I should come in and break it up.

Me: Oh me nerves, don't. I'm some mortified thinking on it now.

My friend: Whadda y'at, tell us the whole of it, what was even the start of it?

Me: Honest to god it was over supper. Nothing more than that at the start. I had the fish and brewis on, scrunchions all fried up and the fat still crackling, and the partner was s'posed to be back by scoff time, said would only be a couple hours down with buddy having a boil-up by the shore. Well the couple hours turned into near two and a half, no call, nothing, and I'm standing there watching the brewis go to mush and the scrunchions gone cold and greasy in the pan.

My friend: Oh no, b'y.

Me: So the partner comes in, all cheerful, like nothing's after happening, and I just says, right sharp, "Where were ya to, I had this on the table an hour and a half ago." Didn't even give a chance to get the boots off.

My friend: Uh oh.

Me: And that was me raising it first, I'll own that much. But then it come back just as loud, "I told ya I'd be late, don't be at me the second I'm in the door," and away we went from there, back and forth, louder every time, till I s'pose that's when ye heard us through the wall.

My friend: Right nice welcome home, I'd say.

Me: It got some ugly too. It wasn't just the supper by the end, it was every little thing that's built up, who's not pulling their weight round the house, who's always off with buddy and never home, and somewhere in the middle of it I says something I never meant, I says "I should never have bought this house with ya, I'm after wasting me whole life on this," which, b'y, that's shockin, that's not even a thing I believe, I just wanted it to hurt in the moment.

My friend: That's a bad one to throw.

Me: And then the partner, not to be outdone, brings up me mudder, the time she was bad sick a few winters back and the partner never came down, was working, fair enough, but it's the one thing I never bring up cause I know it wasn't really a choice that could be helped, and there it was, right in the middle of a row about cold fish and brewis. "Well you never let me forget when I couldn't come see your mudder either," or some such, and I tell you, that near took the legs out from under me.

My friend: God love ya.

Me: After that there wasn't much talking left in either of us, just the shouting, and then the partner went and slammed the bedroom door so hard the little picture frame in the hall come down off the nail. I heard it hit the floor and I just stood there in the kitchen with me arms folded, shaking near.

My friend: I did hear a bang alright.

Me: That'd be it. And after that, nothing. Not a word out of either of us. I sat in the front room the whole evening, the partner stayed back in the bedroom, and it went on like that clean through the night and half the next day, me in one room, the partner in the other, passing in the hall like we was strangers, no good morning, nothing.

My friend: The whole day?

Me: The better part of it, yes. I was some stubborn, I'll say that. It was duckish out, going on evening again, before the partner come out to the kitchen where I was sat with me tea gone cold, and just says, quiet like, "I'm after biling the kettle, you want a cup or what."

My friend: That's how it started back up?

Me: That was it. Nothing grand. Just the kettle. But I knew what it meant, and I says "go on then," and that was the two of us in the kitchen not looking at each other but at least in the same room again.

My friend: And who said sorry first?

Me: I did, actually. I says, "I'm sorry I went at ya the second you come in, before you even had your boots off, that wasn't fair, and I'm some sorry for saying that about wasting me life, I never meant a word of that, you knows I don't feel that way." And the partner just stood there a second, and then says, "No, I'm sorry too, I should've called when I knew I was running late, that was thoughtless, and I never should've brought your mudder into it, that was a low thing to say and I knew it soon as it was out of me mouth."

My friend: That's a proper sorry, not just a word tossed out.

Me: That's what I mean, it wasn't just "sorry," it was for the actual things. And then the partner reaches over, right easy, and says, "Come here to me, flower," which is a thing I hadn't heard out of the partner in ages, not since we were only new together. That was the first sign of it, soon as that word come out I knew we were mending.

My friend: Aw, that's lovely.

Me: We sat there and had the tea, and I heated back up what was left of the fish and brewis, wasn't much good the second time round but neither of us cared, and it was under the table the partner took me hand, first bit of touching in near two days, and that was it sealed right there, over cold scrunchions and a lukewarm mug of tea.

My friend: So all's grand now.

Me: All's grand. Better than before if anything. But go way, I knows you heard every word of it through that wall, so don't be acting all innocent asking me the story like you don't already have the half of it yourself.

My friend: I might have the words, b'y, but I never had the why of it till now.

Me: Well now you got the whole of it, and don't be telling it round either, or I'll be having a row with you next.

My friend: Go on, your secret's safe. Mauzy out anyway, nobody's going nowhere to spread it the day.

Me: True enough. Put the kettle on again so, and I'll tell ya the rest over another mug-up.
