Mother: Come here to me now, the two of ye, sit in close, right in by me. I got something to tell ye and I wants ye to hear it proper.

The eldest: What's after happening?

Mother: Nan died the day, love. This morning, in the hospital. Went quiet, no pain on her, the nurse said.

The eldest: But what does that even mean though, like really mean. Is she just gone, gone forever, or is she somewhere still?

Mother: I'm after asking meself that same question me whole life and I still don't know it for certain, and I won't stand here and lie to ye and say I do. Her body's stopped now. Her heart's not going no more, she won't be breathing, won't be talking, won't be after making ye toutons of a Sunday no more. That part's true and there's no softening it. Where the rest of her's to, the part that was really her, I believes that goes on. But believing's not knowing, and I won't tell ye different.

The youngest: Is she cold now?

Mother: She might be, my love, yes. Her body's not working, so it can't keep warm the way yours does. But she's not hurting, not scared, not feeling ne'er a thing. That's over for her now.

The mother reached out and drew the two of them in close against her, an arm round each, holding on tight.

Mother: Come here, the both of ye. Come here, me duck, right in tight.

The youngest: Will we see her again ever?

Mother: Not to touch her hand or hear her laugh again, no. That's the honest truth of it and I won't dodge it on ye. But she'll be in things, in the toutons, in stories, in us talking about her, and that's real too, just a different real.

Mother: Now. The next few days there'll be a wake, and family in and out of the house something shockin, and a funeral after. It might feel strange and busy all at once. I wanted ye to know that now so it don't catch ye by surprise. Go on now, the two of ye, go on and play.
