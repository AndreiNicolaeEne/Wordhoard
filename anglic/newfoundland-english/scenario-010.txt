The mother: Now then, ye're all going to stand there till I gets the truth of it. The good lamp's smashed to pieces on the floor and I never broke it, so who's after doing it.

The eldest: Wasn't me. I never even been in the front room the day.

The mother: Ye're some quick to say that, aren't ye.

The middle one: 'Twasn't me neither, Mudder. I'd say 'twas the cat, he was up on the sill after dinner and I heard some racket, a right crash, and the window was open too, some drafty in there, coulda blown it right off the shelf.

The mother: The cat, is it. Luh, the cat's been shut in the porch the whole day, I had him out there meself since breakfast, so how's he after knocking anything down in here.

The middle one: Well... maybe he got in when the door was open, I never seen him meself, but sure buddy's always getting into places he's not s'posed to be.

The eldest: That's a right lie, b'y. You're the one always leaning on that sideboard, I seen ye at it plenty.

The middle one: I never. You're the one always poking round after something, ye're the right sleeveen round here, not me.

The youngest: I never touched nothing, I was in me room the whole time, ye can ax.

The mother: Nobody axed you yet, love, but go on.

The middle one: Alright, I mighta brushed by it going for me coat, but I never knocked it, it was rocking already, I swears to God.

The eldest: Rocking, is it. A minute ago 'twas the cat and the wind, now ye're brushing by it. Buddy's after changing his story three times now and he still thinks we's foolish enough to believe him.

The mother: Enough out of the whole shockin lot of ye. You knows one of ye done it and none of ye's owning it, so nobody's getting the telly the night, not one of ye, and that's the end of it. Oh me nerves, I can't be doing with this racket the day.
