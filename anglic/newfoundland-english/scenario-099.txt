Me: Well, look what the tide washed in. B'y, I never.

The old classmate: Go way b'y! What're the odds of that, running into ye right here by the Dominion.

Me: I know it. I'm just after doing me whole week's shop, look at the state of me, bags up to me elbows. Where ya to these days anyway, I never sees ye around no more.

The old classmate: Still out the Goulds, meself. You?

Me: Still up around Kilbride. Some mauzy out the day, isn't it, can't hardly see the church from the road.

The old classmate: Shockin. And they're calling for a silver thaw on the wires tonight too, so that's grand, that is. How's herself and the youngsters?

Me: Good, good, she's got her hands full but she's good. Yours?

The old classmate: Can't complain. Well I could, but sure who'd listen.

Me: True enough, b'y.

The old classmate: So what's ye been at, all these years.

Me: Working, mostly. Same as everyone. God, though, it don't feel like years since we was in that school together.

The old classmate: Don't it just. I was thinking about that the other day, matter of fact. You remember the teacher we had, the one everybody was scared stiff of?

Me: Oh me nerves, don't. I still gets a shiver thinking of it.

The old classmate: The rule was you never spoke unless you was spoke to first. Never mind putting your hand up and waiting, that wasn't good enough either, you had to be called on by name or you kept your mouth shut, full stop. One word out of turn and that was it.

Me: And the punishment, b'y. You remember the punishment.

The old classmate: The lines.

Me: The lines. A hundred times, every single time, didn't matter what you done, big thing or small thing, it was a hundred lines of whatever sentence was picked for ye. I still remembers the feeling in me hand after, like it was somebody else's hand.

The old classmate: And you had to bring it in the next morning, counted out, and if you were even one short you started the whole hundred again.

Me: I can hear the voice still, if I close me eyes. "Now, I've got eyes in the back of me head, and don't any one of ye forget it." Said that every day, near enough, whole class dead quiet.

The old classmate: Stop it, I'm getting the chills just hearing ye say it.

Me: And you remember what happened to poor auld what's-his-name, the one that got sent up front for talking during the lesson?

The old classmate: Oh God, yes. Stood him right up there in front of everybody and made him say the whole thing back, word for word, what he'd been caught saying, out loud, in front of the whole class. Face like a beet the whole time. We were all just sat there not breathing, watching it happen, glad to God it wasn't we.

Me: I still remembers freezing right up meself, one time, getting called on and me mind just went, gone, blank as the wall. Couldn't get one word out. The silence after was worse than anything.

The old classmate: Everybody's been there. I still feels it now saying it out loud.

Me: But do you remember the one time somebody talked back? I still can't believe it happened, to this day.

The old classmate: Oh, I do. That was some shockin thing to see.

Me: Got called out for something, I forget what now, and instead of taking it, just come right out with it, plain as anything, right in front of everyone. And nothing happened. No lines, no nothing. We were all sat there waiting for the roof to come down and it never did.

The old classmate: I still don't know how that one got away with it. We used to talk about it for weeks after, like it was some kind of legend.

Me: A right sleeveen, that one, always was.

The old classmate: You remember the little habit, though, the tic. The throat clearing, before every single sentence, like clockwork.

Me: Lord, yes, we used to do it behind the back the whole time, the whole class at it, clearing our throats every time the teacher turned around. Never got caught either, God knows how.

The old classmate: And yet. Do you remember the one time, with the young one that lost the father that year? Teacher never said a word about lines or rules that whole week, just let that one sit quiet at the back, and even walked the child home once, I heard, in the rain and all. Never told a soul about it either, we only found out after.

Me: I forgot about that till you said it just now. That was some different side of it altogether.

The old classmate: Makes ye wonder, don't it.

Me: What ever became of the teacher after, do you know? I heard retired years back, out around the bay somewhere.

The old classmate: I heard that too, but I also heard passed on a few years after that. Somebody else told me still alive and well and living out near Bay Roberts. I don't rightly know which of it's true.

Me: Nobody ever seems to know for certain, do they.

The old classmate: No, they don't.

Me: Looking back on it now though, all that fear, it wasn't hate we had for the teacher, was it. More like respect, if I'm honest.

The old classmate: That's exactly it. I was terrified of that teacher for two solid years and now I'd near call it the best teaching I ever got.

Me: The both of us still shaking in our boots thinking about hundred lines, and grown adults now, the pair of us.

The old classmate: Ha! Go way, we are and all.

Me: I still feels the flicker of it, though, honest to God. Somebody raises their voice sharp like that even now and something in me wants to sit up straight and fold me hands.

The old classmate: Same here, b'y, same exact here.

Me: Anyway, enough of the old days. How's the rest of the crowd from back then, do you ever hear tell of anybody?

The old classmate: A few. One's out in Alberta still, doing well for themself out there. Another one's gone and got married, twice now if you can believe it.

Me: Go way. And here's we still stuck to the rock.

The old classmate: Wouldn't have it no other way meself. You still doing the mug-up on a Sunday with the crowd, or has that fell off?

Me: Still at it, when we can get everyone together. Had a boil-up down the shore not two weeks back, some good it was too, the whole crowd of us.

The old classmate: I'm some jealous now. We'll have to get a scoff together ourselves sometime, catch up proper, not just standing here in the parking lot with me arms full of groceries.

Me: That'd be right nice. Give us a call, we'll sort something out before the duckish gets any earlier this time of year.

The old classmate: Will do, b'y. Some good seeing ye, honest.

Me: Likewise, my love. Take care of yourself now.

The old classmate: You too. See ye round.
