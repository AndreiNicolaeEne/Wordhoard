Me: Luh, sit down a minute, I got to say something and I'm not letting you off till you hears me out. That skidoo, b'y. Out on the tickle in that mauzy weather Tuesday gone. I'm after not sleeping right since.

My friend: Whadda y'at, sure I'm grand, what's all this for.

Me: You're not grand, you went through the ice to your waist. Right through, b'y. I was standing on the bank and I seen the whole of it, and me hands haven't stopped shaking since, I still can't hold a mug proper. My heart was going like a hammer the whole time your buddy was hauling on that rope.

My friend: Go way b'y, it was only to the waist, I've been out on worse ice than that a hundred times, I knows that tickle better than anyone.

Me: You knows it and it still nearly had you. Do you mind when you were a youngster and went through on the pond, shallow as a boil-up pot, and still come out with your arm broke in two places? This isn't a pond, this is a proper current under that tickle. Next time there's no buddy with a rope out there. Next time it's your mother picking a coffin and your youngsters with nothing left of you but stories at a mug-up.

My friend: ...

Me: Who's left grieving then, eh? Who's saying the rosary over you? I don't want to be the one standing there.

My friend: Alright, alright, oh me nerves, would ya stop. You've made your point, some plain and all. I won't go out alone no more, and I'll wait on the ice report before I goes at all. That's the best I got for ya, but it's real.

Me: That's all I'm asking, b'y. That's all.
