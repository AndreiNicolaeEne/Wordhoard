Me: Alright now, you got to give me your toutons the proper way this time, none of your "a shake of this, a shake of that."

The aunt: Luh, I only got it the one way, in me head, so don't be expecting cups and spoons off of me.

Me: That's fine, just talk me through it, I'm writing it down as ye go.

The aunt: Right so. You starts with your flour, and I'd say four cups, give or take, depends on the day, depends on the flour even.

Me: Four cups. Okay.

The aunt: And your salt, don't forget the salt. I nearly did meself just now, I'm after almost leaving it out altogether, but you got to have your bit of salt in there or 'tis flat as anything.

Me: Salt, got it, after the flour.

The aunt: Then your baking powder, two spoons, mix your dry together first, that's always the way of it. Then you works in your lard, cold, rubbing it in with your fingers till it's like crumbs, and then your milk, however much it takes till the dough comes together. I never measured that part in me life, you just knows it by feel, when it's not sticking to your hand no more.

Me: By feel, right.

The aunt: Now here's the one your grandmother was always on about, b'y, you lets that dough rest. She'd say to me, don't you dare rush that, don't you dare fry it too soon, or they'll be raw in the middle and you'll be some shocked when you bites in.

Me: How long's the rest, though?

The aunt: However long it needs. I lets it sit till near duckish some evenings and I still don't watch no clock for it, I just knows.

Me: And the frying?

The aunt: In your scrunchions fat, and you knows they're done when they's puffed up golden and they floats when you flips them, no timer needed, you just watches the pan.

Me: Could I use butter instead of the pork fat, if I got none?

The aunt: You could, I s'pose, if you're stuck, right, but 'tisn't the same at all. I'd say do it proper with the scrunchions fat if you can get it, b'y.
