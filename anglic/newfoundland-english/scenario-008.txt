The child: Mom, can I get the bike, b'y, the one with the gears and everything?

The mother: No, my love, I'm after tellin' ye three times now, we haven't got the money for it, that's the plain size of it. Nothin' more to it than that.

The child: I'll do the dishes every night for a whole month, and take out the garbage too, if I gets it.

The mother: Chores is chores, ye does them anyway, that's not buyin' nothin', b'y. Now. If ye keeps yer marks up and yer birthday comes round in the spring, we'll see what's left in the jar then, and maybe, maybe, we'll talk about it again. No promises made now, mind.

The child: But buddy up the road already got one, and his one's got a bell and a basket and all!

The mother: What buddy up the road got is buddy up the road's business. His mother and father's affairs is theirs, ours is ours, and I'm not measurin' what we can do against what some other crowd can do. That never settled nothin' in this house.

The child: 'tis not fair, though.

The mother: I knows it don't feel fair, love, oh me nerves, don't be lookin' at me so. Luh, I got a few dollars put by for the day. We'll go down and get ye a mug-up at the corner, and I'll see about them mitts ye been on about. They're not the bike, but they'll do ye some good for now.

The child: ...s'pose that'll do. For now, anyway.

The mother: For now, anyway. Go on and get yer coat, we'll go before it gets duckish out.
