The foreman: Now b'ys, before we're all four sheets to the wind and forgets the whole of it, we got to sort tomorrow out.

The young one: What hour, though. I'm not standin round freezin in the mauzy at six waitin on someone that's after not showin.

The eldest: Six is soft, sure. Give the frost time to come off her first.

Buddy: Seven, I says. No sense breakin our backs before the sun's even up over it.

The foreman: Seven so. Wharf out the tickle, same as we left her. I'm bringin the compressor and the good ladder, you got the drill still charged?

Buddy: Charged and ready, yeah.

The young one: I got the levels and the extra rope, I'll throw the tarps in the truck too case it comes on to rain.

The eldest: It will come on, luh. Sky's some ugly out over the water already, I don't like the look of her a-tall.

The foreman: We're short on the good screws besides, only the one box left after the day. Whoever's doin the flashin better make do.

The eldest: Go way b'y, that's me every time, isn't it, stuck up top with half enough screws and the wind pickin up. Ye loves puttin that on me.

The foreman: Sure you're the only one don't fall off, that's why.

The eldest: Oh me nerves.

The foreman: Now, who's all comin. That's four we needs and only four we got.

Buddy: The young one's brother's not able, he's after pullin somethin in his back hauling gear the day.

The foreman: That's shockin timing, that is.

The eldest: Well I s'pose I'm doin two men's work then on top of the flashin, ye all owes me a scoff after.

Buddy: Owes you nothing, you still owes me from the last one.

The foreman: Seven o'clock, no lyin about it. Right, are we good.

The young one: Right good. Seven, b'y.
