We was down to the wharf duckish, after haulin the boat up for the winter, and somebody left the plug out of her the night before. She'd took on some water overside her moorins by mornin, gear floatin, the little kicker soaked right through, and buddy in charge come down and near lost his nerves over it, everybody else just standin round in the mess.

The one in charge: 'Twas you took that plug out, wasn't it, b'y.

The young one: No.

The one in charge: Don't be tellin me no, I knows 'twas you, you was the last one down here.

The young one: I never went near her.

The one in charge: I'm sayin it again, 'twas you took the plug and never put it back.

The young one: I swears before God I had no hand in it.

And that was all he said on it, flat as that, no more explainin than it needed. His face never moved the whole time buddy was roarin, red as a beet and shockin loud for the whole wharf to hear, but the young one just stood there some calm, lettin him at it, givin him none of what he was after. The two lads that was down tinkerin with her lines the evening before, in the mauzy after supper, never said one word, just stood off lookin at their boots like sleeveens, lettin the young one carry the can for it. No sorry come off no one, no one owned up to nothin, and when buddy run out of steam the young one picked up the bailer and walked off up the ramp, leavin the denial sittin right there where 'twas said, and nobody touched it after.
