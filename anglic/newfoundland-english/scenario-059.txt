It come down right sudden the evening, just gone duckish, the whole house dark as the inside of a boot. Some mauzy out all day already, and then a silver thaw come in on top of it after dark, ice thick as your thumb on the wires out back. Buddy from down the road said after 'twas a pole gone over by the tickle road, snapped clean with the weight of it.

Mother was into the drawer in the kitchen quick, feeling round for the flashlight, and the flashlight was dead as a hammer, batteries gone soft on we months ago and nobody thought to change them.

Mother: Where's the candles to, one of ye go and find the candles.

The eldest: I'm after looking in that drawer, there's ne'er a one in it.

We found two stubs in the end, down the back of the cupboard behind the tins, and one no bigger than me thumb. Wasn't enough to go round the whole house so we give that up and kept the one going in the kitchen, everyone crowded round it like moths. The eldest got on the phone to her workmate to see was they in the dark too.

The eldest: Where ya to, girl, whadda y'at, is your light gone as well?

Father knocked into the good chair going for the woodstove, cursed, and not ten minutes after done it again, same chair, same shin.

Father: Oh me nerves, somebody move that thing.

Me: Sure ye can't see nothing, b'y, leave it till the day.

He never did move it, and nobody was mending anything that night either, too dark and too cold to be bothered rooting for tools. The youngest was some angishore about it, sooking into the mother's side the whole time, scared of the shadows the candle throwed on the wall. Mother got the woodstove going right hot and made a scoff of toutons on top of it, and we had them with molasses standing round, some good after an evening like that. The youngest settled down some once there was food in him.

Come near ten the light still hadn't come back, so we give up waiting on it and went up to bed in the dark, feeling our way up the stairs by the wall, and 'twasn't till the morning we knew the power was back, the clocks all blinking away on the stove.
