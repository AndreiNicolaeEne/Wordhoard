Before day clean good we was already up. Cock nuh even crow proper yet and mi mother a bang de pot-spoon pon de step, calling all a we out de bed. Sky still black-black, only a lil strip a grey behind de coconut tree dem, and de whole yard smelling a wet grass and mud from de backdam.

The mother: Get up, get up, dis nuh time fu sleep, de rice a wait pon we, rain or no rain it nuh gon cut itself.

Mi father stand up by de door wid de lantern in e hand, calling out who gon do wha before we even reach de field good.

The father: Yuh, tek de small basket and go round de edge, pick up wha grain drop off and keep de fowl dem off de heap till we bring de first bundle down.

That was fu de youngest, and e nuh complain one bit, tek de basket and gone running same time, glad e nuh haffi bend down cutting all day like de rest a we.

Mi uncle, big man wid hand dem like plantain bunch, e get de heaviest part put pon him. Mi father point him to de whole back section, de long strip wha did flood worse and de stalk dem grow tick tick together.

The father: Yuh a de strongest one so tek de back field, cut am clean, we go come round and help once we clear de front.

So we start reaping steady, sickle in hand, bend low, cut, tie, lay down, cut, tie, lay down, de sun climbing slow slow behind de cloud dem. By de time de front section clear, mi father call out fu change up.

The father: Leave de cutting now, come help carry, de bundle dem a pile up too much fu leave so.

Nuh fuss pass, everybody just shift, dem wha been cutting tek up de bags and start haul, dem wha been carrying tek de sickle instead, and we keep going same rhythm like before.

Round about mid-morning mi aunty start fall back a lil, she nuh saying nutten, but yuh could see she row getting shorter and shorter behind everybody else, she hand moving slower, she back bend more than usual. Nobody nuh call she out pon it. Mi cousin just quiet quiet move over and start cut half a she row wid she, nuh a word pass between dem, just so, and she keep up better after dat.

Bout eleven mi mother and de youngest come out wid de food, big pot a rice and stew fish, roti wrap up warm in a cloth, and a big jug a mauby cold from de fridge. We all siddown under de mango tree by de trench side, wipe we face wid we shirt tail, and nyam like we nuh eat in a week.

While we eating mi uncle start tease mi cousin bout e pace.

An uncle: Bwoy yuh a cut like yuh sleeping standing up, de grass gon grow back before yuh even done one row.

A cousin: Steups. Better dat dan how yuh a leave half de stalk stand up in de ground behind yuh, yuh a waste good paddy fu speed.

Everybody laugh, even mi father, and dem start up a whole argument bout whose method better, low cut or high cut, till mi mother tell dem stop de noise and finish eat before de food get cold.

The mother: Unu done wid de mouth now, eat and rest a lil, de work nuh going nowhere.

Just so, de wind start pick up hard and de sky behind de backdam turn dark-dark real quick, thunder rolling far off but coming closer. Mi father stand up and look up long, and e seh de word same time.

The father: Rain a come fast, we nuh have time fu play, leave de far section, cover de bundle dem first before we lose wha we already reap.

Change a plan same time, everybody run go tarpaulin de cut paddy stack up by de trench before de water could spoil am. De first drop start fall heavy heavy, we get soak through in a minute, but it nuh stay long, pass over quick and de sun come back out strong after, steam a rise off de ground.

We push through de afternoon hard after dat, nuh much talk now, just cut and tie and carry, cut and tie and carry, one after de next. Round about two mi uncle cutlass blade catch a stone hid down in de mud and chip up bad, e nuh cutting clean no more, just a-drag through de stalk.

An uncle: Cuh dear, dis blade dun fu today, look how e chip.

Mi father nuh even stop long, e reach in de punt and hand him de spare one wha bin deh a de bottom wrap up in a old cloth.

The father: Tek dis one, keep going, we nuh have time fu lose.

By de time light start fu go, mi father walk de field one time and call out to all a we.

The father: Mek haste now, de sun nearly done, we nuh want lef nutten out here fu de night, unu push.

Everybody pick up de pace same time, even de youngest come back from picking grain and start bundle up de loose paddy e been collecting all day long into one heap fu de last carry.

De last section, everybody converge pon am same time, mi uncle coming from de back, mi cousin and mi aunty from de middle, mi father and de rest a we from de front, all a we meet up in dat last strip a paddy and finish am off together, nuh one blade a rice lef standing when we done.

When we done, mi father and mi uncle count up de bag dem wha stack up by de punt side.

The father: Forty-two bag today. Dat more dan wha we bring in same time last year.

Everybody proud fu hear dat, even tired as we was, somebody give a lil cheer.

Soon as de last bag tie off good, everybody just drop down right where dem standing, back a hurt bad, hand dem blister up, nuh energy lef fu even talk much, just siddung pon de bank and breathe long breath, looking out pon de field wha did full a rice dis morning and now bare clean.

Before anybody could rest good though, mi mother call out one more time to de youngest.

The mother: Go close up de koker before de water rise and spoil wha we work all day long fu bring in, then yuh could rest.

De youngest groan loud, but get up slow slow and gone do am, dragging e foot de whole way, and only after dat, when de koker shut and de water hold back, everybody else could finally lay down and rest good.
