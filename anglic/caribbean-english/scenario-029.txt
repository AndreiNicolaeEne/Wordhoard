Yuh really want mi tell yuh how mornings does run eena dis house? Doh mek face, siddown good, cause once mi start pon dis one mi cyan stop till di whole t'ing done, start to finish, same as it play out every blessed day.

Awright. Di granny a di one always up first, before anybody, before di sun even good rise, before any alarm ready fi ring. Nobody nuh tell she fi get up so, she jus does wake sheself, quiet quiet, and go straight fi di little enamel cup she does keep pon di window ledge. She does put on di kettle low, no noise, and while it a heat she does stand up by di back door and water di two pot plant dem she keep dere, one leaf at a time, like she counting dem. Every day. Same cup, same window, same two plant. Yuh could set yuh watch by she and nuh need no watch again.

Den di husband wake. Same grumble, every single morning, like di grumble itself deh pon a timer. Husband: "Which one a unu inna di wash-room awready?" And dat a where di real contest start, cause di eldest done deh in dere from before cock crow, door lock, water a run, and nobody nuh business how long she tekking. 'E does bang di door soft-soft first, den harder. Husband: "Mek haste nuh, mi have work fi go!" And she nuh answer, cause she know if she answer she waste more time. Dat contest deh nuh settle from since, and it nah go settle, cause nobody really want it settle, it a jus part a how di house does start.

Di pickney dem get roused same order, every day, no matter what. Eldest first, cause she tekking di wash-room hostage anyway. Middle one after, always by a shake pon di shoulder and di same words. Me: "Get up nuh, sun deh up awready, yuh want mi call yuh twice?" Youngest last, cause di small one does sleep like dead and only rousing fi di smell a bake pon di fire. And di commands does come in dat same fixed order too, one after di next, loud enough fi carry through di whole house: "Wash yuh face! Brush yuh teet! Come nyam befo' it cold!" Same three, same order, every morning a mi life.

And den di argument. Almost every day, close to di same words. Middle one seh eldest tek she hairbrush and nuh put it back, eldest seh she nuh even touch di brush, and it does end wid middle one steupsing loud, one long sucked-teet dat carry from di bedroom clean to di kitchen, and eldest bawling back, "Yuh always seh dat!" Same argument, same steups, practically same time pon di clock.

Di dog have 'e part too, and 'e nuh miss it. Soon as 'e hear di bread van bell ring out pon di road, 'e start bark, same three bark, den 'e siddown by di back step and wait, cause 'e know bake crust does come 'e way once di frying start. If di van pass and 'e miss di bark somehow, di whole house does notice, cause dat mean somebody nuh watching di time good.

Breakfast does go di same way every day self, bake pon di fire, saltfish fry off wid tomato and onion, tea mek strong. Husband nuh vary 'e order, not one day. Husband: "Black tea, one sugar, nuh more nuh less, and mek sure di bake nuh hard." 'E does tell mi seh a soft bake or none a-tall, and mi does laugh, cause 'e been telling mi dat same t'ing since long before di pickney dem born.

Something does go missing most mornings too, di youngest one shoe, one a di pair, gone. We does hunt it same few places every time: under di bed, behind di door, and somehow, half di time, right dere by where di dog siddung, like di dog tek it fi safekeeping. And once di kettle start whistling pon di fire, dat a di sound dat mean di whole house finally properly awake, everybody know dat whistle mean time done run out fi lounging.

Di eldest a di one always running few minutes late, every day, no matter how early she get roused. And so di rush start at di door, coat grab, umbrella push inna somebody hand cause rainy season nuh play, book bag pass out one by one, and somehow, every day, nothing get left behind, only cause it never do.

Sunday different though. Whole rhythm change and di house gone quiet instead, no shouting, no steups, no bake pon di fire same way. Granny press she white blouse, everybody move slow, church clothes come out, and even di dog seem to know fi hold 'e bark.

But every morning dat a school or work morning, same handful a words does close it off, right dere at di door befo' anybody step out. Granny: "Walk good, mind how unu gwan." And mi does stand up dere every time and watch dem gone, and mi cyan help but find di whole repeating performance foolish and sweet at di same time, di same grumble, same contest, same steups, same missing shoe, same words at di door, day in day out, and here you deh, a guest weh never see one single morning a it, and mi jus tell yuh di whole t'ing like it nuh nothing at all.
