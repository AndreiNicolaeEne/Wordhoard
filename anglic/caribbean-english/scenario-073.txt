Aright, siddown nice, mek yuh get de real story, not no half-piece somebody gon give yuh later and lef out de important part. Yuh reach we now, so yuh haffi know how de whole ting start, from de very first night, straight through.

We reach ten years now since dis ting start, and is fu me cousin we haffi thank fu all a it, cause she da de one who bring de core a we together first. Was one Old Year's Night, back she yard in Kitty, she seh she cyan siddown one more year watching TV wid she mother waiting fi midnight, so she call up whoever she know from work and whoever she know from de street and seh come. Was maybe eight a we dat first night, squeeze up een de yard, black cake and ginger beer pan de table, radio a play, and by de time twelve o'clock bell ring and everybody hug up one anodda, dat was it, dat set de whole ting off. Nobody plan fi start no group, it jus happen so, and it never stop since.

Now de one who almost never stay part a we, dat one did quiet quiet dat first year. Come de first Old Year's Night, sit een one corner, nah gaff wid nobody, and after, e tell me straight seh e nah tink e a go come back, seh e feel like e nah belong, everybody done know one anodda already and e jus deh deh like extra. I nah leave dat one so. I start walk wid am pan de seawall Sunday evening, jus de two a we, nah rushing nutten, till e start open up bit by bit. By de time de nex' Old Year's Night reach, e was de one liming hardest a all a we, cracking joke, and now. I gon come to dat part.

Bout de second or third year, two a we fall out bad. One lend de odda money fi help buy a phone, and when time come fi pay back, de money nah come, and dem stop talk fi bout a month, walking round one anodda like stranger. It was over a domino game one Sunday dat it patch up, dem end up pan de same side by accident, and when de last piece fall and dem win, one a dem jump up shout "duppy tek it!" bout de money dat did missing, meaning de whole debt gone same way duppy tek someting, jus vanish, done, forget it. From dat day, anytime someting gone missing round we, a piece a chicken, a phone charger, a lil bit a rum out de bottle, is "duppy tek it" everybody seh, and everybody know exactly wha dat mean and who it come from, and it still mek people laugh till now, even dem two demself.

We lose one from de group bout six years back. E win de visa lottery and gone a States, and de reason e gi we at de time was straight, e seh e nah want grow old een Guyana wid no work fi match wha e study fah, e seh e haffi tek de chance while e get it. Everybody understand, nobody vex bout it, we gi am a proper sendoff, cook-up rice and de whole yard pack out dat night, but e leaving lef a lil hole dat still deh deh, even now.

Little after dat, one join we new. De cousin bring am een, seh dis de bredda a she in-law, good fella, allyuh gon like am. But we nah jus tek anybody een so quick, we watch am good. Did e show up when e seh e a come, did e chip in fu de food, did e know how fi tek a joke widout get vex. Bout a year we did watching before anybody call am "one a we" straight out, no shame een dat, is how we stay. E pass de test though, and now e deh so deep een it, nobody remember life before am.

De ting dat really bind we tight was de trip we tek up to Bartica dat Easter. Was s'pose to be one simple regatta trip, boat gone up, lime by de river, boat come back down de same night. But de boat engine cut out halfway, and we did stuck out deh pan de water past midnight, no food lef but a lil biscuit and one warm soft drink pass round, mosquito a bite we up bad, and everybody frighten but nobody show it too much, we jus mek joke de whole time fi keep spirit up. By de time we reach back Georgetown de next morning, sun jus a come up, we did tired bad, but we did closer than we ever was before dat. Is one absence we all still feel from dat trip and every trip since, de one who gone a States nah deh pan dat boat wid we, and every year when Easter come round again, somebody gon mention am, seh "e woulda love dis," and it still touch a lil someting, even now.

As it stand today, is fixed how everybody play dem part. De cousin still organize everyting, she call de date, she book de place, she text everybody twice cause she know unu forget de first time. De one who join later, e always late, every single time, wedding, funeral, don't matter, e reach when e ready, and de cousin jus steups and shake she head and tell am de time an hour before everybody else, jus so e even out. And de one who almost never stay, dat one settle right down as de one who keep de peace, when voice start raise bout politics or bout money or bout who nah pay dem share, is dat one calm everybody down, always de one wid de steady word, always de one who seh "wait nuh, mek we hear de odda side first."

De lowest we ever get was over a box-hand, a lil susu we did run amongst weself, everybody putting een every month, one draw dem share een turn. One year, de person hand come round and de money nah deh, short bad, and everybody start eye one anodda, trust gone cold quick, people stop answer phone, stop come to gaff. It tek de cousin fi call one proper sit-down, everybody een one room, nobody excuse.

The cousin: "Wunna cyan gaff behind one anodda back no more bout dis. Whoever short, seh it out now, one time, front a everybody."

The one who was short: "I shoulda seh someting soona. I was short pan rent dat month and I never seh nutten, I did shame. I gon mek it right, every cent, I promise unu dat."

Dem pay back every lil bit over de following few months, and after dat sit-down we mek a new rule, nobody shame fi seh dem struggling, tell we first before it come to dis. We nyam cook-up together dat same night, big pot, everybody bring someting, and by de time everybody done eat, most a de bad feeling did gone.

But de one ting dat nah change from dat very first night to now, is Old Year's Night. Wherever we deh, whoever gone or come, some a we wid pickney a run bout de yard now too, we still gather, still black cake and ginger beer pan de table, still counting down together, still hugging up one anodda when de bell ring. Ten years, and dat one ting nah miss a year yet.

An yuh, since yuh reach we, yuh done already start pay yuh way een. Yuh bring de phulourie last cook-up widout nobody ask yuh, and yuh remember de cousin nah eat pepper, so yuh mek she plate separate widout even mek noise bout it. Small ting, but is dem small ting show we how yuh a mind we, how yuh watching widout being tell to. So welcome yuh proper, yuh nah no guest no more. Yuh close as kin now, same as de rest a we.
