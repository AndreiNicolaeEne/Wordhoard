Me: Bey, I nuh know how yinna mother could say dat bout the peas an' rice, "this could use more seasonin'," right in front everybody, an' she nuh even nyam it good sheself.

My love: Steups, she just so, that's all. She always a run she mouth bout somebody food.

Me: 'E did say it plain plain doh. I nearly drop my fork.

My love: Leave she, love. She mean well.

Me: An' yinna daddy, 'e tell dat same fishin' story from back before we married, third time tonight. 'E ben tell we dat story already di last time we deh there.

My love: (laughs) 'E does love dat story eh. Di whole table sittin' there noddin' like we never hear it before.

Me: Steups.

My love: Yeah, 'e do run on so.

Me: An' then the goodbye! Forty-five minute at the door, yinna mother huggin' me, then talkin' bout the neighbour pickney dem, then the potcake come beg round the yard, then she start back up wid the sip-sip bout somebody wedding...

My love: I know, I know. I was standin' there sayin' "awright, awright," an' she nuh hearin' none a it.

Me: I thought we was gon' sleep in dat yard.

My love: (sighs) You right doh. She does carry on so every time we deh there. I love she to death but Lord.

Me: An' yinna daddy tellin' me "unu must come more often," bey, we just there!

My love: True, true. Every visit da the same show: she a pick at the food, 'e a tell the story, an' the goodbye takin' longer than the whole visit.

Me: Next time we tell dem we cyan stay long, we got somewhere fi go.

My love: We ain' got nowhere fi go.

Me: I know. But we sayin' it anyway.
