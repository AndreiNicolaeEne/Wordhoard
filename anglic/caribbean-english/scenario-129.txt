Bey, that fire day start off ordinary ordinary. Round early afternoon I deh on the porch and I spot this smoke way out on the horizon, just a small grey smudge over the pines, still far far. I figure a nothing but somebody burning bush like they does do out that way regular, so I ain't pay it no mind, just deh there watching, sipping my tea.

But within the hour the wind pick up bad and push that smoke right this way, and my eye dem start to sting for true. My neighbor come to the fence and she seh, "Gyal, you smell that? It a-come fast fast, and if the wind nuh change it go reach we yard next, yinna better watch yourself." That's when watching turn to leaving, time fi go fi true.

Once I decide, I nuh waste one more minute. I swear 'fore God I was out that place in twenty minutes, gone with only what matter, the papers dem, a lil clothes, and the potcake jump straight in the car, ain't even ask permission. My throat gone raw from all that smoke, and the heat pressing on my skin like a hot iron the whole way to the car. I look back one time before I turn the corner and see the sky lit up orange, red, like the whole hill afire behind me, and I just drive, ain't stop for nothing.

Two whole night I stay by my workmate, not knowing nothing bout my own place. Phone ringing off the hook, everybody sip-sipping bout who lost this and who lost that, who house gone and who spare. My workmate say, "Come go lay down, worrying nuh go help nothing tonight." But I couldn't hardly sleep, just turning and turning, wondering if it still deh there.

Third morning them finally let we go back check, and when I reach up the road, the place still deh, standing up just as I leave it, ain't even singed. I tell you, I steups out all that worry and just stand up in the yard and bawl. The Lord spare we this time, true true, but Lord, that fire ben close.
