Every time somebody young come round in de evenin' askin' why old people nuh want dem walkin' certain road after dark, is de same story dat does come up. Dat's how it always start, chile, not because anybody go lookin' for it, but because somebody new to de place, or somebody who ain' been listenin' good all dem years, finally ask outright: why dey nuh let unu walk down dat bend by de old salt pond road when de sun done gone down? And once dat question drop, well, is like a door open, and de story just walk through it de same way it always do.

Dis a de same story every time, mi tell you, and nobody ever see de whole of it, and dat's part a what mek it so. 'E ain' got no shape you could point to and seh, "dat's it right dey." What 'e got is a voice. 'E does take a voice before 'e take anyt'ing else, and dat's how everybody who ever come close to it describe it, generation after generation, de old head dem always seh de same t'ing, you feel it more dan you see it, like somet'ing standin' just outside where de lantern light does reach, and de first t'ing you know 'bout it is it done call your name already.

And is not any old voice neither. Dis t'ing does call in de voice of somebody you love. Somebody dead, somebody far, don't matter, it does sound exactly like dem, down to how dem does say your name, down to de little catch dem does have in dey throat when dem worried 'bout you. And de whole point of it, de only point of it, is fi draw you off de path and 'way from anybody could help you if t'ing gone wrong. It nuh want company. It want you by yourself.

'E only does come after midnight, mind you, and only on dem night when de moon nuh show 'e face at all, new-moon night, when de road black-black and you cyan see your own hand front a you. Full moon, half moon, even a sliver, and people say you safe enough. Is de dark, de real dark, when it does walk.

Right round here, is dat bend on de old salt pond road dey does tie it to, down where de bush thick on one side and de water does sit still and quiet on de other, right before de road curve sharp. Nobody in dis family ever seh it belong to nowhere bigger dan dat one bend. Dey nuh talk 'bout it like it own de whole island, or even de whole settlement. Is dat one spot. You could walk every other road round here in pitch black and come to no harm, is dat bend, and dat bend alone, people mean when dem seh, "don't tek dat road after dark."

Mi first hear dis story when mi was a pickney self, no bigger dan you is now, from mi own grandmother, sittin' out on de step in de evenin' wit' de potcake curl up nyamin' on a bone by 'e foot. She tell mi seh duppy business nuh play business, and mi never forget dat. Mi ben hear a next version too, from mi granddaddy side, but is dis one dat stick. Sometime she tell it plain, like she tellin' you de time a day, no drama in it, just fact, de way you'd tell somebody de church start at eleven. And sometime she tell it wit' a little laugh in 'e voice, like she teasin', but even den, dat laugh nuh reach up to 'e eye. 'E eye stay still de whole time. Mi never did know, as a chile, which one to believe, de plain-fact grandmother or de laughin' one, and to dis day mi still nuh sure dey was two different women.

De first proof anybody does bring out, if you ask dem straight, is de patch a ground right dey by de bend. Nut'ing grow dey. Nut'ing. People try, somebody put in sea grape one time, somebody else try a lil sisal patch, and it always die back, every time, like de ground itself nuh want it. Been like dat longer dan anybody livin' could tell you.

Second proof does come de way dem t'ing always come, secondhand, wit' a somebody who heard it from a somebody. "Mi cousin friend hear it one time, comin' from work late." "De one who does clean at de church, she seh she nuh even walk near dey in daytime no more." Somebody run go tell 'e own sister de next mornin', and by evenin' de whole road hear it. You could never run it back to de actual person it happen to. It just deh, floatin' round in de sip-sip, passin' mouth to mouth till it done wear smooth like a stone.

But de one dey does tell in full, every single time, front to back, no skippin', is what happen to mi own relative, years back now. 'E was comin' home late one night, walkin' dat very road, and 'e hear dem name call, clear, plain, in a voice 'e swear down to dis day was 'e own mother, dead already t'ree year by den. 'E ain' even question it. 'E turn straight off de path toward where de voice comin' from, and 'e gone t'ree, four step into de bush before somet'ing, 'e cyan tell you what, to dis day, mek 'e stop cold and turn back round.

And what 'e swear, word for word, every time de family tell it: "Mi mother voice call mi name, and mi start go to 'e, and den somet'ing tell mi, dat's nuh she. Dat's nuh she at all." Dat's it. Same words every time, from every mouth dat carry de story.

De one detail dat does mek even de doubter in de room go quiet a second is dat de voice call 'e full name, not a nickname, not what people call 'e round de yard, but de name only 'e mother, and nobody else livin', ever use. How a duppy know dat, dem cyan answer.

But de other detail, just as real, is what give de doubter dey opening to laugh de whole t'ing off after, dat mi relative had been drinkin' a lil rum wit' friend before 'e start dat walk home. "Rum call your mother name too, sometime," dem does seh, steups, and mek joke of it. Mi nuh argue wit' dem. Both t'ing true at de same time, and mi learn long time to let dem sit side by side.

De story change a lil dependin' who tellin' it, some mouth mek it soft, like a warnin' wrap in love, and some mouth mek it hard, like a straight-up warnin' and nut'ing more. Mi never mind de difference. Is de same bend, de same voice, de same three-four step, no matter which way it come out somebody mouth.

You hear how de bush a rustle out dey right now? Dat's why mi keep mi voice low. And to dis day, right round here, people still do de one small t'ing: nobody nuh answer if dem name call from somewhere dark till dem check wit' dem own two eye who really callin'. If it ever go call your name, chile, mek sure a your own two eye you trust first, before your two foot move one inch. Yinna could laugh, but watch how quick even de hardest head round here go still when a voice call out from de bush at night.

Mi gon tell you straight, bey, since is jus' you and mi and de dark comin' down now, mi nuh fully believe it meself. Mi done live too long and see too much foolishness people does swear to. But mi never, not one single time in mi whole life, answer a voice call mi name outta de dark 'fore mi check wit' mi own eye first. Not once.
