Me: Bey, pull up dat chair, siddown right deh, leh me tell you bout de people next door since you movin in fresh and I already see how dem eye pass over you when you walk by. I go give you my side, cause dat's de only side I got true, and after dat you can go hear whatsoever else people want to tell you bout it.

Years, bey. Years and years we ben close wid dem people, close like family, closer dan some family even. Back when de old lady ben still livin and my own mother too, de two a dem ben in and out one another yard morning to night like it ben one house wid two front door. De pickney dem grow up together, nyam off de same pot, share de same switch when dem get in trouble, cause whichever mother catch yinna first, yinna gettin lick, ain't matter whose child yinna be. We used to sit out on we step in de evenin and dem sit out on dem own and we holler cross de yard, no wall back den, just a lil low line a rockstone you could step clean over. Christmas we cookin together. Junkanoo mornin we out dey together. Hurricane comin, we battenin up we window same time dem doin dem own. Dat's how it ben, for years and years.

Den one day de husband decide he want build up a proper wall where de old lil rockstone line deh, and dat by itself wasn't no problem, everybody build. De problem was he build it right up pon de line, more dan right up on it if you ask me, cause my mango tree what ben standin deh long before any a we born, e root all a sudden half on de wrong side, and de wall startin to crack from de push, and de water off dem yard runnin straight down into we yard hard every time it rain.

I never say nothin hard bout it, not at first. I gone come stand by de wall one mornin and I ask im nice nice, I seh, bey, dis wall a lil close pon de line, yinna tink yinna could move it back a few inch, or put in some kind a lil drain so de water nuh come so hard pon we side? E seh awright, e go see bout it fi true. Weeks pass, nothing move. I ask im again, same politeness, same "e go see bout it." Months dis time. I let it ride, tellin miself de man busy, e workin two job, e got e own tings fi handle. But when de rainy season come round again and we yard flood worse dan de year before, I gone back a third time and dis time I ain't hide de edge in mi voice. I tell im straight, look, I done ask yinna nice twice already fi move dis wall, and not one ting move, not de wall, not so much as a shovel a dirt fi no drain.

Dat's when it start to turn. Ain't long after, de neighbor from three door down, she da de one who does carry word back and forth tween we house and dem house cause she friendly wid both side, she come round my step one evenin wid she face set up serious, and she tell mi seh dem seh I gone over dey cussin and threatenin, dat I tell im I go tear down de wall miself if e nuh move it. Now bey, I never seh nothin close to dat, not one word, but by de time it pass through she mouth and reach me it done turn into dat. Dat's sip-sip fi you, it does grow leg and walk clean off from where it start out.

So dis time I gone miself, straight, no go-between fi carry nothin, and I ask im to e face why e out dey tellin people I threaten to tear down e wall when all I ever done ask im nice three time fi move it. Steups. E look mi dead in mi eye and seh e nuh know what I talkin bout, seh nobody ever ignore no request from mi, seh I never even ask im proper de first time, like de whole ting never happen at all. Flat out deny it, like I imagine de tree root and de flood water miself.

Well. I ain't argue further right deh, but I ain't forget neither. E used to keep e goat tie up by we side a de fence fi nyam down de grass, and one mornin de goat find e own way loose and gone eat up half my pigeon pea dem I ben growin fi weeks. I ain't seh I had a hand in dat rope comin undone. I ain't seh I ain't neither. All I go seh is de rope ben tie good and tight de whole time before dat, and after all de back and forth it just so happen to come loose right den.

But de worst part, de part dat stick in mi like a hook, wasn't de wall nor de goat nor none a dat. It ben a comment come back to mi through de same go-between, dat de wife tell somebody at church seh we people always did think we too good fi dis street even when we never had nothin, and dat de tree ben always gon fall on somebody house one day de way we let it grow wild. Dat one sting worse dan anything else cause it wasn't even bout de fence no more, it ben bout who we is, and dat kind a ting yinna nuh forget, not never.

I try one time fi mend it back. Bout a year in, I bring a plate over Christmas mornin same like de old days, sweet potato and de guava duff my mother did teach mi fi make, and I knock de door miself. De door open a crack, dem thank mi polite polite, take de plate, and dat ben it. Plate come back empty two day later, left right on we step, no note, no word, nothin. Dat ben dem answer.

By dis time de whole street done pick a side, roughly down de middle. De ones on my side a de road stand up fi we, de ones on dem side stand up fi dem, and even de pickney dem start eyein one another different when dem playin in de road. It come to a head one hot Saturday evenin, right out dey in de street where de whole block could hear.

The husband: Yinna come take down a piece a mi wall in de night and carry off de block dem fi yinnaself, dat's what yinna do.

Me: I never touch yinna wall, not one block, and if block missin yinna best go ask yinnaself where dem gone, cause I ain't no thief and every soul on dis street know it.

Voice raise up high dat evenin, both a we, right out dey where de whole block standin round like it ben a show for true.

Dat ben, what, gettin on eight, nine year now. Long time we ain't exchange one word past a stiff lil nod when we pass. Even now, if you watch, you go see it small small every single day, how dem gate does swing shut soon as I come out on mi step, how even de potcake in dem yard does bark different at mi now, how sip-sip still runnin quiet after all dis time, how neither one a we does go to de same lil gatherin on dis street no more even when everybody else get invite both side.

And I go be honest wid you, bey, since you new and you go find out sooner or later anyhow. All dese years I done tell dis story so many time, I ain't even sure no more which one a we really start it first, de wall or de water or de word dat pass through somebody else mouth. But dat's my side, and dat's de only side I got fi give.
