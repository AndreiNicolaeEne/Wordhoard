The first friend: Bey, I had to come tell you what dis gyal do me. I deh mindin my own business and she out there tellin everybody in the yard seh I'm the one start it. I ain't start nothing.

Me: Mm-hm. Start what, though.

The first friend: The thing wit the phone. She seh I take it and ain't give it back, but I'm the one lend she the money for the phone in the first place! And now she nuh talkin to me proper, just a sip-sip bout me round the neighbourhood like I'm wrong for true.

Me: I hearin you. Lemme hear she side too before I say anything, awright.

The second friend: (turnin up not a hour after) You know she already come tell you she side, I bet, she does always run first. But I ain't start nothing neither. She the one nuh call me back for two whole week, and when I ask she bout the phone she just steups and walk off like I ain't there.

Me: Nobody start nothing, apparently. But lemme ask you something, before all this phone business, how unu was?

The second friend: We was good, man. We was good good.

Me: So when she stop callin you back, how that make you feel.

The second friend: (grudging) I mean... it feel like she movin funny wit me. Like she got new friends now and forget who was there from the start. The phone ain't even the real thing, if I'm honest.

Me: That's what I was gettin at all along. Yinna need to sit down and talk to one another, not run to me and every soul in the yard sip-sippin bout it.

The second friend: I ain't callin she first though.

Me: Then leh me call yinna both together. Come by tomorrow evenin, the two of yinna, and we go sort this out proper, face to face, no sip-sip in between.
