The mother call the two pickney to come sit down by she, close, before she say a word. She wait till both a dem settle good on the couch, one under each arm.

The mother: Come sit down here by me, the two a yinna. I got something I need tell yinna, and I want yinna to listen good.

The eldest: What happen, Mama? You look funny.

The mother: Yinna Grandma... she pass this morning, love. She gone.

The eldest: Gone where? Like... gone forever? What that even mean, for true?

The mother: It mean 'e body done, baby. 'E ain't gon feel no pain, no sickness, nothing no more. 'E spirit gone on somewhere we can't follow right now, and we ain't go see 'e walking round here again. That's what dead mean. It hard, and it hurt, and it okay for we to feel that way.

The youngest: Grandma sleeping now?

The mother: Nuh, love, 'e ain't sleeping. When you sleeping, you wake back up after. This different. This 'e body just stop working, for good.

The youngest: But 'e coming for my birthday, right?

The mother: Nuh, sweetness, 'e ain't coming. I know that hard to hear, but I ain't go tell yinna nothing but the truth.

She pull the two a dem in close, tight, one arm round each.

The mother: Come here, mama sweetness, come to me.

The eldest: We go ever see Grandma again?

The mother: Not the way yinna used to see 'e, no. But yinna go carry 'e, in how yinna remember, in the stories we go tell, in yinna own self, cause plenty a yinna come from 'e. That part don't never leave.

The mother: Alright now, listen me good before yinna run back off. Over the next few days people gon start coming by the house, bringing food, sitting with we, sip-sipping and talking bout yinna grandma. It might get loud in here, it might get sad, all kind a way, and that's alright, that's what it does be like. Then we go have the funeral, and after that things go start to settle back down little by little. I gon tell yinna everything as it come, yinna ain't got to carry this by yinnaself.
