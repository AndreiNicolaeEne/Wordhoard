Awright bey, walk wit' me, I gon show you how it go round here, ain' nobody write none a dis down but everybody know it just de same.

You see dat empty lot by de corner, dat's where you does leave a car if you short, but nuh park front de church gate, not even Sunday mornin' when de whole place look dead, de deacon does watch dat gate like a e own front door. And dat strip a grass from de pole to de fence post side de yellow house, dat's de neighbor ground. Nobody nuh cross it, not even de pickney dem chasin' a ball, dem just run round it like a wall dey cyan see standin' up dey.

After nine, nine-thirty, de street done quiet. Nobody a blow no horn, nobody a run no music loud, nuh sign nowhere seh so, but if you fi forget, somebody go come stand up by dey fence and just look pon you till you remember by yuhself.

When you passin' people, de old head dem get de good mornin' first, always, before you even look pon anybody else, den whoever mindin' dey yard, den whoever else deh outside.

Tools and dem easy, you ask fi borrow, dem lend, and it come back clean, same day if you can manage it. But if it nuh come back, dem nuh go cuss you, nuh. Dem just steups when you pass, and next time you ask, dem tell you seh dem ain' got none, and everybody round here done know why.

De one rule everybody break sooner or later? Sip-sip. Every last one a dem go tell you dem nuh business in nobody business, and every last one a dem deh right dere by de fence, potcake and all, when de gossip startin' up.
