Mi a go tell yuh how di whole ting gone, from marnin till di lock gone pun di door fi di last time.

Da Granny own house dis, big ole wood house wid bottom house dung under and gallery in front weh yuh could see di whole street from. Granny ben gone a hospital back in di dry season and she nuh come back out, and since then di house been standing up empty, nobody living in am, only di grass a grow tall round di bottom house and di zinc a rust. Nobody a Guyana got di time or di money fi keep am up proper, and dem wah move overseas nuh business fi come back fi live in am neitha, so di family sit dung and decide: sell am. New owna wahn tek over end a di month, so we haffi clear out everything before then.

Wasn't jus di close family come out fi it neitha. Aunty and dem husband come, di cousin dem from up di road come, even one ole church sister weh use to sing wid Granny in choir come out fi help, cause dat is how it stay round here, everybody know everybody business and everybody come lend a hand when a old house a get pack up.

We start from di gallery first, cause dat easiest, jus chair and one ole rockin chair and some plant pot done dead long time. Di rockin chair nuh even rock straight no more, one leg gone shaky, but nobody want fi throw way di rockin chair, cause everybody remember Granny sidung deh every evening fanning sheself and watching people pass pun di road.

Inside di hall, dat's where memory start fi come out one by one. Di eldest daughter find a ole fan, one hand fan wid feather all bruk up, and she jus stan up deh wid am in she hand.

The eldest: Allyuh remember dis fan? Mumma use to fan sheself wid dis exact same fan every hot afternoon, and she use to seh if yuh fan too hard yuh a fan way yuh own luck.

She nuh seh nothing more, she jus put am dung soft soft in a box like it something precious, and everybody understand, nobody haffi explain nothing.

Den in a next drawer we find a next thing, and dis one nobody could place. Small tin box, lock rusty, and inside am, a set a foreign coin, none a dem look like nothing we ever spend here, and one lickle piece a paper wid writing pun am done fade out so nobody could read what it seh.

The uncle: A whey dis come from? Mumma neva mention nutten bout no coin from no foreign place.

The cousin: Maybe a fi she fren send am, from when she deh a Suriname dem time deh, remember she use to gyaff bout crossing di river fi go Nickerie.

Somebody run go call di rest to come see, and everybody pass di box round, but nobody could settle it. Dat one get put aside, one a dem mystery weh done gone a grave wid Granny sheself.

By di time we reach Granny room, dat's where di real argument start. Not bout nutten sad, bout di wardrobe, di big mahogany wardrobe weh stand up against di wall since forever. Di eldest daughter seh she want am cause she di one weh help Granny pick am out from di carpenter man way back. Di youngest son seh nuh, he suppose to get am cause he di only one still living close enough fi even carry am home. Dem gyaff back and forth good ten minute, and one a dem steups loud loud.

The youngest son: Steups. Allyuh always seh mi too far, mi too busy, but now allyuh want mi fi tek time off wuk fi help pack up and then gi di wardrobe to somebody weh cyaan even fit am in dem lil apartment abroad?

The eldest daughter: A nuh bout who close, a bout who Granny woulda want fi have am.

In di end di auntie settle am. She seh whoever got room fi am proper in a real house should keep am, and dat end up being di cousin weh still deh living in Georgetown wid a big enough house, and everybody satisfy wid dat, no more fuss pun am.

Den come di next disagreement, whether a next thing should even stay in a di family at all, di ole sewing machine, di Singer treadle one, wid di wheel and di iron leg dem. Some a dem seh keep am, seh a antique, worth good money, and besides, Granny ben sew every pickney frock pun dat exact machine. Di uncle seh nuh, throw am way, it heavy, it rusty, nobody nowadays a sew pun no treadle machine, is jus taking up space fi nutten. Dat one nuh get settle quick, dem lef am to di side fi decide later and move on to di next room.

In di back room, behind one loose board weh nobody woulda ever notice, di cousin find something wrap up tight in a ole cloth. Money. Not plenty, but real money, ole notes gone soft wid age, and a lickle bundle a letter tie up wid string.

The cousin: Look wha mi find. Nobody neva know bout dis.

Everybody gather round, and nobody seh a word fi a good while, jus looking. Nobody ben expect Granny fi hide nutten, she was di kind a woman weh tell yuh everything to yuh face, seh whatever pun she mind straight out, no matter who it hurt.

Di storeroom under di house tek longer dan every other room combine. Trunk load a ole newspaper, bottle after bottle, dem ole enamel basin, tool weh rust up till dem cyaan even open, box after box a paper nobody could throw way without checking every single one first, cause who know wha deh hide inside dem. We deh dung deh good few hour, sweat running, dust a mek everybody sneeze and cough, di pickney dem running back and forth fi carry things to di pile weh going and di pile weh staying.

Round midday everybody stop and nyam together. Somebody bring one big pot a cook-up rice, another one bring roti and curry, and we all sidung pun bench and box in di yard, eating, gyaffing bout Granny, laughing bout foolishness she use to do, and fi a lickle while it nuh even feel like sad wuk, it jus feel like family.

After we eat, we tackle di big china cabinet in di dining room, and dat is di one big piece nobody in di end want. Water ben damage di back a am from a leak years back, di glass crack up bad, and every single person seh nuh, dem nuh got space, dem nuh want am, leh am go. So dat one get carry out fi di dump truck man, and nobody feel no way bout am, everybody jus glad it out di way.

Di family Bible, pun di other hand, everybody agree pun widout even one word a argument. It jus go to di eldest, natural so, cause she di one weh does read from am every Christmas anyway.

By late evening di last room, Granny own bedroom, stand up completely empty. Even di curtain gone, even di lickle nail pun di wall weh use to hold up she picture. Standing in deh empty like dat hit different from any a di other room, quiet in a way weh mek everybody go quiet too.

Wha lef, di crockery, di linen, di small small things, we jus portion am out right deh in di yard, one one, so everybody walk way wid something a Granny fi remember she by.

Last thing, we lock up di house. Di uncle put di padlock pun di gate, and everybody stand up deh a lickle minute, nobody rushing way, jus looking pun di house one last time before we all gone we separate way, back to we own life, wid a piece a Granny house gone home wid every single one a we.
