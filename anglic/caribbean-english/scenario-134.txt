Bey, sip-sip been going round di yard from early seh dis would beat every year, and true enough, it did. From di jump we could tell it a come different. By di time di first tomato start turn red, di count already pass what dis piece a land does normally give fi a whole season, and we ain't even reach di peak yet. When all done, we end up wid near double di regular amount, straight so.

But is di pigeon peas dat really tek di cake dis year. Dem bush get so heavy we had to prop dem up wid stick so di branch dem nuh break off under di weight. Last time we see pigeon peas do so, dat was donkey years back, maybe fifteen, sixteen year, and even dat one nuh touch dis one, nuh way.

Steups, di regular crew alone couldn't manage. Had was to call in extra hand, cousin dem deh all bout di settlement, even di pickney dem out dere helping pick, everybody from morning straight through, cause it woulda turn on di vine before we finish otherwise. Whatever surplus left over after we store what we need fi di house, we trade off di rest, some gone straight to market, some gone door to door, and plenty a we just nyam good all season.

Di old grower, di one been working dis ground longer dan anybody, 'e stand up one evening, shake 'e head slow, and tell we seh 'e ain't never see nothing like it in all 'e days.

The old grower: Yinna young ones don't even know how rare dis is. Rain come right when we need it, not too much, not too little, and di whole season stay mild straight through, no big storm fi trouble we.

And dat's why, to dis day, whenever people liming and di talk turn to farming, dem still seh, "remember dat year," and everybody round here know exactly which one dem mean, wid di potcake stretch out under di tree, half asleep, like even 'e done heard di story too many times.
