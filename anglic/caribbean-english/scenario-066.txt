The new one: Eh eh, allyuh, I keep hearing people talk 'bout some wild animal on this street and nobody ain giving meh the real story. What happen here at all?

Me: Boy siddown nah, leh meh tell yuh the whole thing from top, because this street self still eh done arguing 'bout it.

Orait so. First time anybody say dey see it, was the boy who does deliver the gas bottle, and he tell it to whoever passing by the shop that evening, seh he see something big cross the road by the drain, low down, moving quick, tail long like a snake. And everybody just steups and say the boy imagining thing, seh he watching too much film, seh doh mind him, is probably some big rat he see and frighten heself over nothing. Nobody take it on serious. That was that.

But then not two, three week after, the one who does keep the little shop on the corner, the one everybody does trust because she deh there since God know when and she doh talk foolishness for nothing, she come out one morning to open up and she see it sheself, plain as day, sitting up by she back fence like it own the place. Long snout, grey-brown fur, that pink tail curl up. Manicou. And when she say it, people had to believe, because she is not the kind to make up thing. So now the whole street start to take notice.

After that it was sighting after sighting. It in the bin by the big house on the corner. It in the garden behind, digging up the lady dasheen bed. Somebody see it walking the fence top calm calm in the daytime like it eh 'fraid nobody. It does come out mostly by night, rustling in people yard, knocking over whatever eh tie down proper. And that is when the street start to split in two.

One side saying leave the poor thing alone, is only a manicou, it come down from the hill because building and building push it out from where it used to live, it not looking for trouble, just looking for food, leave it, it will find its own way. The other side saying no, it have to go, it bringing disease, it will bite somebody pickney, it tearing up people property and something have to be done about it quick quick.

Then the thing happen that really turn up the heat. The lady with the fowls, she wake up one morning and one of she fowl missing, and the coop wire pull back like something push through it. And right away, before anybody see anything for true, people start blaming the manicou, seh is it, is it, must be it, who else it could be. Some people saying manicou eh does trouble fowl so, that more sound like mongoose work or even a stray dog, but that never stop the talk. People arguing left right and center without a single soul actually catching the thing in the act.

So they call a little meeting, right there by the corner under the big mango tree, because everybody had something to say and it had to come out sometime.

The one whose fowl gone: Allyuh have to do something 'bout that animal, oui. It come and thief my fowl in broad, well, in the night, and nobody doing nothing 'bout it.

The elder on the block: But how yuh so sure is the manicou, padna? Yuh see it with yuh own eye take the fowl?

The one whose fowl gone: Who else it could be, eh? Since it come here things start missing.

The man who does keep pigeon up the road: Allyuh too quick to blame the poor thing. It never trouble my pigeon and it does pass by there plenty night. Something else take that fowl, mark my word.

The mother from the yellow house: I doh care what take what, I have small children in this yard and I eh want no wild animal running 'bout by night, biting up nobody. That is my only concern.

The elder on the block: Nobody eh get bite yet, eh. Leh we not make more story than what deh there already.

The one who does keep the corner shop: All I know is I see it and it never come for me. It just deh there, minding its business. Is we who build up all round where it used to live.

And so it went, back and forth, voice getting loud, one man banging he hand on the tree trunk, another woman steupsing and walking off in the middle because she say allyuh only wasting time talking, nobody deciding nothing.

Meanwhile the household with the biggest garden, the ones who lose the most dasheen, they decide they going handle it demself. The husband set up some kind of trap he make from a crate and a piece of wire, baited it up with fish head, sit up half the night waiting with a flashlight. Manicou never even come near the trap. Instead it walk straight through the side gate they forget to close and knock down the whole standpipe hose reel looking for water, and the husband end up chasing shadow in the dark half the night for nothing, vex and shame the next morning when the whole street hear how it end.

But the thing that really settle the argument for good was the night it come face to face with the woman two doors down, coming in late from work. She turn the corner by she gate and it was right there, sitting up on the wall, and it hiss and bare it teeth at she in the dark before it jump off and gone. She scream the whole street awake that night, and the next morning she was shaking still telling anybody who stop to listen how she thought she was going to get bite for true.

That put the fire under the whole business. Now it wasn't leave-it-alone versus deal-with-it no more in a calm way, it was people outright vexed, saying enough is enough, while the other side still holding that the woman only frighten sheself because it come on she sudden in the dark, and a manicou that get corner will act up same way any animal would, that doh make it dangerous, that only make it unlucky it pick that spot by she wall that night.

So finally somebody had sense to call up a fella from the Forestry, a man who does deal with wildlife proper, wildlife officer, and he come look 'bout the whole area, walk the yards, check the bins, check where it denning up in the empty lot behind. And he tell the street straight: is a manicou, harmless animal, does eat fruit and small thing, not aggressive unless it corner or frighten bad, and it only there because the bush behind get clear and it lost where it used to forage. He say best thing is secure the bins good, doh leave food out, give it a way to move through without cornering it by nobody wall, and it will settle down or move on natural.

So that is the terms the street settle on after all that noise. Bins get proper lid and some tie down with rope or brick on top. The lady with the dasheen wire up she garden bed a little better. And the corner shop lady, since she was the one everybody trust from the start, she sort of take it 'pon sheself to keep half an eye, mention it if she see it round, nothing formal, just she does watch.

And is so it stay. Sighting still happen time to time, somebody see it crossing by the drain late, or hear it in a bin some night, but nobody run for the wildlife man again, nobody holding no more meeting under the mango tree. The alarm just fade little by little till the whole thing settle into routine, part of the street same way the stray dog by the corner is part of the street. Now when people talk 'bout it at all, is more with a laugh, remembering how the one man chase shadow all night with he crate trap, how the woman get so frighten she wake up the whole block, how two, three week of pure noise come from one animal that never really do nobody real harm. Is a story now, not no threat, and that is the whole of it, start to finish.
