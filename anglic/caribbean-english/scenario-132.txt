Eleven day straight the cold ben a hold we down, and from the very first mornin it a set in worse than anybody reckon. Radio did warn a front comin through but nobody nuh figure on this kinda thing, not here. First light hit and the whole house cold like inside a icebox already.

The mother: "This a no ordinary front, yinna. We go need fire runnin day and night, no stoppin."

So the stove keep goin round the clock from that mornin on, wood stack up by the back step, and whoever wake up first does throw on another piece before the coffee even get hot. Nobody sleep proper, just take turn mindin the fire like it a baby.

By the third day the pipe out by the cistern freeze up solid, first time in all the years we deh in that house, all them years and it never do that.

The uncle: "Bey, this pipe never do this before, not once, and it been standin there since before I born." Then he just steups long and walk off, vex.

The eldest: "I remember the cold back in '81, when the whole yard did white with frost and we lose the tomato plant dem overnight in one shot. But this one here beat that clean. That one ain't hold no more than three, four day. This one a hold on and on like it ain't studyin we at all."

Worst night was the seventh, wind comin straight off the water and cuttin through every crack a body could find. The potcake wouldn't stay out in the yard, 'e come lay down right by the stove door, shakin the whole night. By mornin we lose the young hen we been raisin up, found 'e stiff in the coop, and the whole garden bed gone black and done.

Then the eleventh day come and the sun reach proper, warm touch we skin like a blessing after all that. The mother just stand out in the yard, eyes close, sayin nuttin, just lettin it soak right in.
