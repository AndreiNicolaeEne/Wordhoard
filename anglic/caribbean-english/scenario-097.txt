Me:
Bey, come set down nuh, le' me talk to you a lil bit. Not cause I got all the answers, cause Lord know I don't, but cause I done been where you deh right now, and I ain wan you feel like you on you own in this.

First days, honest, wasn't even real. Wasn't nothing but numb, straight numb, like somebody turn a switch off inside you. The house full up the whole time, people just come go, come go, all week, aunty dem bringing food you nuh even want fi eat, the pickney dem runnin round the yard not even understandin what goin on, phone ringin off the hook, somebody always askin "yinna awright, yinna need anything," and you sayin yeah yeah cause what else it is fi say. It ain leave you no room fi feel nothing direct, cause soon as one wave start to come up in you chest, somebody knockin the door again with another dish. I remember thinkin, when this go done, when everybody gone home, that's when it go hit me. And Lord, it hit.

Cause that first night everybody finally clear on out the house, the actual first time I was by myself in there after, the quiet ain never land so hard in my life. Wasn't no peaceful quiet. Was a quiet with teeth in it. Nothing left fi distract me, no dish fi wash, no relative fi answer, just me and the house and the fact of it, sittin right there in the middle of the front room like a piece of furniture nobody move yet.

The weeks after that, my body just do what it want, wasn't listenin to me at all. Some days I couldn't nyam nothing, three four days pass and I ain eat proper, food just sit there lookin like nothing to me. And sleep, sleep ain come whole no more, it come in these lil raggedy pieces, hour here, forty-five minute there, wide awake at three in the mornin starin the ceilin like it owe me money. Some night I just steups and turn over, mind still runnin, ain even mad at nothing in particular, just runnin.

First month was the strangest part though, cause the world just kept movin. Traffic still bad on the roundabout, people still complainin bout light bill, the news still on with the same foolishness, and I remember thinkin, how yinna still doin all this like nothing happen. Like the whole world suppose to stop and it just didn't. Nobody tell you that part, how fast everybody else does move on while you still stuck in the same hour.

And it wasn't even no big marked day that got me first, wasn't no birthday or nothing I was braced for. Was a plain regular Tuesday, nothing special bout it at all, and it undo me complete, right in the middle of the grocery store of all place. No warnin, no build up, just gone, standin there by the bread with tears just runnin down.

Even months after, it still ain have no pattern to it. A smell could catch you off guard, one whiff of a certain kinda soap, or somebody perfume walk past you on the street, and it wahn pull the whole wave right back up outta nowhere, same as day one. It don't ask you permission first.

The middle stretch, bey, that the part nobody talk bout enough. Long long fog, months of it, where even the good days ain feel good, they just feel gray. You go through the motion, you does laugh when people expect you to laugh, but it all comin through a lil bit of static, like the volume turn down on everything. Sip-sip start goin round too, bout how I holdin up, how I lookin, like people ain got nothing better fi do.

Somebody say something to me in there, real thoughtless..."well at least e ain suffer long", like that suppose to make it lighter, and it sting way more than the person probably ever know. But then it had this other thing, small small, ain cost nobody nothing, a workmate just show up one evenin, ain say much, just bring a plate a food and set with me a hour, ain try fi fix nothing, ain say nothing deep, just deh there with me. That do more for me than any of the official condolence, the church program, the fruit basket, none of that touch what that one visit touch.

Then the anniversary come round and Lord, the dread of it start weeks before, I could feel it comin like weather rollin in. And the day itself. I ain go lie to you, I get through it clumsy, ain do nothing special, ain have no big plan, just made it sundown to sundown in one piece. And that was enough. That was the whole point of it, gettin through, however messy it come out.

Appetite and sleep, they come back, but slow, uneven, one step forward two step back some week. Wasn't no mornin I wake up and it just "back to normal," it never work so straight.

And then it had a day. I remember it clear, where I catch myself laughin for real, from my belly, at something foolish somebody say, and right behind that laugh, guilt come slide in uninvited, like how you feel it entitle fi be there. Like who I think I is, laughin, when e ain even a year gone yet.

By the time the year done, I was carryin it different. Lighter in some part, I ain go lie, but it ain gone, it never gone all the way, it just find a different place fi sit in me.

So I tellin you this, and I want you fi really hear it, it don't stay this hard forever, bey. It don't. But somebody tell me seh grief ain got no schedule, and dem right, ain no chart nobody could give you say by such-and-such month you go feel a certain way. So don't measure your year against nobody else year, not your aunty, not who you see carryin on online, and not against mine either. Your year is your year. You just go through it the way it come.
