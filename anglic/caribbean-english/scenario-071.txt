Eh eh, my friend, sit down dere and lemme tell yuh 'bout de day we just had, 'cause I ent even fully recover from it yet. You know dat big ole samaan been leanin over by de corner for years now, root and dem liftin up de pavement, everybody sayin somebody goin get hurt one day. Well de rain Sunday night take de whole thing down. Trunk fall straight cross de road, from de gutter side right to de fence by de next street, branch and dem tangle up in de light wire, leaf and dead wood scatter from here to Kingdom Come. No car couldn pass, no bike, nothing. De whole street lock off.

Was de man two door down from de corner house first raise de alarm, 'bout six in de mornin, still dark-dark. He bang on gate after gate goin down de street.

The one who raised it: "Allyuh come out nah! Tree block de whole road, we cyah get out to go a work, nobody cyah come in!"

And chile, word spread fast fast, faster dan you could believe. By de time I put on meh slippers and come outside, already it have five, six people standin round de trunk scratchin dey head. But dat wasn't nothin yet, 'cause by seven o'clock de whole road full up. I countin in meh head and I say to meself, dis have to be twenty-odd people out here, and dat is well over a dozen more dan anybody call for. People comin from streets we ent even see dey face before, cousin and in-law and dem, workmate somebody call, de fella who does sell doubles by de junction even leave he cart to come lend a hand.

Work start dividin up itself, nobody even sit down and organize it proper, it just happen. De younger men dem take up cutlass and one chainsaw somebody bring, start in on de smaller branch first, clearin a path so car could get through at least one side. De women dem organizin who bringin what, who mindin de little ones, who go and call de electric company 'bout de wire. And it was de man from de end house, big fella, does work construction, who take on de heaviest part of it, de main trunk itself. Nobody ask him, he just walk up wit de chainsaw and seh, "Aright, dis part here is mine," and dat was dat. He was on dat trunk from mornin straight through, sweat soakin he shirt through, arm shakin by midday and he still not stoppin.

Round 'bout eleven o'clock, dat's when de small disaster happen. Big limb dey was cuttin swing back sudden, nobody see it comin, and it catch de fence and knock right through de windscreen of de car parked dey, de teacher car, de one who does teach up de school. Miss it hittin anybody head by inches, my friend, inches. Everybody freeze up for a second, den start shoutin at once.

The one with the chainsaw: "I tell allyuh to stand back, why nobody listenin!"

Somebody workmate: "You cuttin from de wrong side, dat's why de whole thing swingin like dat!"

And dat lead straight into de argument, 'cause now half de men have one way dey want to cut it and de next half have a next way, one side sayin cut from de top down piece by piece, de next side sayin no, you have to weaken de trunk first or de whole ting go shift again. Voice gettin loud, hand gettin wave up in de air, real heat in it for a good few minutes. It was de oldest man on de street, walkin stick and all, who come and stand right in de middle of it and just seh, real quiet, "Allyuh want to argue or allyuh want to finish 'fore night fall?" And somehow dat settle it down quicker dan anything else coulda. Dey shake it off, get back to work, no hard feelins after, just so.

By early afternoon de whole street smellin like a fete. One mother bring out a big pot of corn soup she start from mornin, next household bring pelau, somebody bring a cooler full a beer and mauby and dem was pourin it out for whoever pass by, work or no work. De children dem was give dey own little job, gatherin up de small twig and leaf into pile for de truck to come take, and dey take it real serious too, runnin back and forth like dey buildin something important.

And de oldest man, de same one who calm de argument, he ent sittin down for none of it. He daughter try to make him take a chair in de shade and he cut she off quick.

The eldest: "Nah, I not sittin down watchin. Gimme a cutlass, I could still hold a blade steady even if I cyah lift like dem young fella."

So dey give him de small branches to trim clean while de big men handle de trunk, and he sit right dey doin dat de whole afternoon, proud as anything.

De funniest moment though, and everybody still laughin 'bout it even now, was when dey finally cut through to de heart of de trunk and out shoot a manicou, just runnin like it own de place, straight through de crowd. People scatterin every which way, one woman drop de whole tray of pelau she was carryin, screamin like it was a jaguar and not a little manicou mindin its business. De thing gone up somebody wall and disappear and everybody just stand up dey lookin at each other and burst out laughin, couldn stop for good five minutes, laughin till dey belly hurt.

We was thinkin de whole ting done by two, three o'clock latest. Well dat never happen. By de time de last of de wood get haul off to de side and de road properly clear, sun already goin down, well past five, three hour later dan anybody figure. But my friend, when I tell yuh what we manage, whole trunk gone, every branch cut and clear, de gutter dey was blockin dug out proper, de fence patch back good enough, even de teacher windscreen situation get sort out 'cause de construction fella seh he go help pay it. When de last piece move off de road, somebody just start to cheer and it catch on down de whole street, real cheer, hand in de air and all.

And dat evenin, my friend, de street feel different. People who ent sit outside in months was out on dey step till late, children playin later dan dey mother woulda normally allow, de same pot of corn soup gettin pass round for a second helpin. Ent last forever, I know dat, by tomorrow everybody back in dey own business. But for dat one evenin, walkin down dat street felt like walkin through one big yard, not just a road wit house on it. Dat's de part I really wanted you to hear.
