=== scenario 62 | neighbors | M | l | corpus ===
scenario: how the neighbors' feud started, told from one side
zones: past-narrative,reported,negation
intent: a resident gives their own side of how the long-running feud with the household next door actually began.
beats:
- how close the two households used to be, before any of it
- the first small friction: something built or planted too near the shared boundary
- a request made to move or fix it, kept polite
- the request brushed off, or forgotten, more than once
- the request repeated with an edge this time
- what got reported back through a third neighbor, changed in the telling
- the flat denial that the request was ever ignored
- a small retaliation, done quietly
- the comment that got back to the teller and stung worst of all
- an attempt at a peace offering, turned down
- other neighbors drifting into one side or the other
- the argument that finally happened out in the open
- an accusation of damage or theft, denied outright
- the long stretch since of not speaking, and how it still shows day to day
- the teller's own admission at the end, that they are no longer entirely sure who started it
held:
- how many years the two households were close before
- the boundary item that started it
- the request made and ignored more than once
- the third neighbor who carried the report
- the flat denial
- the comment that stung worst
- the peace offering, turned down
- how the street split, roughly down the middle
- the open argument
- the accusation, denied
- how many years the silence has lasted
- the daily sign that it is still going
cast: the teller, a longtime resident; the household next door, the other side of the feud, out of scene; a third neighbor who carried reports back and forth, out of scene; the newest arrival on the street, asking about it, then listening the rest of the way through without a word.
text: Someone new to the street has heard about the feud between you and the people next door and wants to know how it started; you are only willing to give your own side of it, and once you begin they only listen, not a single question breaking in. Start with how close the two households used to be, years of it, before any of this. Move to the first small friction: something built or planted too near the line between the properties. Cover the request you made to have it moved or fixed, kept polite, and how it got brushed off or forgotten more than once before you asked again with an edge in it. Bring in what got reported back to you through a neighbor who carried word between the two houses, and how the story had changed by the time it reached you. Include the flat denial that the request was ever ignored in the first place, the small retaliation you did quietly in return, and the one comment that got back to you and stung worse than anything else said. Mention the peace offering you made at some point, and how it was turned down. Note how the rest of the street ended up splitting, roughly down the middle, and the argument that finally happened out where everyone on the block could hear it. Bring up the accusation of damage or theft that got made and flatly denied. Close with how many years the silence has run since, some small way it still shows itself day to day, and your own honest admission, even now, that you are not entirely sure anymore who actually started it.
=== end ===

scenario: the neighbor's tree fell on the fence: the quarrel and the mending
zones: past-narrative,money,face-work
intent: a resident tells the story of the night a neighbor's tree came down on the shared fence, the quarrel over paying for it, and how the two households mended both the fence and the friction.
beats:
- the bad night of wind that brought the tree down
- finding the fence crushed the next morning
- whose tree it technically was, growing right along the line
- the first conversation about who should pay, tense from the start
- the neighbor's first response: an act of the weather, not their doing
- the counter: the tree had been leaning for a long while already
- someone recalling an earlier warning about the tree that went unheeded
- the standoff over splitting the cost, neither side wanting to back down first
- an older neighbor stepping in, unasked, to give both sides a way to back down without losing face
- the compromise finally reached on the cost
- who actually did the clearing and rebuilding, and how the work got divided
- the stiff first hours working side by side
- the small gesture that eased it, food or a tool passed over
- the fence finished, sturdier than the one it replaced
- how the two households talk now, better than before or just even
held:
- the wind that brought the tree down, in one night
- whose tree it was
- the earlier warning about the leaning tree, unheeded
- the standoff, neither side backing down first
- the older neighbor who stepped in to mediate, giving both a way to back down without losing face
- the cost, split roughly down the middle in the end
- how the clearing and rebuilding got divided between the two households
- how many days the rebuilding took
- the gesture that eased the stiffness between them
- how things stand between the two households now
cast: the teller, one half of the fence dispute; the neighbor whose tree fell, the other side of it; an older neighbor who mediated, out of scene; the listener hearing the story, taking it in without a word, never breaking in.
text: A bad night of wind brought a neighbor's tree down across the fence the two properties share, and you are telling someone the whole story of the quarrel that followed and how it finally got mended; they take it in without a word the whole way through. Start with finding the fence crushed the next morning and working out whose tree it actually was, growing right along the line. Cover the first conversation about who should pay for it, tense from the start: the neighbor's first answer that it was simply the wind's doing and not their fault, and your counter that the tree had been leaning for a long while before it fell, with someone recalling an earlier warning about it that had gone unheeded. Walk through the standoff over splitting the cost, neither side wanting to be the first to back down, until an older neighbor stepped in, unasked, and found a way to let both of you back down without losing face over it. Once the cost was settled, tell how the actual clearing and rebuilding got divided between the two households, the stiff first hours of working side by side, and the small gesture, food brought out or a tool handed over without being asked, that finally eased it. Finish with the fence standing sturdier than the one it replaced, and how things sit between the two households now, better than before or simply even again.
=== end ===

scenario: a note about the neighbor's loud party: polite surface, fury underneath
zones: face-work,notice-register,intensifier
intent: a fed-up neighbor writes a determinedly polite note about last night's party noise, temper showing through the courtesy.
beats:
- the noise named, and how late it ran
- the opening line that stays deliberately warm
- the complaint folded in lightly, almost as an aside
- the detail that gives away how many times this has happened
- the request for next time, phrased as the easiest thing in the world
- the sign-off that keeps the polite mask on
held:
- the party noise ran until nearly three in the morning
- the repeat count, not the first time
- the request to keep it down after midnight next time
- the warm sign-off
cast: the note-writer; the neighbor who threw the party, addressed but out of scene.
text: Your neighbor's party ran loud until nearly three in the morning last night, not for the first time, and you are fed up but determined to stay pleasant about it in writing. Write the note: open as warmly as you always do with them, work the noise in almost as an aside rather than a complaint, let slip lightly how many times this has happened this year without letting it sound like a tally, ask them to keep it down after midnight next time as though it were the easiest thing in the world, and close exactly as warmly as you opened, the kind of sign-off that would read as pure friendliness to anyone who did not know how angry you actually are.
=== end ===

- a second sighting, this time by someone the street actually trusts
- what it was seen doing, near the bins or the gardens
- the street splitting into two camps: leave it be, or deal with it
- something lost or damaged and blamed on it, argued over
- a heated argument at a street gathering about what to do
- one household trying to handle it alone, and failing
- a night encounter that startled someone badly
- the disagreement over whether it was actually dangerous
- an experienced outside voice brought in to look at it
- the terms the street finally settled on
- the small changes made afterward, bins secured, a lookout kept
- the sightings settling into routine, the alarm fading
- how the street talks about it now, closer to a story than a threat
held:
- the first sighting, waved off
- the second sighting, taken seriously
- what it was seen doing
- the street split into two camps, roughly even
- something lost or damaged, blamed on it
- the night encounter that startled someone
- the outside voice brought in
- the terms of the truce
- how many weeks it took from first sighting to truce
- how the street talks about it now
cast: the teller, a longtime resident; the newest arrival on the street, asking about it, then listening straight through without a single question breaking in; the rest of the street, referred to collectively or by household; an experienced outside voice consulted, out of scene.
text: Someone new to the street has heard people mention the wild animal that moved in a while back and wants the whole story; once you start, they just listen, straight through to the end. Begin with the first sighting, reported secondhand and waved off at the time as somebody's imagination, and the second sighting not long after, this time by someone the street actually trusts. Cover what it was seen doing, poking around the bins or the gardens, and how the street split into two camps over it, one side wanting to leave it be and the other wanting it dealt with. Bring in the thing that turned up lost or damaged and got blamed on it, argued over without real proof, and the heated argument that broke out at a street gathering about what to do next. Mention one household that tried to handle it alone and failed, and the night encounter that startled someone badly enough to bring the argument to a head, along with the disagreement over whether the animal was actually dangerous or just unlucky in where it wandered. Tell how an experienced outside voice finally got brought in to look at the situation, and the terms the street settled on afterward. Close with the small changes made since, bins secured, someone keeping half an eye out, the sightings settling into routine as the alarm faded, and how the whole business gets talked about on the street now, closer to a story than a threat.

zones: imperative,habitual,spatial
intent: a longtime resident lays out the unwritten rules of the neighborhood for someone new, the habits nobody wrote down but everyone follows.
beats:
- where it is fine to park and where it never is
- the edge of a neighbor's ground that is never crossed, even if nothing marks it
- the hours the whole street keeps quiet without anyone posting them
- the order of greeting people on the street, who gets acknowledged first
- how borrowing and lending tools or supplies actually works here
- what happens if something borrowed does not come back
- the one rule everyone quietly breaks and never mentions
held:
- where parking is fine and where it is not
- the boundary never crossed
- the quiet hours kept
- the order of greeting
- the borrowing habit and its unwritten terms
- the one rule everyone breaks
cast: the teller, a longtime resident; the newcomer being shown around, taking it all in without asking anything back.
text: You are showing someone new to the street around; they listen the whole way without asking anything back, and you walk them through the rules nobody wrote down but everyone here just knows. Tell them where it is fine to leave a vehicle or a cart and where it is never done, no matter how empty it looks. Point out the edge of a neighbor's ground that nobody crosses, even though nothing marks it. Explain the hours the whole street keeps quiet without a single sign posted anywhere. Cover the order people greet each other in when passing on the street, who gets acknowledged first and why. Walk them through how borrowing and lending tools or supplies actually works here, and what happens, quietly but for certain, if something borrowed does not come back. Finish with the one rule everyone on the street breaks sooner or later and nobody ever mentions out loud.

- the condition: pay upfront, lose it
- the story of someone who fell for it
- the signs to watch for

Describe who runs it, the kind of person who is always friendly and always has a deal going. Tell them the pitch, an offer that sounds too good to be worth questioning, and the condition that makes the whole thing work: if you pay anything upfront, you will never see it or the person again. Bring up someone who fell for it and what it cost them, told as a caution rather than gossip. Give the signs to watch for before it gets that far, the ones that should have given it away sooner. scenario: the day the whole street worked together, told at its end
zones: past-narrative,numbers,exclamation
intent: at the end of a long day, a resident tells someone what it was like when the whole street turned out together to get a shared job done.
beats:
- the task that needed the whole street's hands
- who first called for it and how fast word spread
- the early arrivals, well over a dozen more than expected
- how the work got divided up among everyone
- the person who ended up doing the heaviest lifting
- a small disaster partway through
- an argument that flared over how to do something, and got smoothed over
- food and drink brought out by more than one household
- the children given their own small piece of the work
- the oldest person there insisting on a role too
- a moment everyone ended up laughing at the same thing
- finishing hours later than anyone expected
- counting up what actually got done by the end
- the cheer or toast when it was finally finished
- how the street felt different that evening, even if only for a while
held:
- the shared task
- how word spread, and the count that turned out, well over a dozen more than expected
- who did the heaviest lifting
- the small disaster partway through
- the argument, smoothed over
- the children's piece of the work
- the oldest person's role
- finished a good three hours later than expected
- the tally of what got done
- the cheer at the end
cast: the teller, one of the residents; the rest of the street, referred to collectively and by role such as the oldest one there and the children; the listener hearing the story that evening, taking it in without a word, never breaking in.
text: At the end of a long day you are telling someone what it was like when the whole street turned out together to get a shared job done; they listen straight through without a word. Start with the task itself, the kind of thing too big for one household alone, and who first called for everyone to pitch in and how fast word spread up and down the street. Cover the early arrivals, well over a dozen more people turning out than anyone expected, and how the work ended up divided among everyone there, with one person taking on the heaviest lifting of the day. Bring up the small disaster partway through, and the argument that flared over how something should be done before it got smoothed over. Mention the food and drink that more than one household brought out over the course of the day, the small piece of the work handed to the children, and the oldest person there insisting on a role too rather than sitting it out. Include the one moment everyone ended up laughing at the same thing. Close with the job finishing a good three hours later than anyone expected, the tally of what actually got done by the end, the cheer that went up once it was finished, and how the street felt different that evening, even if only for a while.
