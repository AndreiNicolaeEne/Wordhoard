# Marriage scenarios

=== scenario 23 | marriage | M | l | corpus ===
scenario: the full account of the wedding, told to someone who missed it
zones: past-narrative,food,kin
intent: the newly married one gives a friend who missed the wedding the whole day, start to finish.
beats:
- waking too early and barely eating from nerves
- the scramble over one thing forgotten right before leaving
- both families standing together in one place for the first time
- who cried first on arrival
- a line of the vows lost, then found again
- the exchange that sealed it
- the papers signed, a witness standing for each side
- the long wait for photographs, cluster after cluster of kin arranged
- two guests seated too close for comfort
- the dish gone within minutes
- the dish nobody touched
- a toast nobody could get through without breaking
- the one dancer who never once sat down
- an old family story dragged out at the table
- the quiet at the very end, just the two of you with the mess
held:
- woke very early, could hardly eat
- one specific item forgotten in the rush
- both families in one place for the first time
- who cried first
- one line of the vows lost, then found again
- the exchange that sealed it
- the papers signed, one witness each side
- the long wait for photographs, cluster after cluster arranged
- two guests seated too close for comfort
- the dish gone within minutes
- the dish nobody touched
- the toast nobody could finish dry-eyed
- the dancer who never sat down
- the old family story told again at the table
cast: the newly married couple, both sets of parents, a sibling or two on each side, all gathered for the day; the friend hearing the story stays out of scene.
text: You got married and a close friend who could not be there wants to hear all of it. Tell the whole day in order: how early you woke and how little you managed to eat, the scramble over the one thing forgotten right before you had to leave, both families standing together in one place for the first time in your life, who cried first when you arrived, the line in the vows you lost and found again, the exchange that sealed it, the papers signed with a witness standing for each side, the long wait for photographs while someone tried to arrange cluster after cluster of kin just right, the seating mix-up that put two people too close for comfort, the dish that was gone within minutes and the one nobody touched, the toast nobody could get through without breaking, the one dancer who would not sit down all night, the old family story that got dragged out and told to the whole table anyway, and the quiet at the very end once the last guests were gone and it was just the two of you with the mess. Tell it the whole way through, the way you would to someone hearing every bit of it for the first time.
=== end ===

=== scenario 24 | marriage | D | m | corpus ===
scenario: the wedding-planning argument about the guest list
zones: numbers,negation,kin
intent: a couple planning their wedding argue over trimming a guest list that will not fit.
beats:
- the list running well over the number the place can seat
- one proposing to cut distant relatives and coworkers first
- the other refusing to cut one particular name
- the counter: someone from the other side's list has to go instead
- the old grievance about whose family always gets more seats
- a smaller second gathering floated and shot down
- the recount that is still over
- one giving a little ground, nothing settled
held:
- the list running over by about thirty
- a proposed cut of about half that
- one protected name
- the recount still over by about a dozen
cast: the couple planning their wedding; they label their own turns however such a couple would.
text: A couple sits down to trim their wedding guest list, which runs well over the number the place can seat, over by around thirty. One suggests starting with distant relatives and coworkers neither of them talks to often, enough to bring it down by about half that. The other refuses to touch one particular name, insisting that relative has to be there no matter what. The first counters that if that name stays, someone has to go from the other side's list instead, and the old argument about whose family always gets more seats comes back up. Someone floats a smaller second gathering for the names that do not fit, and it gets shot down flat. They recount together and the list is still over, by a dozen or so. One of them gives a little ground; nothing is fully settled. They label their own turns however such a couple would. Write the argument.
=== end ===

zones: money,reported,kin
intent: after a money quarrel with the spouse, giving the sibling the whole account.
beats:
- what the quarrel was actually about, one specific expense
- who brought it up first, and how
- an old money mistake dragged back in
- the sharpest thing said, and by whom
- the silence afterward, and how long it lasted
- how it finally ended, who backed down first
- the teller's own verdict, given to the sibling
held:
- the one expense that started it
- who brought it up first
- the old mistake dragged back in
- the silence and about how long it lasted
- who backed down first
cast: the teller; their spouse, the subject of the account, out of scene; the sibling hearing it, out of scene.
text: You had a money quarrel with your spouse and you are giving your sibling the whole account. Tell them what it was actually about, one specific expense that started it, who brought it up first and how, the old money mistake that got dragged back in even though it had nothing to do with this, the sharpest thing either of you said and who said it, the silence afterward and about how long it lasted, and how it finally ended, including who backed down first. Give your own verdict on the whole thing at the end, the way you would to someone who already takes your side.
=== end ===

=== scenario 27 | marriage | M | s | control ===
scenario: a note for a housemate about the mess
zones: imperative,notice-register,negation
intent: a fed-up note left for the one you share a home with, about the mess piling up again.
beats:
- naming exactly where the mess is piled
- noting this is not the first time
- the demand: what to do, and by when
- the refusal to clear it up again
- how it is signed off
held:
- where the mess is piled
- the repeat count, this makes the third time
- the deadline, before tonight
- the refusal to do it again
cast: the couple sharing a home; the note addressed to the other, who stays out of scene.
text: You are annoyed about the mess piling up again in the place you share with the person you live with, and you are leaving them a note. Write the note: say exactly where it is piled, that this makes the third time this week, ask for it dealt with before you are back tonight, and make plain you are not clearing it up for them again. Sign it off the way the two of you always sign these, a word rather than a name.
=== end ===

=== scenario 29 | marriage | M | l | corpus ===
scenario: what every morning in this house looks like, told to a guest
zones: habitual,temporal,imperative
intent: the whole household's morning routine, laid out beat by beat for a guest staying over.
beats:
- who is always up first, before anyone else and before any alarm
- the one task that person always does in the quiet before the house wakes
- the second person waking to the same grumble every day
- the standing contest over the wash-room that never gets settled
- the children, if there are any, roused in the same fixed order every morning
- the commands called out to get everyone moving, in the same order they always come
- the argument that happens most mornings, close to the same words each time
- the animal's fixed part in the routine, if the house keeps one
- breakfast made the same way every day, and the one who never varies their order
- the item that goes missing most mornings, hunted for in the same few places
- the routine sound that means the house is properly awake
- the person always running the same few minutes late
- the rush at the door: coats grabbed, bags handed over
- the one day of the week the whole rhythm changes
- the same handful of words said at the door every morning before anyone leaves
held:
- who wakes first, before any alarm
- the one quiet task done before the house wakes
- the same daily grumble
- the wash-room contest, never settled
- the fixed waking order for the children
- the same commands, same order, every morning
- the argument that repeats most mornings
- the animal's fixed part, if there is one
- the same breakfast order, never varied
- the same item gone missing most mornings
- the routine sound that means the house is awake
- the same few minutes someone is always late
- the rush at the door
- the one day a week the rhythm changes
cast: the household, a couple and the children in the house if there are any; the guest hearing about it stays out of scene.
text: You have a guest staying over and they ask what mornings are actually like in this house, so you walk them through it; they only listen, and the telling stays yours the whole way, the household's voices coming in only as you quote them. Tell them who is always up first, before anyone else and often before any alarm, and the one small task that person always does in the quiet before the house properly wakes. Tell them how the second person wakes to the same grumble every day, and the standing contest over the wash-room that never gets settled. If there are children in the house, cover how they get roused in the same fixed order every morning, and the same commands called out to get everyone moving, always in the same order. Mention the argument that happens most mornings, close to the same words each time. If there is an animal in the house, give it its fixed part in the routine too. Describe breakfast made the same way every day, and the one person who never once varies their order. Note the item that goes missing most mornings and gets hunted for in the same few places, and the routine sound, whatever it is in this house, that means everyone is finally properly awake. Name the person who is always running the same few minutes late, and the rush at the door: coats grabbed, bags handed over, nothing forgotten only because it never is. Note the one day of the week the whole rhythm changes and the house goes quiet instead. Close with the same handful of words said at the door every morning before anyone leaves, and your own fondness for how absurd the whole repeating performance is, telling it to a guest who has never seen a single morning of it.
=== end ===
