scenario: the card game retold hand by hand, including the cheating accusation
zones: past-narrative,numbers,reported
intent: someone retells a long card game to a friend who was not there, hand by hand, including the cheating accusation that flared up partway through and was never settled.
beats:
- four players sit down and the stakes are agreed before a card is dealt
- the early hands go by ordinary, nobody clearly ahead
- one player's luck begins to turn
- the run stretches to several hands in a row
- a quiet remark about how clean the shuffle looks, brushed off the first time
- the same player deals again
- something noticed in how the cards leave the bottom of the deck
- the accusation spoken aloud, naming the player outright
- the accused's flat denial
- the table splits, siding one way or the other
- the played hands gone back over, settling nothing
- the game continues anyway, uneasy from then on
- the final hand and who actually takes it
- the tally kept at the end of the night
- the argument still unsettled whenever the same group plays since
held:
- four players at the table
- stakes agreed before the first card was dealt
- the winning run stretched to four hands in a row
- the shuffle remark made once, brushed off
- the suspected move: dealing from the bottom of the deck
- the accusation named the dealer outright
- the denial was flat, no admission in it
- the table split roughly down the middle over it
- the earlier hands were gone back over and settled nothing
- eleven hands played in total that night
- the final hand went to a different player than the accused
- the accused ended the night up by two hands overall
- the argument still comes up unsettled whenever the group plays again
cast: you, one of four players at the table, telling it now; the other three players, one of them the one accused; the friend hearing the story, out of scene.
text: You sat down to a card game with three others, four at the table in all, and the stakes were agreed before a single card went out, small enough that nobody minded losing. Tell the whole night to a friend who was not there, hand by hand. The early hands went by ordinary, nobody clearly ahead, until one player's luck began to turn and held on past the point where it looked like luck alone, stretching to four hands running. Someone made a quiet remark about how clean the shuffle looked, and it was brushed off the first time. When the same player dealt again, something in how the cards left the bottom of the deck caught an eye, and this time it did not stay quiet: the accusation was spoken aloud, naming the dealer outright. The denial that came back was flat, no give in it at all. The table split over it, some siding with the accusation and some with the denial, and the hands already played were gone back over and argued out, settling nothing either way. Everyone kept playing anyway, though the ease of the night was gone from then on. Give the final hand, who actually took it, and the tally kept at the close: eleven hands played in total, the accused up by two over everyone else by the time the night ended. Tell it the way you would to someone who missed the whole thing, ending on how the argument still comes up unsettled, every time the same group sits down to play again.
=== end ===

One of them brings up an older match between the two of them for comparison, claiming it proves a pattern, and the other rejects the comparison outright. Neither is willing to concede a single point by the time they let it drop. Write the exchange.

Give the performance itself, and the one moment in it that drew the loudest laugh of the whole night, the moment that still gets brought up. Tell the whole season to someone who was not there for any of it. The first weeks of training were hard in a way you had not expected, the body unprepared for what was suddenly being asked of it, every session leaving you more wrecked than the last. You settled into a routine after that and kept to it most days, whatever the weather or the mood. An early setback nearly stopped the whole thing before it had properly started, an injury or a bout of exhaustion bad enough that training stopped for a stretch, and for a while you were not sure you would get back to it at all. You cut back the plan once you did return, more careful than before. For part of the season a training partner worked alongside you, and having company on the hard days made a real difference on the ones it did not. One training day in particular stands out above the rest, one that nearly ended the whole attempt outright, body and will both giving out at once. The doubt hit hardest around the middle of it all, the finish still far enough off that it barely felt real. A small breakthrough somewhere past that point renewed your resolve, nothing dramatic, just a session that went better than any before it. Toward the end you tightened the routine further, careful now with rest and with food both, and in the final week you eased off rather than pushing harder, trusting the months already put in. The night before the feat itself you barely slept, lying awake going over it long before there was any need to. Give the feat itself: the hardest stretch of it coming near the very end, when everything in you wanted to stop and did not. Give the finish, and what it actually felt like once it was over, nothing like what you had pictured beforehand. In the days after, the body needed real time to recover, several days before it felt like your own again. Tell it the way you would to someone who was not there for any of it, closing on how the whole season changed what you now believe you are capable of.
