# Food scenarios

=== scenario 30 | food | D | m | corpus ===
scenario: explaining a grandmother's recipe from memory
zones: imperative,numbers,food
intent: a person recites a grandmother's recipe from memory for someone who wants to learn it, correcting themselves as they go.
beats:
- the other person asks for the recipe outright
- the first ingredient is named with only a rough, guessed amount
- a second ingredient is almost left out, then added back in when remembered
- the order of the steps is worked through one at a time
- the one step the grandmother always warned never to rush
- a way of telling the dish is done that involves no clock
- an admission that one part was never measured, only judged by eye
- a question about swapping an ingredient, answered with a qualified yes
held:
- four ingredients named across the telling
- the rough guessed amount given for the first ingredient
- the step that cannot be rushed, tied to a wait of about twenty minutes
- the sign that tells you the dish is done
- the one ingredient judged only by eye, never measured
- the substitution asked about and the qualified answer given
cast: two people, one who learned the dish from a grandmother and has never written it down, the other asking to be taught it; they label their own turns however they would speak of each other.
text: Someone is trying to get a family recipe out of a relative who learned it from their grandmother and never wrote it down. Set the scene: the other person asks outright for the recipe, and the relative recites it from memory, correcting themselves more than once along the way. Cover the first ingredient and the rough amount given for it, a second ingredient almost left out and then added back in, the order of the steps, the one step the grandmother always warned never to rush, a way of telling when it is done that involves no clock, an admission that one part was never measured and is only judged by eye, and a question about substituting an ingredient answered with a qualified yes. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

scenario: describing the perfect meal to someone who has never had it
zones: food,comparative,intensifier
intent: you describe your idea of the perfect meal to someone who has never experienced anything like it.
beats:
- you name the occasion or feeling that calls for this meal
- the dish that stands out most is singled out and described
- the exact texture or temperature that has to be right is specified
- a comparison is drawn to the closest thing the listener might know, and why it falls short
- the smell that reaches you before the food itself
- how the meal is eaten, and why that matters
- the number of people it is normally shared among
- the single moment in the meal you always wait for
held:
- the meal runs to three or more parts
- the one dish that stands out most
- the exact texture or temperature that has to be right
- the nearest comparison, named as close but not it
- how the meal is eaten
- the number of people it is normally shared among
cast: you, describing the meal; the listener stays silent and out of scene, never speaking back.
text: You are telling someone who has never had it what your idea of a perfect meal is, and they only listen, never speaking back. Describe it to them directly: what occasion or mood calls for this meal, the dish that stands out most, the exact texture or temperature that has to be right, something close in their own experience that this still beats, the smell that reaches you before the food does, how the meal is eaten, the number of people it is normally shared among, and the single moment in the meal you always wait for. Write what you tell them.
=== end ===

Write the exchange.

- the customer answers and asks something back about the owner's business

- a joke or bit of ribbing passes between them
- the food is finished and handed over
- payment is made and a goodbye assumes a next visit
held:
- how many times a week the customer usually comes
- the small change made to the usual order today
- one topic raised about the customer's life
- one topic raised about the owner's business
- the joke or ribbing shared between them
- the goodbye line that assumes a next time
cast: the stall owner and the regular customer; they label their own turns however they would speak of each other.
text: A regular customer walks up to a food stall they visit often, and the owner already half-knows their order before they say it. Set the scene: the owner greets them and starts the usual order from memory, the customer confirms it with one small change today, and the two fall into talk while the food is made, covering the customer's life, how business has been for the stall lately, and a joke or bit of ribbing between them. Mention how many times a week the customer usually comes. End with the food handed over, payment made, and a goodbye that assumes a next time. Write the exchange.

=== scenario 35 | food | D | m | corpus ===
scenario: the street-food debate: whose stall is best and why
zones: comparative,food,intensifier
intent: two friends argue over which food stall nearby is the best, and neither backs down.
beats:
- one friend declares their favorite stall the best, unprompted
- the other names a rival stall just as fast
- the first defends their pick, praising a specific dish or quality
- the second finds fault with that pick and defends their own the same way
- one recalls a specific bad experience they had at the rival stall
- the argument escalates with an exaggerated claim
- a third stall is named that neither will call bad
- they agree to go eat at both picks again to settle it properly
held:
- the two rival stalls named
- the specific dish or quality praised for the first stall
- the bad experience recalled at the rival stall
- the exaggerated claim made in the heat of it
- the compromise stall neither will call bad
cast: two friends close enough to argue like this; they label their own turns however they would speak of each other.
text: Two friends are arguing about which food stall nearby is the best, and neither will back down. Set the scene: one declares their pick the best outright, the other names a rival stall just as fast, and each defends their choice by praising a specific dish or quality while finding fault with the other's pick. One brings up a specific bad experience they had at the rival stall. The argument escalates with an exaggerated claim from one side, until a third stall gets named that neither can call bad, and they agree to go eat at both picks again to settle it properly. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

scenario: hosting the holiday meal for the first time: planning, disasters, verdicts
zones: past-narrative,food,exclamation
intent: you tell the whole story of hosting a shared holiday meal for the first time, the planning, what went wrong, and how it was judged in the end.
beats:
- deciding to be the one hosting this year, and the nerves that came with it
- drawing up a menu more ambitious than sensible
- the guest count growing past what was first planned
- the shopping trip and the one thing that could not be found anywhere
- a late night before spent on prep
- a morning schedule timed out on paper
- the schedule not surviving contact with the actual morning
- the first real disaster: something burned, overcooked, or ruined outright
- the scramble to fix or replace it
- a guest arriving early and getting pulled straight into helping
- the stove or oven running out of room, dishes competing for space and time
- one dish coming out far better than expected
- the meal finally starting later than planned
- the first honest reactions once people take a bite
- someone's blunt verdict on the dish that did not work
- the half-joking vow at the end about whether to host again
held:
- the number the guest count grew to
- the ambitious menu item that pushed things over
- the one shopping item that could not be found
- how many hours the prep night ran late
- the dish that was the first disaster, and what happened to it
- the guest who arrived early, named by relation
- the number of dishes competing for stove or oven space
- the dish that came out far better than expected
- how much later than planned the meal started
- the dish that got the loudest praise
- the vow made at the end
cast: you, the first-time host; the kin and guests who came, referred to by relation or role only.
text: You hosted a shared holiday meal for the first time this year, and you are telling someone who was not there the whole story: the planning, the disasters, and how it was all judged in the end. Tell it in order: deciding to be the one hosting this time and the nerves that came with it, the menu you drew up that turned out more ambitious than sensible, how the guest count kept growing past what you first planned, the shopping trip and the one thing you could not find anywhere, the late night before spent on prep, the morning schedule you timed out on paper that did not survive contact with the actual morning, the first real disaster where something burned, overcooked, or was ruined outright, the scramble to fix or replace it, a guest who arrived early and got pulled straight into helping, the stove or oven running out of room with dishes competing for space and time, one dish that came out far better than you expected, the meal finally starting later than planned, the first honest reactions once people took a bite, someone's blunt verdict on the dish that did not work, the dish that disappeared first and got the loudest praise, and the half-joking vow at the end about whether you would ever host again. Tell the whole day, the way you would to someone who missed it.
=== end ===

scenario: harvest day: who does what, dawn to done
zones: past-narrative,imperative,temporal
intent: you narrate the full arc of a harvest day, from before first light to the last task done, and who did what across it.
beats:
- waking before light and the first task handed out
- the easiest job given to whoever is youngest and able to help
- the heaviest part of the work put on whoever is strongest
- the shift in tasks once the first section is cleared
- someone falling behind and getting quiet help rather than blame
- a meal brought out partway through the work
- a joke or an argument over pace or method
- the weather turning or threatening midway
- the change of plan the weather forces
- the push through the afternoon once it holds or clears
- something going wrong, a tool failing or a section spoiled
- the word passed to move faster as the light starts to go
- the last section finished with everyone converging on it
- the tally of what got brought in by the end
- the collapse once the work is actually done
- one last small chore handed to someone before anyone is allowed to rest
held:
- the number of people working the day
- who is given the easiest job, by role
- who takes the heaviest part, by role
- what goes wrong midway, a tool or a section named
- how the weather turns or threatens
- the tally of what is brought in by the end
- the last small chore assigned before rest
cast: you, narrating the day; the people working alongside you, referred to only by role and relative position such as the youngest, the strongest, and the one who fell behind.
text: You are telling the whole story of one harvest day, from before first light to the moment the last task was done, and who did what across it. Tell it in order: waking before light and the first task handed out, the easiest job given to whoever is youngest and able to help, the heaviest part of the work put on whoever is strongest, the shift in tasks once the first section is cleared, someone falling behind and getting quiet help rather than blame, the meal brought out partway through, a joke or an argument over pace or method, the weather turning or threatening and the change of plan it forces, the push through the afternoon once it holds or clears, something going wrong, a tool failing or a section spoiled, and how it gets handled, the word passed to move faster as the light starts to go, the last section finished with everyone converging on it together, the tally of what got brought in by the end, the collapse once the work is actually done, and one last small chore handed to someone before anyone is allowed to rest. Tell the whole day, start to finish.
