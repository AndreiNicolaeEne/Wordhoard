=== scenario 90 | death | D | s | control ===
scenario: condolences said simply to a grieving neighbor
zones: face-work,endearment,formulaic-verse
intent: a neighbor offers plain, careful condolences to another neighbor freshly grieving.
beats:
- catching the neighbor at a quiet moment, not intruding further
- the condolence said plainly, the same few words almost anyone would reach for
- a small, specific offer of practical help
- the grieving neighbor's short, tired thanks
- the fond word used once
- a promise to check back tomorrow
held:
- the condolence kept to a few plain sentences
- the offer is specific: a meal brought over the next day
- the fond word said once, in the thanks given back
- the promise to check back the next day
cast: a neighbor offering condolences; the grieving neighbor; they label their own turns however such neighbors would call each other.
text: One neighbor catches another at a quiet moment, careful not to intrude any further than the moment allows. What gets said is short, the same few plain words almost anyone would reach for here: that they are sorry, that there are no better words for it, and that they are close by if anything is needed. No question is asked about how it happened, not now. Instead comes something practical and specific, a meal brought over the next day, one less thing to think about. The grieving neighbor thanks them, tired, the fond word they have always used for each other coming back just once, plain. Before stepping away, a promise is made to check on them again tomorrow. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

scenario: the full account of the funeral, told to someone who missed it
zones: past-narrative,kin,food
intent: you give someone who missed the funeral the whole account of the day, start to finish.
beats:
- word of the death reaching everyone fast, and the two hard days of scrambling before the funeral itself
- one relative traveling a full day to make it, arriving with barely time to spare
- the house full from early morning, more food arriving than anyone had asked for
- what people wore, plain and dark, nothing more needing saying about it
- everyone gathering in one room before the walk out, quieter than a room that full should be
- who helped carry them
- one person breaking down suddenly, unplanned, and nobody rushing to stop it
- who spoke, and the one line from it that everyone still repeats
- an unexpected laugh in the middle of it, over a memory that would not stay serious
- the walk to the resting place, longer than you remembered it being
- the few words said there, plain and short
- the handful of earth let go
- the walk back, and the meal laid out at the house after
- the relative nobody expected to see, showing up anyway
- the house emptying out by evening, quiet arriving all at once
- what you wish they had been there to see for themselves
held:
- word spread within a day, and the funeral was held two days after that
- one relative traveled a full day to arrive just in time
- the house filled past what it usually holds
- one person broke down at a specific, unplanned moment
- one line from what was spoken is repeated by everyone since
- the meal after was put together by whoever was closest to the family
- one relative nobody expected showed up anyway
cast: you, giving the account; the relative who missed it, listening now, never taking a turn of their own; the dead relative and the wider family, present only in the telling.
text: Someone close to you has died, and you are giving the full account of the funeral to a relative who could not be there, listening now, never taking a turn of their own. Word reached everyone fast, and there were two hard days of scrambling before the funeral itself could be held. One relative traveled a full day to make it, arriving with barely enough time to spare before the house filled. By early morning the house was already full, more food arriving than anyone had asked for, plates covering every surface. People wore plain, dark clothes, nothing more needing saying about that. Before the walk out, everyone gathered in one room, quieter than you expected a room that full to ever be. A few of you carried them out together. Partway through, one person broke down suddenly, without warning, and for a moment nobody moved to stop it, just let it happen. Someone spoke, and one line from what they said has been repeated by nearly everyone since. In the middle of it all, somebody remembered something that would not stay serious, and the laugh that followed took everyone by surprise, including the one who let it out. The walk to the resting place felt longer than you remembered it being, though you have walked it before. The words said there were plain and short, nothing dressed up. A handful of earth was let go, and that was that. The walk back felt shorter, and the meal laid out at the house after was put together by whoever was closest to the family, enough to feed everyone twice over. One relative nobody expected to see showed up anyway, and it meant more than anyone said aloud. By evening the house had emptied out, and the quiet arrived all at once, heavier than the noise had been. Give them the whole account, the way you would to someone who was not there to see any of it, and close by telling them plainly what you wish they had been there to see for themselves.
=== end ===

scenario: conversation at the wake, memory by memory
zones: past-narrative,reported,exclamation
intent: the closest few who stay latest into the night trade memories of the person they lost, one by one.
beats:
- the house thinning out to just the closest four or five, sitting up together
- the first memory offered, small and ordinary
- a gentle correction from someone else about a detail
- a laugh nobody meant to let out
- a second memory, a habit or phrase the dead person always used
- someone repeating the phrase aloud, exactly as they used to say it
- a memory that surprises the room, something not everyone knew, drawing a startled exclamation before it settles quiet
- someone breaking the silence by passing food or drink around again
- an old disagreement between two of them surfacing briefly
- it getting dropped, neither pressing it further tonight
- a story about the dead person being impressively stubborn, told with real fondness
- the room agreeing, without needing to say it outright, on the one trait they will always be remembered by
- one of them crying quietly while the talk keeps going gently around them
- the talk turning practical, who will handle what in the coming days
- the last one still there closing the night out with a small, unplanned toast
held:
- the number left sitting up, four or five
- the phrase the dead person always used, repeated exactly
- the surprising memory not everyone knew
- the old disagreement surfaced and dropped
- the trait the room settles on remembering them by
- the plain, unplanned toast that closes the night
cast: the closest few who stay latest at the gathering after the death, kin and friends both; they label their own turns however such people would call each other.
text: By the middle of the night the house has thinned out to just the closest four or five, sitting up together while everyone else has gone home. Someone starts it off with a small, ordinary memory, nothing dramatic, just something the dead person used to do. Another gently corrects a detail in it, and the first laughs and admits they always got that part wrong. Somebody else lets out a laugh they clearly did not mean to, and nobody minds. A second memory comes next, a habit or a phrase the dead person said so often that everyone in the room can still hear it, and one of them says it out loud exactly the way it used to be said, word for word. Then someone offers a memory that surprises the rest of them, something not everyone in the room even knew, and it draws a startled exclamation from someone before the room settles quiet again for a moment. Someone breaks the silence by passing the food or drink around again, an excuse to move. An old disagreement between two of them surfaces briefly, something unrelated that has nothing to do with tonight, but neither presses it any further, not tonight. Somebody tells the story of the one time the dead person was impressively, famously stubborn about something small, and it is told with real fondness, no edge left in it now. Without anyone saying so outright, the room seems to settle on the one trait they will always be remembered by. One of them starts crying quietly partway through, and the talk keeps going gently around them rather than stopping, nobody making a show of it. Eventually it turns practical, who will handle what in the coming days, said plainly and without ceremony. The last one still awake closes the night out with a small, unplanned toast, nothing prepared, just what comes to mind. Write the exchange, the memories traded back and forth exactly as such people would trade them.
=== end ===

intent: a parent sits two children down and tells them, plainly and gently, that their grandparent has died.
beats:
- gathering the children close, sitting down properly for it
- the plain statement that the grandparent has died
- the older child's searching question, wanting to understand
- the parent's honest, gentle answer
- the younger child's more literal question
- the parent's patient response to that too
- a hug offered and taken, the fond word said for the younger one as they're pulled in
- a question about whether they will see the grandparent again, answered honestly
- the parent naming plainly what happens over the next few days
held:
- two children, one older, one younger
- the grandparent named by relation, not further specified
- the older child's question and the younger child's question, kept distinct
- the fond word used once, for the younger child
- what happens next, named plainly
cast: a parent; two children, siblings, one older and one younger; they label their own turns the way such kin would.
text: A parent sits the two children down properly, close, before saying anything at all. Then it is said plainly: their grandparent has died. The older one asks a searching question, wanting to actually understand what that means, and the parent answers as honestly and gently as they can, not reaching for anything easier than the truth. The younger one asks something more literal and simpler, and gets just as patient an answer. A hug is offered and taken, both children pulled in close, the fond word said for the younger one as they're pulled in tight. One of them asks, in a small voice, whether they will ever see the grandparent again, and the parent answers that honestly too, not dodging it. Before letting them go back to whatever they were doing, the parent tells them plainly what happens over the next few days, so it does not come as another surprise later. Write the exchange.

=== scenario 97 | death | M | l | control ===
scenario: the first year of grief, described to someone entering their own
zones: temporal,body,past-narrative
intent: you lay out for a newly grieving friend, sitting with them now, what your own first year of grief actually looked like.
beats:
- the first days, numb, arrangements crowding out the reality of it
- the first time truly alone in the house afterward, the silence landing hard
- the body's reaction in the weeks after: appetite gone, sleep in strange short bursts
- the first month, the world visibly moving on faster than you were ready for
- the first ordinary date that passed and undid you without warning
- a smell or object months later triggering a wave out of nowhere
- the long middle stretch, a fog that dulled even the good days
- one thoughtless comment from someone that stung more than it should have
- one small, unexpected kindness that helped more than anything planned
- the anniversary of the death, approached with real dread
- getting through that day however clumsily, and it being enough
- the slow, uneven return of ordinary appetite and sleep
- the day you caught yourself laughing for real, and the guilt right behind it
- by the year's end, carrying it differently, lighter but never gone
- the closing reassurance: it does not stay this hard, though there is no set timeline
held:
- the numb first days, arrangements crowding everything else out
- the body's reaction: appetite gone, sleep broken, for the first weeks
- the first ordinary date that undid you
- one thoughtless comment, one unexpected kindness
- the one-year mark, approached with dread and gotten through
- the closing reassurance, plainly stated
cast: you, further into your own grief; the friend, newly grieving, sitting with you now, listening, never taking a turn of their own.
text: You are sitting with a friend who has just lost someone, further into your own grief than they are into theirs, laying out honestly what your first year actually looked like while they only listen, never taking a turn of their own. Tell them the first days were mostly numb, the arrangements and the people coming and going crowding out the reality of it so there was almost no time to feel anything directly. Tell them about the first time you were truly alone in the house afterward, once everyone had finally gone home, and how hard the silence landed once nothing was left to distract from it. Cover what your body did in the weeks after, appetite gone for days at a stretch, sleep arriving in strange short bursts instead of anything whole. Tell them the first month was strange because the world kept visibly moving on around you faster than you were anywhere near ready for. Name the first ordinary date, nothing marked as important beforehand, that passed and undid you completely without any warning at all. Tell them that months later, a smell or some small object could still trigger a wave out of nowhere, with no pattern to when it would happen. Describe the long middle stretch honestly, a fog that dulled even the days that should have been good ones. Mention the one thoughtless comment someone made that stung far more than it should have, and set beside it the one small, unexpected kindness that helped more than anything planned or official ever did. Tell them about the anniversary of the death, how you approached it with real dread for weeks beforehand, and that you got through that day however clumsily you managed it, and that being enough was the whole point. Cover how your appetite and sleep came back slowly and unevenly, never all at once. Tell them about the day you caught yourself laughing for real, and the guilt that followed right behind it, uninvited. By the year's end, tell them, you were carrying it differently, lighter in places but never actually gone. Close by telling them plainly that it does not stay this hard forever, though there is no fixed timeline for when it eases, and that they should not measure their own year against anyone else's, including yours.
=== end ===
