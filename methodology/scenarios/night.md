# Night scenarios

=== scenario 121 | night | D | s | corpus ===
scenario: the mysterious noise in the street last night, asked about
zones: interrogative,spatial,temporal
intent: one neighbor asks another whether they heard the strange noise outside last night, and they try to work out what it could have been.
beats:
- one neighbor asking whether the other heard the noise
- the time placed, sometime after midnight
- where it seemed to come from, out in the street rather than either house
- the guess floated, an animal knocking something over, and the doubt about it
- the agreement to keep an ear out if it happens again
held:
- the noise happened after midnight
- it came from out in the street, not from either house
- the one guess floated, an animal knocking something over, unconfirmed
- the agreement to keep listening if it happens again
cast: two neighbors; they label their own turns however such neighbors would.
text: One neighbor asks another whether they heard the noise outside last night, sometime after midnight, out in the street rather than close to either house. Neither is sure what caused it: one guesses it was an animal knocking something over, but is not convinced. They go back and forth on what else it could have been, and end by agreeing to keep an ear out if it happens again tonight. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

scenario: the local legend or ghost story as the old people tell it
zones: past-narrative,reported,oath
intent: an elder tells the town's own legend exactly as it has always been told, the shape of what it does, when it shows itself, and the proof people still point to, half believing it even while telling it.
beats:
- how the subject usually gets brought up, late in an evening or when someone new asks
- the shape of the thing itself, felt more than seen
- what it is said to do, call out in a voice like someone you love to draw you off the path
- when it is said to come, only after midnight and only with no moon
- the one local spot it gets tied to, never placed as belonging anywhere bigger
- how the teller first heard it, as a child, from an elder in the family
- whether that elder told it as plain fact or with an uneasy laugh
- the first proof usually offered, a patch of ground that stays bare
- a second proof, something reported secondhand from someone else's telling
- the one relative's encounter that gets told in full every time
- what that relative swears, word for word, as it gets repeated
- the detail that makes even doubters go quiet for a second
- the detail that gives skeptics their opening to laugh it off
- the honest admission that the story shifts depending on who tells it
- the small precaution still quietly kept, never answering a name called from the dark
- the teller's own half-belief, admitted at the end
held:
- what it does, calls out in the voice of someone loved, meaning to draw you off the path
- when it comes, only after midnight, only with no moon
- the proof, a patch of bare ground at the one local spot, nothing has ever grown there
- the relative's encounter, years back, three or four steps taken toward the voice before stopping
- the precaution, never answer a name called from the dark until you have checked with your own eyes
cast: the teller, an elder of the house or street; the younger listener who asked to hear it, listening the whole way through without a word; the elder who first told it to the teller, out of scene; the relative in the one full encounter, out of scene.
text: Someone younger has asked you, an elder of the place, to tell the story that has always been told about the thing that calls out after dark, and once you start they only listen, not a single word breaking in the whole way through. Start with how the subject usually comes up, late in an evening or whenever someone new wants to know why people warn against walking certain stretches after dark. Describe the shape of it the way it has always been described, felt more than seen, something that takes a voice before it takes any form at all. Lay out what it is actually said to do: call out in a voice that sounds exactly like someone you love, meaning to draw you off the path and away from anyone who could help. Place when it is said to come, only after midnight, and only on nights with no moon at all. Name the one spot near here it gets tied to, without ever pretending it belongs to anywhere bigger than that, a bend in a road, a stretch by water, whatever your own place points to. Tell how you first heard it yourself, as a child, from an elder in your own family, and whether that elder told it as plain fact or with a laugh that did not quite reach the eyes. Bring in the first piece of proof usually offered, a patch of ground at that spot where nothing has ever grown, no matter what gets planted there. Add a second piece of proof, something reported the way it always gets reported, secondhand, from somebody who heard it from somebody else. Then tell the one encounter that gets told in full every single time, a relative of yours, years back, who heard their own name called from off the path and had gone three or four steps toward it before something made them stop and turn back. Repeat what that relative swears to this day, word for word, the way the family always repeats it. Name the one detail in the story that makes even the doubters go quiet for a second, and the other detail, just as real, that gives the doubters their opening to laugh it off anyway. Admit honestly that the story changes a little depending on who is telling it, softer from some mouths and harder from others, and that you have never minded the difference. Close with the small thing people here still quietly do because of it, never answering if their name gets called from somewhere dark until they have checked with their own eyes who is actually calling, and your own honest admission that you do not fully believe it yourself and have never once broken that rule.
=== end ===

=== scenario 123 | night | D | l | corpus ===
scenario: a strange dream described over the morning drink
zones: past-narrative,interrogative,exclamation
intent: over the morning drink, one person describes a strange dream in careful detail while the other keeps interrupting with questions and reactions.
beats:
- the two of them settling with the morning drink, one visibly distracted
- prompted, the dreamer admitting they cannot stop thinking about last night's dream
- the dream opening on an ordinary walk home, familiar at first
- the road stretching on far longer than it should
- arriving to find a room in the house that was never there before
- the listener's first interrupting question, about the room
- inside it, a parent no longer living, sitting there calm as anything
- the parent speaking, though the words will not come back clear now, only the feeling of them
- the listener's exclamation at that, properly unsettled
- the dreamer trying and failing to pull the exact words back
- the dream shifting again, into an urgent search for something misplaced
- the search turning more frantic the longer it goes on
- the listener's second interrupting question, what was actually being looked for
- the panic climbing until it breaks the dreamer awake
- waking with the heart still racing, unsure for a moment which was real
- the two of them turning it over together afterward, half-joking about what it means
held:
- the walk in the dream stretched on far longer than the real distance ever does
- the extra room, never there in waking life
- the parent no longer living, appearing calm and seated
- what was searched for goes unnamed, even to the dreamer
- the saying about dreaming of someone gone, invoked and half-believed
cast: the dreamer, telling it over the drink; the listener close to them, sharing the drink and the household, asking and reacting throughout; the parent no longer living, appearing only inside the dream, out of scene in the present.
text: Two people sit down together with the morning drink, and one of them is somewhere else entirely, still caught up in a dream from the night before. Asked what is wrong, they admit they cannot stop turning it over. Have them describe it properly: it started as an ordinary walk home, nothing strange about it at first, except the road kept going on and on, far past where home should have turned up. When they finally arrived, the house had a room in it that has never existed in waking life, and the listener breaks in right there, wanting to know what kind of room it was. Inside it was a parent no longer living, sitting as calm and ordinary as if no time had passed at all, and speaking, though the actual words will not come back clearly now, only the feeling of having been told something. The listener exclaims at that, properly unsettled, and the dreamer tries and fails a few times to pull the exact words back before giving up on it. Then the dream shifted again, into an urgent search for something that had gone missing, nothing turning up where it should have been, the search growing more frantic the longer it went on. The listener interrupts a second time, asking outright what was actually being looked for, and the dreamer admits they could not say, even inside the dream itself. The panic kept climbing until it broke the sleep outright, and they woke with the heart still racing, lying still a long moment before being sure which of the two, the dream or the room they woke in, was the real one. Let the two of them finish by turning it over together, half-joking about what it is supposed to mean when someone gone turns up so calm in a dream, neither one quite settling on an answer. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

scenario: followed in the dark, or believing so: the walk home retold
zones: past-narrative,body,spatial
intent: you retell the walk home when you were sure, or nearly sure, that someone was following you in the dark.
beats:
- leaving later than planned, the walk home already darker than usual
- the first sense that something is off, footsteps behind landing close to your own
- testing it without turning around, slowing once and speeding up once
- both times, the steps behind seeming to match
- the decision to cut through the better-lit or busier way instead of the usual shortcut
- the one glance back allowed, telling nothing certain either way
- the last stretch, walked faster than admitted at the time
- reaching the door, the plain relief of it shut behind you
- whether it was ever confirmed as nothing, or never found out either way
held:
- left later than planned
- the steps behind matched pace twice, tested deliberately
- cut through the better-lit or busier way home
- never got a clear look back
- reached the door safely
cast: you, walking home alone; whoever or whatever was behind, never confirmed, out of scene; whoever the account is told to afterward, out of scene.
text: You left later than you meant to, and the walk home was already darker than you like it to be by the time you set out. Not far in, you got the sense someone was behind you, footsteps landing close to the same time as your own. Tell how you tested it without turning around outright, slowing your pace once and then speeding it up once, and how both times the steps behind you seemed to match. Cover the decision you made to cut through the better-lit or busier way home instead of your usual shortcut, and the one glance back you allowed yourself, which told you nothing certain either way. Describe the last stretch, walked faster than you would ever admit to at the time, and the plain relief of getting the door shut behind you. Close with whether you ever found out it was nothing at all, or whether you still do not know, and how you tell it now, half embarrassed at how sure you were in the moment.
=== end ===

scenario: reacting to shocking news: the first words to whoever is nearest
zones: exclamation,interrogative,reported
intent: someone bursts in with shocking news and blurts it to whoever happens to be nearest, who reacts before any details are clear.
beats:
- someone arriving clearly shaken, before any explanation
- the news blurted out at once, incomplete
- the listener's first reaction, pure disbelief
- a quick question demanding more
- the teller struggling to get the fuller version out straight
- the two of them reacting together, still catching up
held:
- the news arrives with no warmup, blurted at once
- the listener's first reaction is pure disbelief
- at least one question demanding more before anything calms down
cast: the one bringing the news; a housemate who happens to be nearest; they label their own turns however such people would.
text: Someone bursts in clearly shaken and blurts out shocking news before they have even caught their breath, to whoever happens to be standing nearest. The one hearing it reacts first with pure disbelief, then fires off a quick question demanding more before anything else gets said. The one who brought the news struggles to get the fuller version out straight, stopping and starting. Let the two of them react together for a moment, still catching up to what was just said. They label their own turns however such people would. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

=== scenario 126 | night | D | m | corpus ===
scenario: the thing that cannot be explained, sworn to a skeptic
zones: oath,negation,intensifier
intent: someone insists, swearing to it, on something strange they experienced that has no explanation, against a friend who will not accept it.
beats:
- the skeptic doubting something the other insists happened
- the teller swearing flatly they were not half-asleep or imagining it
- the teller describing plainly what actually happened
- the skeptic offering a rational explanation
- the teller ruling it out with one specific detail
- the skeptic trying a second explanation
- that explanation ruled out too, the teller's insistence growing more intense
- the teller swearing an oath on something they hold dear
- the exchange ending unresolved, neither side giving ground
held:
- at least two rational explanations offered and ruled out in turn
- the specific detail that rules out the first explanation
- the oath sworn, on something the teller genuinely holds dear
- the exchange ends unresolved
cast: the one who saw or experienced it, insisting; a friend of theirs, playing the skeptic; they label their own turns however such friends would.
text: One person insists on something strange that happened to them, and a friend of theirs refuses to believe a word of it. Set the scene: the skeptic's first guess is that the other was half-asleep or imagining things, and the teller swears flatly that they were wide awake and thinking clearly the whole time. Have the teller describe what actually happened, plainly, without dressing it up. The skeptic offers a rational explanation for it, something ordinary that would account for it easily, and the teller shoots that down with one specific detail that rules it out completely. The skeptic tries a second explanation, and that gets ruled out too, the teller's insistence growing more intense each time rather than less. Close with the teller swearing an oath on something they genuinely hold dear that every word of it is true, and the skeptic still shaking their head, unconvinced, neither one giving any ground by the end. They label their own turns however such friends would. Write the exchange.
=== end ===
