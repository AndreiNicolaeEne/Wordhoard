scenario: getting everyone together for tonight: the back-and-forth
zones: temporal,interrogative,tags-backchannel
intent: two friends volley messages back and forth to pin down who is free and settle a plan for the wider group tonight.
beats:
- one proposes a time and place for tonight
- the other flags a problem with that time right away
- they go down the group one by one, checking who is in
- one friend is confirmed unable to come, and why
- back-and-forth over a fallback time, neither fully happy
- a fallback time is settled on
- one offers to pass the word to the rest
- the final headcount given, still not exact
held:
- the time first proposed
- the friend who cannot come, and the stated reason
- the fallback time finally settled on
- the headcount, around six or seven
- who volunteers to spread the word
cast: two friends coordinating tonight's plan on behalf of the wider group; they label their own turns however such friends would.
text: Two friends are trying to get the wider group together for tonight, going back and forth to make it happen. One proposes a time and names where everyone should meet. The other flags a problem with that time right away and the two start working around it. They go down the group one by one, checking who is actually free. One friend turns out unable to come, and the reason gets named plainly. They go back and forth over a fallback time, neither quite happy with what the other suggests, until something is finally settled that works well enough for most people. One offers to pass the word to the rest of the group so it does not fall on the other alone. They land on a headcount, somewhere around six or seven, though even that is not fully exact. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

Tell them about the low point where the whole thing nearly broke apart, and how it got mended. Name the one tradition that has held since the very start. Close by naming what the newest member has already done to belong, small as it might be, and welcome them into it plainly, close as kin by now. Tell the whole history, the way you would to someone who is about to become part of it.

scenario: getting the drunk friend home, retold with affection
zones: past-narrative,body,exclamation
intent: someone tells, with real fondness, the whole ordeal of getting a badly drunk friend home safely.
beats:
- the state the friend was in by the end of the night
- the first sign it had gone too far
- the friend insisting they were fine, contradicted at once by something physical
- the negotiation to leave, resisted at first
- the walk home, an arm kept around them the whole way
- a stop along the way to be sick or sit down
- a passerby's reaction to the state of them
- a wrong turn taken in the confusion
- the attempt to get water into them, refused, then relented
- something maudlin and overly sentimental said along the way
- the struggle with the door on arrival
- getting them safely down, onto a bed or a couch
- the promise, made that night, never to drink like that again
- the check-in the next morning
- the friend's hazy memory of the night, filled back in, mostly
held:
- the first clear sign it had gone too far
- the physical evidence that contradicted the friend's own claim of being fine
- the wrong turn taken
- the maudlin thing said along the way
- the promise made that night
- the state of the friend's memory the next morning
cast: you, telling the story now; the very drunk friend from that night, out of scene now but present throughout the telling; a passerby, unnamed, briefly in the story.
text: You are telling the story now, with real affection, of the night you had to get a badly drunk friend home. Tell it in order: the state they were in by the end of the night, and the first clear sign it had actually gone too far. They insisted they were fine, and something physical contradicted that at once, legs that would not hold or words that would not come out straight. Getting them to actually leave took some negotiating, resisted at first. Tell the walk home, an arm kept around them the whole way, and the stop somewhere along it to be sick or just sit down for a while. Mention a passerby's reaction to the state the two of you were in. Cover the wrong turn taken in the confusion, and the struggle to get any water into them, refused at first and then given in to. Include the one maudlin, overly sentimental thing they said somewhere along the way, the kind of thing that only comes out this drunk. Tell the struggle with the door once you finally made it, and getting them safely down onto a bed or a couch. Include the promise they made that very night never to drink like that again, and how little that promise was believed even as it was made. Close with the check-in the next morning and how much of the night they actually remembered, filled back in for them, mostly. Tell the whole ordeal the way you would to someone who loves this friend as much as you do.
=== end ===
