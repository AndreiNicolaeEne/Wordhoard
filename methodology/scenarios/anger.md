scenario: swearing at the dropped thing: the eruption and the recovery
zones: oath,exclamation,body
intent: you drop and break something carried across a room, erupt in cursing, then catch yourself and recover into rueful laughter as you clean it up.
beats:
- carrying something that slips and drops
- it hitting the floor and breaking completely
- the immediate eruption of real oath and exclamation forms, unscripted here
- catching yourself, half-checking if anyone heard
- the anger draining into a short laugh or sigh
- crouching to pick up the pieces
held:
- the dropped object breaks completely, left unnamed so each node supplies its own
- the eruption is a real burst of oath and exclamation forms, never specified here
- a beat of catching yourself, checking whether anyone heard
- the anger resolving into a short laugh or sigh
- ending mid-recovery, crouched down picking up the pieces
cast: you, alone when the thing breaks; no one else present or addressed.
text: You are carrying something across a room when it slips from your hand and hits the floor, breaking completely. Write the eruption that follows: whatever oaths and exclamations you would actually reach for, let loose without filtering, aimed at nothing and no one, heat flooding through you for a second with the anger of it. Give it only a few seconds before you catch yourself, checking without meaning to whether anyone heard. Let the anger drain into a short laugh or a sigh at your own reaction, then have you crouch down and start picking up the pieces. Write the eruption and the recovery, never dictating which words you use, only that they come and then pass.
=== end ===

zones: interrogative,negation,money
intent: money kept aside for the month goes missing, and a search around the house narrows into a direct, flatly denied accusation.
beats:
- checking the place money is kept and finding it lighter than it should be
- counting it twice to be sure
- checking a second place it is sometimes moved to
- asking around the house, mildly at first, whether anyone moved or saw it
- flat denials from everyone asked
- one detail narrowing suspicion to whoever was alone in the house that day
- a direct question put to that person
- an immediate, flat denial back
- voices rising into an outright, unresolved accusation
held:
- the money was kept in one usual place, a sum in the low hundreds, meant for the month's needs
- the amount confirmed missing on a second count
- one household member was alone in the house on the day it went missing
- the direct question asked once, denied flatly
- the accusation stated outright before the argument breaks off, nobody convinced
cast: the one who discovers the money missing; the rest of the household, questioned in turn; the one among them who ends up accused; they label their own turns the way such household members would call each other.
text: Someone goes to where money is normally kept aside, in the low hundreds, meant for the month's needs, and finds it lighter than it should be. They count it twice to be sure, then check the other place it is sometimes moved to, just in case, but it is not there either. They start asking around the house, mildly at first: has anyone moved it, seen it, borrowed it meaning to say so later. Each person asked gives the same flat denial. One detail narrows things: only one person was alone in the house on the day it must have gone missing. That person gets asked directly, not mildly this time. The denial comes back immediate and flat, no hesitation in it. Voices start rising, and the accusation gets said outright, not implied anymore. It breaks off without anyone backing down, nobody convinced by what the other has said. Write the exchange.

zones: negation,oath,face-work
intent: someone wrongly blamed for a mistake in front of others answers with one flat, composed denial and a single oath, refusing to give the accuser a reaction.
beats:
- something going wrong and blame landing immediately on one person
- the accusation made to their face, in front of others
- an even answer, no matching of the raised voice
- a flat denial, no elaboration
- the accuser pressing, restating the charge
- a plain oath of innocence, given once, not repeated
- the ones who actually caused it staying silent
- composure held throughout, no outburst given
- the matter left unresolved, no apology offered
held:
- the mistake is blamed on one person who did not do it
- the accusation is made in front of others
- the flat denial is said once, without elaboration
- the oath is given once, plainly, not repeated
- the true cause stays silent throughout
- composure holds to the end, no outburst
cast: the one wrongly accused; the one making the accusation, older or senior in standing; others present who already know the truth and stay silent; they label their own turns the way such people would call each other.
text: Something has gone wrong, a task botched or a plan ruined, and blame lands immediately on one person who had no part in it. The accusation is made to their face, in front of the others standing around. They answer evenly, refusing to raise their voice to match the one accusing them. The denial comes flat and short, no elaboration offered, nothing explained further than it needs to be. The accuser presses again, restating the same charge as though repeating it might make it true. The accused swears, once, plainly, that they had no part in it, and does not repeat the oath a second time. The ones who actually caused the trouble stay silent through all of it, letting the wrong person carry the blame. Through the whole exchange the accused keeps their face composed, giving the accuser none of the reaction that was clearly wanted. It ends without resolution, no apology offered by anyone, and the accused walks off with the denial still standing exactly where they left it. Write the exchange.

scenario: the deep apology, years late, to the person wronged
zones: past-narrative,face-work,endearment
intent: years after a cruel thing said in anger split you from your sibling, you sit them down and deliver the full, overdue apology, owning it completely and asking nothing in return.
beats:
- sitting your sibling down deliberately, naming that something overdue needs saying
- naming plainly that this is the apology for what happened years ago
- going back to the fight itself, a disagreement that got away from both of you
- the moment your own anger took over and cruel words got said, aimed to wound
- naming what those words did without repeating them
- the silence that started that same night, neither backing down
- the years since, contact thinning to almost nothing
- at least one earlier moment you almost said something and pulled back
- naming what finally brought you to say it now
- taking responsibility plainly, no excuse offered
- naming what you understand now about what it cost them
- the fond word used once, feeling strange and overdue
- asking for nothing in return, not even forgiveness on the spot
- a pause, the weight of having finally said it
- acknowledging the years that cannot be gotten back
- closing on what you hope for now, without pushing
held:
- years passed since the original falling-out
- the fight's cause kept generic, a disagreement that escalated
- the cruel words are not repeated, only their effect named
- the silence started that same night and never broke on its own
- at least one earlier moment an apology almost happened and did not
- the trigger that finally brought this apology now, named
- the fond word used once
- no demand made for immediate forgiveness
cast: you, delivering the apology; your sibling, the one wronged, sitting and listening now, never taking a turn of their own.
text: You have asked your sibling to sit with you, deliberately, because there is something overdue that needs saying, and they are listening now, never taking a turn of their own. Say plainly, first, what this is: the apology for what happened between you, so many years ago now that the number itself has started to feel unreal. Go back to the fight itself, a disagreement that got away from both of you the way such things do, growing past whatever started it until it was about everything at once. Name the moment inside it when your own anger took over completely, and something cruel came out of your mouth aimed straight at them, meant to wound, and it worked exactly as intended. You do not need to repeat the words themselves, only to name plainly what they did: the specific hurt they landed, the thing about your sibling those words went after and how precisely they knew it would land there. Tell them the silence that followed started that very night, neither of you willing to be the one to back down first, and that the silence simply never broke afterward, not on its own. Walk through the years since: how contact between you thinned from regular to occasional to almost nothing, holidays managed around each other, whole seasons passing without a word exchanged that mattered. Admit to at least one moment across those years where you almost said something, and did not, held back by pride, or by the fear that opening it again would only make it worse than the silence already had. Name honestly what finally brought you to say it now, whatever pushed this apology past the years of almost: distance grown unbearable, or simply enough time passing that carrying it any further felt impossible. Take responsibility plainly for the words themselves, offering no excuse for having said them, not the provocation, not the anger, nothing that would soften what you actually did. Tell them what you understand now, clearly, about what those words actually cost, naming as specifically as you can what you believe it took from them over these years, not just from the two of you together. Use the fond word you have always had for each other, once, and let it feel strange and overdue coming out of your mouth after so long carrying none of that softness between you. Ask for nothing in return, not forgiveness offered on the spot, not even a response yet, only that they have heard it said plainly and completely. Let a pause sit after you finish, the weight of having actually said it settling over both of you. Acknowledge honestly that the years themselves cannot be gotten back, whatever happens next, and that you are not pretending otherwise. Close by telling them plainly what you hope for now, without pushing them toward it, only naming it as a hope. Write the whole apology, years late and finally said in full.
=== end ===

scenario: the argument about politics at the family table, reported afterward
zones: reported,negation,kin
intent: you report to a listener, after the fact, the argument that broke out at the family table when someone raised the one subject everyone knows never to raise, tracing its shape without ever naming what it was about.
beats:
- setting the scene: a family meal, most of the table relaxed, several courses in
- naming that there is one subject this family has long since learned never to raise
- someone bringing it up anyway, almost sideways
- the table going quiet for a second, sensing what is coming
- one relative unable to let the comment pass, responding first
- a second relative countering hard, voices rising
- the specific charge: one accusing the other of not even understanding the position
- a flat denial fired back, no ground given
- a third relative trying to change the subject and failing
- an old grievance from a past argument on the same subject dragged back in
- someone invoking the unspoken rule directly
- the reminder not working, the argument continuing over it
- one person pushing back from the table, done with it but not leaving the room
- an uncomfortable silence settling over the rest of the meal
- someone trying, later, to smooth it over with an unrelated comment, partly working
- the family quietly agreeing, once again, never to bring it up, though everyone expects it to recur
held:
- the subject itself never named, held only as the one thing everyone has learned not to raise
- who first raises it, kept generic, one relative not specified beyond role
- the argument's shape: initial comment, first response, hard counter, an old grievance dragged in, a reminder of the unspoken rule, someone pushing back from the table
- the argument ends unresolved, absorbed into an uncomfortable silence
- an attempt to smooth over the rest of the meal, partly working
- the family's unspoken agreement to avoid the subject again, though everyone expects it to recur
cast: you, reporting the argument afterward; the listener, hearing it now, never taking a turn of their own; the family at the table, kin of varying relation, who label their own turns the way such kin would.
text: You are telling someone what happened at the family table, and they are listening now, never taking a turn of their own while you lay it all out. Start by setting the table itself: a family meal, most of it relaxed, several courses already worked through, everyone comfortable enough to have let their guard down a little. Name plainly that there is one subject at that table that everyone in the family has long since learned never to raise, not because anyone said so outright but because everyone has felt what happens when it comes up. Tell them how it started: someone brought it up anyway, almost sideways, a comment that was not even meant to start anything, just something said in passing. Describe the table going quiet for a second, everyone at it sensing exactly what was coming before a word of argument had actually been said. One relative cannot let the comment pass and responds first, mild at first, testing the ground. A second relative counters hard, and from there the voices start rising properly. Report the specific charge that gets thrown: one accusing the other of not even understanding what they are talking about, dismissing the whole position outright. Give the flat denial fired straight back, refusing the charge completely, no ground given. A third relative tries to change the subject, reaching for anything else to talk about, and it does not work, the argument pulling everyone's attention back to it. An old grievance from some earlier argument on the very same subject gets dragged back into this one, proof to both sides that this has happened before and will happen again. Someone invokes the unspoken rule directly, reminding the table that they had agreed, at some point, not to talk about this here, of all places. It does not work either; the argument keeps going right over the reminder. One person pushes back from the table, done with it, though not fully leaving the room, just putting distance between themselves and the rest. An uncomfortable silence settles over what is left of the meal, cutlery sounding too loud in it. Later, someone tries to smooth it over with an unrelated comment, something about the food or an unrelated bit of news, and it only partly works, the table never quite returning to how relaxed it was before. Close the report by telling your listener how it ended: no one apologizing outright, the family quietly agreeing, the way it always does, never to bring the subject up again, though everyone at that table already knows perfectly well that it will happen again eventually, at some other meal, sooner or later. Write the whole report, the subject itself never named once, only the shape of what it does to that table every time it surfaces.
