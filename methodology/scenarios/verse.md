# Verse scenarios

=== scenario 144 | verse | V | s | corpus ===
scenario: a chain of proverbs an elder strings together to make one point
zones: formulaic-verse,comparative,imperative
intent: an elder strings three old sayings together, one after another, to press home a single piece of advice for a younger relative facing a decision.
beats:
- the elder addresses the younger relative directly and names the trouble the advice concerns
- the first saying, built as a comparison between two things, is delivered
- the second saying follows at once, making the same point a different way, built as a plain instruction
- the third saying closes the chain, shorter and sharper than the first two
- the elder drops the saying-voice and states the point once, plainly, with no saying left to hide behind
held:
- exactly three sayings make the chain
- the first saying takes the shape of a comparison
- the second saying takes the shape of an instruction
- the third saying is shorter than the other two
- the elder names the trouble the advice concerns before the sayings start
- the chain ends with the point stated plainly, outside of any saying
cast: an elder, speaking; a younger relative, addressed within the scene, facing a decision.
text: An elder sits a younger relative down over some trouble the younger one is facing and answers not with a lecture but with old sayings, three of them, one after another. Give the piece this shape: the elder names the trouble plainly before starting, then delivers the first saying built as a comparison between two things, follows it at once with a second saying that makes the same point a different way, this time as a plain instruction, closes the chain with a third saying, shorter and sharper than the first two, and finally drops the saying-voice altogether and states the point once, plainly, with no saying left to hide behind. Write the chain of sayings.
=== end ===

=== scenario 145 | verse | V | s | corpus ===
scenario: a riddle exchange, riddles and answers
zones: formulaic-verse,interrogative,imperative
intent: two people trade riddles back and forth at a gathering, each posing a riddle and the other guessing before the answer comes out.
beats:
- the first riddle posed, framed as a question describing some ordinary thing without naming it
- a guess offered and turned down, or a demand for the answer
- the first riddle answered
- the second riddle posed in return, turning the game around
- that riddle answered in turn
- the exchange closing with one side conceding or calling for another round
held:
- exactly two riddles are traded, one from each side
- each riddle is framed as a question that describes a hidden thing without naming it
- at least one wrong guess or a demand for the answer happens before an answer is given
- both riddles are answered before the exchange closes
- the exchange ends with a concession or a call for another round, not a plain stop
cast: two people at a gathering, evenly matched, trading turns; they label their own turns however such people would call each other.
text: Two people at a gathering fall into a riddle game, trading turns. Give the piece this shape: one poses a riddle first, framed as a question that describes some ordinary thing without naming it, the other offers a guess that gets turned down, or simply demands the answer, and the riddle gets answered, then the other poses a riddle of their own in return, answered in turn, and the exchange closes with one side conceding the round or calling for another. They label their own turns however such people would call each other. Write the riddle exchange.
=== end ===

=== scenario 146 | verse | V | s | control ===
scenario: a praise song or boast for a person or place
zones: formulaic-verse,intensifier,comparative
intent: someone sings or chants a boast, praising a chosen person or place, piling comparison after comparison to make the claim large.
beats:
- direct address naming who or what is being praised
- a first comparison claiming the subject surpasses an ordinary rival or measure
- a second, larger comparison building past the first
- one line that pushes the claim to its biggest, boldest point yet
- a refrain or repeated line that returns at least once
- a closing line that seals the boast
held:
- the subject of the praise is named as a person or a place, one clearly chosen
- the address to the subject falls at the very start of the piece
- at least two comparisons are made, the second larger than the first
- one line pushes the claim to its biggest, boldest point in the piece
- a refrain repeats at least once
- the piece closes on a sealing line, not trailing off
cast: the one singing or chanting the boast; the person or place being praised, present in the address but not given a speaking turn.
text: Someone stands up at a gathering to sing or chant a boast in praise of a chosen person or place. Give the piece this shape: open with a direct address naming who or what is being praised, follow with a first comparison claiming the subject surpasses some ordinary rival or measure, build past it with a second, larger comparison, push the claim to its biggest, boldest point yet with one line, return at least once to a refrain or repeated line running through the piece, and close on a line that seals the boast rather than trailing off. Write the praise song.
=== end ===

=== scenario 147 | verse | V | s | corpus ===
scenario: a lament for someone or something lost
zones: formulaic-verse,endearment,past-narrative
intent: someone chants a lament for a person or a thing that is gone, naming the loss and what it meant.
beats:
- the direct address to the one or the thing that is lost, using a tender term
- naming plainly what is gone
- one memory or trait recalled from before the loss
- a repeated cry or refrain marking the grief
- the closing line letting the lament come to rest
held:
- the lost one or thing is addressed with a tender term throughout
- what is lost is named plainly, once, early in the piece
- exactly one memory or trait is recalled from before the loss
- a refrain or repeated cry returns at least twice
- the lament closes by coming to rest, not cutting off abruptly
cast: the one lamenting; the person or thing lost, addressed directly, never present, never speaking.
text: Someone chants a lament for a person or a thing now gone. Give the piece this shape: address the one or the thing lost directly, using a tender term for them, name plainly what is gone, recall one memory or trait from before the loss, return at least twice to a repeated cry or refrain that carries the grief, and close by letting the lament come to rest rather than cutting off. Write the lament.
=== end ===

=== scenario 148 | verse | V | s | corpus ===
scenario: a work chant or vendor's call, extended
zones: formulaic-verse,numbers,food
intent: a vendor or a work crew calls out an extended chant, naming the goods or the task and a count, to draw people in or keep pace together.
beats:
- an opening call naming the goods being sold or the work being done
- a rhythmic repeated phrase that anchors the chant
- a line naming a count or a price, given as a bare numeral
- a second call naming another item or another round of the same task
- a closing repeat of the opening call
held:
- the chant concerns food, either sold or prepared
- the chant is called aloud, meant to carry to a crowd or coworkers
- one count or price is given as a bare numeral
- the opening call is repeated at the close
- a rhythmic phrase anchors the middle of the chant, returning more than once
cast: a vendor calling out to passersby, or a work crew calling to keep pace together.
text: A vendor calling out over food at a stall, or a work crew calling to keep pace together over food being prepared or hauled, breaks into an extended chant. Give the piece this shape: open with a call naming the goods being sold or the task at hand, anchor the middle with a rhythmic phrase that returns more than once, drop in a count or a price as a bare numeral, add a second call naming another item or another round of the same task, and close by repeating the opening call. Write the chant.
=== end ===

=== scenario 149 | verse | V | s | control ===
scenario: a blessing and a curse, the paired forms
zones: formulaic-verse,oath,future-promise
intent: someone delivers a blessing and, right after, its paired opposite, a curse, both sworn as if they will surely come true.
beats:
- the blessing spoken first, naming a good future sworn to come
- a solemn line marking the blessing as sworn, not just wished
- the turn, marked plainly, from blessing to curse
- the curse spoken, naming a bad future sworn to come, mirroring the blessing's shape
- a closing line that seals both, sworn together
held:
- the blessing and the curse mirror each other in shape, same length and form
- a solemn, sworn note appears in both the blessing and the curse
- the future named in the blessing is good, sworn to come
- the future named in the curse is bad, sworn to come
- the closing line seals both together, not just the curse alone
cast: the one delivering both the blessing and the curse; the ones the blessing and curse are aimed at, addressed but out of scene.
text: Someone delivers a blessing and, right after it, the paired curse, both sworn as if they will surely come true. Give the piece this shape: speak the blessing first, naming a good future and marking it as sworn, not just wished, mark the turn plainly from blessing to curse, speak the curse, naming a bad future in the same sworn, mirrored shape as the blessing, and close with one line that seals both together. Write the blessing and the curse.
=== end ===

=== scenario 150 | verse | V | s | corpus ===
scenario: a personal prayer in plain words
zones: formulaic-verse,endearment,temporal
intent: someone says a personal prayer in plain words at the close of the day, naming what they are grateful for and what they are asking for.
beats:
- an opening address, plain and direct, marking the start of the prayer
- naming one thing from the day being thanked for
- naming one person held close, addressed with a tender term
- one plain request asked for going forward
- a closing line that marks the prayer as finished
held:
- the prayer is said at the close of the day, marked by that time
- exactly one thing from the day is named as being thanked for
- one person is named by a tender term, held close in the prayer
- exactly one request is asked for, plainly, going forward
- the prayer closes with a marked ending line, not trailing off
cast: the one praying, alone; the person held close in the prayer, referenced, out of scene.
text: At the close of the day, someone says a personal prayer in plain words, no ceremony beyond what the moment itself carries. Give the piece this shape: open with a plain, direct address marking the start of the prayer, name one thing from the day being thanked for, name one person held close, addressed with a tender term, ask plainly for one thing going forward, and close with a line that marks the prayer as finished rather than trailing off. Write the prayer.
=== end ===
