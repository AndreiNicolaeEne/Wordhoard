# Work scenarios

=== scenario 38 | work | M | m | corpus ===
scenario: a hard day's physical work, talked through at its end
zones: body,past-narrative,money
intent: you talk through a single hard day of physical labor, from first task to finish, once the work is finally done.
beats:
- the job started before your body felt ready for it
- the first task set the pace for the rest of the day
- a point where the body first complained, a strain or a blister
- a short break taken, barely long enough to matter
- the hardest stretch of the afternoon, when quitting crossed your mind
- a task finished faster than expected, a small lift
- the tally of what got done by the end
- the pay named for the day
- the plan for tomorrow, another day of it or a day to rest
held:
- how many hours the day ran, ten
- the first place the body complained, hands or back
- the hardest stretch, placed roughly in the afternoon
- the task finished ahead of expectation
- the pay for the day, forty
- what tomorrow holds, another day the same or a day off
cast: you, narrating the day's work; the listener you are telling this to, out of scene.
text: You worked a hard day of physical labor today, and now that it is finally done you are talking someone through it, someone who was not there for any of it. Tell it in order: how the job started before your body felt ready, the first task that set the pace for everything after, the point your hands or your back first complained, the short break you took that was barely enough, and the stretch in the afternoon that was hardest, when you thought about quitting early, across the ten hours the day actually ran to. Mention one task that got finished faster than you expected, a small lift in an otherwise grinding day, the pay you were owed for it, forty, and what tomorrow holds, another day the same or a day to rest the body. Tell it the way you would once the work is finally behind you.
=== end ===

intent: a work crew settles what tomorrow's job requires while unwinding with drinks after today's.

- the crew settles in with drinks once today's work is behind them
- someone raises tomorrow's job before it slips anyone's mind
- the start hour gets argued over and finally settled
- the site or task for tomorrow gets named
- who is bringing which tool and who is handling which part gets divided up
- a worry gets raised, materials running short or the weather turning
- one person gets stuck with the hardest part and protests
- a headcount is taken and one person is confirmed unable to make it
- they settle it, half-joking, and agree to be there on time
held:
- the start hour settled on, seven
- the number of people needed tomorrow, four
- the worry raised, materials or weather
- who takes the hardest part, by role
- who is confirmed missing tomorrow, by role
cast: the crew winding down after work, a lead and two or three others; they label their own turns however they would speak of each other.
text: A work crew is winding down with drinks once today's job is finished, and before anyone drifts off, tomorrow's job needs settling. Set the scene: someone brings it up before it gets forgotten, and the start hour gets argued over before they settle on seven. The site or task for tomorrow gets named, and who is bringing which tool and who is handling which part gets divided up among the four who are needed. A worry gets raised, materials running short or the weather turning against them, and one person ends up stuck with the hardest part of it, protesting half seriously. A headcount is taken, and one of them is confirmed unable to make it. They settle it, half-joking about who owes who now, and agree to be there on time. They label their own turns however they would speak of each other. Write the exchange.

=== scenario 41 | work | M | l | corpus ===
scenario: the story of quitting: what was said and what was meant
zones: reported,past-narrative,negation
intent: you tell the full story of quitting a job, laying the polite words spoken against what you actually meant underneath them.
beats:
- how long you had been at the job before deciding to leave
- the final straw, small on its own, the last of many
- rehearsing the polished version of what you would say, beforehand
- asking to speak with whoever was in charge
- sitting down and the opening line you used, careful and diplomatic
- saying it was not about one thing, when it partly was
- the real reason held back in that moment, named now to the listener
- the reaction from whoever was in charge, surprise or none at all
- a counter offered, more pay or a changed condition
- turning the counter down, giving the polite reason instead of the true one
- the notice period agreed on, a plain number of days
- a private doubt right after, wondering if it was the right call
- the last day, goodbyes that were mostly hollow
- one honest moment on the last day that nearly said the real thing
- how it has felt since, relief outweighing regret or the other way round
- the closing verdict, what you would say now if the conversation happened again
held:
- how long you had been there, two years
- the final straw named
- the opening line used, and what it left out
- the real reason, told now, plainly
- the counter offered
- the notice period, two weeks
- how it has felt since
cast: you, narrating; whoever was in charge at the job, out of scene, referred to by role; the listener you are telling this to, out of scene.
text: You quit a job you had held for two years, and you are telling the whole story now, the polite words that were actually said against what you actually meant by them. Tell it in order: how long you had been there before you finally decided to leave, and the final straw, small on its own but the last of a long line, that pushed you to it. Cover rehearsing the polished version of what you would say beforehand, asking to speak with whoever was in charge, and sitting down to say the opening line you had prepared, careful and diplomatic, saying it was not about one thing in particular when it partly was. Name, to the listener now, the real reason you held back in that moment. Cover the reaction from whoever was in charge, surprised or not, the counter they offered, more pay or a changed condition, and how you turned it down, giving the polite reason instead of the true one. State the notice period you agreed on, two weeks, and a private doubt that hit you right after, wondering if you had done the right thing. Cover the last day itself, goodbyes that were mostly hollow, and the one honest moment in it that nearly said the real thing out loud. Close with how it has actually felt in the time since, relief outweighing regret or the other way round, and the verdict you would give now if you had that conversation to have again. Tell the whole of it, what was said and what was really meant.
=== end ===

intent: you narrate an overnight shift that fell apart hour by hour, and the exhausted push to the end of it.

- arriving to the shift already short-handed
- the first small sign, early in the night, that something is off
- the first real failure, a machine, a process, or a delivery gone wrong
- the scramble to patch it without stopping everything else
- a call made for help that goes unanswered at first
- someone senior finally pulled in, unhappy about being called
- a second problem stacking on the first at the worst possible time
- someone nearly hurt, or lightly hurt, in the scramble
- a decision to stop part of the work rather than push through broken
- a brief lull where it looks like it might be turning around
- a third thing breaking just as it starts to calm
- the tally of what is ruined or lost, mounting through the night
- the exhausted push through the last hours before light
- the sky finally lightening as the shift ends

- the first failure named
- how long the call for help went unanswered
- the injury, minor, named
- the tally of what was lost by the end
- how many hours over the shift ran
cast: you, narrating; the coworkers on shift with you, referred to by role; the listener you are telling this to, out of scene.
text: You worked a night shift where everything went wrong, one thing after another, and you are telling it now hour by hour, still half in disbelief. Tell it in order: arriving to find the shift two people short before anything even started, the first small sign early in the night that something was off, and the first real failure, a machine, a process, or a delivery gone wrong. Cover the scramble to patch it without stopping everything else, a call for help that went unanswered for the first while, and someone senior finally pulled in, unhappy about being called away for it. Cover a second problem stacking on top of the first at the worst possible moment, someone nearly hurt, or lightly hurt, in the scramble to keep things moving, and a decision made to stop part of the work rather than push through it broken. Mention the brief lull where it looked like it might be turning around, and the third thing breaking just as it started to calm. Cover the tally of what ended up ruined or lost mounting through the night, the exhausted push through the last hours before light, the sky finally lightening as the shift ended, and walking out too tired to feel any relief yet. Tell the whole night, hour by hour, the way it actually went.

- the question the new hand asks
- the moment the tone sharpens
- how it finally lands, on the third try

zones: reported,negation,numbers
intent: a customer collects a finished garment from the tailor and argues that it is not what was actually ordered.

- the customer notices something different from what was expected
- the customer states plainly what was actually ordered
- the tailor insists that is what was agreed
- the customer recalls the specific detail, a count of buttons and the cut of the hem
- the tailor goes to check a note or the original measurements
- the note contradicts one of them, and it comes out which
- a resolution is worked out, an alteration promised within a number of days
held:
- what was ordered, three buttons and a straight hem
- what was delivered instead, two buttons and an angled hem
- the tailor's insistence that this is what was agreed
- the note checked, and what it actually shows
- the number of days promised to fix it, two
cast: the tailor and the customer collecting the finished garment; they label their own turns however they would speak of each other.
text: A customer has come to collect a finished garment from the tailor and finds it is not what they ordered. Set the scene: the customer notices something different right away and states plainly what was actually asked for, three buttons and a straight hem, not the two buttons and the angled hem sitting in front of them. The tailor insists this is exactly what was agreed. The customer holds firm on the detail, and the tailor goes to check a note or the original measurements. The note contradicts one of them, and it comes out which. They work out a resolution, an alteration promised within two days. They label their own turns however they would speak of each other. Write the exchange.
