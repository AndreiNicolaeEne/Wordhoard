scenario: telling someone you like them, badly, for the first time
zones: interrogative,body,endearment
intent: A person tries to tell someone they like them for the first time and makes a clumsy job of it.
beats:
- the opening line gets lost partway through and is started over at least twice
- the hands stay busy with something nearby so the eyes do not have to meet
- the face goes hot and gives the feeling away before any words do
- a question slips out first instead of the plain statement
- the other one waits it out without jumping in to help
- the plain thing finally gets said, clumsy and fast, with a fond word slipped in at the end
held:
- the opening line restarted at least twice
- the hands kept busy to avoid eye contact
- the face going hot
- the question that comes out instead of a statement
- the wait without help from the other one
- the fond word at the end
cast: two people about the same age, one working up to say it to the other; they label their own turns however such people would call each other.
text: Two people about the same age are alone together for a moment after being around others all day. One of them has been meaning to say something and starts now, badly: the opening line gets lost partway through and has to be started over at least twice. Their hands stay busy with something nearby so their eyes do not have to meet the other one's, and their face goes hot and gives the feeling away before their mouth does. Instead of the plain statement, what comes out first is a question, something like whether the other one has ever thought about the two of them as more than they are now. The other one does not jump in to help, letting the silence sit until the first one finishes. Eventually the plain thing gets said outright, clumsy and too fast, and a fond word slips in at the end that neither of them quite expected. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

=== scenario 15 | love | M | m | corpus ===
scenario: the love letter
zones: endearment,future-promise,temporal
intent: A person writes a love letter that moves from memory into present feeling and a promise for what comes next.
beats:
- opens by saying plainly how they feel, no dancing around it
- returns to one ordinary moment when the feeling first turned serious
- names one specific detail about the other person that only someone close would know
- says how the days are different with the other person in them
- admits how much harder the days apart have become
- includes one small worry never said aloud, the kind that only comes out on paper
- makes one clear promise about what is ahead, something meant to be kept
- closes with the private word used for them alone
held:
- the plain statement of feeling at the start
- the ordinary moment first noticed, not a big occasion
- the one specific loved detail
- the admission about days apart
- the small worry never said aloud
- the promise about the future
- the private closing word
cast: the writer, and the person they love, addressed directly but never appearing as a scene character.
text: You are writing a letter to someone you love, meant for them to read alone. Say plainly how you feel right at the start, no dancing around it. Go back to one ordinary moment, now some time in the past, when you first noticed the feeling had turned serious, something small rather than a big occasion. Name one particular thing about them that only someone close would know, the kind of detail nobody else would think to mention. Say how your days are different with them in your life, and admit honestly how much harder the days apart from them have become. Include one small worry you have never said aloud, the kind of thing that only comes out on paper. Make one clear promise about what is ahead for the two of you, something you actually intend to keep. Close the letter with the word you use for them when nobody else is listening.
=== end ===

scenario: refusing a suitor gently, leaving no wound
zones: negation,endearment,face-work
intent: One person turns down another's romantic interest as gently as they can, careful not to wound them.
beats:
- the suitor states their intention plainly
- the other one thanks them for saying it honestly before answering
- the no is given straight, without hedging that could raise false hope
- one genuine reason or compliment is offered, not a flaw
- reassurance is given that nothing is wrong with the suitor
- something of the relationship is kept intact, an offer to stay close some other way
- the exchange closes on a warm word of address so the suitor's dignity stays whole
held:
- the suitor's plain statement of intention
- the thanks given before the no
- the no stated straight, no false hope
- the one genuine reason offered
- the reassurance that nothing is wrong with them
- what is kept intact afterward
- the warm word of address at the close
cast: two people, one who has just asked and one who answers; they label their own turns however such people would.
text: Someone has worked up the nerve to say plainly that they want to be more than what they currently are to the other person. The other one hears them out fully before answering. When they do answer, they start by thanking the other for saying it so honestly, then give the no straight, without leaving room to hope it might change later. They offer one genuine reason, something true and kind about the other person rather than a flaw, so the refusal does not read as rejection of who they are. They make clear that nothing is wrong with the suitor and that the fit or the timing simply is not there. They keep something of the relationship intact, an offer to stay close in whatever way still works between them. The whole exchange closes on a warm word of address, something kind rather than romantic, so the one who asked leaves with their dignity whole even though the answer was no. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

zones: kin,interrogative,reported
intent: A matchmaking aunt argues for a match on her niece or nephew's behalf and is turned down without a fight breaking out.
beats:
- the aunt opens by reporting secondhand praise she has heard about the candidate
- she lists three things in the candidate's favor: work, temperament, and family
- she asks straight out what could possibly be wrong with someone like that
- the younger one offers an excuse, careful not to insult the choice itself
- the aunt counters with a relative who resisted the same way and is glad now
- the younger one holds the line but keeps it respectful, being kin to an elder
- the aunt lets it go for now but leaves the offer open
held:
- the secondhand praise reported at the start
- the three things named in the candidate's favor
- the direct question about what could be wrong
- the excuse given in return
- the precedent of a relative who is glad now
- the respectful refusal held firm
- the offer left open for later
cast: an aunt, kin and elder to the one she is matchmaking for; the younger one she is trying to match; they label their own turns however such kin would address each other.
text: An aunt has come to make her case for a match she has been planning for a while, and she does it properly: she opens by reporting what she has heard about the person she has in mind, secondhand praise passed along like it settles the matter. She lists three things in the candidate's favor, their work, their temperament, and the family they come from. When the younger one hesitates, the aunt asks straight out what exactly could be wrong with someone like that. The younger one offers an excuse, being too busy or not ready, careful not to insult the choice itself. The aunt is not satisfied and brings up a relative who resisted just the same way and is glad now that they did not win that argument. The younger one holds firm but keeps it respectful, this being an elder they cannot simply dismiss. The aunt lets it go for the moment but makes clear the offer still stands whenever the younger one changes their mind. Write the exchange.

scenario: how two people met, courted, and married, told at the anniversary
zones: past-narrative,temporal,kin
intent: A couple tells the full story of how they met, courted, and married, for the guests at their anniversary.
beats:
- the first meeting, ordinary and unplanned
- the detail from that first exchange that has stuck in memory since
- the early hesitation named, on one side
- what changed the mind that needed changing
- the first time they properly spent time together outside of chance
- the obstacle that got in the way of the courtship going smoothly
- how that obstacle got overcome
- the running joke or small object from that stretch of time, still referenced
- the moment one of them realized it had turned serious
- how the other one found out
- the proposal, told plainly, including who did the asking
- how long it was between the proposal and the wedding
- how the families took the news
- one detail from the wedding day itself that still comes back
- a hard stretch weathered together since, without dwelling on it
- what has stayed exactly the same since that first meeting
held:
- the ordinary first meeting
- the memorable detail from the first exchange
- the early hesitation named
- what changed the mind
- the first proper time together
- the obstacle to the courtship
- how it was overcome
- the running joke or object still referenced
- the moment it turned serious
- the proposal, plainly told
- the span between the proposal and the wedding, about a year
- the families' reaction
- one wedding-day detail
- the hard stretch weathered together
- what has stayed the same since the start
cast: the couple; one of them does the telling, the partner only smiles along; the guests at the anniversary are listening but out of scene.
text: You and your partner are marking an anniversary today, and the people around the table want the whole story, start to finish, so you tell it properly; your partner leaves the telling to you tonight, only smiling along. Start with how you actually met, something ordinary rather than a grand setup, and include the detail that has stuck in memory ever since, some small thing one of you said or did in that first exchange that neither of you has forgotten. Admit that one of you was not sure at first, hesitant for a reason worth naming, and tell how that hesitation wore off, what changed the mind that needed changing. Move into the courting itself: the first time you properly spent time together outside of chance, and the thing that got in the way of it going smoothly, whether that was distance, disapproval from someone close, or simply bad timing with work and life. Tell how you got past that obstacle, plainly, without making it sound easier than it was. Bring in the running joke or the small object from that stretch of time that has followed you since, mentioned without needing to be explained to the room, because most of them already know it. Name the moment one of you realized this had turned serious, and how the other one found out. Tell the proposal the way it actually happened, not dressed up, including who did the asking. Say roughly how long it was between the proposal and the wedding, about a year. Say how the families took the news, whether that approval came easily or took its time. Pick one detail from the wedding day itself that still comes back to you, the kind of thing photographs never captured. Bring up one hard stretch you have weathered together since, without dwelling on it, and say what carried you through it. Close by saying what has stayed exactly the same about the two of you since that very first meeting, and lift a glass to the room before you sit back down.
=== end ===

- the fond word the two of them always use gets dropped in on purpose
- the grieving friend manages a small laugh despite everything

- the backchannel agreement while listening
- the self-blame named
- the direct negation of that self-blame
- the reassurance of worth
- the practical offer made
- the fond word dropped in
- the small laugh at the end

text: One friend has just had a relationship end and comes to the other to talk it through. They tell what happened and why it ended, without needing to be asked twice. The listening friend stays mostly quiet at first, just backing them up with small sounds of agreement to keep them talking. At some point the grieving friend starts blaming themselves, listing what they think they did wrong. The listening friend shuts that down directly, saying plainly that it was not their fault and naming the real reason it did not work. They follow with reassurance that this does not say anything bad about who the grieving friend is. They offer something practical, staying the night or a plan to get out of the house for a while, so the grief has somewhere to go besides sitting still. scenario: gossip about who was seen with whom at the festival
zones: reported,interrogative,intensifier
intent: Two people trade gossip about who was seen together at a festival, working out whether it means anything.
beats:
- one opens with a report of who was seen together, heard from someone who was there
- the other presses right away for the exact details
- the scene gets described: standing off from the crowd, laughing, closer than usual
- an intensified reaction of disbelief follows
- a question is asked about whether it could really mean what it looks like
- a second piece of evidence gets brought in to back the first sighting
- speculation follows about who else has already heard
- a note of doubt gets added, that it could still be nothing
- they agree the only way to know for sure is to ask directly
held:
- the pair seen together, reported secondhand
- the detail of exactly how they were seen
- the intensified reaction
- the follow-up question about what it means
- the second piece of evidence
- the speculation about who else has heard
- the doubt raised at the end
cast: two people who both attended the festival, trading the gossip; they label their own turns however such people would.
text: Two people run into each other after a shared festival and one of them has news: someone was seen with someone else, heard from a person who was actually there. The other one presses right away, wanting the exact details, where it happened and how close the two looked. The one telling it lays out the scene, the two of them standing off from the crowd, laughing at something, closer than friends usually stand. The listener reacts with real disbelief, an emphatic word thrown in because it seems like too much to be nothing. They ask straight out whether it could really mean what it looks like, or whether it is being read into too far. The teller brings up a second piece of it, something else noticed the same day that backs the first sighting up. They wonder aloud who else has already heard by now, given how these things travel. Before it goes any further, one of them adds a note of doubt, that it could still be nothing, and they agree the only real way to know is to ask one of the two people directly. Write the exchange.
