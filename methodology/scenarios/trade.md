- the seller names a price
- the buyer offers less, pointing out a flaw or offering to take more
- the seller resists at first
- the seller comes down partway
- they settle on a price between the two
held:
- the fruit being sold
- the seller's opening price, twelve
- the buyer's counter-offer, eight
- the reason given for the lower offer
- the settled price, ten
cast: the seller and the buyer at a market stall; they label their own turns however they would speak of each other.
text: A buyer stops at a market stall to haggle over the price of fruit. Set the scene: the buyer asks what it costs, and the seller names a price of twelve for the batch. The buyer offers eight instead, pointing out a flaw in the fruit or offering to take more of it. The seller resists at first, then comes down to ten, and the buyer accepts. They label their own turns however they would speak of each other. Write the exchange.

zones: past-narrative,reported,money
intent: retelling how an official hinted for an extra payment and how it was dodged.
beats:
- the errand that put you in front of this official, a paper needing a stamp or signature
- the first hint dropped, indirect
- playing dumb, letting the hint hang
- the hint repeated, a little plainer
- the dodge, naming a rule, promising to return another day, or mentioning someone you knew
- the tense pause where it could have gone either way
- how it resolved, the paper obtained anyway or a delay endured instead
- the closing verdict, told now with a laugh or a shake of the head
held:
- what the paper was for
- how the hint was dropped
- what was done to dodge it
- how it resolved, the paper obtained or a delay endured
- the closing verdict
cast: you, narrating; the official you dealt with, referred to by role, out of scene; the one you are telling this to, out of scene.
text: You once needed a paper stamped or signed at an office, and you are telling someone now about the official there who hinted for an extra payment before it would go through, and how you got out of it without paying. Tell it in order: what the paper was for and why you needed it done that day, the first hint the official dropped without saying it outright, how you played dumb and let it hang in the air, the hint repeated a little plainer the second time, and what you actually did to dodge it, naming a rule, saying you would come back another day, mentioning someone you knew, whatever it was. Cover the tense pause where it could have gone either way, and how it actually resolved, whether you got the paper anyway or sat through a delay instead of paying. Close with your own verdict on the whole thing, telling it now the way you would to someone who has never had to deal with someone like that.
