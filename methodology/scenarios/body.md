scenario: describing an illness and its home remedies to a worried relative
zones: body,imperative,kin
intent: A person unwell describes their illness and the home remedies they are using to ease a worried relative's mind.
beats:
- the relative checks in, worried, asking how bad it really is
- the sick person says when it started and how
- what today feels like against yesterday
- the first home remedy tried, and why
- the second remedy tried when the first did not fully work
- rest and plain food described
- the line that would send them to be properly seen
- the relative's offer to help, half accepted
held:
- the illness started three days ago
- the two home remedies tried, in order: a salted gargle, then a cool cloth
- the line for going to be seen: the fever climbing further, or breathing turning hard
cast: the person who is unwell; a worried relative, kin to them; they label their own turns the way such kin would speak to each other.
text: A relative gets in touch, worried, wanting to know how bad it really is. The sick person lays it out plainly: it started three days ago with an ache in the joints and a scratchy throat, and yesterday a fever came on top of it. Today is a little easier than yesterday, though still far from right. Asked what they have been doing about it, they walk through the remedies in order: first a gargle of warm salted water for the throat, and when the fever did not fully break on its own, a cool cloth laid across the forehead and changed through the night. They have kept to broth and weak tea, and slept whenever the body would let them. The relative tells them plainly to rest properly and not push through it, and the sick person sets their own line: if the fever climbs any further, or breathing turns hard, they will go and be seen, but not before that. The relative offers to bring food over or sit with them for an hour, and the offer is half accepted, half waved off. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

scenario: the morning after, retold with regret
zones: past-narrative,body,negation
intent: A person recounts, with regret, the physical wreckage of the morning after a night of overdoing it at a celebration.
beats:
- a night of celebration pulls you in completely
- saying yes to one more helping, then another
- drinking more than intended
- dancing on past when you meant to leave
- the last clear memory before things blur
- waking to the first wave of headache and nausea
- the light and sound feeling like too much
- the effort just to sit up
- a household member teasing you gently
- the promise sworn to yourself, undercut by knowing it is not the first time
- small tasks feeling impossible for most of the morning
- eating something plain to settle the stomach
- flashes of memory returning
- an apology owed for something said or done
- feeling like yourself again by evening
- the closing regret, knowing it will probably happen again
held:
- the occasion was a celebration, unspecified further
- the recovery took most of the day, until evening
- one apology is owed for something from the night before
- the promise sworn is not the first time it has been sworn
cast: the person recounting, once overindulgent the night before; a household member present the next morning, teased gently, in scene; a friend the story is told to days later, kept out of scene.
text: Someone close to you was celebrating and pulled you into it completely: you said yes to one more helping, then one more after that, drank more than you meant to, and stayed up dancing long after you told yourself you would leave. The last clear thing you remember is somewhere in the middle of all that, everything after it blurring into pieces. You woke the next morning to the first wave of it, head pounding, stomach turning at the very thought of food, the light in the room feeling too bright and every sound too loud. Just sitting up took real effort. Someone else in the house was up before you, moving normally, and could not resist teasing you a little for the state you were in, not unkindly. You made yourself a promise right there, lying still, that you would never let a night get away from you like that again, even knowing it probably was not the first time you had sworn it. Small tasks that would normally take nothing at all, a short walk, one simple chore, felt like more than you could manage for most of the morning. You managed something plain to eat, nothing rich, just enough to settle your stomach, and kept mostly quiet and still until the worst of it passed. Pieces of the night before came back to you in flashes, and one of them was something you said or did that you now owe somebody an apology for, and you turn it over more than once, embarrassed. By evening you finally felt like yourself again, steady enough to eat properly and think straight. Write the account you give of it, days later, to a friend who was not there: the whole night as far as you can piece it together, the state you woke up in, the promise you made to yourself, the apology you still owe, and the regret sitting underneath all of it, knowing full well you will probably let it happen again someday.
=== end ===

beats:
- noticing something wrong with the animal that evening
- deciding to stay through the night rather than leave it
- the first hour of watching, no change
- coaxing it to take a little water
- a bad turn partway through, worry spiking
- staying close anyway, keeping it warm, talking low
- a small sign of improvement near dawn
- the relief once it seems steadier
- exhaustion catching up once the worry lifts
held:
- the watch runs the whole night, into dawn
- the animal is coaxed to take a little water partway through
- the worst moment comes partway through the night
- by morning it is steadier, not fully recovered
cast: the person nursing the animal; the animal, theirs; whoever asks about it the next day, kept out of scene.
text: One of your animals starts showing something wrong with it that evening, off its feed and not moving right, and you decide against leaving it alone for the night. You stay close from the first hour, watching for any change, though for a long while there is none. You try to get it to take a little water, coaxing it since it will not drink on its own. Partway through the night it takes a bad turn, and your worry spikes hard enough that you consider whether you have done the wrong thing keeping it home. You stay anyway, keeping it warm, talking to it low the way you would to calm anything frightened. Near dawn you catch the first small sign it might be turning a corner, a shift in how it is breathing, an ear twitching at a sound the way it normally would. By full morning it is steadier, not right yet but steadier, and only once you are sure of that does your own exhaustion catch up with you all at once. Write the account you give the next day to whoever asks how the night went.
=== end ===

=== scenario 89 | body | M | l | corpus ===
scenario: learning a physical skill late in life: a season of humiliation, told fondly
zones: past-narrative,body,comparative
intent: An adult recounts, fondly despite the humiliation, the season they spent learning a physical skill late in life.
beats:
- deciding, later than anyone else around, to spend the better part of a year learning the skill
- the first lesson, hopeless from the start
- the instructor noting plainly it will take longer at this age
- being outpaced within days by learners half the age
- the body aching that first week in long-forgotten places
- one bad fall, the breath knocked clean out
- wanting to quit right after that fall
- someone talking you into coming back
- slow, humiliating progress, one small thing at a time
- a small audience of onlookers, some encouraging, some amused
- comparison kept only against yourself a month back
- the day it finally clicked, around the fourth month
- never catching up to the fastest learners, and being at peace with that
- pride in what can now be done that could not be done at the start
- looking back on the whole season fondly
held:
- started later than everyone else, an adult among much younger learners
- the season ran close to a year
- one bad fall, breath knocked out
- the thing clicked around the fourth month
- never caught up to the fastest learners, at peace with it
cast: the person telling the story, once a late beginner; the instructor, in scene; the younger learners and onlookers, in scene; the listener being told this fondly, kept out of scene.
text: You decided, later than anyone else around you ever had, to spend the better part of a year learning a skill that everyone else seemed to have picked up as children. The first lesson was hopeless: your body would not do what your mind was asking of it, and the instructor, patient but blunt, told you plainly it would take longer at your age. Within days you were being outpaced by learners half your age, and that stung more than you expected it to. The whole first week your body ached in places you had forgotten could ache at all, muscles you had not used in years making themselves known every time you moved. There was one fall in particular that stands out: you went down hard, knocked the breath clean out of yourself, and lay there for a moment certain you were done with the whole idea. You wanted to quit after that, and said so, but someone talked you into coming back one more time. Progress after that was slow and often humiliating, one small thing gotten right after weeks of getting it wrong, while a small crowd of onlookers watched, some encouraging, some clearly amused. The only comparison that ever helped was against yourself a month back, never against the ones who had picked it up in an afternoon. Then, around the fourth month, something clicked, a feeling in the body more than anything you could explain in words, and the thing that had been impossible was suddenly just difficult. You never did catch up to the fastest learners, and by the end you had made peace with that, proud instead of the things you could now do without thinking that you could not do at all when you started. Looking back on it now, the whole humiliating season sits fondly in memory rather than sourly, the falls and the soreness and the being laughed at all folded into something you are glad you did. Write the account you give, fondly, of that whole season, to someone who was not there to see any of it: what pushed you to start so late, the worst of the humiliation, the fall, the slow climb, the day it finally clicked, and where you landed by the end.
=== end ===
