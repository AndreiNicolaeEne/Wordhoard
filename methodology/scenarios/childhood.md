# Childhood scenarios

=== scenario 6 | childhood | D | m | corpus ===
scenario: teaching a child the rules of the favorite game
zones: imperative,interrogative,numbers
intent: An older child teaches a younger one the rules of their favorite outdoor game before the group arrives to play.
beats:
- naming how many players are needed and marking the boundary
- picking who chases first
- demonstrating what actually counts as a tag
- answering what happens if a runner steps outside the boundary
- explaining the freeze rule and how to get unfrozen
- agreeing how a round ends and who chases next
- promising the first round will not count against mistakes
held:
- the minimum number of players needed to start
- the fixed boundary marking the play area
- what counts as a tag
- the freeze-and-unfreeze rule
- the first round is practice and does not count
cast: an older child teaching; a younger child learning; they label their own turns the way such children would call each other.
text: An older child teaches a younger one the rules of their favorite outdoor game before the rest of the group arrives to play. They lay it out in order: how many players are needed to start, where the boundary runs on each side, and how the chaser gets picked for the first round. They demonstrate what actually counts as a tag. The younger one asks what happens if a runner steps outside the boundary while being chased, and gets a straight answer. The older one explains the freeze rule: touched, you freeze until another still-running player reaches out and taps you loose. They settle how a round ends and who chases next, and the older one promises the first round will not count against the younger one if they get something wrong. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

=== scenario 7 | childhood | D | s | corpus ===
scenario: a counting-out rhyme and the squabble over who is out
zones: formulaic-verse,numbers,exclamation
intent: A group of children counts out who is it with a rhyme, and a fight breaks out over whether the count was fair.
beats:
- the children gather to count out before the game starts
- the rhyme runs its course and lands on one child
- that child protests the count as unfair
- another child cites the rule that arguing means starting over
- the group restarts, louder, until a result holds
held:
- the rhyme picks exactly one child as out
- the group is small, three to six children
- arguing about the count means starting the rhyme over
- the second count is the one that finally holds
cast: the children counting out; no fixed roles beyond who is caught by the rhyme; they label their own turns the way such children would call each other.
text: A group of children forms a circle to count out who will be it before their game starts. One child recites the counting rhyme, pointing around the circle, and the count lands on one of them. That child protests at once, insisting the count went too fast or skipped someone, and demands a do-over. Another child reminds everyone that arguing means starting the whole rhyme again from the top, so the count runs a second time, louder, and this one holds. Write the exchange, rhyme and protest both, ending with who is finally out.
=== end ===

=== scenario 8 | childhood | D | m | corpus ===
scenario: explaining to a child why they cannot have the thing
zones: negation,conditional,kin
intent: A parent explains, gently but firmly, why their child cannot have the thing they keep asking for.
beats:
- the child asks for the thing again
- the parent gives the real reason plainly
- the child offers a bargain, a chore or a wait
- the parent sets a condition for a possible later yes
- the child compares themselves to another child who already has one
- the parent explains why the comparison does not settle it
- the parent offers something smaller instead
- the child accepts it, grudgingly
held:
- this is at least the second time the child has asked
- the bargain the child offers
- the condition set for a possible future yes
- the comparison to another child, and why it is turned aside
- the smaller thing offered in place of the original
cast: a parent, their child; they label their own turns the way such a parent and child would speak to each other.
text: A parent explains to their child why they cannot have the thing they keep asking for. The child asks again, and the parent gives the real reason plainly, without dodging it. The child tries to bargain: an extra chore, or waiting for a particular day. The parent sets a condition under which the answer could change later, if it ever does. The child tries once more, pointing to another child who already has one, and the parent explains why that does not settle it here. The parent offers something smaller instead, not the thing itself but not nothing, and the child accepts it, grudgingly, for now. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

=== scenario 9 | childhood | D | s | control ===
scenario: teasing a sibling about their new haircut
zones: exclamation,comparative,kin
intent: A sibling teases another over a haircut just done, comparing it to something absurd.
beats:
- the sibling with the new haircut walks in
- the other sibling reacts with exaggerated shock
- a comparison lands, the haircut likened to something absurd
- the teased sibling fires back, defending or turning the joke around
- one more jab lands before it settles
held:
- the haircut was done that same day
- the comparison the teasing sibling makes
- the counter the teased sibling gives
- how it ends, a shared laugh or a flat threat to tell
cast: two siblings; they label their own turns the way such siblings would call each other.
text: A sibling walks in with a haircut done that same day, and the other sibling cannot let it go. They react with exaggerated shock, then land a comparison, saying the haircut looks like something absurd. The teased sibling fires back, either defending the cut or turning the comparison around on the teaser. One more jab lands before it settles, either into a shared laugh or a flat threat to tell someone. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

=== scenario 10 | childhood | D | m | corpus ===
scenario: the interrogation over the broken thing: who did it, nobody did it
zones: interrogative,negation,reported
intent: A parent questions two or three children over who broke something, and each denies it.
beats:
- the broken thing is discovered
- the children are called together and asked directly
- the first child denies it outright
- the second child denies it too and blames something else
- the parent points out a detail that does not fit either story
- the children start blaming each other
- one account shifts slightly under the pressure
- the parent settles on a consequence that falls on everyone since nobody owns it
held:
- the thing that broke
- the number of children questioned, two or three
- the alternate explanation offered in place of a confession
- the detail the parent notices that contradicts the story
- the shared consequence at the end
cast: a parent, two or three children who are siblings; they label their own turns the way such a family would speak to each other.
text: Something in the house has been broken, and the parent calls the children together to find out who did it. Asked directly, the first child denies it outright. The second child denies it too and offers another explanation, blaming an accident, a draft of wind, or the family pet. The parent points out a detail that does not fit either story, and the children start blaming each other instead of answering. Under the pressure, one child's account shifts slightly from what they said a moment before. Nobody confesses outright, and the parent settles on a consequence that falls on all of them equally, since nobody will own it. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

=== scenario 11 | childhood | D | s | corpus ===
scenario: a superstition explained in passing to a doubting child
zones: conditional,oath,kin
intent: An older relative explains a household superstition to a doubting child, half in earnest.
beats:
- the child is about to do the ordinary thing that triggers the superstition
- the older relative stops them and names it
- the child questions it, half-laughing
- the relative lays out the claimed consequence and swears it is true
- the counter-action is offered as a fix
- the child does the counter-action anyway, just in case
held:
- the action that triggers the superstition
- the claimed consequence if it is ignored
- the counter-action that undoes it
- the relative's oath that it is true
cast: an older relative, a child; they label their own turns the way such kin would speak to each other.
text: A child is about to do something ordinary around the house when an older relative stops them and names the superstition attached to it. The child doubts it, half-laughing, and asks what actually happens if they just go ahead. The relative lays out the claimed consequence and swears it is true, whether or not the child believes it. They offer the fix: one small counter-action, done right after, undoes the bad luck. The child rolls their eyes but does the counter-action anyway, just in case. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

=== scenario 12 | childhood | M | l | control ===
scenario: the childhood dare that went wrong, confessed years later
zones: past-narrative,body,exclamation
intent: An adult confesses, years later, the childhood dare that went wrong and the injury it caused.
beats:
- the group of three or four children gathered at their usual spot, restless and daring each other
- one of them naming the dare: climb the tree to the highest branch you can reach and jump into the water below
- the taunting that pushes the confessor to try it, in two parts: being doubted, and being reminded of backing out once before
- the climb itself, going higher than intended
- the jump
- misjudging the water and knocking the wind out on landing
- the leg opening on something submerged, unseen
- the others pulling the confessor out, panicking together
- the huddle right there deciding the story together, in two parts: what happened and who would tell it
- going home and telling the invented story, believed without question
- the cut being treated under the false account
- the scar that stayed
- the scar being asked about for years afterward, always answered the same false way
- what finally pushes the confessor to tell the truth now, years later
- the plain confession itself, with an apology folded in
held:
- the group size, three or four children
- the dare, jumping from the highest reachable branch into the water below
- the injury, the leg opened on something submerged, needing stitches
- the invented cover story, a fall from a bike
- the scar, visible and asked about for more than twenty years
- the years elapsed since childhood, more than twenty
- the parent being confessed to now, kept out of scene
cast: the confessor, once a child in the dare, now an adult; the parent being confessed to, kept out of scene; the other children who took the dare together, unnamed.
text: As a child you took a dare from the three or four kids you ran with then: climb the big tree at your usual spot to the highest branch you could reach, and jump into the water below. You had backed out of something like it once before and the others would not let you forget it, so this time you went higher than you meant to. When you jumped you misjudged the water, knocked the wind out of yourself, and opened your leg on something submerged you never saw coming. The others pulled you out and panicked along with you, and right there, still soaked, you agreed on a story together: you would say you had cut your leg falling off a bike. You went home and told it, it was believed, the cut got treated under that story, and the scar has stayed ever since, asked about for more than twenty years, always answered the same false way. Write the confession you finally give now, as an adult, to the parent who believed the story back then: the real dare, the real jump, the real reason for the scar, what has pushed you to tell it after all this time, and the apology that comes with it.
=== end ===

=== scenario 13 | childhood | M | s | control ===
scenario: a child's achievement described with unconcealed pride
zones: comparative,exclamation,kin
intent: A parent describes their child's recent achievement to someone else, pride showing openly.
beats:
- the child finishes reading a whole book alone, start to finish
- the parent remembers how hard reading used to be for the child
- the length of the struggle before this, specified
- the child announcing the finish unprompted
- the parent unable to hold back the pride
held:
- the achievement, finishing a whole book alone
- the length of the earlier struggle, over a year
- the child announced it themselves, unprompted
- the parent's pride, said openly and without holding back
cast: a parent describing their child's achievement; the child, referred to only by relation; the listener, kept out of scene.
text: Your child just finished reading a whole book alone, start to finish, after struggling with it for over a year and giving up on it more than once. They announced it themselves, unprompted, prouder than they usually let themselves be. Write how you describe this to someone, comparing it plainly to how far the child has come, letting your own pride show without holding any of it back.
=== end ===
