scenario: a life-story fragment: how the family came to this place
zones: past-narrative,spatial,kin
intent: someone tells how their family came to settle in the place they now call home.
beats:
- a brief look back at the place the family had been before, not dwelt on
- the reason for leaving, whatever it was that pushed them out of the old place
- one relative going first, alone, generations back
- the journey itself, told as the hard thing it was
- arriving with very little
- the rough stretch after, with no steady work at all at first
- where they first stayed, borrowed from another family, not owned
- the point work was finally found, a day's labor here and there at first
- the rest of the family being sent for once there was enough saved to bring them over
- a little over two years apart before the family was whole again
- a cousin who did not survive the journey, spoken of only by that relation
- one object carried from the old place that is still kept to this day
- a trade taken up and kept for good
- a place to live found and settled into within the first few years
- children later born right there, which is what finally made the place home
- how the story is told now, passed down the way it is being told here, first heard as a child
held:
- one relative went first, alone, generations back
- the family stayed apart for a little over two years before being reunited
- a cousin did not survive the journey, spoken of only by that relation, never a name
- one object was carried from the old place and is still kept
- children were later born in the new place, marking it home
- the push to leave is named only as whatever it was, not pinned to one cause
- the first stretch had no steady work at all
- the first shelter was borrowed from another family, not owned
- work was eventually found, small at first, a day's labor here and there
- the rest of the family was sent for only once there was enough saved to bring them over
- a trade was taken up and kept for good
- a place to live was found and settled into within the first few years
- the story has been told down at least one generation before reaching the narrator
- the narrator first heard it as a child, the same way it is being told now
cast: the one who went first, a relative from generations back; those who followed later; a cousin lost along the way; the narrator, addressed as you, telling it to a descendant kept out of scene.
text: You are telling the story of how your family came to live in this place, the way it has been passed down and the way you would tell it now. Cover it in full: a brief look back at the place the family had been before, not dwelt on, just enough to set where they started, the reason for leaving, whatever it was that pushed them out of the old place, one relative going first, alone, generations back, the journey itself, told as the hard thing it was, arriving with very little, the rough stretch after, with no steady work at all at first, where they first stayed, borrowed from another family rather than owned, the point work was finally found, a day's labor here and there at first, the rest of the family being sent for once there was enough saved to bring them over, a little over two years apart before the family was whole again, a cousin who did not survive the journey, spoken of only by that relation, never a name, one object carried from the old place that is still kept to this day, a trade taken up and kept for good, a place to live found and settled into within the first few years, children later born right there, which is what finally made the place home, and how the story is told now, passed down the way you are passing it down here, remembering your own place hearing it as a child. Write the story whole.
=== end ===

scenario: the teacher everyone feared, remembered with an old classmate
zones: past-narrative,reported,exclamation
intent: two old classmates, meeting again by chance, swap memories of the teacher everyone feared.
beats:
- the chance meeting of two old classmates after years apart
- the small talk before the conversation turns to school days
- the teacher coming up, remembered as feared by the whole class
- the rule that made the teacher feared, stated plainly
- the exact punishment, remembered in detail
- reported speech, a line the teacher used to say, recalled almost word for word
- a shared memory of a punishment both of them watched happen
- one classmate's own memory of being called on and freezing up
- the story of the one student who talked back that one time
- the class's reaction to that, recalled with real amazement
- a habit or tic of the teacher's that got mocked behind their back
- one moment the teacher was, surprisingly, kind to someone
- what became of the teacher later, as far as either of them has heard
- the shift from fear to something like respect, admitted now
- the two of them laughing about it together
- one of them confessing they still feel a flicker of the old fear
held:
- the punishment was writing the same line a hundred times, every time
- one line the teacher used to say is reported almost verbatim
- one student's act of talking back is recalled, by relation to the class, not by name
- one habit of the teacher's was mocked behind their back
- both classmates now feel differently about the teacher than they did as children
- the meeting between the two classmates happens by chance, not by plan
- the small talk comes before the school talk
- the punishment memory is one both of them watched happen, not just one
- one classmate admits to freezing up when called on
- the class's reaction to the talking-back incident is real amazement, recalled fresh
- one moment of the teacher's kindness is remembered, unexpected
- neither of them knows for certain what became of the teacher, only what they heard
- one of them still feels a flicker of the old fear even now
- the conversation moves on afterward to other catching up
cast: two old classmates catching up; the teacher, remembered, out of scene; they label their own turns however such people would call each other.
text: Two old classmates run into each other after years apart and the talk turns to school. Write the exchange: the small talk first, then someone brings up the teacher everyone feared, the rule that made the teacher feared stated plainly, the punishment remembered exactly, writing the same line out a hundred times, every time, one of them recalling almost word for word a line the teacher used to say to the whole class, a shared memory of a punishment they both watched happen, one of them admitting they still remember freezing up when called on, the story of the one time a student talked back and got away with it, told with real amazement even now, a habit or tic of the teacher's that the class used to mock behind their back, one moment, unexpected, when the teacher was kind to someone, what either of them has heard became of the teacher since, neither of them knowing for certain, the two of them agreeing that fear has turned, looking back, into something more like respect, real laughter shared over it, and one of them admitting they still feel a flicker of the old fear even now. Let the talk drift on afterward to catching up on everything else. They label their own turns however such people would call each other. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

=== scenario 100 | memory | M | l | control ===
scenario: a year of hardship survived, told to encourage someone in one now
zones: past-narrative,money,temporal
intent: someone tells a younger person, struggling through a hard year now, about the worst year they themselves survived.
beats:
- naming that one particular year was the hardest, without needing to date it
- what started it, the loss of steady work or income
- what got given up first, small comforts
- what got given up next, bigger sacrifices
- the awkwardness of accepting or borrowing help from someone
- the stretch of close to six months with barely any work at all
- the specific day that felt like the very bottom of it
- a small stroke of luck or unexpected help that kept things from getting worse
- work found again afterward, small at first
- the slow climb back that took about eight months
- what was learned about who could really be counted on
- what was learned about the teller themselves
- the point, looking back, where it started to feel survivable
- turning to address the listener directly, naming that they are in their own hard year now
- closing with something real offered going forward
held:
- the hard year is named only as "that year," not pinned to a calendar date
- the low-work stretch lasted close to six months
- the help borrowed was enough to get through a couple of months, not a named amount
- the climb back took about eight months
- the offer of help at the end is concrete, time, help, or presence promised going forward
- what started the year was a loss of steady work or income
- small comforts were given up first
- bigger sacrifices came after that
- accepting help was awkward, since it had never been needed before
- one specific day is named as the lowest point
- one small stroke of luck or unexpected help kept things from getting worse
- work found again afterward is described as small at first
- one lesson learned concerns who could actually be counted on
- one lesson learned concerns the teller themselves
- the listener is told directly that they are known to be in a hard year now
cast: the one telling it, addressed as you; the listener, a younger person in their own hard year, referenced but kept out of scene; those who helped during the hard year, held only as roles.
text: You once lived through the hardest year you have had, and you are telling it now to someone going through their own hard stretch, to give them something to hold onto. Tell it in full: name that one year as the worst without pinning it to a calendar date, what started it, the loss of steady work or income, what got given up first, the small comforts, and then what got given up after that, the bigger sacrifices, the awkwardness of accepting help or borrowing from someone, enough to get through a couple of months, when you had never had to before, the stretch of close to six months with barely any work at all, the specific day that felt like the very bottom of it, a small stroke of luck or unexpected help that kept things from getting worse, work found again afterward, small at first, the slow climb back that took about eight months, what you learned about who could actually be counted on, what you learned about yourself in the middle of it, and the point, looking back, where it finally started to feel survivable. Then turn to the person you are telling this to, name plainly that you know they are in their own hard year right now, and close with something real you are offering them, your time, your help, your presence, going forward. Write it as you would say it to them.
=== end ===

scenario: the reunion: who changed, who did not, reported after
zones: comparative,past-narrative,reported
intent: two people who went to a reunion recount afterward, to someone who was not there, who had changed and who had not.
beats:
- one of them recapping the reunion to someone who was not there
- noting someone who had changed almost beyond recognition
- noting someone else who seemed exactly the same
- a direct comparison drawn between how one person was then and how they are now
- word about who was not there and why, passed along secondhand
- one piece of surprising news learned at the reunion, about how someone's life turned out
- the two of them turning the comparison on themselves, agreeing on how much they have or have not changed
- closing with a plan or a wish to do it again
held:
- one person changed almost beyond recognition
- one person seemed exactly the same
- one piece of secondhand news concerns someone who was not there
- one piece of surprising news concerns how a life turned out
- the two recounting agree on their own degree of change
- the comparison of the changed person is drawn directly against how they used to be
- a plan or a wish to meet again closes the exchange
cast: two people recounting the reunion together, former age-mates; the listener, out of scene; they label their own turns however such people would call each other.
text: Two people who went to a reunion are telling someone who was not there how it went. Write the exchange: one of them starts recapping, noting someone who had changed almost beyond recognition, and someone else who seemed exactly the same, a direct comparison drawn between how one of those people was back then and how they are now, word passed along about who did not show up and why, one piece of surprising news learned there about how somebody's life actually turned out, and the two of them turning the comparison on themselves, agreeing, more or less, on how much they have or have not changed. Close with a plan, or at least a wish, to do it again before as much time passes. They label their own turns however such people would call each other. Write the exchange.
=== end ===

=== scenario 102 | memory | M | l | corpus ===
scenario: a memoir of the childhood home and street, room by room
zones: past-narrative,spatial,habitual
intent: someone walks, room by room, through the childhood home and street they grew up on.
beats:
- starting at the entrance, what arriving home always felt like
- the main room, what usually went on in it
- the kitchen, its smell and one habit tied to it
- where everyone slept, who shared with whom
- the spot in the house that belonged, in everyone's mind, to one relative
- a hiding place or a corner claimed as one's own
- the yard or the space just outside the door
- the neighbors, close enough to be seen or heard from the house
- a sound the street made at the same time every day
- a sight that passed at the same time every day
- one feature of the house that was always broken or always talked about
- how the place felt different as the seasons turned
- games or gatherings that happened right out on the street
- the last clear memory of being there
- what the place is now, still standing, changed, or gone
- closing, turning the memory back to whoever is listening
held:
- who shared a room with whom is stated plainly
- one house feature was always broken or notable
- one recurring street sound happened at the same time every day
- one recurring sight passed at the same time every day
- what the place is now is stated plainly at the end
- the entrance is remembered by a specific feeling of arriving
- the main room is tied to one recurring activity
- the kitchen is tied to one specific smell and one habit
- one spot in the house belonged, in everyone's mind, to one relative
- one hiding place or corner was claimed as the narrator's own
- the yard or outside space is described as its own place
- the neighbors are close enough to be seen or heard from inside
- the house felt different across the turning seasons
- games or gatherings happened out on the street itself
- one last clear memory marks the end of living there
cast: the one remembering, addressed as you; siblings and other relatives, held only by relation; neighbors, held only by relation to the household; the listener, out of scene.
text: You are walking someone through the home and street you grew up on, room by room, the way you would if you were standing in the doorway pointing things out. Cover it in full: the entrance and what arriving there always felt like, the main room and what usually went on in it, the kitchen, its smell and the one habit that always happened there, where everyone slept and who shared a room with whom, the spot in the house that belonged, in everyone's mind, to one relative, a hiding place or a corner you thought of as your own, the yard or the space just outside the door, the neighbors, close enough to be seen or heard from the house, a sound the street made at the same time every day, a sight that passed at the same time every day, one feature of the house that was always broken or always being talked about, how the place felt different as the seasons turned, games or gatherings that happened right out on the street, the last clear memory you have of being there, and what the place is now, whether it is still standing, changed past knowing, or gone altogether. Close by turning back to whoever you are telling this to. Write the memoir room by room.
=== end ===
